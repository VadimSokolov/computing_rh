# Chapter 22. Friable approximants of Xi after Miyatake and the cosh ladder

Which real zeros does the friable approximant of the Riemann Xi function share with Xi, and what does this test case say about computational criteria for the Riemann Hypothesis (RH)? Keep only the integers composed of the primes up to $P$ (the $P$ friable, or smooth, integers) in Riemann's theta series. The result, the friable approximant $\Xi_P$, differs from $\Xi$ by an exponentially small deficit, of size about $4\pi q^2e^{-\pi q^2}$ with $q$ the first prime left out, and yet has infinitely many zeros off the real line. The chapter proves that the folded kernel has a kink, that no summable reweighting of the theta series has infinitely many real zeros, that the deficit is positive and a sum of Lorentzians, and that real zeros live in the negative humps of $\Xi$ and are lost in pairs, with an exact count given the verification of RH to a finite height. A heuristic formula (checked by computation) puts the breakdown at height about $4q^2$. It then reads an identity of Miyatake probabilistically (Lévy's stochastic area, a Pólya Gamma clock, a logistic partition of unity), studies the exponential tail and the threshold $b=2$ of the transform of the kernel divided by $\cosh(bu)$, and places the bottom of the cosh ladder against the quasi Riemann abscissa, with a Hermite Biehler equivalence for the paired form. Nothing here proves RH.

## From the literature

- Riemann's theta series representation of $\xi$ and the zeros of $\Xi$ on the critical line (Hardy 1914; Titchmarsh 1986)
- friable approximants and the symmetrised Pólya kernel (Miyatake 1951; Hejhal 1990), truncated theta series in incomplete gamma form (Haglund 2009)
- the cosh ladder: reciprocals of $\cosh(bu)$ times the kernel are generalised gamma convolutions for $b\ge\frac12$ (de Bruijn 1950; Lagarias 1999)
- the de Bruijn Newman constant is nonnegative (Rodgers and Tao 2020)
- verification of RH to height $3\cdot10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021); the bound $|\zeta(\frac12+it)|\ll t^{1/6}\log t$ with explicit constant (Hiary 2016)
- the Biane Pitman Yor identity (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001), the Pólya Gamma mixture (Polson, Scott and Windle 2013), Ingham's density estimate (Ingham 1940), Chebotarev's theorem (Chebotarev 1928), the Davenport Heilbronn function (Davenport and Heilbronn 1936)

## New results

Statements are proved unless marked: the imaginary axis proposition is a computation on a grid, the count conjecture and the quasi Riemann abscissa conjecture are open; the zero count heuristic and the problems are not catalogued.

- [The friable approximant of Xi is exponentially close to Xi in a strip, with the accuracy set by the first omitted prime](../RESULTS.md#friable_approximant_error_bound_in_strip_set_by_first_omitted_prime) (Theorem 22.2.1, page 763; proved): For $|\operatorname{Im}t|\le\frac32$ the friable approximant satisfies $|\Xi_P(t)-\Xi(t)|\le|t^2+\frac14|\,\delta_P$ with $\delta_P\le e^{-\pi q^2}/(\pi q^2(1-e^{-2\pi q}))$. New in this book.
- [Certified tracking: a Rouche criterion for a real zero of the friable approximant near a simple zero of Xi](../RESULTS.md#certified_tracking_of_a_simple_zero_of_xi_by_a_real_zero_of_the_friable_approximant) (Corollary 22.2.2, page 763; proved): If a simple zero $\gamma$ of $\Xi$ has $|\Xi|>|t^2+\frac14|\delta_P$ on a circle of radius $r\le\frac32$ about it, then $\Xi_P$ has exactly one zero in the disc, it is real, and its shift from $\gamma$ is given to first order by $-(\Xi_P-\Xi)(\gamma)/\Xi'(\gamma)$. New in this book.
- [The folded kernel of the friable approximant has a kink of exact size c_P, so Xi_P tends to c_P on the real line and has finitely many real zeros](../RESULTS.md#kink_constant_of_the_friable_approximant_and_finitely_many_real_zeros) (Theorem 22.2.3, page 764; proved): The folded kernel of $\Xi_P$ has a corner at the origin of size $c_P=\sum_{m\notin\mathcal F(P)}(4\pi m^2-1)e^{-\pi m^2}\sim4\pi q^2e^{-\pi q^2}>0$, so $\Xi_P(t)\to c_P$ along the real axis and $\Xi_P$ has finitely many real zeros. New in this book.
- [No reweighting of Riemann's theta series by summable weights has infinitely many real zeros](../RESULTS.md#no_summable_reweighting_of_the_theta_series_has_infinitely_many_real_zeros) (Theorem 22.2.4, page 764; proved): For nonzero real weights $w_n$ with $\sum|w_n|n^{-\sigma}<\infty$ for every $\sigma>0$ (this includes the friable truncations), the reweighted function has finitely many real zeros and infinitely many nonreal zeros. New in this book.
- [The deficit Xi_P minus Xi is positive on the real line and decreases as the prime bound grows](../RESULTS.md#deficit_of_the_friable_approximant_is_positive_and_decreasing_in_the_prime_bound) (Theorem 22.3.1, page 765; proved): For every prime $P$ and real $t$, $\Xi_P(t)-\Xi(t)>0$, and the deficit decreases strictly as $P$ increases, so $\Xi_P>\Xi_{P'}>\Xi$ on $\mathbb{R}$ for $P<P'$. New in this book.
- [Uniform Lorentzian bound: the deficit of the friable approximant is a sum of Lorentzians with an explicit uniform relative error](../RESULTS.md#deficit_of_the_friable_approximant_is_a_sum_of_lorentzians_with_explicit_error) (Theorem 22.3.3, page 766; proved): For $a\ge9\pi$ the cosine transform $C_a$ of $e^{-ae^w}e^{w/4}$ equals the Lorentzian $\mathcal L_a(t)=e^{-a}b/(b^2+t^2/4)$ with relative error at most $\delta(a)\le5.61/a$, uniformly in real $t$, and the deficit $D_P$ is the corresponding sum over the omitted integers. New in this book.
- [Curvature: the logarithm of the deficit of the friable approximant is uniformly almost convex for t at least 14](../RESULTS.md#uniform_curvature_bound_for_the_log_of_the_deficit_of_the_friable_approximant) (Theorem 22.3.4, page 766; proved): For every prime $P$ and $t\ge14$, $(\log D_P)''(t)\ge-0.0239$ for the deficit $D_P=\Xi_P-\Xi$, with an explicit bound in terms of the least omitted prime. New in this book.
- [Real zeros of the friable approximant live in the negative humps of Xi, in pairs, and surviving humps are nested in P](../RESULTS.md#real_zeros_of_the_friable_approximant_live_in_negative_humps_of_xi_in_pairs) (Theorem 22.4.1, page 767; proved): Every real zero of $\Xi_P$ lies in a negative hump of $\Xi$ and each hump holds an even number of zeros; for humps below the verified height of RH and shorter than $18.29$, a hump holds none, one double or two simple zeros, according to the sign of $\max(|\Xi|-D_P)$ on it, and surviving humps are nested in $P$. New in this book.
- [Count: the number of positive real zeros of the friable approximant is twice the number of negative humps of Xi deeper than the deficit](../RESULTS.md#count_of_real_zeros_of_the_friable_approximant_is_twice_the_number_of_deep_humps) (Theorem 22.4.2, page 768; proved): Under the hypotheses of the hump theorem the number $R_P$ of positive real zeros of $\Xi_P$ is twice the number of humps with $\max(|\Xi|-D_P)>0$, there is a unique height $\tau_P^+$ beyond which $\Xi_P>0$, and $R_P\le2\lceil N(\tau_P^+)/2\rceil$. New in this book.
- [Conjecture: the number of real zeros of the friable approximant is the zero count at the breakdown height plus a correction from the peaks of Hardy's function](../RESULTS.md#conjecture_on_the_number_of_real_zeros_of_the_friable_approximant_and_the_peaks_of_hardys_function) (Conjecture 22.5.1, page 770; open): The number $R_P$ of positive real zeros of $\Xi_P$ is conjectured to equal $N(\tau_P^*)$ plus $\frac2{\pi^2}\log\frac{\tau_P^*}{2\pi}$ times the mean log peak of Hardy's function on negative humps near $\tau_P^*$, up to an error of order $\sqrt{\log q\,\log\log q}$. New in this book.
- [Kink budget: the limit of the friable approximant with a smooth even kernel replacing the pole term](../RESULTS.md#kink_budget_when_the_pole_kernel_is_replaced_by_a_smooth_even_kernel) (Proposition 22.8.1, page 774; proved): If the pole kernel $e^{-u/2}$ in the folded kernel of $\Xi_P$ is replaced by an even smooth kernel $\kappa$ with derivatives $O(e^{-u/2})$, the limit of the modified function along the real axis is $c_P-\frac12-\kappa'(0+)$. New in this book.
- [Reweighting: among weights in [0,1] vanishing on a given set, the indicator of the complement has the smallest deficit](../RESULTS.md#reweighting_with_weights_in_zero_one_has_smallest_deficit_for_the_indicator_of_the_complement) (Proposition 22.8.2, page 775; proved): For weights $0\le w_n\le w_n'\le1$ the deficits satisfy $D_w\ge D_{w'}\ge0$ with $D_{w'}>0$ unless all $w_n'=1$, so the friable truncation has the smallest deficit among weights in $[0,1]$ that vanish on the omitted set. New in this book.
- [The friable approximants for P = 2 and 3 are uniformly close to Xi on the imaginary axis and have no zero there](../RESULTS.md#friable_approximant_is_uniformly_close_to_xi_on_the_imaginary_axis_for_p_2_and_3) (Proposition 22.9.1, page 776; computed): The relative error $\sup_y|\Xi_P(iy)/\Xi(iy)-1|$ is at most $6.8\times10^{-13}$ for $P=2$ and $3.4\times10^{-35}$ for $P=3$, so neither has a zero on the imaginary axis; the suprema of a proved bound were computed on a grid. New in this book.
- [Shift identity: Xi is the average of two shifts of the transform of Phi divided by cosh, which contains Miyatake's identity](../RESULTS.md#shift_identity_xi_is_the_cosine_of_a_shift_applied_to_the_transform_of_phi_over_cosh) (Proposition 22.11.1, page 779; proved): For every $b>0$ and complex $z$, $\Xi(z)=\frac12\{g_b(z+ib)+g_b(z-ib)\}$, that is $\Xi=\cos(b\,d/dz)g_b$; Miyatake's identity is the case $b=\frac92$. New in this book.
- [Levy area, Polya Gamma clock and Thorin atoms: the transform of Phi over cosh is Xi smoothed by Levy's stochastic area](../RESULTS.md#transform_of_phi_over_cosh_is_a_levy_area_smoothing_and_a_polya_gamma_mixture_of_the_flow) (Theorem 22.11.2, page 779; proved): The transform $g_b$ equals the expectation of $\Xi(x+2b\mathcal A)$ for Lévy's stochastic area $\mathcal A$, and the expectation of the de Bruijn Newman function at a Pólya Gamma random negative time, so $\Xi\mapsto g_b$ is convolution with an extended generalised gamma convolution with Thorin atoms at the odd multiples of $\pi/(2b)$. New in this book.
- [Logistic partition: 2 xi is the sum of two logistic weighted expectations of a power of a random variable](../RESULTS.md#logistic_partition_of_unity_splits_xi_into_two_pieces_of_the_transform_over_cosh) (Proposition 22.11.3, page 780; proved): For $c>0$, $2\xi(s)$ is the sum of the expectations of $\Upsilon^s/(1+\Upsilon^c)$ and $\Upsilon^{s+c}/(1+\Upsilon^c)$, for the random variable $\Upsilon$ of the Biane Pitman Yor identity, and the logistic weight is a Pólya Gamma mixture. New in this book.
- [Exponential tail: the transform of Phi over cosh has a residue term C_b e^{-pi z/(2b)} exactly when b exceeds 2](../RESULTS.md#exponential_tail_of_the_transform_of_phi_over_cosh_appears_exactly_above_b_equal_two) (Theorem 22.12.1, page 780; proved): For $b>2$ the transform $g_b$ equals $C_be^{-\pi z/(2b)}$ plus a faster decaying error, with $C_b=\frac{2\pi}b\Phi(\frac{i\pi}{2b})$, uniformly in horizontal strips; for $b<2$ there is no residue and $g_b(x)=O(e^{-\tau x})$ for every $\tau<\pi/4$. New in this book.
- [Cusp asymptotics: the residue constant C_b of the transform of Phi over cosh as b decreases to 2](../RESULTS.md#cusp_asymptotics_of_the_residue_constant_as_b_decreases_to_two) (Theorem 22.12.2, page 781; proved): With $\delta=\pi(b-2)/(4b)$, as $b\downarrow2$ the constant $C_b$ is asymptotic to $-\frac{\pi^3}{32b}(2\delta)^{-1/2}\delta^{-4}e^{-\frac\pi8\cot\delta}$ and is negative and super exponentially small. New in this book.
- [For b above 2 with C_b nonzero the transform of Phi over cosh has finitely many real zeros and is not in the Laguerre Polya class](../RESULTS.md#transform_of_phi_over_cosh_for_b_above_two_is_not_in_the_laguerre_polya_class) (Corollary 22.12.3, page 781; proved): For $b>2$ with $C_b\ne0$ the transform $g_b$ has finitely many real zeros and infinitely many nonreal zeros, so it is not in the Laguerre Pólya class; in particular Miyatake's transform ($b=\frac92$, $C_{9/2}>0$) is not. New in this book.
- [The bottom of the cosh ladder is at most the largest real part of a zero of zeta minus one half](../RESULTS.md#bottom_of_the_cosh_ladder_is_bounded_by_the_quasi_riemann_abscissa) (Proposition 22.13.1, page 783; proved): The bottom rung $B_{\cosh}$ of the cosh ladder satisfies $B_{\cosh}\le\sigma_{\max}-\frac12$, where $\sigma_{\max}$ is the supremum of the real parts of the nontrivial zeros of zeta. New in this book.
- [Conjecture: the bottom of the cosh ladder lies between the asymptotic and the global quasi Riemann abscissa](../RESULTS.md#conjecture_bottom_of_the_cosh_ladder_equals_the_asymptotic_quasi_riemann_abscissa) (Conjecture 22.13.2, page 784; open): The bottom rung of the cosh ladder is conjectured to satisfy $\sigma_{\max}^\infty-\frac12\le B_{\cosh}\le\sigma_{\max}-\frac12$ with $\sigma_{\max}^\infty=\limsup_{\gamma\to\infty}\beta$; the upper bound is proved. New in this book.
- [Paired form: zeta has no zero to the right of alpha exactly when the real and imaginary parts of xi on the line alpha interlace](../RESULTS.md#paired_form_of_the_cosh_ladder_hermite_biehler_equivalence_with_zero_free_half_plane) (Theorem 22.13.3, page 785; proved): For $\alpha>\frac12$, zeta has no zero with real part at least $\alpha$ exactly when $E_\alpha(z)=\xi(\alpha-iz)$ is Hermite Biehler, exactly when the real and imaginary parts of $\xi(\alpha+it)$ have only real simple strictly interlacing zeros; hence RH holds if and only if this holds for every $\alpha>\frac12$. New in this book.
- [Real zeros of the real and imaginary parts of xi on a vertical line right of one half have asymptotically full density](../RESULTS.md#density_of_real_zeros_of_the_real_and_imaginary_parts_of_xi_on_a_line_to_the_right_of_one_half) (Theorem 22.13.4, page 787; proved): For $\alpha>\frac12$ with no zero of zeta on the line, $\theta_\alpha(T)-\theta_\alpha(-T)=\pi(N^\pm(T)-2N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T))+O(\log T)$, so $\operatorname{Re}\xi(\alpha+it)$ and $\operatorname{Im}\xi(\alpha+it)$ each have at least $N^\pm(T)-2N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T)-O(\log T)$ real zeros in $[-T,T]$. New in this book.
