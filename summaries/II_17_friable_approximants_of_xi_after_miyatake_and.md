# Volume II, Chapter 17. Friable approximants of Xi after Miyatake and the cosh ladder

Which real zeros does the friable approximant of the Riemann Xi function share with Xi, and what does this test case say about computational criteria for the Riemann Hypothesis (RH)? Keep only the integers composed of the primes up to $P$ (the $P$ friable, or smooth, integers) in Riemann's theta series. The result, the friable approximant $\Xi_P$, differs from $\Xi$ by an exponentially small deficit, of size about $4\pi q^2e^{-\pi q^2}$ with $q$ the first prime left out, and yet has infinitely many zeros off the real line. The chapter proves that the folded kernel has a kink, that no summable reweighting of the theta series has infinitely many real zeros, that the deficit is positive and a sum of Lorentzians, and that real zeros live in the negative humps of $\Xi$ and are lost in pairs, with an exact count given the verification of RH to a finite height. A heuristic formula (checked by computation) puts the breakdown at height about $4q^2$. It then reads an identity of Miyatake probabilistically (Lévy's stochastic area, a Pólya Gamma clock, a logistic partition of unity), studies the exponential tail and the threshold $b=2$ of the transform of the kernel divided by $\cosh(bu)$, and places the bottom of the cosh ladder against the quasi Riemann abscissa, with a Hermite Biehler equivalence for the paired form. Nothing here proves RH. The unregularised symmetrised Euler product has zeros near its poles whose real parts tend to one (a note of Nicholas Polson). A ball arithmetic computation shows that the truncated two term family has a nonreal zero near height 188.43 for every truncation $N\ge3$, so those spins are not Lee Yang.

## From the literature

- Riemann's theta series representation of $\xi$ and the zeros of $\Xi$ on the critical line (Hardy 1914; Titchmarsh 1986)
- Friable approximants and the symmetrised Pólya kernel (Miyatake 1951; Hejhal 1990), truncated theta series in incomplete gamma form (Haglund 2009)
- The cosh ladder: reciprocals of $\cosh(bu)$ times the kernel are generalised gamma convolutions for $b\ge\frac12$ (de Bruijn 1950; Lagarias 1999)
- The de Bruijn Newman constant is nonnegative (Rodgers and Tao 2020)
- Verification of RH to height $3\cdot10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021); the bound $|\zeta(\frac12+it)|\ll t^{1/6}\log t$ with explicit constant (Hiary 2016)
- The Biane Pitman Yor identity (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001), the Pólya Gamma mixture (Polson, Scott and Windle 2013), Ingham's density estimate (Ingham 1940), Chebotarev's theorem (Chebotarev 1928), the Davenport Heilbronn function (Davenport and Heilbronn 1936)

## New results

Statements are proved unless marked: the imaginary axis proposition is a computation on a grid, the count conjecture and the quasi Riemann abscissa conjecture are open; the zero count heuristic and the problems are not catalogued. The Pólya mixture section adds the polar part of the Miyatake kernel at $\cos\frac{2\pi}9$, the proof that this exponent is forced, and a Meixner convolution form of the polar transform; the interlacing subsection adds Euler product criteria for the zeros of the real and imaginary parts of $\xi$ on two lines to alternate or not, and a collision law for one pair of zeros under a random vertical average (proved). A note of Nicholas Polson adds the positive transform of every theta term with the Laplace law as a mixture (proved) and computed real and nonreal zeros of the theta series cut after one or two terms.
