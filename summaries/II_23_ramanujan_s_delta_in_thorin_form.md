What happens when the level dictionary of the preceding chapter is applied to Ramanujan's discriminant form $\Delta$, whose $L$ function $L(s,\Delta)=\sum\tau(n)n^{-s}$ has the shape of $\zeta$ with every ingredient explicit? This chapter is a reworking of unrefereed notes of Polson (November 2026), assembled from four of them after a referee's reading, and only what survived that reading is kept. Every result carries one of the status words proved, computed, conditional, heuristic or open, no priority is claimed for anything in it, and the classical identities (Jacobi's identity, the Hadamard product, Deligne's theorem) are credited as such. The unrefereed zero free half plane of OpenAI (2026) is never used as a theorem. The flow material for $\Delta$ (the Thorin flow, the Laguerre form, the Pólya approximant and the velocity table) sits in Volume I Chapter 16, next to the de Bruijn Newman section, and is not repeated here.

**Setting.** Put $\Lambda(s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(s,\Delta)$, entire with $\Lambda(s)=\Lambda(12-s)$, real and positive on the real line because $\Delta(iw)=e^{-2\pi w}\prod(1-e^{-2\pi nw})^{24}>0$. Its zeros lie in $\frac{11}2<\operatorname{Re}s<\frac{13}2$, and $\mathrm{RH}_\Delta$ says they lie on $\operatorname{Re}s=6$. By Dobner and de Bruijn the de Bruijn Newman constant of $\Delta$ lies in $[0,\frac18]$. The kernel $\Phi_\Delta$ is positive and even, and $(\log\Phi_\Delta)''$ has maximum $-7.78927$ at the origin, below $-2\pi$ (computed). Proposition `dl:prop:admissible` (proved) says the centred function $\Lambda(s+\frac{11}2)$ is admissible, so the dictionary of the Iwaniec chapter applies verbatim.

**The level dictionary.** Theorem `dl:thm:level` (proved) states, for $\alpha\ge6$, that three conditions are equivalent: $\Lambda$ has no zero with real part above $\alpha$, the function $\Lambda(\alpha)/\Lambda(\alpha+\sqrt s)$ is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC) transform, and $\operatorname{Re}\Lambda'/\Lambda>0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha$. The Thorin measure has no drift and, in the variable $r=\sqrt x$, density $\frac1\pi\operatorname{Re}\Lambda'/\Lambda(\alpha+ir)$, the sum of Poisson terms over all zeros; zeros on the level give atoms. Corollary `dl:cor:rh` (proved) records $\mathrm{RH}_\Delta$ as the case $\alpha=6$ (the Lagarias Hinkkanen criterion for $\Delta$), with unit atoms at $\gamma^2$, and the unconditional GGC property for every $\alpha\ge\frac{13}2$. The descent from a level to a lower one is the Cauchy semigroup, legitimate only while the lower level stays at or above the largest real part of a zero. Strong log concavity of the kernel gives, by Brascamp Lieb, $\operatorname{Var}V_\alpha\le\frac1{2\pi}$ (proved), and $\operatorname{Var}V_6=0.1123518$ (computed).

**Lévy density and the square root.** Theorem `dl:thm:levy` (proved) gives, for $\alpha$ above the largest real part, the Lévy density of the GGC as an explicit sum over zeros of $\operatorname{Re}[e^{x(\alpha-\rho)^2}\operatorname{erfc}((\alpha-\rho)\sqrt x)]$, nonnegative. Remark `dl:rem:sqrt` explains why the square root matters: without it each conjugate pair contributes an oscillating signed Lévy density, and the reciprocal is never a GGC transform (proved), while the stable half subordinator averages the oscillation into a positive kernel. Proposition `dl:prop:deligne` (proved) shows that Deligne's bound is equivalent to the Euler product of $\zeta^2/L(\cdot+\frac{11}2,\Delta)$ having nonnegative exponent coefficients $4\sin^2(k\theta_p/2)/k$, hence to a compound Poisson law with discrete Lévy measure. This is the only place Deligne's bound enters, and the resulting law is infinitely divisible but not a GGC. A later subsection on Hecke relations (from an unrefereed note) proves that for every level one eigenform the Euler side of the Thorin density of $L(s,f)$ is negative on an interval, so it is never a GGC on its own, while $|S_{f,\beta}|\le2S_{\zeta,\beta}$ and the Hecke cone of products of symmetric power $L$ functions with nonnegative weights are proved. Positivity of the compensated density $\tau_\alpha$ for $\alpha>12.4$ is only computed, from a prime sum argument and high precision checks at $\alpha=8$ and $9$, not certified. The prime formula for the Thorin density (the Weil explicit formula at the Poisson kernel of height $\alpha-6$) converges absolutely exactly for $\alpha>\frac{13}2$ (proved).

**Tables and a window.** The first ten zeros of $\Lambda(6+it)$ run from $9.222379$ to $32.77488$, with $27$ zeros below height $60$ (computed). The Thorin density at level $7$ is $0.035637$ at the origin, and the minimum over $0\le r\le35$ sits at $r=0$ and scales like $\alpha-6$, with ratio rising to $0.035763=\operatorname{Var}V_6/\pi$ (computed). Proposition `dl:prop:window` (proved, given Keiper's computation that every zero with $|\gamma|\le6397$ is on the line) shows that the density is positive for every $\alpha>6$ and $|r|\le6396$, by pairing each off line zero with its partner under $\rho\mapsto12-\bar\rho$. It replaces a tempting but wrong argument that bounds far zeros by their size. The Turán inequalities for the moments of $V_6$ hold for $k\le40$ with a margin peaking at $0.0344$ near $k=3$ (computed); they are weak, and necessary only.

**The sphere.** Theorem `dl:thm:sphere` (proved) identifies $\prod(1-e^{-nt})^3$ with $4\pi p_t(x,-x)$, the antipodal heat kernel of Brownian motion on $S^2$, and with the distribution function of the maximum of three copies of $\sup_nE_n/n$. Hence $\Delta(iw)=e^{-2\pi w}[4\pi p_{2\pi w}(x,-x)]^8$, because $24=8\times3$. Three readings follow: the circle analogue is Kolmogorov's distribution, eight spherical motions all at the antipode, and a parity weighted rigid rotor partition function. The power hierarchy $\eta^{3m}$ for $m=1,2,4,8$ ($m=1$ is $L(s,\chi_{-4})$ with ordinates halved, $m=8$ is $\Delta$) has recomputed zero counts on $[0,60]$ in a table (computed); the flow columns of the source note were not reproduced by the referee and are left out, and nothing singles out $m=8$. Remark `dl:rem:trunc` (computed) reports that truncating the Jacobi series throws zeros off the axis for $K=1,2$, for the truncations tested only.

**Hitting times.** Under $\mathrm{RH}_\Delta$ the variable with transform $\Lambda(6)/\Lambda(6+\sqrt s)$ has mean $0.05618$ and, for simple zeros, is a Krein string hitting time, the Volume I statement shifted and not new. Proposition `dl:prop:sub` (proved, a corollary of the basepoint subordination theorem) writes $T_\alpha$ above the line as the conditioned variable plus a Brownian first passage time at a level proportional to it, with a pairwise form that needs no hypothesis. A table of clock probabilities and Laplace transforms is computed from $\Lambda$.

**Primes, truncations and the Möbius mixture.** Remark `dl:rem:r1` (computed) gives the Thorin density at level $\frac{13}2$ and a heuristic reason why Riesz smoothing of the prime sum blurs the structure; a total variation comparison with Keiper's zeros has not been done. Proposition `dl:prop:r4` (computed) shows that the symmetrised truncations $F_N$ of the product have nonreal zeros for $N\le2$, with an explicit one at $28.79373902+7.55852217i$, and none found for $N=3,4$; a second symmetrisation through the functional equation also fails at $N=1$, so the route is closed for the symmetrisations tried and not for every one. Proposition `dl:prop:r5` (proved) shows that no positive mixture of the form $\sum_n\mu(n)n^{-1}g(x/n^2)$ with $g$ the Laplace transform of a variable with the stated moments is eventually of one sign, by Landau's theorem for one signed Mellin integrals. Remark `dl:rem:r6` (computed) gives the limits $0.01471$ for $\zeta$ and $0.035763$ for $\Delta$ of the density at the origin divided by the height.

**Ramanujan's formula as a density.** Returning to $\zeta$, Theorem `dl:thm:Z` (proved) turns Ramanujan's lost notebook formula, first proved by Berndt and Dixit, into the density of $Z=\frac12(U_1+U_2)+E$, with $U_i$ Pólya variables and $E$ an explicit EGGC with Thorin atoms at $\pm1$ and $\pm(3+4k)$. The density is a completely monotone function of $e^{2x}$ (by Binet) plus an elementary term, $f_Z(0)=0.2270675$ (computed), the inequality $\frac{\gamma-\log2\pi\alpha}{2\alpha}+\sum_k\phi(k\alpha)<0$ follows unconditionally, and RH holds if and only if every zero of the characteristic function in $|\operatorname{Im}t|<1$ is real. Remark `dl:rem:Zid` (proved) shows that $Z$ is not infinitely divisible, since its characteristic function vanishes at real $t$, so no EGGC route transfers to it.

**Is this a path?** No (`dl:sec:path`). The level theorem is an equivalence and reformulates $\mathrm{RH}_\Delta$, the window result is a consequence of the computation it assumes, the sphere and hierarchy restate the problem, the Turán table is a weak necessary condition, and the truncations fail for small $N$. A proof would still have to exclude a pole of $\Lambda(\alpha)/\Lambda(\alpha+\sqrt s)$ off the cut for every $\alpha\in(6,\frac{13}2)$, where the Euler product is not known to converge.

**Open problems.** Problem `dl:prob:primes` (open) asks for the density at level $\frac{13}2$ from the prime formula by a smoothing that does not blur the Poisson kernel, compared in total variation with Keiper's zeros. Problem `dl:prob:lee` (open) asks for which $N\ge3$ the transform of the truncated even kernel is real rooted.
