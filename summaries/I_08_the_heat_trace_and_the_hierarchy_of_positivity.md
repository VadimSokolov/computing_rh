# Volume I, Chapter 8. The heat trace and the hierarchy of positivity

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 8. How much of the Thorin condition on the reciprocal Xi function, which is the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), follows from sign conditions on the heat trace $W(t)=\sum_k e^{-a_kt}$ of the zeros of the Riemann $\xi$ function, and what can finite information not decide? The chapter climbs the Bondesson hierarchy from the bottom: each rung is a sign condition on $W$, the lower rungs are unconditional theorems proved from a finite verification of RH (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and Weil's explicit formula for the primes, and the top rung is RH. It defines the Thorin functional from the gamma factor and the primes with no reference to a zero, and proves that $W$ is positive and decreasing, so that the clock is infinitely divisible and self decomposable. It shows that the derivatives of $W$ alternate in sign up to order $1.16\times10^{25}$ by a window theorem (positivity of the Williamson measures), and that $W$ is completely monotone on $(0,1.70]$, the proof running in ball arithmetic. Positive semidefinite Hankel matrices of the derivatives at one time are equivalent to RH, the numbers of negative eigenvalues of Hankel blocks of Stieltjes moments tend to the number of off line quadruples of zeros, and RH is equivalent to nonnegativity of a Gaussian smoothing of the zeros along a ray. An unconditional compound Poisson defect describes what the off line zeros add to the clock. A perturbation of $\xi$ with four off line zeros passes finite heat trace tests, so finite information cannot decide RH, and a certified zero of a Mellin transform of the Pólya density closes the Bernstein Pick route. The chapter also compares the spacings of the first 1700 zeros with the Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE) surmise and measures the cell distance to first order. The exercises of the chapter are included.

## From the literature

- Weil's explicit formula (Weil 1952) and the verification of RH to a finite height (Platt and Trudgian 2021; Hutchinson 1925)
- Self decomposability of laws with $W(t)/t$ as Lévy density (Sato 1999), unimodality (Yamazato 1978), Mase's theorem on indecomposable factors (Mase 1975), Linnik's class $I_0$ (Linnik 1964)
- Williamson's theorem on $k$ monotone functions (Williamson 1956)
- Hermite's count of nonreal roots by Hankel forms (Hermite 1856), with Grommer (1914), Chebotarev (1928) and Baricz and Štampach (2018)
- The explicit Riemann von Mangoldt formula (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022)
- Solutions of the van Dantzig problem (Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar 2024)
- Pair correlation (Montgomery 1973) and spacings of zeros (Odlyzko 1987), with the Wigner surmise for the GUE
- Zero density estimates (Ingham 1940; Guth and Maynard 2026)

- [The clock is infinitely divisible and self decomposable, unconditionally](../RESULTS.md#clock_infinitely_divisible_and_self_decomposable) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.1, page 100; proved): Using only the verification of RH below height 1000, the heat trace is positive and decreasing, so the clock is the Laplace transform of an infinitely divisible, self decomposable law with Lévy density W(t)/t, with the numerical inequalities of the proof certified in ball arithmetic. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Complete asymptotic expansion of the heat trace W(t) at t = 0 with the coefficients d_n and b_k, and the logarithmic divergence of the clock](../RESULTS.md#small_time_expansion_of_heat_trace_w_at_the_origin_with_euler_number_coefficients) (Volume I, Result 8.1.1, page 100; proved): With no hypothesis, $W(t)$ has the asymptotic expansion $\frac{\log(1/t)-\gamma_E-4\log2-2\log\pi}{8\sqrt{\pi t}}+\sum d_nt^n+\sum b_kt^{k-1/2}$ as $t\downarrow0$, with $d_0=\frac78$; the two term case is in Polson 2017, and the integral of $W(t)/t$ from $\tau$ diverges so the clock has infinitely many jumps. New in this book.
- [Truncation error of the residues of the reciprocal clock when the product over zeros is cut after K factors](../RESULTS.md#truncation_error_of_residues_of_reciprocal_xi_product_over_first_zeros) (Volume I, Proposition 8.1.3, page 103; proved): For simple zeros, the residue of the truncated product at the pole $-a_k$ differs from the true residue by the factor $\prod_{j>K}(1-a_k/a_j)$, which under RH is close to one exactly for $\gamma_k\le(\gamma_K/\log\gamma_K)^{1/2}$ and not beyond. New in this book.
- [Hankel positivity of the heat trace at one time is equivalent to RH](../RESULTS.md#hankel_positivity_at_one_time_iff_rh) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.4, page 104; proved): At any single time t, positive semidefiniteness of the two Hankel matrices of derivatives of the heat trace, equivalently of Weil's quadratic form on the span of r^m exp(-r^2 t/2), is equivalent to RH. First stated in Polson (2017, version 24 of 2026), On Hilbert's 8th problem, arXiv:1708.02653.
- [RH is equivalent to boundedness of the heat trace of the zeros on a vertical line](../RESULTS.md#rh_equivalent_to_boundedness_of_heat_trace_on_vertical_line) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.5, page 104; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if the heat trace $v\mapsto W(\sigma+iv)$ of the zeros is bounded on the real line, for one, equivalently every, $\sigma>0$. New in this book.
- [Positive definite Hankel blocks of the heat trace up to size 3 x 10^7](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_hankel_blocks_positive_definite_to_size_3e7) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.6, page 105; proved): If every zero of xi below height 20000 N (1 + log N)^(1/2) lies on the critical line, the leading N by N Hankel blocks of the derivatives of the heat trace are positive definite at every time, and the verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12 gives this unconditionally for every N up to 3 times 10^7. First stated in Polson (2017, version 24 of 2026), On Hilbert's 8th problem, arXiv:1708.02653.
- [Derivative signs of the heat trace up to order 10^17](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_derivative_signs_to_order_1e17) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.7, page 106; proved): Using only the verification of RH below height 10^12, the derivatives of the heat trace alternate in sign, (-1)^k W^(k)(t) > 0, for every t > 0 and every order k up to 10^17. First stated in Polson (2017, version 24 of 2026), On Hilbert's 8th problem, arXiv:1708.02653.
- [Hankel matrices of Stieltjes moments count the zeros off the critical line (eigenvalue form of the Grommer and Chebotarev criterion)](../RESULTS.md#hankel_inertia_counts_zeros_off_critical_line) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.8, page 108; proved): In an eigenvalue form of the classical determinant criterion of Grommer (1914) and Chebotarev (1928), the numbers of negative eigenvalues of the Hankel matrices of the Stieltjes moments of the heat trace are nondecreasing in the matrix size and tend to the number of distinct zeros of xi to the right of the critical line in the upper half plane, so RH holds if and only if they are all zero. New in this book.
- [Heat trace completely monotone on (0, 1.29]; every order up to t = 0.019](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_completely_monotone_near_origin) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.10, page 111; proved): Every derivative of the heat trace of order at least one alternates in sign for t up to 0.019 using no information on the zeros, and with the verification of RH below height 10^12 the heat trace is completely monotone on (0, 1.29], with the numerical inequalities of the proof certified in ball arithmetic. New in this book.
- [Heat trace derivative along a ray is the Gaussian smoothing of the zeros](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_derivative_along_ray_is_gaussian_smoothing_of_zeros) (Volume I, Proposition 8.1.11, page 115; proved): For u at least 2 and k equal to u^2 t, not necessarily an integer, the k th derivative of the heat trace (defined by its sum over the zeros), divided by an explicit factor, equals the Gaussian smoothing of the zeros at resolution 1/(2 sqrt t) up to an explicit relative error on each zero within u/2 of u and an explicit remainder, with no hypothesis on the zeros. New in this book.
- [RH is equivalent to nonnegativity of the Gaussian smoothing of the zeros](../RESULTS.md#rh_iff_gaussian_smoothing_of_zeros_nonnegative) (Volume I, Proposition 8.1.12, page 116; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if the Gaussian smoothing of the zeros of xi, a sum of Gaussian bumps in complexified ordinates, is nonnegative for every real centre and every resolution. New in this book.
- [There is no Gaussian smoothing criterion for RH on a line with real part alpha above one half](../RESULTS.md#no_gaussian_criterion_on_vertical_line_alpha_above_one_half_under_rh) (Volume I, Proposition 8.1.13, page 116; conditional on The Riemann Hypothesis: every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part $\frac12$): Under RH the Gaussian smoothing of the zeros shifted to the line $\operatorname{Re}s=\alpha$ with $\frac12<\alpha<1$ takes negative values for some $u$ and $t$, so positivity of the Gaussian smoothing cannot be a criterion for RH on that line. New in this book.
- [Derivative signs of the heat trace up to order 1.16 x 10^25](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_derivative_signs_to_order_1e25) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.14, page 118; proved): From the verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12, every derivative of the heat trace alternates in sign, equivalently the Williamson measures are positive, up to order 1.16 times 10^25, with the numerical inequalities of the proof certified in ball arithmetic. New in this book.
- [Compound Poisson defect of the off line zeros: the clock plus an independent compound Poisson variable is a GGC](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_compound_poisson_defect_of_off_line_zeros) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.15, page 120; proved): Unconditionally, the heat trace of the zeros of xi is the Laplace transform of a Thorin measure minus an explicit nonnegative remainder carried by the off line zeros, so the clock plus an independent compound Poisson variable of rate below $10^{-12}$ (a bound certified in ball arithmetic from the verification of RH to height $3\times10^{12}$ and an explicit zero counting bound) is a generalized gamma convolution, and the remainder vanishes exactly when RH holds, that is, when the heat trace is completely monotone. New in this book.
- [Problem: derivative signs of the heat trace to order c H squared with c above 1.70](../RESULTS.md#open_range_of_derivative_signs_of_the_heat_trace_beyond_1_70_h_squared) (Volume I, Problem 8.1, page 120; open): At the verified height $H=3\times10^{12}$ the derivatives of the heat trace have the alternating signs at every time up to order $1.70(H-23)^2$, and the book asks whether the range can be increased to $cH^2$ with an explicit $c>1.70$, suggesting a scale from a heuristic model of four zeros. New in this book.
- [Heat trace of the zeros of xi is completely monotone on (0, 1.70] and has the right sign at every order up to 1.52e25 (certified)](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_completely_monotone_up_to_time_1_70_and_order_1_52e25_certified) (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.17, page 121; proved): Unconditionally, the heat trace of the zeros of xi is completely monotone on $(0,1.70]$ and its derivatives have the alternating signs for all times $t>0$ and every order up to $1.70(H-23)^2>1.52\times10^{25}$ with $H=3\times10^{12}$, proved with a ball arithmetic computation (certified) of the numerical inequalities for the orders above $1.16\times10^{25}$. New in this book.
