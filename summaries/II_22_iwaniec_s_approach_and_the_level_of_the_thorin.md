What does Iwaniec's approach to a Landau Siegel zero and to a quasi Riemann hypothesis mean inside the Thorin framework of the two volumes? This chapter is a reworking of an unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026), written in the book's form and corrected after a referee's reading. Every result carries one of the status words proved, computed, conditional, heuristic or open, and no priority is claimed for anything in it. The unrefereed zero free half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ of OpenAI (2026) is never used as a theorem, and statements that use it are conditional. The answer to the title question of the last section is no: the chapter places Iwaniec's target inside the Thorin language, computes what can be computed, and adds no method towards the Riemann hypothesis.

Iwaniec's approach is reported through Conrey's survey of the third conference on the Riemann hypothesis, which the authors of the note did not read, so the account is heuristic. One takes a family of $L$ functions with a multiple zero at $s=\frac12$, the model being the elliptic curves $y^2=x^3+Ax+B^2$ with the point $(0,B)$ on each, and tames the Möbius function by approximating it with the coefficients of the family. Three conjectures of Iwaniec would exclude a Landau Siegel zero, and harder constructions of the same kind would give a quasi Riemann hypothesis, a concrete $\beta<1$ with no zero to the right of $\operatorname{Re}s=\beta$. The chapter records a caution: if $(0,B)$ has infinite order then every curve of the family has $L(E,1)=0$, so the moment Iwaniec bounds must concern a derivative or another family, and its formula is not reproduced.

**The dictionary.** An admissible $L$ function is $\xi$, the completed $L$ function of a real primitive Dirichlet character (or the product for a pair of complex conjugate characters), or the completed $L$ function of an elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$ with centre $\frac12$. For a level $\alpha\ge\frac12$ with $\Lambda(\alpha)\ne0$ put $\varphi_\alpha(s)=\Lambda(\alpha)/\Lambda(\alpha+\sqrt s)$, and let $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ say that $\Lambda$ has no zero with real part above $\alpha$. Theorem `iw:thm:dictionary` (proved) states that three conditions are equivalent: $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$, the statement that $\varphi_\alpha$ is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution (GGC), and the positivity of $\operatorname{Re}\Lambda'/\Lambda$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha$. When they hold the Thorin measure $U_\alpha$ has no drift and is the sum of one factor measure per zero, with density $u_\alpha(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt t}\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Lambda'}{\Lambda}(\alpha+i\sqrt t)$ away from atoms at $\gamma^2$ contributed by zeros on the line $\operatorname{Re}s=\alpha$. For $\xi$ the equivalence of the first two conditions is the Poisson theorem of the earlier chapters, cited and not reproved; the new part is the extension to Dirichlet and elliptic curve $L$ functions. Corollary `iw:cor:qrh` (proved) notes that the Riemann hypothesis is the case $\alpha=\frac12$, where $U_{1/2}$ is the sum of unit atoms at $\gamma^2$, and that at $\alpha=1$ the condition is automatic and nonvanishing on $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ says that $U_1$ has no atoms.

**The factor lemma.** Lemma `iw:lem:factor` (proved) handles the building block $f(s)=(\delta^2+\gamma^2)/((\sqrt s+\delta)^2+\gamma^2)$. For $\delta\ge0$ it is a GGC transform with Thorin measure of total mass one and an explicit density, the sum of two Cauchy densities of width $\delta$ centred at $\pm\gamma$ in the variable $\sqrt t$, which tends weakly to a unit atom at $\gamma^2$ as $\delta\downarrow0$. For $\delta<0$ the function has a pole on the principal sheet and is not a GGC transform. A real zero gives the analogous factor of mass $\frac12$. The dictionary follows by expanding the Hadamard product about $\alpha$, with a Lagarias type argument for the positivity of the real part of the logarithmic derivative.

**The Cauchy semigroup and mass growth.** Theorem `iw:thm:semigroup` (proved) shows that under $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ the boundary measures satisfy $D_{\alpha+h}=P_h*D_\alpha$ with $P_h$ the Cauchy kernel, by the Herglotz representation, and conversely that a nonnegative $D$ with $D_{\alpha+h}=P_h*D$ for all $h>0$ forces $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$. Proposition `iw:prop:mobius` (proved, classical) says that for $\xi$ the GGC property at level $\alpha$ is equivalent to $\psi(x)=x+O(x^{\alpha+\varepsilon})$ and to $M(x)=O(x^{\alpha+\varepsilon})$, which is why Iwaniec phrases the problem as taming the Möbius function. Theorem `iw:thm:mass` (proved, under $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$) shows that uniformly in $\alpha\in[\frac12,1]$ the Thorin mass up to $T^2$ equals $N(T)+O(\log T)$, so lowering the level sharpens the shape of the measure and not its total. The classical zero free regions are curved and contain no half plane with $\alpha<1$, so they do not lower the level, and the $\frac78$ claim gives the measure for every $\alpha\ge\frac78$ only conditionally.

**The level explicit formula.** For band limited test functions $\phi$, Theorem `iw:thm:weil` (proved) defines a functional $W_\alpha(\phi)$ from a finite prime sum, the archimedean term and a Laplace transform of $\widehat\phi$, and shows that it equals the sum over zeros of $\operatorname{Re}L(\alpha-\rho)$. Each term is a Cauchy convolution of $\phi$ when $\alpha>\beta$, equals $\phi(\gamma)$ when $\alpha=\beta$, and when $\alpha<\beta$ has the opposite sign plus a correction $2\operatorname{Re}\phi(\gamma+i(\alpha-\beta))$ from the entire extension of $\phi$. Under $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ it is the integral of $\phi$ against $D_\alpha$, hence nonnegative for $\phi\ge0$. Corollary `iw:cor:residue` (proved) is the residue identity: the prime side functional and the naive zero side density differ exactly by $2\sum_{\beta>\alpha}\operatorname{Re}\phi(\gamma+i(\alpha-\beta))$. A Bernstein estimate (proved) shows that while $(\beta-\alpha)X$ is small a zero to the right of the level moves $W_\alpha$ with the same sign as a zero on the level, so positivity for one fixed test function cannot separate the two. Below the level $W_\alpha$ is finite but need not be a measure: for an explicit nonnegative test function at $\alpha=0.1$ the prime sum gives $-0.371$ and 120 zeros give $-0.369$ (computed), which does not depend on RH. Proposition `iw:prop:weilavg` (proved) shows that the full line sum $\sum_\rho\operatorname{Re}\phi(\gamma+i(\alpha-\beta))$ equals $\frac12[W_\alpha+W_{1-\alpha}]$, the level average that at $\alpha=\frac12$ is Weil's functional, so Weil's criterion becomes equivalent to positivity of $W_{1/2}$ on nonnegative band limited functions. Proposition `iw:prop:converse` (proved) shows that for every $\alpha>0$, $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ holds if and only if $W_\alpha\ge0$ on nonnegative band limited functions, and already for the family $\phi_0(1\pm\cos a y)$, $a\ge5$, with $\phi_0=(\sin y/y)^4$.

**The three readings at the origin.** The first is rank. Forced central zeros are an atom at the origin: as $\alpha\downarrow\frac12$ the Thorin measure of a curve of rank $r$ tends to $\frac r2\delta_0$ plus the unit atoms at $\gamma^2$ (conditional on GRH). Proposition `iw:prop:rank` (proved, the identity part conditional on GRH) bounds the rank by the origin coefficient, $r\le(\alpha-\frac12)\kappa_\alpha$ under $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ and unconditionally $r\le\frac12\kappa_1$, with equality up to $O((\alpha-\frac12)^2)$ under GRH. The second is a Landau Siegel zero at $1-\eta$, which at level one unconditionally puts mass at least $\frac1\pi\arctan(Y/\eta)$ on $(0,Y]$ and forces $\kappa_1\ge1/\eta$ (proved), a near atom of mass $\frac12$ at the origin. This is the classical statement in new clothing, since $\kappa_1$ is $L'/L(1,\chi)$ shifted by a known amount, and the book records the unrefereed claim of uniform exclusion of such zeros only as an unrefereed claim. The third is central values: the quotient $\Lambda(\frac12)/\Lambda(\alpha)$ is given unconditionally by a product over zeros, and under GRH with $\Lambda(\frac12)\ne0$ it is $\varphi_{1/2}((\alpha-\frac12)^2)$ (conditional), so mass near the origin makes the central value small and an atom makes it vanish. Ranks and central values are governed by the origin coefficients $\kappa_\sigma$ for all $\frac12<\sigma\le\alpha$ and not by one level alone (proved). The reading does not apply to Iwaniec's family, whose central values all vanish, and any Thorin reading of a moment bound there is heuristic and not attempted.

**The computed tables.** All numerical entries are computed, and the referee recomputed those for $\zeta$, $L(s,\chi_{-4})$, five of the twelve curves and the level explicit formula. The origin coefficient of $\zeta$ is $\operatorname{Re}(\xi'/\xi)(1)=0.0230957089661$, matching $1+\frac{\gamma_E}2-\frac12\log4\pi$ to more than twenty digits. The density formula agrees with the sum of factor densities over 300 zeros to $1.1\times10^{-6}$, the semigroup step from $\alpha=\frac34$ to $1$ matches to $2\times10^{-5}$, and the mass up to height 100 is $29.11$ at level one and $29.05$ at level $\frac34$ against $N(100)=29$ (the second conditional beyond verified heights). For five curves of the family the bound $\kappa_1\ge2r$ holds with a small remainder, for example $\kappa_1=2.276$ for rank 1 and $7.159$ for rank 3, and a second table shows $\delta\kappa_{1/2+\delta}\to r$ with the remainder close to $\delta^2\sum\gamma^{-2}$. The curve ranks are found numerically. For $L(s,\chi_{-4})$ one finds $\kappa_1=0.0778$, and a phantom zero at $1-\eta$ puts mass at least $0.437$ of $D_1$ on $(0,0.1]$ for $\eta=0.02$. The explicit formula table for one Fejér test function shows the prime side agreeing with the zero side to $10^{-3}$ at every level from $1.5$ down to $0.3$, while below $\alpha=\frac12$ the naive Cauchy sum and the density at $\gamma_1$ turn negative and the prime side stays positive, the residue terms accounting for the whole difference. This is one test function and not a positivity statement.

**A counterexample for the full line functional.** For $\frac12<\alpha<1$ the full line functional $\frac12(W_\alpha+W_{1-\alpha})$ does not characterise $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ (computed). Taking a band limited function $f$ that vanishes at $\gamma_1$ and $\phi=ff^*$, which is nonnegative, the prime side, which does not use the zeros, gives $-0.0679$ at $\alpha=0.75$ and $-0.0101$ at $\alpha=0.6$, and 400 zeros give the same values. The mechanism is that the term of $\gamma_1$ is $f(\gamma_1+i\eta)^2<0$ with $\eta=\alpha-\frac12$, and the other terms are smaller by a factor of order $(\eta/(\gamma_2-\gamma_1))^6$. Hence the forward implication from $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ to positivity of the full line functional fails whenever $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ holds, in particular under RH.

**Is there a path to RH?** No. The chapter gives three reasons. The ladder: the statements $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ form a monotone family linked by the semigroup, every rung is a zero free half plane, and the dictionary renames the rungs and adds none (the positivity theorem of the earlier chapter already says positivity at a level is equivalent to absence of zeros to its right). The cost of a rung: a fixed nonnegative test function cannot separate a zero just right of the level from one on it, and Weil's criterion is equivalent to RH, so using it assumes what is to be proved; the authors suggest (heuristic) that any further rung needs test functions adapted to the zeros, and the total variation checks of Volume II are consistent with $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ but cannot prove it. Where leverage could come from: any property of the measures $D_\alpha$ that persists under a small step down in level must include the existence of the nonnegative deconvolution, which is the conclusion, so positivity of the density alone, log concavity, and a vacuous signed solution hypothesis all fail; complete monotonicity or hyperbolic complete monotonicity of $\varphi_\alpha$ are properties of the right kind, but nothing here proves they persist (heuristic). The honest summary is that Iwaniec's target, the $\frac78$ claim and the Thorin descent are one statement, and the chapter does not add a method.

**Open problems.** The chapter lists one. It is whether the family deconvolution test can be run: compute the family average of $D_\alpha$ at a level above one from averages of $a_E(p)$ on the double zero progressions, invert the semigroup down to level $\frac12$, and measure the rate at which the recovered atom at the origin converges to the average rank (open; an analogue of the explicit formula for $\Lambda_E$ is not proved here). The earlier question of Weil positivity at a level is no longer open: positivity of $W_\alpha$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ (proved), and the full line functional is settled negatively by the counterexample above.
