# Chapter 35. Balazard, Saias and Yor meet Thorin

Is the criterion of Balazard, Saias and Yor, which expresses the Riemann hypothesis as a Cauchy average of log of zeta on the critical line, the same harmonic analysis as the book's Thorin measures, and can the average be computed? The chapter proves the criterion at every level theta and every height through the Nevanlinna formula of a half plane, which characterises the quasi Riemann hypothesis QRH(theta), and links it to positivity of the Thorin measures U_sigma, the 2 sin squared lemma and the extended generalised gamma convolution (EGGC) property of the reciprocal of Xi. That reciprocal has a positive Levy density with no hypothesis; so does the Davenport and Heilbronn analogue, which has zeros off the line (three zero computations certified in ball arithmetic). Under QRH(theta), log of zeta and the horizontally continued argument of h_theta have equal variances and zero covariance under harmonic measure; without it the defects of the complex moments are integrals along horizontal cuts from the zeros to the line. The Cauchy averages are computed by quadrature, plain and vertical likelihood Monte Carlo and the weighted trapezoid rule. On an exact half disc form of the criterion the sorted trapezoid rule has mean square error at most of order N^-4, and N^-5 after a change of variable, the whole line truncation is computed, and a smoothed Riesz identity in the plane gives grid rules, Voronoi and Delaunay weights with proved root mean square rate N^-3/2 at independent uniform nodes when the box holds no zero, and a zero count from the horizontal logarithmic derivative. Nothing here proves the Riemann hypothesis, and the tables are floating point computations.

## From the literature

- The criterion at level one half and height zero (Balazard, Saias and Yor 1999), and the argument of positivity of the Thorin density as a zero free half plane (Lagarias 1999)
- The Davenport and Heilbronn function (Davenport and Heilbronn 1936) and its zeros off the line (Spira 1994; Balanzario and Sanchez-Ortiz 2007)
- Vertical likelihood Monte Carlo (Polson and Scott 2014) and the weighted trapezoid rule on sorted nodes with its N to the minus four bound (Yakowitz, Krimmel and Szidarovszky 1978)
- The contour argument and the tail of the truncated criterion under RH, disproving a conjecture of Borwein, Bradley and Crandall (Bui, Lester and Milinovich 2014; Borwein, Bradley and Crandall 2000); finite radius and relative entropy forms (Wunderlich 2026)
- Verification of the Riemann hypothesis to height 3 times 10^12 (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and the zero counting bound (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022)
- The zero free half plane at 7/8, unrefereed and unchecked here (OpenAI 2026)
