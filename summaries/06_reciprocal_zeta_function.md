# Chapter 6. The reciprocal zeta function

This chapter asks whether the zeros, the primes and the gamma factor of the Riemann zeta function assemble into a Thorin measure, that is, whether the reciprocal zeta function, in the clock variable, is a generalized gamma convolution (GGC) or at least infinitely divisible. From the Dirichlet series and Euler product it computes Thorin type measures of an Euler factor and, under the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), of the normalised reciprocal zeta function, the signed measure $dM$ with $M-1$ the argument function $S(\sqrt t)$, and from the Weierstrass product that of the gamma factor. The Lai and Vere-Jones identity gives the Vere-Jones representation by hyperbolic secants and sawtooth measures, and an explicit canonical function (heat trace) that yields a criterion for RH. The sine squared lemma and the Meixner law show that the reciprocal gamma factor is a GGC after a stable tilt, and the floor of the reciprocal Xi function, whose infinite divisibility follows from Polson (2026), is a GGC exactly when RH holds.

## From the literature

- the Thorin representation of generalized gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992)
- the hyperbolic secant law as a Brownian exit time and a GGC, and its gamma powers $C_t$ with Laplace transform $(\cosh\sqrt{2\lambda})^{-t}$ (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- the Meixner hypergeometric law and the completeness problem for dilates of the hyperbolic secant (Lai and Vere-Jones 1979)
- the Riemann von Mangoldt formula (see Titchmarsh 1986) and the explicit formula (Weil 1952), in its heat trace form (Polson 2026)
- the local Riemann Hypothesis for Mellin transforms of even Hermite functions (Bump, Choi, Kurlberg and Vaaler 2000), rederived here with no priority claimed, and Meixner Pollaczek expansions of $\Xi$ (Kuznetsov 2008; Romik 2021)
- Williamson's theorem on $n$ monotone functions (Williamson 1956)
- the verification of RH to height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and a bound for the zero count (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022)
- infinite divisibility and self decomposability of the reciprocal Xi function (Polson 2026)
