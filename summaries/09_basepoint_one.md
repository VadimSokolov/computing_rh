# Chapter 9. The basepoint one

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis: Thorin Measures, Pólya Densities and Hitting Times* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 9. This chapter works out the shifted clock $T_\alpha$ of the reciprocal Xi function at the basepoint $\alpha=1$, the random variable with Laplace transform $\xi(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha+\sqrt u)$ for the completed Riemann zeta function $\xi$. It asks what the Thorin measure and the law of $T_1$ are, whether the Thorin density comes from the sine squared lemma, and whether $T_1$ is a hitting time to which Kent's theorem applies. The Thorin measure of this generalized gamma convolution (GGC) is the Poisson smoothing of the zeros by Cauchy kernels, positive with no hypothesis because no zero lies on $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ (the threshold theorem of Polson 2026 at $\alpha=1$). The chapter derives a Lévy form of $\log\xi(1+s)$, proves with no hypothesis (using ball arithmetic and explicit zero counts) that the Thorin density is smallest at the origin, giving an independent positive stable one half summand, and shows that this minimum at every height on every line with real part between one half and one is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). It certifies the heat trace from the primes and represents every shifted clock $T_\alpha$ with $\alpha>\frac12$ as a subordinated hitting time of sticky Brownian motion.

## From the literature

- $2\xi(s)$ as the Mellin transform of a sum of two Bessel hitting times (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- generalized gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992); Kent's theorem on diffusion hitting times, with Krein's inverse spectral theory (Kent 1980; Dym and McKean 1976)
- the complete Riemann zeta distribution (Nakamura 2015); Lagarias's positivity criterion for RH (Lagarias 1999)
- sticky Brownian motion (Itô and McKean 1965); inverse first passage (Anulova 1980; Ekström and Janson 2016)
- verification of RH to $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and of GRH for the characters modulo 4 and 12 (Platt 2016); explicit zero counts (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022; Bennett, Martin, O'Bryant and Rechnitzer 2021) and bounds for $\psi(y)-y$ (Büthe 2018; Broadbent, Kadiri, Lumley, Ng and Wilk 2021; Johnston and Yang 2023)

## New results

- [Compensated sine squared formula at the basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#compensated_sine_squared_formula_basepoint_one) (Proposition 9.1.2, page 380; proved): At the basepoint one the Thorin density equals an explicit digamma expression plus a compensated sine squared sum over the primes, and the limit exists by the prime number theorem. New in this book.
- [Lévy form of the completed zeta function at the basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#levy_form_of_xi_at_basepoint_one) (Proposition 9.1.3, page 382; proved): The logarithm of $\xi(1+s)/\xi(1)$ has an explicit Lévy form whose drift is the sum of the reciprocals of the zeros and whose signed Lévy measure consists of a gamma density, a pole term and prime atoms, the compensated integral converging by the prime number theorem. New in this book.
- [Minimum of the Thorin density at the origin and stable summand at the basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_minimum_at_origin_basepoint_one) (Proposition 9.1.4, page 385; proved): With no hypothesis on the zeros, certified with the first 1800 zeros in ball arithmetic and explicit zero counts above height 2000, the Thorin density of the shifted clock at the basepoint one is smallest only at the origin, so the clock is the sum of an independent positive stable one half variable and a generalized gamma convolution. New in this book.
- [Minimum at the origin and stable summand at every basepoint from one](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_minimum_at_origin_every_basepoint_from_one) (Proposition 9.1.5, page 388; proved): The same minimum at the origin and the same splitting into a positive stable one half summand and a generalized gamma convolution hold at every basepoint at or above one, certified with ball arithmetic and explicit zero counts above height 2000. New in this book.
- [Lévy form below the basepoint one and zero free half planes](../RESULTS.md#levy_form_below_basepoint_one_and_zero_free_half_plane) (Proposition 9.1.6, page 390; proved): Below the basepoint one the Lévy form of $\xi$ converges and gives a positive Thorin density when all zeros lie to the left of the basepoint, and conversely its validity at a basepoint forces a zero free half plane to the right of it. New in this book.
- [Comparison of the Thorin density across vertical lines](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_ratio_comparison_across_vertical_lines) (Theorem 9.1.8, page 395; proved): The ratio of the real part of the logarithmic derivative of a completed function on a vertical line to the distance of that line from the critical line is nonincreasing as the line moves right, under a hypothesis on the zeros to the right of the critical line that holds under RH, for lines at distance at least $1/\sqrt2$ from the critical line, and below the verified height. New in this book.
- [Explicit lower bound of the Thorin density by comparison with the Euler product](../RESULTS.md#explicit_lower_bound_of_thorin_density_by_comparison_with_euler_product) (Corollary 9.1.9, page 396; proved): On a line to the right of one the Euler product and the digamma function give an explicit lower bound for the real part of the logarithmic derivative of a completed zeta or Dirichlet L function, and under the comparison hypothesis the bound, multiplied by the ratio of the distances of the lines from the critical line, holds on every line to the left and is of order a positive multiple of the logarithm of the height. New in this book.
- [Minimum of the Thorin density at the origin and its equivalence with RH](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_minimum_at_origin_below_one_equivalent_to_rh) (Theorem 9.1.11, page 397; proved): RH, respectively GRH for the Dirichlet L function of the character modulo 4 or modulo 12, is equivalent to the Thorin density of the corresponding completed function on every line with real part between one half and one being smallest at the origin and only there, a property certified in ball arithmetic with no hypothesis up to the verified height. New in this book.
- [Certified positivity of the heat trace at the basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#heat_trace_at_basepoint_one_positive_certified_from_primes) (Proposition 9.1.17, page 404; proved): The heat trace of the shifted clock at the basepoint one is certified positive at every time, with an explicit lower bound, from the primes up to ten million and published explicit bounds for Chebyshev's error, in ball arithmetic. New in this book.
- [No Brownian first passage to a random level at the basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#no_brownian_first_passage_to_random_level_at_basepoint_one) (Proposition 9.2.1, page 407; proved): The shifted clock at the basepoint one is not the first passage time of a Brownian motion with drift to a random level independent of the motion. New in this book.
- [Subordination of the tilted centre clock at the basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#basepoint_one_clock_subordination_of_tilted_centre_clock) (Theorem 9.2.2, page 408; proved): The shifted clock at the basepoint one is the exponentially tilted centre clock run through the inverse local time of a sticky Brownian motion, equivalently the tilted clock plus a Brownian first passage time to a level proportional to it. New in this book.
- [Kent string hitting time of the shifted clock at the basepoint one under RH](../RESULTS.md#basepoint_one_clock_kent_string_hitting_time_under_rh) (Corollary 9.2.3, page 409; conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis (every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part one half) and the simplicity of every nontrivial zero): Assuming RH and simple zeros, the tilted centre clock is a Kent hitting time of a Krein string with spectrum the squared moduli of the zeros, and the shifted clock at the basepoint one is the first hitting time of a level by this string diffusion run on the local time of a sticky Brownian motion. New in this book.
- [Subordination of the centre clock at every basepoint above the centre](../RESULTS.md#shifted_clock_subordination_every_basepoint_above_centre) (Theorem 9.2.5, page 415; proved): For every basepoint above the centre the shifted clock exists with no hypothesis on the zeros and is a Brownian hitting time built from the tilted centre clock, with a stable one half tail, and, by the threshold theorem of Polson 2026, it is a generalized gamma convolution exactly when no zero lies to the right of the basepoint. New in this book.
- [Lévy measure and self decomposability of the shifted clock](../RESULTS.md#shifted_clock_levy_measure_and_self_decomposability) (Corollary 9.2.6, page 416; proved): The shifted clock at every basepoint above the centre is infinitely divisible and self decomposable, with an explicit Lévy measure obtained from that of the centre clock. New in this book.
