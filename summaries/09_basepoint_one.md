# Chapter 9. The basepoint one

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis: Thorin Measures, Pólya Densities and Hitting Times* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 9. This chapter works out the shifted clock $T_\alpha$ of the reciprocal Xi function at the basepoint $\alpha=1$, the random variable with Laplace transform $\xi(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha+\sqrt u)$ for the completed Riemann zeta function $\xi$. It asks what the Thorin measure and the law of $T_1$ are, whether the Thorin density comes from the sine squared lemma, and whether $T_1$ is a hitting time to which Kent's theorem applies. The Thorin measure of this generalized gamma convolution (GGC) is the Poisson smoothing of the zeros by Cauchy kernels, positive with no hypothesis because no zero lies on $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ (the threshold theorem of Polson 2026 at $\alpha=1$). The chapter derives a Lévy form of $\log\xi(1+s)$, proves with no hypothesis (using ball arithmetic and explicit zero counts) that the Thorin density is smallest at the origin, giving an independent positive stable one half summand, and shows that this minimum at every height on every line with real part between one half and one is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). It certifies the heat trace from the primes and represents every shifted clock $T_\alpha$ with $\alpha>\frac12$ as a subordinated hitting time of sticky Brownian motion.

## From the literature

- $2\xi(s)$ as the Mellin transform of a sum of two Bessel hitting times (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- generalized gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992); Kent's theorem on diffusion hitting times, with Krein's inverse spectral theory (Kent 1980; Dym and McKean 1976)
- the complete Riemann zeta distribution (Nakamura 2015); Lagarias's positivity criterion for RH (Lagarias 1999)
- sticky Brownian motion (Itô and McKean 1965); inverse first passage (Anulova 1980; Ekström and Janson 2016)
- verification of RH to $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and of GRH for the characters modulo 4 and 12 (Platt 2016); explicit zero counts (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022; Bennett, Martin, O'Bryant and Rechnitzer 2021) and bounds for $\psi(y)-y$ (Büthe 2018; Broadbent, Kadiri, Lumley, Ng and Wilk 2021; Johnston and Yang 2023)
