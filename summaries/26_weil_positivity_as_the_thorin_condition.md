# Chapter 26. Weil positivity as the Thorin condition

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis: Thorin Measures, Pólya Densities and Hitting Times* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 26. Can Weil's positivity criterion for the Riemann Hypothesis be read as a statement about a Thorin measure, and how far can the primes alone certify it? The chapter restricts Weil's functional to Gaussian test functions and their derivatives in the heat time, which gives the heat trace of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function, a Laplace transform with a Thorin (generalised gamma convolution) measure supported on the squared ordinates. It shows that RH holds if and only if this heat trace is completely monotone, if and only if the Thorin measure lies on the positive axis, and splits the trace into an archimedean part, an integral against the Lévy density of a log Gamma variable, and a prime part, a positive series of Fréchet distribution functions governed for small heat time by the prime 2. The sign structure of the derivatives of the archimedean part gives a window law for their first sign changes, with one sided certificates from primes up to a bound that use Rosser and Schoenfeld type estimates. Further equivalent forms are an energy form (finite discounted energy of the normalised prime error), Hankel positivity of normalised cumulants, and a sine squared functional at a single heat time, by Landau's theorem. The heat trace is computed to 110 digits from the Gamma function and the primes and matches the first 1700 zeros. The window law, the GUE match for short intervals and the certificate windows are computed or heuristic, and marked so.

## From the literature

- Weil's explicit formula and positivity criterion, with Guinand's summation formula (Weil 1952, Guinand 1948, Bombieri 2000)
- Weil positivity and the trace formula at the archimedean place (Connes and Consani 2021)
- Hermitian forms attached to zeta functions (Yoshida 1992)
- Explicit estimates for the prime counting function (Rosser and Schoenfeld 1962) and the verification of RH to $3\cdot10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- Variance of primes in short intervals and pair correlation (Cramér 1922, Goldston and Montgomery 1987, Montgomery and Soundararajan 2004)
- Zeta functions over the zeros of the zeta function (Voros 2010) and second order regular variation (de Haan and Stadtmüller 1996)
- Total positivity (Karlin 1968) and the explicit formula in the form of von Koch (1901)
