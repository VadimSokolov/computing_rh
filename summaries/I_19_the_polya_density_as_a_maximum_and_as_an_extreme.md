# Volume I, Chapter 19. The Pólya density as a maximum and as an extreme value law

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 19. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function*, edition 2, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24086101. Which parts of the maximum structure behind the Pólya density of Riemann's xi function survive the passage to the Thorin measure of the clock? The Pólya kernel comes from the density of the square root of a sum of two independent copies of the maximum $M_4$ of independent exponential variables, while the clock $1/G(\lambda)=\xi(\frac12)/\xi(\frac12+\sqrt\lambda)$ is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC) exactly when the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) holds. The chapter factors the tilted maximum through the zeta distribution of Khinchin, sets the maximum and the clock in the language of Wald couples, and proves a Stieltjes criterion for RH in which an off line zero appears as an atom of negative mass. It gives an explicit GGC within a stated Laplace distance of the clock, its Gumbel norming constant from the first zero, its finite order Bondesson and hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) properties, with RH equivalent to the infinite order versions, and a finite arithmetic form of the basepoint density below $\alpha=1$. The symmetrised finite stages of the maximum give, through Hurwitz's theorem, a sufficient condition for RH. The clock is not a maximum of independent exponential variables: the maximum structure belongs to the theta function and not to the zeros. The chapter then reads the Pólya density as an extreme value law (Smith and Tawn pictures, hitting times and the Thorin variable, B splines and Hadamard products, an explicit string given by the Liouville operator, and a string test from kernel moments). It examines three reformulations of RH and finds that none is a new path, poses open problems, and claims no proof of RH.

## From the literature

- The law with moments $2\xi(s)$ as, up to a constant factor, the maximum of a normalised Brownian excursion (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- The zeta distribution and the infinite divisibility of $\zeta(\sigma+it)/\zeta(\sigma)$ for $\sigma>1$ (Khinchin 1938; Gnedenko and Kolmogorov 1954; Lin and Hu 2001)
- The logistic law as a normal scale mixture (Andrews and Mallows 1974; Stefanski 1991); van Dantzig pairs (Lukacs 1968); Wald couples (Polson 2018)
- The limit laws of maxima (Gnedenko 1943); Bondesson's theorem that GGC Laplace transforms are the HCM functions (Bondesson 1992)
- Lagarias's criterion (Lagarias 1999); $\Lambda\ge0$ (Rodgers and Tao 2020); RH verified to $3\times10^{12}$ and $\Lambda\le0.2$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- Zeros of sections of $\zeta$ (Turán 1948; Montgomery 1983), also symmetrised (Spira 1967; Gonek and Montgomery 2013)

## New results

Theorems are proved, the finite arithmetic form below $\alpha=1$ is conditional, and the problems are open.

- [Pólya kernel from a sum of two maxima of exponential variables](../RESULTS.md#polya_kernel_from_sum_of_two_maxima_of_exponentials) (Volume I, Proposition 19.2.1, page 552; proved): The square e^{2Y} of the variable e^Y built from Riemann's xi function has the law of a sum of two independent copies of the maximum M_4 of independent exponential variables, and the Pólya kernel is recovered from the density of the square root of that sum by an explicit change of variables. New in this book.
- [Khinchin factorisation of the tilted maximum of exponential variables](../RESULTS.md#khinchin_factorisation_of_tilted_maximum_of_exponentials) (Volume I, Theorem 19.3.1, page 553; proved): For sigma above one, the maximum M_4 of independent exponential variables, tilted by the factor m^{sigma/2} and multiplied by an independent geometric factor 4^{-K} that modifies only the prime 2, has the law of a gamma variable divided by pi times the square of a zeta distributed integer. New in this book.
- [Wald couples of the maximum of exponential variables and the logistic law](../RESULTS.md#wald_couples_of_maximum_of_exponentials_and_logistic_law) (Volume I, Theorem 19.4.2, page 554; proved): The maximum M_4 of independent exponential variables, paired with a uniform variable on (-1,1) scaled by the square root of pi over two, is a Wald couple, as is the pair of sums of two independent copies, and the square root of M_4 times a standard normal variable has the logistic law. The van Dantzig pair involved is classical and the logistic statement is due to Andrews and Mallows and to Stefanski; the book's addition is their reading through the maximum in the language of Wald couples. New in this book.
- [Stieltjes criterion for RH through the smoothed Thorin density of the clock](../RESULTS.md#stieltjes_criterion_for_rh_through_smoothed_thorin_density_of_clock) (Volume I, Theorem 19.5.1, page 555; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to each of the following: the clock is a GGC, the function G'/G, minus the logarithmic derivative of its transform 1/G, is a Stieltjes function, its heat trace W is completely monotone, and the smoothed Thorin density r_eta(x), the imaginary part of G'/G at minus x minus i eta divided by pi, is nonnegative for all real x and all eta above zero. The form through r_eta is new, and the others are in the book. New in this book.
- [An off line zero makes the smoothed Thorin density of the clock negative](../RESULTS.md#off_line_zero_makes_smoothed_thorin_density_of_clock_negative) (Volume I, Lemma 19.5.2, page 556; proved): If a zero has real part beta above one half and ordinate gamma, the smoothed Thorin density r_eta of the clock at the point gamma squared minus delta squared, with delta equal to beta minus one half, tends to minus infinity as eta increases to twice delta times gamma. An explicit formula gives its value for eta below that and shows when it is negative. New in this book.
- [An explicit GGC within a stated Laplace distance of the clock](../RESULTS.md#explicit_ggc_within_laplace_distance_of_the_clock) (Volume I, Proposition 19.6.2, page 558; proved): The sum of independent exponential variables divided by the squares of the ordinates of the zeros up to height 3 times 10^12 is a GGC whose Laplace transform is within 2.5 times 10^-11 of the transform of the clock, by a certified bound that uses the verification of RH up to that height and nothing about the zeros above it. New in this book.
- [Term of one zero in the Thorin density of the basepoint family in the lambda chart](../RESULTS.md#term_of_one_zero_in_thorin_density_of_basepoint_family_lambda_chart) (Volume I, Proposition 19.7.1, page 559; proved): In the Laplace variable lambda the term of a single zero in the Thorin density at basepoint alpha is a sum of two Poisson kernels divided by twice the square root of lambda. It equals a factor kappa above one times a function that is a Cauchy density when delta is positive, has total mass the sign of delta, and near its peak is a Lorentzian of full width 4 |delta| gamma up to a relative error of order delta squared over gamma squared. New in this book.
- [The clock is not a maximum of independent exponential variables](../RESULTS.md#clock_is_not_a_maximum_of_independent_exponential_variables) (Volume I, Proposition 19.8.1, page 562; proved): The negative logarithm of the distribution function of the clock is not completely monotone, so the clock is not the maximum of independent exponential variables with any rates, nor of a Poisson cloud of exponential clocks. The proof compares the first two exponential terms of the tail of the clock, and the coefficient of the second is negative where complete monotonicity would force it to be nonnegative. New in this book.
- [Gumbel limit for maxima of independent copies of the clock and of the maximum M_4](../RESULTS.md#gumbel_limit_for_maxima_of_independent_copies_of_the_clock) (Volume I, Proposition 19.9.1, page 563; proved): The maximum of n independent copies of the clock, scaled by the square of the first zero ordinate and centred at an explicit logarithm, converges to the Gumbel law, and divided by log n it tends to the reciprocal of that square; the maximum of n copies of M_4, scaled by pi and centred at log(2n), has the same Gumbel limit. This is Gnedenko's theorem applied to the exponential tails of M_4 and of the clock; the book's addition is the reading of the norming constant of the clock as the first atom of its Thorin measure, with the constant given by the first residue. New in this book.
- [Convergence of the finite maxima and of the finite stages of xi](../RESULTS.md#finite_maxima_converge_to_maximum_and_finite_stages_to_xi) (Volume I, Theorem 19.11.1, page 566; proved): Truncating the maximum M_4 at N exponential variables of each kind gives a maximum that differs from M_4 only on an event of probability at most C sqrt(N) e^{-pi sqrt(N)/2}, and the finite stage xi_N of the xi function and its symmetrisation converge to xi uniformly on compact subsets of the open critical strip at the rate N^{1/4} e^{-pi sqrt(N)/4}. New in this book.
- [Zeros of the symmetrised finite stages and a sufficient condition for RH](../RESULTS.md#hurwitz_zeros_of_finite_stages_and_sufficient_condition_for_rh) (Volume I, Corollary 19.11.2, page 567; proved): By Hurwitz's theorem the zeros of the finite stages converge to the zeros of xi in the open strip, a simple zero on the critical line is approached by exactly one zero of the symmetrised stage, which lies on the line, and if the symmetrised stages have no zeros in the right half of the strip up to heights tending to infinity along some sequence of stages then RH holds. Whether RH implies that hypothesis on the stages is open. New in this book.
- [Finite order GGC, Bondesson and HCM classes of the clock, and RH as the infinite order case](../RESULTS.md#finite_order_ggc_bondesson_and_hcm_classes_of_the_clock) (Volume I, Theorem 19.12.2, page 571; proved): From the verification of RH up to height 3 times 10^12 the clock lies in the finite order GGC and Bondesson classes up to order 1.16 times 10^25 and its Laplace transform is hyperbolically completely monotone up to order 9 times 10^12, while RH is equivalent to the clock being a GGC, to its being in the Bondesson class, and to its transform being hyperbolically completely monotone. New in this book.
- [Finite arithmetic form of the basepoint density below basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#finite_arithmetic_form_of_basepoint_density_below_basepoint_one) (Volume I, Proposition 19.13.1, page 573; conditional on Part (b) assumes the Riemann hypothesis. Part (a) holds without any hypothesis, and part (c) holds without any hypothesis when delta is at least one half and otherwise needs that every zero lies within delta of the critical line, which the Riemann hypothesis gives for every delta above zero): For basepoint alpha equal to one half plus delta, a Fejér tapered sum over the primes below e^X, two explicit pole terms and one gamma integral define a quantity A_X, which is half the sum over all zeros rho of a function that is nonnegative on the real line, evaluated at (rho minus one half)/i, is nonnegative under RH, and tends to the real part of the logarithmic derivative of xi at alpha plus it as X grows when every zero lies within delta of the critical line. New in this book.
- [Zero freeness of the symmetric finite stages of the maximum](../RESULTS.md#open_zero_freeness_of_symmetric_finite_stages_of_the_maximum) (Volume I, Problem 19.1, page 575; open): Find heights tending to infinity such that, for infinitely many N, the symmetric finite stage of the maximum has no zeros in the right half of the critical strip below that height, for instance heights of order the square root of N; either statement would prove RH. Numerical counts for N up to 96 suggest that the zeros stay on the line up to a height of order N, and a mechanism for that is the first thing to find. New in this book.
- [A termwise positive continuation of the prime form below basepoint one](../RESULTS.md#open_termwise_positive_continuation_of_prime_form_below_basepoint_one) (Volume I, Problem 19.2, page 576; open): Is there a continuation of the sine squared prime form of the Thorin density below basepoint one that is positive term by term, for instance through smoothed prime sums with a cutoff near e^{1/epsilon} whose loss can be controlled by the logarithmic derivative of xi at alpha, the gamma sum and the pole terms? A positive answer for every basepoint in (alpha_0, 1] would prove the zero free region beta at most alpha_0. New in this book.
- [Existence of the half flow clock for small positive times](../RESULTS.md#open_existence_of_half_flow_clock_for_small_positive_times) (Volume I, Problem 19.3, page 576; open): For times t between 0 and 0.2, does the law with Laplace transform R_t(lambda/2), half of the clock of the de Bruijn Newman flow, exist as a probability law, and is it infinitely divisible? Unconditionally it is known to exist at t = 0, where it is half the clock, and to exist and be a GGC for t at least 0.2; under RH it exists and is a GGC for every t at least 0. New in this book.
- [An analogue of the Khinchin factorisation at one half](../RESULTS.md#open_khinchin_analogue_of_tilted_maximum_at_one_half) (Volume I, Problem 19.4, page 577; open): Is there an analogue at sigma equal to one half of the Khinchin factorisation of the tilted maximum, that is, a factorisation of the tilted law of the clock or of the Pólya variable through a law built from the primes, with the explicit formula replacing the Euler product? New in this book.
- [Each term of the theta series kernel is an explicit combination of two Gumbel kernels of fractional order](../RESULTS.md#gumbel_decomposition_of_each_term_of_the_theta_series_kernel) (Volume I, Theorem 19.16.1, page 578; proved): With $x=-4u-\log(\pi n^2)$, $\phi_n(u)\,du=\pi^{-1/4}n^{-1/2}\Gamma(\frac54)[\frac58g_{9/4}(x)-\frac34g_{5/4}(x)]\,dx$ with $g_k(x)=e^{-kx}\exp(-e^{-x})/\Gamma(k)$, and its Fourier transform is $\frac14n^{-s}\xi(s)/\zeta(s)$ with $s=(1+iz)/2$. New in this book.
- [Zeros of the Dirichlet polynomial of the Dyck chain at n equal to 25600 below height 60 (computed)](../RESULTS.md#dyck_chain_polynomial_has_thirteen_zeros_left_of_the_line_below_height_sixty_at_n_25600) (Volume I, Result 19.16, page 579; computed): For the Dirichlet polynomial $F_n(s)=\mathbb{E}Y_n^s$ of the Dyck chain at $n=25600$, an argument principle count in ball arithmetic gives 13 zeros with $-4<\operatorname{Re}s<6$ and $0<\operatorname{Im}s<60$, none with real part above $\frac12$ (between $0.0907$ and $0.4553$), 13 being the number of ordinates $\gamma_k$ below 60; a computation at this $n$ only. - [Lattice bias expansion of the Dirichlet polynomial of the Dyck chain (conjecture)](../RESULTS.md#open_lattice_bias_expansion_of_dyck_chain_dirichlet_polynomial) (Volume I, Conjecture 19.16.2, page 580; open): Conjecture that $F_n(s)=2\xi(s)-\frac{3s}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\xi(s-1)+O(1/n)$ locally uniformly on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ for the Dirichlet polynomial of the Dyck chain, consistent with the mean height of de Bruijn, Knuth and Rice. - [First order displacement of the zeros of the Dyck chain polynomial from the zeros of xi (conditional)](../RESULTS.md#dyck_chain_zeros_first_order_displacement_given_lattice_bias_conjecture) (Volume I, Proposition 19.16.3, page 581; conditional on The lattice bias conjecture, that $F_n(s)=2\xi(s)-\frac{3s}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\xi(s-1)+O(1/n)$ locally uniformly on $\operatorname{Re}s>0$): Assuming the lattice bias conjecture, each simple zero $\rho$ of $\xi$ has exactly one zero of $F_n$ at $\rho+\delta_nc_{\rm D}(\rho)+O(1/n)$ for large $n$, with $\delta_n=3/(2\sqrt{2n})$. - [Phase formula for the lattice bias coefficient at a zero on the critical line](../RESULTS.md#lattice_bias_coefficient_phase_formula_on_critical_line) (Volume I, Proposition 19.16.4, page 581; proved): For a simple zero $\frac12+i\gamma$ of $\xi$ the displacement coefficient $c_{\rm D}$ equals $-C_{\rm D}(\gamma)e^{-i\Theta(\gamma)}/Z'(\gamma)$ with explicit positive $C_{\rm D}$ and phase $\Theta$, so the sign of its real part is that of $-\cos\Theta(\gamma)/Z'(\gamma)$. - [Lattice bias displacement of the Dyck chain zeros, computed for 200 zeros of xi and the zero near the 64th (computed)](../RESULTS.md#lattice_bias_displacement_computed_first_200_zeros_and_zero_near_rho_64) (Volume I, Result 19.16, page 582; computed): In ball arithmetic $\operatorname{Re}c_{\rm D}(\rho_k)<0$ for $k\le63$ and $>0$ first at $k=64$, and the Dyck chain zero near $\rho_{64}$ lies to the right of the critical line for $10^5\le n\le10^8$, agreeing with the first order law; a computation and no proof. New in this book.
- [All zeros of Xi are real if and only if the reciprocal xi law is a GGC, and real and simple if and only if it is the hitting time of a string](../RESULTS.md#rh_equivalent_to_ggc_reciprocal_xi_law_and_to_the_existence_of_a_string) (Volume I, Corollary 19.18.1, page 584; proved): RH holds if and only if $L$ is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution with positive Thorin measure $\sum_k\delta_{\gamma_k^2}$; the zeros are all real and simple if and only if $L$ is the Laplace transform of the hitting time of a Krein string with positive mass; the same holds for any even real entire $\widehat H$ of order below two with $\widehat H(0)>0$. New in this book.
- [Under RH with simple zeros the tail of the hitting time is a sum of exponentials and its excess is exponential with rate the first squared ordinate](../RESULTS.md#principal_eigenvalue_of_the_string_from_the_tail_of_the_hitting_time_under_rh) (Volume I, Proposition 19.18.2, page 584; conditional on RH, simple zeros, and $|\xi'(\rho)|\ge e^{-C|\gamma|}$ for some constant $C$ and all zeros $\rho=\frac12+i\gamma$): If RH holds, the zeros are simple and $|\xi'(\rho)|\ge e^{-C|\gamma|}$ for some constant $C$ and all zeros $\rho$, then $\mathbb P(T>x)=\sum_kc_ke^{-\gamma_k^2x}$ with $c_k=\prod_{j\ne k}(1-\gamma_k^2/\gamma_j^2)^{-1}$, so the excess of $T$ over a high threshold is asymptotically exponential with rate $\gamma_1^2$. New in this book.
- [Hankel test: the zeros of an even entire kernel with infinitely many zeros are real if and only if two families of Hankel determinants are positive](../RESULTS.md#hankel_determinants_positive_if_and_only_if_all_zeros_of_a_candidate_kernel_are_real) (Volume I, Proposition 19.21.1, page 588; proved): For an even real entire $\widehat H$ of order below two with infinitely many zeros and $\sum_k|w_k|<\infty$, all zeros are real and nonzero if and only if $D_n=\det[\mu_{i+j+1}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}$ and $D_n'=\det[\mu_{i+j+2}]_{i,j=0}^{n-1}$ are positive for all $n\ge1$. New in this book.
- [Crossing symmetric Hankel positivity of the zeta amplitude is equivalent to RH](../RESULTS.md#crossing_symmetric_hankel_test_iff_rh_amplitude) (Volume I, Theorem 19.22.1, page 591; proved): RH holds if and only if both Hankel matrices built from the even Taylor coefficients of the crossing symmetric pole sum amplitude of the zeros of xi are positive definite for every order. New in this book.
