# Volume II, Chapter 10. The level one measure and the backward Cauchy flow

Can the Thorin measure of reciprocal xi be computed at level one from the primes alone, and can the Cauchy semigroup be run backward from it to the critical line? The chapter computes the level one Thorin measure of the Riemann xi function in closed form: a real analytic density equal to the real part of the logarithmic derivative of xi on the line Re s = 1, a smooth gamma part minus a compensated prime sum over the von Mangoldt function. Its Fourier transform has point masses at the logarithms of prime powers, and the identity with the zero description is a Weil type explicit formula for one test function. The band limited backward Cauchy flow, a Fejér regularised deconvolution with the multiplier $e^{(1-\theta')|\omega|}$, converges to the level $\theta'$ Thorin measure if and only if the zeta function has no zeros to the right of $\theta'$, so the Riemann Hypothesis is convergence at every $\theta'$ above one half. Positive deconvolution by the EM algorithm has a total Kullback Leibler residual that detects the same zero free half plane. Computations from samples of the density on Re s = 1 locate 109 zeros to 0.035, an EM injection test with controls separates an off line quartet, and a generative Bayesian computation under a GUE prior counts zeros in unit bins. A further section recovers the atoms by nonnegative least squares from the Pólya density at small resolution, where an off line pair gives a signature that no positive measure can reproduce. The chapter argues that none of this is a path to the Riemann Hypothesis.

## From the literature

- Generalised gamma convolutions and the Thorin measure (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992)
- The explicit formula for the zeros (Weil 1952) and the equivalence of the prime number theorem error term with a zero free half plane (Titchmarsh 1986)
- The EM algorithm for positive linear inverse problems (Shepp and Vardi 1982; Vardi and Lee 1993) and its information geometry (Csiszár and Tusnády 1984)
- Montgomery's pair correlation (Montgomery 1973) and Odlyzko's computations of the zeros (Odlyzko 1987), the source of the GUE prior
- Generative Bayesian computation (Polson and Sokolov 2025)

## New results

The closed form of the level one measure is proved; the numerical recoveries are computed in floating point and are diagnostics, not certificates.

New in this book.
- [Computation: the Fourier backward flow from samples of the level one density on Re s = 1 places a peak within 0.035 of each of 109 zeros](../RESULTS.md#computed_backward_flow_locates_109_zeros_from_samples_on_the_line_one) (Volume II, Result 10.3.1, page 173; computed): A the Arb library (python flint) computation, not a theorem, samples $u_1$ on $[0,400]$ from $\zeta$ and $\zeta'$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ only and shows peaks within $0.035$ of the $109$ zeros in $100<v<300$ at $\theta'=\frac34$ and within $0.027$ at $\theta'=\frac12$; the sharp cutoff rings and the Fejér window does not.
