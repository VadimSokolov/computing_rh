# Chapter 28. Truncations of the beta lattice and Hermite coefficients

How do the zeros of the Dirichlet L function modulo 4 move when its beta lattice is truncated, and can truncations decide GRH? The Mellin transform is a sum over a lattice of beta type variables, truncated at index n with a random remainder. It proves local uniform convergence of the truncations, with GRH for the character chi 4 equivalent to zero free compact subsets of the left half strip for large n, and gives an explicit expansion of the tail cumulants. A simple zero drifts at a rate that is a negative power of n, with an explicit coefficient. The sign of the real part of the drift is read off the derivative of the Hardy function, so a one sided criterion fails at any order where a critical zero is approached from the left, and infinitely many Gram anomalies with a margin force infinitely many zeros with negative real drift. Symmetrised truncations give a sufficient condition for GRH. The second half generates the Hermite coefficients of the Riemann Xi function by Gaussian smoothing, shows that the normalised coefficient is Xi at a turning point up to an error of order a to the fourth, locates the first negative coefficient near the first ordinate, and certifies real simple zeros from a verified sign change count. A statement on mean run lengths of signs is conditional on simple zeros giving exactly one sign change each. The count of 379 zeros below height 500 is computed.

## From the literature

- The Hardy function and zeros on the critical line (Titchmarsh 1986)
- Failure of Gram's law and the Rosser rule (Trudgian 2011), and the first $1.5\times10^9$ zeros simple and on the line (van de Lune, te Riele and Winter 1986)
- Fast evaluation of zeta at many points (Odlyzko and Schönhage 1988)
- Incomplete gamma approximants of the Xi function and conjectures on their zeros (Haglund 2009)
- The de Bruijn and Newman constant is nonnegative (Rodgers and Tao 2020)
