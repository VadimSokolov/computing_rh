# Volume I, Chapter 12. The basepoint one and hitting times at the basepoints

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 12. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function*, edition 2, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24086101. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/computing_rh/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/computing_rh.
<!-- cite:end -->

This chapter works out the shifted clock $T_\alpha$ of the reciprocal Xi function at the basepoint $\alpha=1$, the random variable with Laplace transform $\xi(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha+\sqrt u)$ for the completed Riemann zeta function $\xi$, and asks three questions: what its Thorin measure and law are, whether its Thorin density comes from the sine squared lemma, and whether $T_1$ is a hitting time to which Kent's theorem applies. The Thorin measure of this generalised gamma convolution (GGC) is the Poisson smoothing of the zeros by Cauchy kernels, positive with no hypothesis because no zero lies on $\operatorname{Re}s=1$. The chapter derives a Lévy form of $\log\xi(1+s)$, proves with no hypothesis (ball arithmetic and explicit zero counts) that the Thorin density is smallest at the origin, giving an independent positive stable one half summand, and shows that the same minimum on every line with real part between one half and one is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). It then treats the hitting time question: $T_1$ is not a diffusion hitting time in the sense of Kent and Krein because its Thorin measure is absolutely continuous, and it is not a Brownian first passage time to an independent random level (proved). The centre clock is a subordinated hitting time, the density of $T_1$ is expressed through harmonic measure, and an exact Brownian boundary with a Wald identity, valid at every basepoint above the centre, is derived; boundaries across the basepoints are compared. Statements at basepoints below one are conditional on the absence of zeros to the right.

## From the literature

- $2\xi(s)$ as the Mellin transform of a sum of two Bessel hitting times (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- generalised gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992); Kent's theorem on diffusion hitting times, with Krein's inverse spectral theory (Kent 1980; Dym and McKean 1976)
- Lagarias's positivity criterion for RH (Lagarias 1999)
- sticky Brownian motion (Itô and McKean 1965); inverse first passage (Anulova 1980; Ekström and Janson 2016)
- verification of RH to $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021); explicit zero counts (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022)
