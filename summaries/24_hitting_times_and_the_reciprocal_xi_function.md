# Chapter 24. Hitting times and the reciprocal xi function

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis: Thorin Measures, Pólya Densities and Hitting Times* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 24. Can the reciprocal of the normalised Riemann xi function, which is the Laplace transform of the law of a clock, be shown to be a hitting time, and what would that say about the Riemann Hypothesis? The chapter studies the law $\mu$ with Laplace transform $1/G$, where $G$ is the Hadamard product over the zeros, and asks whether the argument that gives a Krein string hitting time under RH can be run backwards. It computes the density of $\mu$ by a residue expansion over the zeros that needs no hypothesis in grouped form, bounds its tail and proves log concavity on a half line, treats truncations of the Hadamard product, Jensen polynomial laws and extreme value representations, and shows by a Thorin rigidity theorem that any factorisation into two generalised gamma convolutions forces RH. On the prime side it builds hitting times from truncated Euler products, proves a stable limit of index three halves, and constructs prime built hitting times for the base point laws under the quasi Riemann hypothesis. It adds the inverse first passage boundary of Roberts and Shortland, the Biane, Pitman and Yor variable and its Davenport and Heilbronn counterpart, and a coefficient ladder in which the Taylor coefficients of xi form a Pólya frequency sequence exactly when RH holds. Each route ends in an equivalence with RH whose unconditional part stops where classical knowledge stops.

## From the literature

- the Krein string and Kent's eigenfunction expansion of hitting times, and Krein's class of entire functions (Kent 1980, Krein)
- generalised gamma convolutions, Thorin measures and Dirichlet means (Bondesson 1992, James, Roynette and Yor 2008)
- the Brownian laws whose Mellin transform is xi (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001) and the hyperbolic laws (Pitman and Yor 2003)
- Jensen polynomials of the xi function (Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier 2019) and the verification of the Riemann Hypothesis to height $3\cdot10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- Lagarias's criterion $\operatorname{Re}\xi'/\xi>0$ for RH (Lagarias 1999) and the inverse first passage problem (Anulova 1980, Roberts and Shortland 1995)
- the Davenport and Heilbronn function with zeros off the critical line (Davenport and Heilbronn 1936)
