# Volume I, Chapter 11. Infinite divisibility, necklaces and solved models

This chapter asks where the normalised reciprocal zeta function $\varphi(s)=\zeta(\tfrac12)/((1-4s)\zeta(\tfrac12+\sqrt s))$ sits among infinitely divisible laws, and how far truncations of the Biane, Pitman and Yor lattice go towards the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). Unconditionally, and certified in interval arithmetic, it proves that the canonical function $w$ is positive, so that $\varphi$ is the Laplace transform of an infinitely divisible law with Lévy density $w(x)/x$, which is not self decomposable, not in the Bondesson class and not a generalised gamma convolution (GGC). It reads the Vere-Jones weights as necklace polynomials, extends the canonical function across the critical strip, and states the Laguerre Pólya class and the Lee and Yang property in Thorin measure terms for the solved models. For simple zeros $\rho$, zeros of the truncated lattice transforms converge to $\rho/2$ at rate $1/n$: the one sided Conjecture T is false, while the symmetrised Conjecture S is proved above explicit heights for $n\le24$ and $n=32,36$ and only checked numerically below them. It also proves Weil positivity on the Bernstein cone and reads the de Bruijn Newman flow as a path of GGCs. A final section writes the reciprocal Xi function as a harmonic mean and scale mixture of reciprocal hyperbolic cosine transforms and proves that the clock is not a gamma scale mixture. A note of Nicholas Polson shows that the approximate functional equation truncation with N equal to 2 has all but finitely many zeros on the critical line while the truncation with N equal to 3 has zeros off the line at arbitrarily large heights.

## From the literature

- Thorin measures and generalised gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992), closed under independent products (Bondesson 2015); unimodality of self decomposable laws (Yamazato 1978)
- Selberg's central limit theorem for $S(t)$ (Selberg 1946); verification of RH to height 50 (Backlund 1918) and $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021); simplicity of the first $1.5\times10^9$ zeros (van de Lune, te Riele and Winter 1986); an explicit Riemann von Mangoldt bound (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022)
- Necklace polynomials (Metropolis and Rota 1983)
- Pólya's theory of real zeros (Pólya 1926), Cardon's theorems (Cardon 2004, 2005), zeros of $\xi(s+a)+\xi(s-a)$ for $a\ge\frac12$ (Lagarias 2005), the Lee and Yang theorem (Lee and Yang 1952), Newman's characterisation of Lee and Yang spins (Newman 1974)
- The Biane, Pitman and Yor identity (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001; Williams 1990)
- Weil's criterion (Weil 1952; Bombieri 2000)
- The de Bruijn Newman constant (de Bruijn 1950; Newman 1976; Rodgers and Tao 2020; Polymath 2019; Platt and Trudgian 2021)

## New results

The positivity of the canonical function is proved with ball arithmetic; the truncation statements are proved above the stated heights and computed below them.

- [The normalised reciprocal zeta function is infinitely divisible](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_zeta_infinitely_divisible_unconditional) (Volume I, Theorem 11.1.1, page 185; proved): Without any hypothesis on the zeros, the normalised reciprocal zeta function is the Laplace transform of an infinitely divisible law with Lévy density $w(x)/x$, because its canonical function $w$ is positive; the proof is certified with the verification of the Riemann Hypothesis to height 50 and interval enclosures. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Negative bias of the Gaussian smoothed prime number theorem error](../RESULTS.md#gaussian_smoothed_prime_number_theorem_error_negative_bias) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.2, page 187; proved): The error of the prime number theorem, smoothed with a Gaussian weight in $\log y$, has a definite negative bias, about $-0.343\,(\pi x)^{-1/2}$ at large $x$, whereas a pointwise bound on the error of size $\sqrt y\log^2y$, even the one that the Riemann Hypothesis implies, controls it only up to order $x$. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [The reciprocal zeta function in the Bondesson hierarchy: not self decomposable, not a GGC](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_zeta_bondesson_hierarchy_classification) (Volume I, Theorem 11.1.3, page 188; proved): The normalised reciprocal zeta function is infinitely divisible but not self decomposable, not in the Bondesson class and not a mixture of exponentials, hence not a generalised gamma convolution, with no hypothesis on the zeros. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Density of the reciprocal zeta law at the origin](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_zeta_density_rises_from_origin) (Volume I, Theorem 11.1.4, page 190; proved): The density of the law with Laplace transform equal to the normalised reciprocal zeta function starts at $-\zeta(\frac12)/4$ with the positive slope $-\zeta(\frac12)/16$, so it is not monotone, not log convex and not completely monotone. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [The reciprocal zeta law is not a sum, product or limit of GGC variables](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_zeta_law_not_built_from_ggc_operations) (Volume I, Corollary 11.1.6, page 191; proved): The random variable with Laplace transform equal to the normalised reciprocal zeta function is not a sum, a product or a weak limit of independent generalised gamma convolution variables. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [The GGC class is not closed under the harmonic mean](../RESULTS.md#ggc_class_not_closed_under_harmonic_mean) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.7, page 191; proved): The harmonic mean of two independent standard exponential variables has an explicit density and is not a generalised gamma convolution, so the class of generalised gamma convolutions is not closed under harmonic means of independent variables. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Vere-Jones weights of the zeta function are necklace polynomials](../RESULTS.md#vere_jones_weights_are_necklace_polynomials) (Volume I, Theorem 11.1.8, page 192; proved): A 2 adic variant of the cyclotomic identity of Metropolis and Rota factors $1/(1-qy)$ into factors $1+y^n$ with necklace polynomial exponents, which makes the Vere-Jones weights of the zeta function at prime powers necklace polynomials at $p^{-1/2}$; the authors have not found the identity stated in this form, but it may well be known in the combinatorics of necklaces. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Sign rule for the necklace weights of the zeta function](../RESULTS.md#necklace_weights_sign_rule) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.9, page 193; proved): At $q=p^{-1/2}$ the necklace exponent $b_{2^a}$ is positive, and for odd $n>1$ the exponent $b_n$ is nonzero with sign $(-1)^{\omega(n)}$, where $\omega(n)$ counts the distinct prime factors of $n$. Corrects a statement of Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Lévy representation of the reciprocal zeta function across the critical strip](../RESULTS.md#levy_representation_reciprocal_zeta_across_critical_strip) (Volume I, Theorem 11.1.12, page 195; proved): For each $\sigma$ in $(0,1)$ the shifted reciprocal zeta function has an explicit canonical function $w_\sigma$ in three equivalent forms, with the asymptotic $\zeta'(\sigma)/\zeta(\sigma)$ over $2\sqrt{\pi x}$, and it is infinitely divisible with Lévy density $w_\sigma(x)/x$ whenever $w_\sigma$ is nonnegative, a positivity that the chapter proves only for $\sigma=\frac12$. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [The shifted reciprocal zeta function is never a GGC on a line of the strip](../RESULTS.md#shifted_reciprocal_zeta_never_ggc_on_the_strip) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.13, page 197; proved): On every line $\sigma$ in $(0,1)$ the shifted reciprocal zeta function is not a generalised gamma convolution and, if infinitely divisible, not self decomposable, because the factor that removes the pole makes its canonical function increase at the origin. Corrects a statement of Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Laguerre Pólya class as integer atom GGCs](../RESULTS.md#laguerre_polya_class_integer_atom_ggc) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.15, page 200; proved): A real even entire function $G$ of order below 2 lies in the Laguerre Pólya class exactly when $G(0)/G(i\sqrt s)$ is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution whose Thorin measure has integer masses at the squared zeros. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Lee and Yang single spin laws as integer atom GGCs](../RESULTS.md#lee_yang_property_integer_atom_ggc) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.16, page 201; proved): A symmetric law with Gaussian moments of all orders has the Lee and Yang property exactly when the reciprocal of its moment generating function at $\sqrt s$ is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution with integer atoms at the squared zeros of its characteristic function. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [Random harmonic series and the strip of the Pólya kernel](../RESULTS.md#random_harmonic_series_matches_polya_kernel_strip) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.17, page 202; proved): The random harmonic series $H_\kappa$ has a double exponential tail whose leading constant equals that of the Pólya kernel exactly at $\kappa^*=6.61845\ldots$, and its density is analytic in the strip $|\operatorname{Im}t|<\pi/4$, the strip of analyticity of the Pólya kernel. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [The reciprocal Xi transform is a harmonic mean of Brownian exit time transforms](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_xi_transform_harmonic_mean_of_brownian_exit_time_transforms) (Volume I, Proposition 11.1.23, page 207; proved): The Laplace transform of the clock, the reciprocal Xi function, is the harmonic mean over the Pólya density of the Laplace transforms $1/\cosh(x\sqrt s)$ of scaled Brownian exit times, so by Jensen's inequality it is at most the Laplace transform of the corresponding scale mixture. New in this book.
- [A harmonic mean of two GGC transforms that is not a Laplace transform of a positive measure](../RESULTS.md#harmonic_mean_of_two_ggc_transforms_not_a_laplace_transform) (Volume I, Example 11.1.24, page 207; counterexample, proved): The harmonic mean of the Laplace transforms $(1+s)^{-2}$ and $(1+2s)^{-2}$ of two generalized gamma convolutions (GGC) is $1/(1+3s+\frac52s^2)$, whose inverse transform $2e^{-0.6t}\sin(0.2t)$ is negative on $(5\pi,10\pi)$, so a harmonic mean of GGC transforms need not be the Laplace transform of a positive measure. New in this book.
- [A two point scale mixture of Gamma(2) that is infinitely divisible and not a GGC](../RESULTS.md#gamma_two_scale_mixture_infinitely_divisible_but_not_ggc) (Volume I, Example 11.1.25, page 207; counterexample, proved): A scale mixture of a Gamma(2) variable by an independent variable equal to $1$ or to a positive $\lambda\ne1$ with probability one half each is infinitely divisible but not a generalized gamma convolution, because its Laplace transform has nonreal zeros. New in this book.
- [The clock is not a Gamma scale mixture, for any shape](../RESULTS.md#clock_is_not_a_gamma_scale_mixture) (Volume I, Theorem 11.1.26, page 208; proved): The clock is not a scale mixture of Gamma($a$) laws for any shape $a>0$, because the Laplace transform of such a mixture is at least a constant times $s^{-a}$ for large $s$, while that of the clock is at most a constant times $e^{-x\sqrt s}$ for every $x>0$. New in this book.
