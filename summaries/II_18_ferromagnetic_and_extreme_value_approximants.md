# Volume II, Chapter 18. Ferromagnetic and extreme value approximants with certified real zeros

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume II: Cauchy Deconvolution and Certified Computation* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 18. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume II: Cauchy Deconvolution and Certified Computation*, edition 2, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24086102. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/computing_rh/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/computing_rh.
<!-- cite:end -->

Can the Pólya density of the Riemann Xi function be approximated by explicit laws whose zeros can be controlled, and how close does this bring one to the Riemann Hypothesis (RH)? The chapter has two halves with one method: replace the Pólya law $p=\Phi/\Xi(0)$ by a law with real zeros that can be certified. In the first half, RH holds exactly when the total variation distance from the Pólya law to the class of ferromagnetic laws is zero, and the chapter bounds this distance above, with no hypothesis, by distances to laws proved ferromagnetic: Pólya's approximations, tilts that shrink the strip of analyticity, multipliers $e^{-a\cosh\ell t}$ on the Pólya kernel (Bessel type laws), and laws that keep the first zeros, with distances enclosed in ball arithmetic and a computer assisted positivity proof giving distance below $1.932\times10^{-6}$. It proves that RH is equivalent to an identity in law with a series of Maxwell variables, and that a cusped weight $e^{-a|t|}$ never gives a ferromagnetic tilt. The second half writes each term of the theta series as a combination of two Gumbel kernels of fractional order and builds three families of approximations (folded Gumbel kernels, Pólya's two sided kernel, the Dyck chain). It shows that the zeros of Xi are all real if and only if the reciprocal xi law is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC), and real and simple if and only if it is the hitting time of a Krein string (Kent's theorem), and reduces RH to a height rule for the folded kernels, with a Rouché certificate in ball arithmetic for the first four levels. It studies shifted Bessel kernels through a Liouville boundary problem and a cotangent law, a Pólya frequency characterisation of RH (Schoenberg), Devroye's alternating series and an expansion of the mean excess function, and a detection rule for the first nonreal pair along the de Bruijn Newman flow. Longer runs on the open problems are floating point and marked computed. Each route stops at a known place, and none proves RH. It proves that Hermite syntheses of the Pólya density converge with no hypothesis on the zeros and are positive once the Gaussian variance is at least half the variance, for zeta and for the Davenport and Heilbronn function.

## From the literature

- Pólya's theorems on real zeros and approximations of $\Xi$ (Pólya 1918; 1926), de Bruijn's strip theorem (1950)
- The Lee and Yang property and Newman's single spin laws (Lee and Yang 1952; Newman 1975)
- Kent's theorem on hitting times of one dimensional diffusions and Krein's inverse spectral theory of strings (Kent 1980; Krein)
- Fractional order statistics and the Gumbel limit process (Stigler 1977), peaks over threshold laws (Pickands 1975; Balkema and de Haan 1974)
- Schoenberg's characterisation of Pólya frequency functions (Schoenberg 1951)
- The de Bruijn and Newman constant, its lower and upper bounds (Rodgers and Tao 2020; Polymath 2019; Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- Biane's Liouville string for Pólya's kernel and Devroye's alternating series for densities
- Hurwitz's theorem and Rouché's theorem

- [Zeros of Re K_{mu+i nu}(a) for integer mu solve a Liouville problem with a rational spectral boundary condition](../RESULTS.md#shifted_bessel_kernels_give_a_liouville_boundary_value_problem_with_rational_boundary_law) (Volume II, Theorem 18.3.3, page 564; proved): For integer $\mu\ge0$ and $a>0$, $\operatorname{Re}K_{\mu+i\nu}(a)=A_\mu(\nu^2)K_{i\nu}(a)+B_\mu(\nu^2)\,aK_{i\nu}'(a)$ with real polynomials $A_\mu,B_\mu$, so a zero $\nu$ with $B_\mu(\nu^2)\ne0$ is an eigenvalue $\lambda=\nu^2$ of $-g''+a^2e^{2\tau}g=\lambda g$ with $g'(0)=\beta_\mu(\lambda)g(0)$, $\beta_\mu=-A_\mu/B_\mu$.
