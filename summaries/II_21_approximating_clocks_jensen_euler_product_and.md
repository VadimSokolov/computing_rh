# Volume II, Chapter 21. Approximating clocks: Jensen, Euler product and boundary hitting times

RH holds if and only if some generalised gamma convolutions (GGC), the approximating clocks, converge weakly to the reciprocal Xi law $\mu$, whose Laplace transform is $1/G$ with $G(u)=\xi(\frac12+\sqrt u)/\xi(\frac12)$. This chapter tests the candidate approximations and asks of each whether it is built from data known without the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) and whether it can see an off line zero, in particular that of the Davenport Heilbronn function. It treats Jensen hitting times, laws built from the Taylor coefficients of $G$ (the probabilistic form of the Jensen and Pólya criterion), extreme value hitting times given by fractional order statistics and B splines, and prime side hitting times from truncated Euler products, with a Brownian truncated Euler product and a universal stable limit of index three halves. A Thorin rigidity theorem shows that any factorisation of $\mu$ into two GGC laws forces RH. The chapter states what the prime side limit would have to be, discusses diffusion approximations, and presents the curved boundary of Roberts and Shortland for the inverse first passage problem, the Brownian variable of Biane, Pitman and Yor, the probabilistic Riemann function of Marc Yor and coauthors, and the Davenport Heilbronn test, which every proposed argument must fail. A coefficient ladder shows that the Taylor coefficients of $F(s)=\xi(\frac12+\sqrt s)/\xi(\frac12)$ form a Pólya frequency sequence exactly when RH holds, with an angle theorem and an unconditional count of rungs from the verified height. Each route ends in an equivalence with RH or in an open problem. Statements are proved unless marked computed or conditional on RH or the quasi Riemann hypothesis.

## From the literature

- the Krein string and Kent's eigenfunction expansion of hitting times (Kent 1980; Krein)
- generalised gamma convolutions, Thorin measures and Dirichlet means (Bondesson 1992; James, Roynette and Yor 2008)
- the Brownian laws whose Mellin transform is $\xi$ (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001) and the hyperbolic laws (Pitman and Yor 2003); the Barnes $G$ function (Nikeghbali and Yor 2009)
- Jensen polynomials of the $\xi$ function (Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier 2019)
- the verification of RH to height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- the inverse first passage problem (Anulova 1980; Roberts and Shortland 1995)
- diffusion approximations (Roberts, Gelman and Gilks 1997)
- the Davenport and Heilbronn function with zeros off the critical line (Davenport and Heilbronn 1936; Spira 1994)
- Schoenberg's characterisation of Pólya frequency sequences (Schoenberg 1951)
