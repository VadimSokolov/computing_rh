# Chapter 4. Weak limits, hyperbolic complete monotonicity and a Bosch type theorem

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis: Thorin Measures, Pólya Densities and Hitting Times* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 4. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/computing_rh/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/computing_rh.

Can closure of generalized gamma convolutions (GGC) under weak limits, or Bondesson's characterization of GGC Laplace transforms as hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) functions, settle the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) without locating zeros? For the reciprocal Xi function (the clock) the chapter recalls (Polson 2017) that the Cauchy flow of Thorin measures converges vaguely to the measure of the zeros on the critical line whether or not RH holds, so weak closure cannot reach RH, and shows that HCM of the clock is RH in other forms (Stieltjes and Pick function conditions, HCM of the de Bruijn Newman deformation at every positive time). An elasticity criterion for HCM functions transfers Bosch's method for powers of the half Cauchy law: the clock composed with x^p is HCM exactly when |p| is at most one minus twice the largest zero angle over pi, with RH the extreme case, and unconditionally for |p| within about 10^-13 of one, by the verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12. Further results: the Thorin density of this family, a natural boundary argument with the Dedekind eta function, a first gap condition for the Mellin variable W (under RH and further hypotheses), and open questions.

## From the literature

Closure of generalized gamma convolutions under convergence in law (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992); Bondesson's theorem that a Laplace transform is a GGC transform if and only if it is HCM (Bondesson 1992; James, Roynette and Yor 2008); Bondesson's representation of HCM functions (Bondesson 1992); Bosch's theorem on powers of the half Cauchy law (Bosch 2015); the proof of Bondesson's conjecture on stable densities (Bosch and Simon 2016); Lagarias's criterion for RH (Lagarias 1999); the de Bruijn Newman constant (de Bruijn 1950; Newman 1976), nonnegative (Rodgers and Tao 2020), at most 0.22 (Polymath 2019) and at most 0.2 (Platt and Trudgian 2021); Lamperti's stable ratio density (Lamperti 1958); the Mellin representation of xi (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001); Pólya's gap theorem (Pólya 1923, 1929).

- [RH as a weak limit criterion for generalized gamma convolutions](../RESULTS.md#rh_iff_clock_is_weak_limit_of_ggc_transforms) (Corollary 4.1.2, page 63; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if the reciprocal Xi function is the pointwise limit of Laplace transforms of generalized gamma convolutions on a set of points with an accumulation point in the positive half line. First stated in Polson (2026), On Hilbert's 8th Problem: the reciprocal Xi clock, Thorin measures, van Dantzig pairs, zero free approximations, hitting times and the reciprocal zeta function, first edition, Independently published.
- [The reciprocal Xi function is HCM if and only if RH holds](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_xi_hcm_iff_rh) (Corollary 4.1.3, page 64; proved): The reciprocal Xi function with basepoint alpha at least one is hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) unconditionally, and with basepoint one half (the clock) it is HCM if and only if the Riemann Hypothesis holds; the equivalence for the clock is noted in versions 21 and 23 of Polson's arXiv paper 1708.02653, and both parts follow from Polson (2026) by Bondesson's theorem. New in this book.
- [Deformed reciprocal Xi function is HCM exactly for t at least Lambda](../RESULTS.md#deformed_reciprocal_xi_hcm_iff_time_at_least_lambda) (Corollary 4.2.5, page 71; proved): The reciprocal Xi function deformed by the de Bruijn Newman heat flow to time t is HCM exactly when t is at least the de Bruijn Newman constant, so RH is equivalent to HCM for every positive t. New in this book.
- [Question: for which beta is the density of W^beta HCM](../RESULTS.md#question_hcm_powers_of_mellin_variable_w) (Question 4.2.7, page 72; open): The book asks for which powers beta the density of W to the power beta is HCM, where W is the positive variable with Mellin transform Xi(0)/Xi(z). New in this book.
- [Question: does HCM of W^beta for a single beta imply RH](../RESULTS.md#question_single_power_hcm_implies_rh) (Question 4.2.8, page 72; open): The book asks whether HCM of the density of W to the power beta for one single beta already implies the Riemann Hypothesis. New in this book.
- [Elasticity criterion for HCM functions (Bondesson's representation restated as a Pick condition)](../RESULTS.md#elasticity_criterion_for_hcm_functions) (Theorem 4.3.2, page 73; proved): A positive function on the half line is HCM exactly when it extends to a zero free analytic function off the negative axis whose elasticity is a Pick function; this restates Bondesson's representation of HCM functions, with a direct proof of sufficiency. New in this book.
- [Bosch type theorem for the reciprocal Xi function](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_xi_power_hcm_threshold) (Theorem 4.3.5, page 75; proved): The reciprocal Xi function composed with the power x to the p is HCM exactly when the absolute value of p is at most one minus twice the largest zero angle over pi, so RH is the extreme case p equal to one. New in this book.
- [Equivalent forms of RH: HCM of all powers, Pick elasticity](../RESULTS.md#rh_iff_hcm_of_all_powers_below_one_and_pick_elasticity) (Corollary 4.3.6, page 76; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to HCM of the reciprocal Xi function composed with x to the p for every p of absolute value below one, to the elasticity of the reciprocal Xi function being a Pick function, and to an angular sign condition on xi prime over xi. New in this book.
- [Unconditional HCM range for the reciprocal Xi function composed with a power](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_xi_power_hcm_unconditional_range) (Corollary 4.3.7, page 76; proved): From the verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12, the reciprocal Xi function composed with x to the p is HCM for all p of absolute value at most one minus 1/(3 pi times 10^12), which exceeds one minus 1.1 times 10^-13. New in this book.
- [Stieltjes sign of minus the logarithmic derivative of the clock outside a thin parabolic region](../RESULTS.md#stieltjes_sign_of_clock_outside_parabolic_region) (Theorem 4.3.9, page 77; proved): From the verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12, minus the logarithmic derivative of the reciprocal Xi function has the sign of a Stieltjes function at every point of the upper half plane outside an explicit thin parabolic region. New in this book.
- [Question: HCM exponent of the deformed reciprocal Xi function for negative time](../RESULTS.md#question_hcm_exponent_deficit_negative_heat_time) (Question 4.3.11, page 79; open): The book asks how fast one minus the HCM exponent of the deformed reciprocal Xi function tends to zero as the heat time rises to zero from below, assuming RH. New in this book.
- [Thorin measure of the reciprocal Xi function composed with a power](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_of_reciprocal_xi_power) (Theorem 4.3.12, page 79; proved): For p up to the threshold, the reciprocal Xi function composed with s to the p is the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution without drift, whose Thorin measure for p below the threshold has a density given by xi prime over xi along a ray, with unit atoms added at the threshold. New in this book.
- [Thorin measure of X_p under RH: scaled Lamperti laws (Bosch's variable)](../RESULTS.md#thorin_measure_of_reciprocal_xi_power_under_rh_lamperti_kernel) (Corollary 4.3.13, page 80; conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis): Under RH the Thorin measure of X_p is p times the sum over the zeros of the law of gamma^(2/p) L_p, where L_p is a ratio of independent positive p stable variables whose power with exponent p is Bosch's variable. New in this book.
- [RH is equivalent to the existence of the GGC variable X_p for every p in (0, 1)](../RESULTS.md#rh_iff_reciprocal_xi_power_is_ggc_transform_for_all_p) (Corollary 4.3.14, page 81; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if the reciprocal Xi function composed with s to the p is a generalized gamma convolution transform for every p between zero and one, and unconditionally it is one for p up to one minus 1/(3 pi times 10^12), in particular for p up to one minus 1.1 times 10^-13, with an explicit Thorin density. New in this book.
- [Question: construct X_{1/2} from Brownian motion without the zeros](../RESULTS.md#question_construct_x_half_without_zeros) (Question 4.3.15, page 81; open): The book asks for a construction of the variable X_{1/2}, with Laplace transform xi(1/2)/xi(1/2+s^(1/4)), from Brownian motion, Bessel processes or the Biane Pitman Yor variables without reference to the zeros, noting that a construction surviving p to one would prove RH. New in this book.
- [Question: prove positivity of u_p for all p without locating zeros](../RESULTS.md#question_thorin_density_positive_for_all_powers) (Question 4.3.16, page 81; open): The book asks for a direct proof, without locating zeros, that the explicit density u_p built from xi prime over xi along a ray is nonnegative for every p between zero and one, a statement equivalent to RH. New in this book.
- [Thorin density u_p is positive beyond a finite point, from the primes](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_of_power_positive_beyond_strip_from_primes) (Proposition 4.3.18, page 82; proved): For each p in (0,1) the Thorin density u_p of the reciprocal Xi function composed with s to the p is positive beyond a finite point, by a lower bound that involves the gamma factor and the prime sum but no zero of zeta. New in this book.
- [Cauchy analogue of the clock: eta cubed density with natural boundary, not HCM](../RESULTS.md#cauchy_clock_density_eta_cubed_natural_boundary) (Theorem 4.3.20, page 83; proved): The Cauchy analogue of the clock variable has the density (4/pi) times the cube of the Dedekind eta function at 4it/pi, whose natural boundary is the imaginary axis, so its Laplace transform is HCM but its density is not. New in this book.
- [Question: natural boundary of the clock density](../RESULTS.md#question_natural_boundary_of_clock_density) (Question 4.3.21, page 83; open): The book asks whether, under RH and simple zeros, the density of the clock variable has the imaginary axis as a natural boundary, which would show that the clock variable is not HCM. New in this book.
- [Natural boundary of the clock density under RH, simple zeros and gap hypotheses](../RESULTS.md#clock_density_natural_boundary_under_gap_hypotheses) (Theorem 4.3.22, page 84; conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis; all zeros of the zeta function are simple; hypothesis (G), $\inf_n(\gamma_{n+1}^2-\gamma_n^2)>0$; hypothesis (D), $\log(1/|\zeta'(\tfrac12+i\gamma_n)|)=o(\gamma_n^2)$): Under RH, simple zeros and two hypotheses on the gaps between zeros and on the size of the derivative of zeta at the zeros, the imaginary axis is a natural boundary of the density of the clock variable, which is therefore not HCM. New in this book.
- [Question: for which p are X_p and its Cauchy analogue HCM](../RESULTS.md#question_which_powers_of_clock_variable_are_hcm) (Question 4.3.24, page 85; open): The book asks for the set of exponents p for which the variable X_p, with Laplace transform the reciprocal Xi function composed with s to the p, has an HCM density, and the same set for the Cauchy family. New in this book.
- [Lower bound for the density of log W: existence of W (second proof)](../RESULTS.md#mellin_variable_w_density_lower_bound_unconditional) (Theorem 4.3.25, page 86; proved): From the verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12, the density of log W is at least 0.9998 times the density built from the real zeros alone, so the positive variable W with Mellin transform Xi(0)/Xi(z) exists unconditionally. New in this book.
- [HCM criterion for powers of the Mellin variable W](../RESULTS.md#elasticity_criterion_for_powers_of_mellin_variable_w) (Proposition 4.3.26, page 87; proved): The density of W to the power beta is HCM exactly when the density g of log W has no zeros in a strip of half width pi over absolute beta and minus g prime over g has nonnegative imaginary part in the upper half of that strip. New in this book.
- [First gap theorem: HCM of W^beta forces |beta| at least gamma_2 minus gamma_1](../RESULTS.md#first_gap_necessary_condition_for_hcm_of_w_power) (Theorem 4.3.27, page 88; conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis; all zeros of the zeta function are simple; $\log(1/|\zeta'(\rho_n)|)=O(\gamma_n)$, where $\rho_n=\tfrac12+i\gamma_n$ are the zeros of zeta in the upper half plane): Under RH, simple zeros and a growth bound on one over the derivative of zeta at the zeros, the density of W to the power beta can be HCM only if the absolute value of beta is at least the first gap gamma_2 minus gamma_1, which is 6.8873. New in this book.
- [Question: first gap conjecture for HCM powers of W](../RESULTS.md#question_first_gap_conjecture) (Question 4.3.28, page 88; open): The book asks whether the density of W to the power beta is HCM exactly when the absolute value of beta is at least the first gap gamma_2 minus gamma_1 between zeros of zeta. New in this book.
- [Refined first gap conjecture for sums of Laplace variables](../RESULTS.md#question_first_gap_conjecture_refined) (Question 4.3.29, page 90; open): The book asks whether, for e to the power of a sum of independent Laplace variables over frequencies lambda_n, the HCM threshold of the powers is exactly the first gap lambda_2 minus lambda_1 when the first gap is the largest or the characteristic function decays faster than any exponential.
