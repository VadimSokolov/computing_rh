What does Iwaniec's approach to a Landau Siegel zero and to a quasi Riemann hypothesis mean inside the Thorin framework of the three volumes? The chapter reworks an unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026), written in the book's form and corrected after a referee's reading; every result carries one status word (proved, computed, conditional, heuristic or open) and no priority is claimed. Iwaniec's approach, taken from Conrey's survey, is reported heuristically: a family of $L$ functions with a multiple zero at $s=\frac12$ (elliptic curves $y^2=x^3+Ax+B^2$) and the Möbius function approximated by the coefficients of the family. For an admissible $L$ function (the completed $\xi$, a real primitive Dirichlet $L$ function, or an elliptic curve $L$ function) and a level $\alpha\ge\frac12$, $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ says there is no zero with real part above $\alpha$. The dictionary theorem (proved) shows that $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ is equivalent to the Laplace transform $\Lambda(\alpha)/\Lambda(\alpha+\sqrt s)$ being a generalised gamma convolution (GGC), and to positivity of $\operatorname{Re}\Lambda'/\Lambda$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha$, with an explicit Thorin density $U_\alpha$. Since the zero free half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ is a theorem of OpenAI (2026), the measure $U_\alpha$ exists unconditionally for $\xi$ and every Dirichlet $L$ function at every $\alpha\ge\frac78$; below $\frac78$ each level keeps its status. The chapter proves the factor lemma, the Cauchy semigroup in the level and the mass growth $N(T)+O(\log T)$ uniformly in $\alpha$ (both under $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$, so unconditional for $\alpha\ge\frac78$), a level explicit formula whose positivity for all nonnegative band limited test functions is equivalent to $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$ (Weil's criterion is the average at the centre), and three readings at the origin (rank, a Landau Siegel zero, central values). It computes tables for $\zeta$, $L(s,\chi_{-4})$ and five curves of the family, and a computed counterexample shows that the full line functional does not characterise $\mathrm{QRH}(\alpha)$. The answer to its title question is no: the dictionary renames the rungs of the ladder and adds none, and the chapter adds no method toward the Riemann hypothesis (RH).

## From the literature

- Iwaniec's approach as reported by Conrey (Conrey 2003) and Iwaniec's own account (Iwaniec 2006), neither read in full by the authors
- the theorems of Gross and Zagier (1986) and Kolyvagin (1989) on analytic rank one
- Lagarias's positivity criterion for $\operatorname{Re}\zeta'/\zeta$ (Lagarias 1999)
- Bondesson's closure properties of generalised gamma convolutions (Bondesson 1992) and Thorin's representation (Thorin 1977)
- Weil's explicit formula criterion (Weil 1952)
- the classical equivalence of $\psi(x)=x+O(x^{\alpha+\varepsilon})$ and $M(x)=O(x^{\alpha+\varepsilon})$ with a zero free half plane (Landau; Titchmarsh 1986; Ingham 1932)
- explicit formula rank bounds (Mestre 1986; Brumer 1992)
- the zero free half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ for $\zeta$ and every Dirichlet $L$ function, a theorem of OpenAI (2026), and the unrefereed claims on Landau Siegel zeros

## New results

The chapter's new statements are the dictionary, the level explicit formula and its converse, the origin readings and the computed tables and counterexample.
