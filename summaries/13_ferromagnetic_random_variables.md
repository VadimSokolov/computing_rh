# Chapter 13. Ferromagnetic random variables

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis: Thorin Measures, Pólya Densities and Hitting Times* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 13. Which random variables are ferromagnetic, and is the Pólya law ferromagnetic? A symmetric random variable is ferromagnetic if its moment generating function vanishes only on the imaginary axis, the Lee and Yang property of a single Ising spin. The Pólya variable has density p = Φ/Ξ(0) and moment generating function ξ(1/2+s)/ξ(1/2), so the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) says exactly that it is ferromagnetic. The chapter develops the class: the clock (a generalised gamma convolution, GGC, with one Thorin atom per pair of zeros, giving a van Dantzig pair), closure under weak limits (RH holds if and only if the Pólya law is a limit in law of ferromagnetic laws), mixtures of uniform laws (the shoulder theorem) and the absence of a Gaussian component. It builds ferromagnetic approximations of the Pólya law within total variation distance 2 × 10^-6, certified in ball arithmetic, and shows that RH is equivalent to an identity in law with a series of Maxwell variables. It studies e^{2X}, a size biased variable of Biane, Pitman and Yor: not a GGC, self decomposable, with hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) powers e^{βX} for |β| ≥ 5.5791422, by a computer assisted proof in ball arithmetic. Coupled Bessel kernel spins with a nonnegative interaction have ferromagnetic nonnegative combinations, and a weak coupling moves the zeros of the two spin transform by order J or by the square root of J (notes of Nicholas Polson). A last section treats two arithmetic spin chains, Knauf's number theoretical chain (Stern's diatomic sequence, partition function converging to zeta(β-1)/zeta(β) for Re β > 2) and the Farey chain in a field, with a Perron identity, computed Fisher zeros near the points 1+ρ and the Lee and Yang property (a note of Nicholas Polson).

## From the literature

The Lee and Yang theorem (Lee and Yang 1952); Newman's single spin laws, Hadamard form and cumulants (Newman 1974; 1975); Lieb and Sokal (1981). The Griffiths Simon and Ellis, Monroe and Newman classes (Simon and Griffiths 1973; Ellis, Monroe and Newman 1976) and the GHS inequality for the Pólya law (Newman 1991). Van Dantzig pairs (Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar 2024). Pólya's theorems on real zeros (1918; 1926); de Bruijn's strip theorem (1950); Turán inequalities (Csordas, Norfolk and Varga 1986); Jensen polynomials (Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier 2019). The Biane, Pitman and Yor variable (2001), Kent (1978), Fourati (2016). Stern's diatomic sequence (Stern 1858) and the Calkin Wilf enumeration of the rationals (Calkin and Wilf 2000); Knauf's number theoretical spin chain (Knauf 1993; 1998), its phase transition at β = 2 (Contucci and Knauf 1997), the Farey fraction chain (Kleban and Özlük 1999; Contucci, Kleban and Knauf 1999; Fiala, Kleban and Özlük 2003); the mean sum of partial quotients (Yao and Knuth 1975). The half plane Re s > 7/8 is announced by OpenAI (2026), not refereed.

## New results

Notes of Nicholas Polson add the Mellin transform of the clock of a ferromagnetic law: a residue expansion, a zeta combination for sums of k uniform spins, computed zeros off one vertical line for four and six spins, the density (1+cos t)/2pi and a deformation of the uniform pair, a Bessel pair with no zero in a box, and the open problem of which laws have all nonreal zeros on one line.

A note of Nicholas Polson adds that the Pólya law is not a scale mixture of the triangular law and that its kernel has one inflection point. A second note of Nicholas Polson adds the ferromagnetic laws with density proportional to a multiplier times exp(-a cosh(l t)), expressed through Macdonald functions,; the book shows that no sequence of them on a convergent kernel tends in law to the Pólya law, which answers a question of the note. A third note of Nicholas Polson adds the Perron identity for Knauf's partition function, computed Fisher zeros and a computed Lee and Yang threshold for the Farey chain and two conjectures that are open. A fourth note of Nicholas Polson adds a proof that the components of the theta series in Riemann's formula and their mixture are not ferromagnetic. The results of this chapter are proved (one is a counterexample and one is conditional on the announced zero free half plane Re s > 7/8), computed (the Fisher zeros of Knauf's chain, the Lee and Yang threshold of the Farey chain and the zeros of the Mellin transforms of clocks) or open problems.
