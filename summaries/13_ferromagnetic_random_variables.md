# Chapter 13. Ferromagnetic random variables

Which random variables are ferromagnetic, and is the Pólya law ferromagnetic? A symmetric random variable is ferromagnetic if its moment generating function vanishes only on the imaginary axis, the Lee and Yang property of a single Ising spin. The Pólya variable has density p = Φ/Ξ(0) and moment generating function ξ(1/2+s)/ξ(1/2), so the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) says exactly that it is ferromagnetic. The chapter develops the class: the clock (a generalised gamma convolution, GGC, with one Thorin atom per pair of zeros, giving a van Dantzig pair), closure under weak limits (RH holds if and only if the Pólya law is a limit in law of ferromagnetic laws), mixtures of uniform laws (the shoulder theorem) and the absence of a Gaussian component. It builds ferromagnetic approximations of the Pólya law within total variation distance 2 × 10^-6, certified in ball arithmetic, and shows that RH is equivalent to an identity in law with a series of Maxwell variables. It studies e^{2X}, a size biased variable of Biane, Pitman and Yor: not a GGC, self decomposable, with hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) powers e^{βX} for |β| ≥ 5.5791422, by a computer assisted proof in ball arithmetic.

## From the literature

The Lee and Yang theorem (Lee and Yang 1952); Newman's single spin laws, Hadamard form and cumulants (Newman 1974; 1975); Lieb and Sokal (1981). The Griffiths Simon and Ellis, Monroe and Newman classes (Simon and Griffiths 1973; Ellis, Monroe and Newman 1976) and the GHS inequality for the Pólya law (Newman 1991). Van Dantzig pairs (Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar 2024). Pólya's theorems on real zeros (1918; 1926); de Bruijn's strip theorem (1950); Turán inequalities (Csordas, Norfolk and Varga 1986); Jensen polynomials (Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier 2019). The Biane, Pitman and Yor variable (2001), Kent (1978), Fourati (2016). The half plane Re s > 7/8 is announced by OpenAI (2026), not refereed.

## New results

The results of this chapter are proved (one is a counterexample and one is conditional on the announced zero free half plane Re s > 7/8) or are open problems.

- [Clock and van Dantzig partner of a ferromagnetic law](../RESULTS.md#ferromagnetic_law_clock_and_van_dantzig_partner) (Theorem 13.1.4, page 521; proved): The clock of a ferromagnetic law is a generalised gamma convolution with drift equal to the Gaussian part and one unit Thorin atom at the square of each positive zero of its characteristic function, and the law has a partner of Pólya frequency type forming a van Dantzig pair with it. New in this book.
- [Ferromagnetic laws are closed under convergence in law](../RESULTS.md#ferromagnetic_laws_closed_under_convergence_in_law) (Theorem 13.1.5, page 523; proved): A limit in law of ferromagnetic random variables is ferromagnetic, the variances converge and the moment generating functions converge locally uniformly on the complex plane. New in this book.
- [RH holds exactly when the Pólya law is a limit in law of ferromagnetic laws](../RESULTS.md#rh_iff_polya_law_is_a_limit_in_law_of_ferromagnetic_laws) (Corollary 13.1.7, page 524; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the Pólya law being a limit in law of ferromagnetic laws. New in this book.
- [A uniform law mixed with an atom at the origin is not ferromagnetic](../RESULTS.md#mixture_of_uniform_law_with_atom_at_origin_is_not_ferromagnetic) (Theorem 13.2.1, page 525; counterexample, proved): The mixture of the uniform law on [-1,1] and a point mass at the origin is symmetric and unimodal but not ferromagnetic, so mixtures of ferromagnetic laws need not be ferromagnetic. New in this book.
- [Shoulder theorem for symmetric laws on an interval](../RESULTS.md#shoulder_theorem_for_ferromagnetic_laws_on_an_interval) (Theorem 13.2.3, page 526; proved): A symmetric density on [-1,1] that vanishes at plus and minus 1 and is nonincreasing on [0,1] gives a ferromagnetic law if it is concave on [0,1], and does not if it is convex on [0,1] and not linear on its support. New in this book.
- [Ferromagnetic laws among the kernels 1 minus |x|^b, k monotone kernels and products of uniform laws](../RESULTS.md#ferromagnetic_laws_among_power_kernels_and_products_of_uniform_laws) (Corollary 13.2.4, page 526; proved): The law with density proportional to 1 minus |x|^b is ferromagnetic exactly when b is at least 1, the k monotone kernel exactly when k is at most 2, the densities (1 minus x squared) to the power m with 0 < m at most 1 are ferromagnetic, and a product of two independent uniform variables is not. New in this book.
- [Densities nondecreasing in |x| and mixtures of uniform laws on shells are ferromagnetic](../RESULTS.md#shell_mixtures_of_uniform_laws_are_ferromagnetic) (Theorem 13.2.5, page 527; proved): Every symmetric density on [-1,1] that is nondecreasing in |x|, equivalently every mixture of uniform laws on the shells a at most |x| at most 1, is ferromagnetic. New in this book.
- [The Pólya law has no Gaussian component and no ferromagnetic summand with finitely many zeros](../RESULTS.md#polya_law_has_no_gaussian_component_and_no_finite_zero_summand) (Theorem 13.3.1, page 531; proved): With no hypothesis on the zeros, an independent summand of a symmetric law whose log moment generating function is o(s^2) has log moment generating function o(s^2) as well, so the Pólya law has no Gaussian component and is not a sum of independent ferromagnetic variables one of which is nonzero with finitely many zeros. New in this book.

New in this book.

New in this book.
- [RH is equivalent to an identity in law between the smoothed Pólya variable and a series of Maxwell variables](../RESULTS.md#rh_iff_polya_variable_smoothed_by_gaussian_equals_maxwell_series_in_law) (Theorem 13.4.5, page 538; proved): The Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if the Pólya variable plus an independent Gaussian of half its variance has the law of the series of independent Maxwell variables divided by the ordinates of the zeros. New in this book.

- [Factorisation of the exponential of the Pólya variable against a Biane Pitman Yor variable](../RESULTS.md#factorisation_of_exponential_of_polya_variable_against_biane_pitman_yor_variable) (Theorem 13.5.6, page 544; proved): Adding an independent generalised gamma convolution without drift to the size biased Biane Pitman Yor variable B_q gives (pi/2) S_{2+q}, and in particular e to the 2X plus an independent generalised gamma convolution has the law of (pi/2) S_{9/4}.
