# Volume I, Chapter 18. Hitting times, Krein strings and the Pólya Bessel clock

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 18. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function*, edition 2, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23861971. This chapter studies Pólya's function $P(\theta)=K_{i\theta/2}(2\pi)$, the Macdonald function that is the building block of his approximation $\Xi^*$ to the Riemann Xi function, as the one model in which every statement of the Thorin programme is a theorem with an explicit witness. Biane showed that $\Xi$ and $P$ are Mellin transforms of Brownian hitting times whose Thorin measures are spectral measures of radial Brownian motions on $SU(2)$ and on $SL_2(\mathbb C)/SU(2)$. The chapter restates Biane's dictionary with corrected constants, the Wick rotation and curvature family, and shows that the zeros of $P$ are the Dirichlet spectrum of a Liouville operator (a Krein string). On the index side $K_0(2\pi)/K_{\sqrt u}(2\pi)$ is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC) with the squared zeros as Thorin atoms, and its clock is the hitting time of an explicit diffusion; its Poisson trace is completely monotone as the trace of a semigroup, and the sine squared lemma and a Klein Gordon criterion (Rindler modes) are proved for it. The analogy with $\Xi$ stops at the de Bruijn Newman flow: by a theorem of Newman the function $P$ has a finite de Bruijn Newman constant, although its zeros show no sign of colliding in the computed range. A side by side comparison with $\Xi$ lists which statements are theorems for $P$ and remain open for $\Xi$. The exercises cover the law of the clock of $\Xi$, its Brownian boundaries, Li's criterion and the Dirichlet beta function. No proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is claimed.

## From the literature

- Pólya's replacement of the kernel $\Phi$ by $\Phi^*$ and real zeros of $\Xi^*$ (Pólya 1926)
- Biane's dictionary of hitting times and spectral measures, and the Liouville operator of the zeros, going back to Titchmarsh (Biane 2009)
- $2\xi(s)$ as a Mellin transform of Bessel hitting times (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- Kent's eigenvalue expansion of hitting times and Krein's inverse spectral theory (Kent 1980; Dym and McKean 1976)
- the de Bruijn Newman constant (de Bruijn 1950; Newman 1976)
- generalised gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992)

- [Laplace transform of Bessel hitting times with curvature](../RESULTS.md#laplace_transform_of_bessel_hitting_times_with_curvature) (Volume I, Proposition 18.1.6, page 507; proved): The hitting time of radius a by the radial Brownian motion on the three dimensional space form of curvature kappa has an explicit Laplace transform, is a GGC with equally spaced Thorin atoms shifted by minus kappa/2, and is an exponential tilt of the flat case. The flat case is classical (Ciesielski and Taylor 1962), and for kappa < 0 the process is the Bessel process of dimension three with drift (Salminen and Vallois 2007), the norm of a three dimensional Brownian motion with drift, where the formula also follows from the Cameron Martin formula; the formula for the whole family, its Thorin measure and its reading as an exponential tilt are the book's. New in this book.
- [Exact quantisation rule for the zeros of Pólya's Bessel function](../RESULTS.md#exact_quantisation_rule_for_zeros_of_polya_bessel_function) (Volume I, Proposition 18.1.8, page 510; proved): The positive zeros of nu maps to K_{i nu}(2 pi) are exactly the solutions of an explicit phase equation, so their counting function is the integer part of a smooth function with an explicit asymptotic expansion. New in this book.
- [Index side Thorin measure of Pólya's Bessel function](../RESULTS.md#index_side_thorin_measure_of_polya_bessel_function) (Volume I, Theorem 18.1.9, page 512; proved): For Pólya's Bessel function the reciprocal K_0(2 pi)/K_{sqrt u}(2 pi) is, without any hypothesis, the Laplace transform of the GGC whose Thorin atoms are the squares of the positive zeros of nu maps to K_{i nu}(2 pi). New in this book.
- [Clock of Pólya's Bessel function as a hitting time of an explicit diffusion](../RESULTS.md#clock_of_polya_bessel_function_hitting_time_of_explicit_diffusion) (Volume I, Theorem 18.1.10, page 513; proved): The index side clock of Pólya's Bessel function, the GGC with Thorin atoms at the squares of the positive zeros of nu maps to K_{i nu}(2 pi), is the clock of a hitting time of a level by an explicit diffusion, planar Brownian motion killed at rate one half and Doob transformed by K_0. New in this book.
- [Sine squared forms of the Bessel clock, the drifted clock and Pólya's clock](../RESULTS.md#sine_squared_forms_of_bessel_clock_drifted_clock_and_polya_clock) (Volume I, Proposition 18.1.13, page 516; proved): The Laplace exponents of the sum of two Bessel hitting times, of the drifted first passage time and of Pólya's clock have sine squared forms whose kernels are the classical Planck and Macdonald measures and, for Pólya's clock, a completely monotone wave trace of a selfadjoint operator. New in this book.
- [Klein Gordon criterion for real zeros](../RESULTS.md#klein_gordon_criterion_for_real_zeros) (Volume I, Theorem 18.1.14, page 518; proved): If an even positive kernel is the boundary value of a solution of a Klein Gordon equation with a real potential bounded below, with the stated decay and nonvanishing conditions, then every zero of its Fourier transform is real and its square is a Dirichlet eigenvalue. New in this book.
- [De Bruijn Newman constant of Pólya's function is finite](../RESULTS.md#de_bruijn_newman_constant_of_polya_function_is_finite) (Volume I, Proposition 18.1.15, page 520; proved): The Gaussian deformations of Pólya's Bessel function have only real zeros exactly from a finite time Lambda_P at most zero onward, by an argument of Newman, so its de Bruijn Newman constant is not minus infinity, contrary to a conjecture of the first author's earlier note. New in this book.
- [A property that survives Gaussian damping cannot imply real zeros](../RESULTS.md#gaussian_damping_property_cannot_imply_real_zeros) (Volume I, Proposition 18.1.16, page 523; proved): No property of even positive kernels that implies real zeros and survives a fixed Gaussian damping can hold for Riemann's kernel Phi, for Pólya's asymptote or for the Bessel kernel, which excludes a family of constructions with room to spare, using the theorems of Rodgers and Tao and of Newman. New in this book.
- [Open question on a diffusion for the clock of the Riemann xi function](../RESULTS.md#open_diffusion_for_clock_of_riemann_xi_function) (Volume I, Question 18.1.17, page 524; open): Is there, without assuming RH, a diffusion on a half line with an entrance boundary whose hitting time of a level has the Laplace transform of the clock of Riemann's xi function scaled by four? New in this book.
- [Open question on a Klein Gordon extension of Pólya's kernel](../RESULTS.md#open_klein_gordon_extension_of_polya_kernel) (Volume I, Question 18.1.18, page 525; open): Does the kernel of Riemann's xi function, rescaled, admit a Klein Gordon extension with a real potential asymptotic to e^{2x}, which would imply RH? New in this book.
- [Open question on the de Bruijn Newman constants of Pólya's functions](../RESULTS.md#open_de_bruijn_newman_constants_of_polya_functions) (Volume I, Question 18.1.19, page 525; open): What are the de Bruijn Newman constants of Pólya's Bessel function and of his function Xi star, at what height do the first nonreal zeros appear, and do they appear through collisions of real zeros? New in this book.
- [Open question on a family joining the two clocks](../RESULTS.md#open_family_joining_the_two_clocks) (Volume I, Question 18.1.20, page 525; open): Is there a one parameter family of laws joining the drifted first passage time and the sum of two Bessel hitting times, each member with a reciprocal symmetry encoding a functional equation, along which the index side Thorin measure can be followed? New in this book.
