# Chapter 8. Probability, hitting times and other criteria

What else does the Pólya density compute about the Riemann Hypothesis (RH)? The clock $1/G$ of Riemann's xi function is the Laplace transform of a random variable $T$, self decomposable with no hypothesis on the zeros and a generalized gamma convolution (GGC) exactly when RH holds, and the Pólya route computes its law with no zero as input. The chapter studies this clock variable (hazard rate, Kent approximants, Monte Carlo through Williams' representation), proves that it is not a Brownian first passage time to an independent random level, and computes a curved Brownian boundary for it from the Volterra equation. It computes the Keiper Li coefficients, reads Li's criterion as positivity of the Thorin functional, and follows the de Bruijn Newman flow to the collisions of zeros, with a Hankel test and collision time bounds in terms of Laguerre's quantity suggested by Biane's free convolution with a semicircle law. It then evaluates the Dedekind eta kernel, Dirichlet $L$ functions and Ramanujan's $\Delta$, recovers zeros by nonnegative deconvolution, and treats Pólya's Bessel function $K_{i\theta/2}(2\pi)$, for which the Thorin programme holds as theorems. No proof of RH is claimed.

## From the literature

- Kent's eigenvalue expansion of hitting times (Kent 1980) and Krein's inverse spectral theory (Dym and McKean 1976).
- Williams' Bessel hitting time representation of $\xi$ (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001); Talbot inversion (Talbot 1979).
- Tangent approximation and inverse problem for first passage boundaries (Roberts and Shortland 1995; Ekström and Janson 2016).
- Li's criterion (Li 1997), Keiper's coefficients (Keiper 1992), Bombieri and Lagarias (1999), Lagarias's positivity criterion (Lagarias 1999).
- The de Bruijn Newman constant (de Bruijn 1950; Newman 1976), $\Lambda\ge0$ (Rodgers and Tao 2020), $\Lambda\le0.2$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021), Lehmer pair bounds (Csordas, Smith and Varga 1994).
- Free convolution with a semicircle law (Biane 1997); Pólya's Bessel function (Pólya 1926); Biane's dictionary of hitting times and spectral measures, with corrected constants, and the Liouville operator of the zeros, going back to Titchmarsh (Biane 2009); Hecke's converse theorem (Hecke 1936).

- [The clock is not a Brownian first passage time to an independent random level](../RESULTS.md#clock_not_first_passage_time_to_random_level) (Proposition 8.2.1, page 252; proved): The clock variable of the Riemann xi function is not, for any constant drift and any independent random level, the first passage time of Brownian motion with that drift to that level, and the proof uses no hypothesis on the zeros. New in this book.
- [Keiper Li coefficients as values of the Thorin functional](../RESULTS.md#keiper_li_coefficients_as_thorin_functional) (Proposition 8.3.1, page 260; proved): With no hypothesis on the zeros, each Keiper Li coefficient is the Thorin functional of the zeros paired with an explicit rational function that is nonnegative on the positive axis, so Li's criterion is nonnegativity of that functional on the cone spanned by these functions. New in this book.
- [Critical point lower bound for the collision time of zeros under the backward heat flow](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_critical_point_collision_bound) (Theorem 8.4.6, page 278; proved): While all zeros stay real and simple, a gap between two zeros of a real polynomial cannot close under the backward heat flow before the time one over Laguerre's quantity at the critical point of the gap, and z squared minus a squared attains the bound. New in this book.
- [Biane form lower bound for the collision time of zeros under the backward heat flow](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_biane_form_collision_bound) (Theorem 8.4.8, page 279; proved): While all zeros of a real polynomial stay real and simple, a gap between two adjacent zeros cannot close under the backward heat flow before the time one over the minimum of Laguerre's quantity over the gap, a bound at least as strong as the one from the critical point and attained by z squared minus a squared. New in this book.
- [Local lower bound for the closing of a gap with a correction from the nonreal zeros](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_local_bound_nonreal_zeros) (Theorem 8.4.11, page 281; proved): For the minimum m of Laguerre's quantity over a gap between real zeros, the upper right Dini derivative is at most one plus an explicit correction, computed from the nonreal zeros, times m squared, which bounds the time at which the gap closes from below. New in this book.
- [Lower bound for the closing of a gap from the quantity delta, with no knowledge of the nonreal zeros](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_delta_collision_bound) (Corollary 8.4.12, page 282; proved): A quantity delta computed from the function and its first four derivatives at the minimiser of Laguerre's quantity over a gap between real zeros controls the growth of that minimum under the backward heat flow, and when delta stays at most zero until the collision the gap cannot close before Biane's time, one over the initial minimum of Laguerre's quantity, even when nonreal zeros are present. New in this book.
- [Upper bound (deadline) for the closing time of a gap between real zeros](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_collision_deadline) (Theorem 8.4.13, page 282; proved): If the share Q of the other zeros and the sum W over the nonreal zeros stay below constants rho and W bar for which 3 rho plus the square root of 9 rho squared plus 6 W bar is less than the initial minimum of Laguerre's quantity over a gap between two real zeros, the gap closes, by a collision of one of its bounding zeros with another zero, no later than an explicit logarithmic time. New in this book.
