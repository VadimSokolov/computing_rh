# Volume II, Chapter 6. The Cauchy deconvolution operator

When can a measure be recovered from its convolution with a Cauchy density, and how stably? Cauchy deconvolution inverts the Poisson semigroup, so it is the Cauchy problem for Laplace's equation, a severely ill posed inverse problem, and it is the operator behind the Thorin measures of the book: the explicit formula is this deconvolution at level one half, and the Thorin family $\mathfrak U_\theta=P_{\theta-\theta'}*\mathfrak U_{\theta'}$ is the family of its partial deconvolutions. The chapter proves uniqueness for admissible measures, describes the range of convolution on $L^2$, gives a positive datum with only a signed source, and proves that the critical width of a positive datum, beyond which no positive source exists, is attained when finite. A nonnegative source at width $c$ exists exactly when the harmonic extension continues as a nonnegative harmonic function below the line (Herglotz and Riesz representation), which at the level one Thorin density gives Lagarias's criterion for the Riemann Hypothesis; for the zeta family the critical width is one minus the supremum of the real parts of the zeros. Stability is Hölder between levels by the three lines theorem, with the sharp exponent $(b+w)/(a+w)$ for finite signed sources, and logarithmic at the boundary for Sobolev sources; the minimax statistical rate is logarithmic over smoothness classes and polynomial, as an upper bound, for sources analytic in a strip. Atomic sources are a different problem: a finite sum of atoms gives a sum of damped exponentials, recovered by Prony's method and the matrix pencil with polynomial ill posedness, with a square root rate for two atoms merging symmetrically. The chapter surveys density deconvolution, Bayesian inverse problems, positivity preserving algorithms (EM, Richardson and Lucy), super resolution and analytic continuation in physics, and poses three open problems on estimating the critical width, on rates under positivity, and on three lines with positivity on a window.

## From the literature

- The Cauchy problem for Laplace's equation as the original ill posed problem (Hadamard 1923)
- The Herglotz and Riesz representation of nonnegative harmonic functions on a half plane (Herglotz and Riesz; see Axler, Bourdon and Ramey 2001)
- Lagarias's positivity criterion for the Riemann Hypothesis (Lagarias 1999)
- Minimax logarithmic rates for supersmooth noise (Carroll and Hall 1988; Fan 1991) and deconvoluting kernel estimators (Stefanski and Carroll 1990); sharp rates for supersmooth targets (Butucea and Tsybakov 2008)
- Posterior contraction for linear inverse problems (Knapik, van der Vaart and van Zanten 2011, 2013); mixing measures (Chen 1995; Nguyen 2013; Gao and van der Vaart 2016)
- EM for positive inverse problems (Richardson 1972; Lucy 1974; Vardi and Lee 1993)
- Prony, the matrix pencil and super resolution (Barkhuijsen et al. 1987; Hua and Sarkar 1990; Donoho 1992; Candès and Fernandez Granda 2014; Schiebinger, Robeva and Recht 2018; Li and Liao 2021)
- Analytic continuation in physics (Jarrell and Gubernatis 1996; Fei, Yeh and Gull 2021)

- [Uniqueness of Cauchy deconvolution for admissible measures](../RESULTS.md#cauchy_deconvolution_is_unique_for_admissible_measures) (Volume II, Proposition 6.1.1, page 127; proved): Two admissible measures with the same convolution with a Cauchy density of width $a$ are equal, by the semigroup property and Fubini's theorem. New in this book.
- [The range of convolution with a Cauchy density on L2, unboundedness of the inverse, and a positive bounded datum outside the range](../RESULTS.md#range_of_cauchy_convolution_on_l2_and_the_failure_of_the_naive_description) (Volume II, Proposition 6.1.2, page 127; proved): On $L^2$ the range of $P_a*$ is the set of $u$ with $\int e^{2a|\omega|}|\widehat u|^2<\infty$, the inverse is unbounded between all Sobolev norms, and $2+\cos(x^2)$ is not $P_a*\mu$ for any admissible $\mu$ although its Poisson extension continues to an entire harmonic function. New in this book.
- [A positive datum with a signed source: the two atom example and its critical width](../RESULTS.md#positive_data_with_a_signed_source_and_the_closed_form_of_the_critical_width) (Volume II, Example 6.2.1, page 128; proved): For $\mu=\delta_0-q\delta_d$ the datum $P_a*\mu$ is nonnegative if and only if $q\le(r-d)/(r+d)$ with $r=\sqrt{d^2+4a^2}$, so a positive datum can have a signed source and no positive one at width $a$; the partial deconvolutions are positive exactly up to an explicit critical width, with $c^*=\frac14$ for $q=9-4\sqrt5$, $a=\frac12$, $d=1$. New in this book.
- [The critical width of a positive datum for Cauchy deconvolution is attained when finite](../RESULTS.md#critical_width_of_a_positive_datum_is_attained) (Volume II, Proposition 6.2.2, page 128; proved): For a nonnegative $u$ with $\int u/(1+x^2)<\infty$, a positive source at width $c$ exists for every finite $c\le c^*(u)$ and for no larger $c$, where $c^*(u)$ is the supremum of the widths with a positive source, which is attained when finite. New in this book.
- [Share of the level one density explained by a positive source at width one half](../RESULTS.md#level_one_density_share_explained_by_positive_source_at_width_half) (Volume II, Proposition 6.2.3, page 129; proved): Unconditionally the level one density splits as a Poisson smoothing of a positive measure plus a nonnegative remainder whose mass on a window of height T is O(T), so the explained share of the mass is one minus O(1 over log T). New in this book.
- [A positive source exists at width c if and only if the harmonic extension continues as a nonnegative harmonic function to the half plane y > -c](../RESULTS.md#existence_of_a_positive_source_iff_nonnegative_harmonic_extension_below_the_line) (Volume II, Theorem 6.2.4, page 130; proved): For a continuous $u\ge0$ with $\int u/(1+x^2)<\infty$, $u=P_c*\nu$ with $\nu\ge0$ if and only if the Poisson extension of $u$ extends to a nonnegative harmonic function on $y>-c$, with the linear term of the Herglotz and Riesz representation shown to vanish. New in this book.
- [Gram decomposition of the Pick matrix of the logarithmic derivative of xi](../RESULTS.md#gram_decomposition_of_pick_matrix_of_xi_log_derivative) (Volume II, Lemma 6.2.5, page 131; proved): The Pick matrix of the logarithmic derivative of xi at nodes on the line Re z equals one is a sum over the zeros of Schur products of a rank one matrix with a matrix built from the positive semidefinite Cauchy matrix. New in this book.
- [Pick matrix criterion for a zero free half plane of xi](../RESULTS.md#pick_criterion_for_zero_free_half_plane_of_xi) (Volume II, Theorem 6.2.6, page 131; proved): Xi has no zero to the right of the line with real part lambda exactly when all Pick matrices built from the logarithmic derivative of xi at nodes on the line Re z equals one are positive semidefinite, and nodes in a single segment suffice. New in this book.
- [A negative Pick eigenvalue proves a zero of xi to the right of level lambda](../RESULTS.md#negative_pick_eigenvalue_certifies_zero_right_of_level_lambda) (Volume II, Corollary 6.2.7, page 132; proved): If the Pick matrix of the logarithmic derivative of xi has a negative eigenvalue at some finite set of nodes, then xi has a zero with real part above lambda, and ball arithmetic would make this a certified proof. New in this book.
- [Hölder stability of Cauchy deconvolution between levels by the three lines theorem, with sharp exponent](../RESULTS.md#three_lines_holder_stability_of_cauchy_deconvolution_between_levels) (Volume II, Theorem 6.3.1, page 133; proved): $\|P_b*\mu\|_2\le\|P_a*\mu\|_2^{(b-b_0)/(a-b_0)}\|P_{b_0}*\mu\|_2^{(a-b)/(a-b_0)}$ for $b_0<b<a$, so data errors $\varepsilon$ at width $a$ and a bound $M$ at width $b_0$ give an error $\varepsilon^\kappa(2M)^{1-\kappa}$ at width $b$, and the exponent is attained. New in this book.
- [Sharp Hölder exponent (b+w)/(a+w) for Cauchy deconvolution of finite signed sources](../RESULTS.md#sharp_holder_exponent_for_finite_signed_sources) (Volume II, Proposition 6.3.2, page 134; proved): For a finite signed measure $\nu$ of total variation at most $B$ and $h=P_w*\nu$, $\|P_b*h\|_2\le\sqrt2D^{1-\kappa}\|P_a*h\|_2^\kappa$ for $w\ge0$ and $-w<b<a$, with $\kappa=(b+w)/(a+w)$ and $D=B/\sqrt{2\pi(b+w)}$, and no larger exponent holds uniformly over this class. New in this book.
- [The stability exponent is attained inside the positive cone](../RESULTS.md#exponent_b_over_a_attained_by_positive_measures_in_stability_estimate) (Volume II, Proposition 6.3.3, page 134; proved): For positive measures of bounded mass the Holder exponent b over a for recovering the data at width b from data at width a cannot be improved, so positivity costs nothing in the rate and gains nothing. New in this book.
- [Two level Hölder bound for positive sources of Cauchy deconvolution with data in L2 on the line](../RESULTS.md#two_level_bound_for_positive_sources_of_cauchy_deconvolution) (Volume II, Corollary 6.3.4, page 135; proved): For nonnegative sources with data $u_j=P_a*\mu_j\in L^2$ within $\varepsilon$, $\|P_b*(\mu_1-\mu_2)\|_2\le\varepsilon^\kappa((a/d)(\|u_1\|_2+\|u_2\|_2))^{1-\kappa}$ for $0<d<b<a$, with no a priori bound needed. New in this book.
- [Logarithmic stability of Cauchy deconvolution for sources in a Sobolev ball, sharp in order and in the constant](../RESULTS.md#logarithmic_stability_of_cauchy_deconvolution_at_the_boundary_is_sharp) (Volume II, Theorem 6.3.5, page 135; proved): If $\|\mu\|_{H^s}\le M$ and $\|P_a*\mu\|_2\le\varepsilon$ then $\|\mu\|_2\le C_sM(2a/\log(M/\varepsilon))^s$ for $\varepsilon/M$ small, and no bound of smaller order holds uniformly over the Sobolev ball. New in this book.
- [Sharp logarithmic rate of Cauchy deconvolution for positive measures of bounded mass](../RESULTS.md#sharp_logarithmic_rate_for_positive_measures_cauchy_deconvolution) (Volume II, Theorem 6.3.6, page 136; proved): For positive measures of mass at most M the bounded Lipschitz distance is bounded by a constant times M a over log(1 over eta), and a construction with Fejer densities shows that positivity does not improve this logarithmic rate. New in this book.
- [Saturation of positive kernels, bias of order the inverse square of the log](../RESULTS.md#saturation_of_nonnegative_convolution_kernels_limits_positive_linear_methods) (Volume II, Proposition 6.3.7, page 137; proved): A nonnegative band limited convolution kernel cannot approximate faster than the inverse square of its bandwidth, so a positive linear method with bandwidth capped by noise has bias of order one over log squared. New in this book.
- [The positive part of an estimate is a pointwise contraction](../RESULTS.md#positive_part_is_pointwise_contraction_toward_nonnegative_truth) (Volume II, Proposition 6.3.8, page 137; proved): Taking the positive part of any real estimate of a nonnegative function never increases the pointwise error, so positivity can be imposed after inversion at no cost in any L p norm. New in this book.
- [Polynomial upper rate for density deconvolution with Cauchy noise and a target analytic in a strip](../RESULTS.md#spectral_cutoff_deconvolution_of_cauchy_noise_with_a_target_analytic_in_a_strip) (Volume II, Proposition 6.4.1, page 138; proved): For Cauchy noise of width $a$ and a density with $\int e^{2w|\omega|}|\widehat f|^2\le2\pi M^2$, the spectral cutoff estimator with $\Omega=\log n/(2(a+w))$ has root mean integrated squared error $O(n^{-w/(2(a+w))})$, an upper bound.
