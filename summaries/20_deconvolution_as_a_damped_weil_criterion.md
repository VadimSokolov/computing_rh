# Chapter 20. Deconvolution as a damped Weil criterion

This chapter asks whether the deconvolution theorem for the Thorin density of the Riemann $\xi$ function can be carried out: RH holds exactly when the Thorin density at a basepoint to the right of the zeros is the Cauchy (Poisson) smoothing of a positive measure on the critical line. The deconvolved object always exists, with no hypothesis: it is Weil's distribution, the zero sum of Weil's explicit formula, computed from the gamma factor, the pole and the primes. Its Fourier dual is a damped Weil criterion: Weil's quadratic form damped by $e^{-\varepsilon|x|}$ is nonnegative exactly when no zero has real part above $\frac12+\varepsilon$, so $\varepsilon=0$ is Weil's criterion, $\varepsilon=\frac12$ holds unconditionally, and the zero free half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ announced in October 2026 (not refereed, not assumed here) is the case $\varepsilon=\frac38$. Restricting the test functions to support $[-a,a]$ gives a ladder of finite problems in the primes up to $e^{2a}$. If no zero has real part $\ge\frac12+\varepsilon$ and the Thorin density has a strict, nondegenerate global minimum at the origin, not approached at infinity (true at $\varepsilon=\frac12$, assumed below it), the least eigenvalue decreases to $2\xi'/\xi(\frac12+\varepsilon)$ at an algebraic rate in $a$; at $\varepsilon=0$, under RH, it falls faster than any power of $a$. The eigenvalue computations are numerical, not certificates.

## From the literature

Weil's explicit formula and positivity criterion (Weil 1952; Iwaniec and Kowalski 2004); positivity of Weil's form on short windows where no prime enters (Yoshida 1992), other test functions of small support (Bombieri 2000), and a second proof of Yoshida's range (Connes and Consani 2021); a certified lower bound for the least eigenvalue on a short window, an empirical decay law, and under RH an upper bound $\exp(-ae^a)$ for large $a$ (Zhu 2026, preprint); low zeros from the lowest eigenvector of a truncated form (Connes, Consani and Moscovici 2025, preprint); the nonnegativity of the de Bruijn Newman constant (Rodgers and Tao 2020); the time and band limiting operator (Slepian and Pollak 1961); the Bochner Schwartz theorem (Gel'fand and Vilenkin 1964); the half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ (OpenAI 2026, announced, not refereed).

## New results

The book proves the hypotheses of the ladder theorem only at $\varepsilon=\frac12$.

New in this book.
- [Ladder theorem: least eigenvalue of the damped Weil form on a window](../RESULTS.md#ladder_theorem_least_eigenvalue_damped_weil_form_algebraic_rate_and_line_dichotomy) (Theorem 20.2.1, page 709; conditional on Parts (a) and (b), for a level $0<\varepsilon<\frac12$: that the closed half plane $\operatorname{Re}s\ge\frac12+\varepsilon$ contains no zero of $\zeta$ (that $\varepsilon$ is to the right of the zeros). The book certifies this only up to a finite height, and the strict minimum at the origin on every line $\frac12<\alpha<1$ at once is equivalent to RH.; Parts (a) and (b), for a level $0<\varepsilon<\frac12$: that $\liminf_{|\theta|\to\infty}v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(\theta)>v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(0)$, that is, that the minimum is not approached at infinity. All hypotheses of (a) and (b) hold at $\varepsilon=\frac12$ with no assumption on the zeros, and the second half of (c), the negativity for large $a$ if RH fails, needs no hypothesis. For $\varepsilon>\frac12$ the chapter does not discuss whether the hypotheses on $v_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ hold): At a level $\frac12+\varepsilon$ to the right of the zeros where the Thorin density has a strict, nondegenerate global minimum at the origin not approached at infinity (true at $\varepsilon=\frac12$, an assumption below it), the least eigenvalue of Weil's form damped by $e^{-\varepsilon|x|}$ on test functions supported in $[-a,a]$ decreases to $2\xi'/\xi(\frac12+\varepsilon)$ at a rate between $c/a^2$ and $(\pi^2v''(0)/4+o(1))/a^2$, with $v(\theta)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\frac12+\varepsilon+i\theta)$, while at $\varepsilon=0$, under RH, it is nonnegative and $O(a^{-N})$ for every $N$, and if RH fails it is negative for all large $a$.
