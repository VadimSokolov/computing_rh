# Volume I, Chapter 20. Ferromagnetic random variables

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 20. Which random variables are ferromagnetic, and is the Pólya law ferromagnetic? A symmetric random variable is ferromagnetic if its moment generating function vanishes only on the imaginary axis, the Lee and Yang property of a single Ising spin. The Pólya variable has density $p=\Phi/\Xi(0)$ and moment generating function $\xi(\frac12+s)/\xi(\frac12)$, so the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) says exactly that it is ferromagnetic. The chapter develops the class. It gives the Hadamard form and cumulants of a ferromagnetic law, and shows that its clock is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC) with one Thorin atom per pair of zeros, giving a van Dantzig pair. It proves closure under weak limits, so that RH holds if and only if the Pólya law is a limit in law of ferromagnetic laws, and studies mixtures of uniform laws on an interval (the shoulder theorem, laws mixed towards the edges, a moment test). It proves necessary conditions for the Pólya law, that it is a mixture of uniform laws and has no component with finitely many zeros (no Gaussian component). The Mellin transform of a clock is computed, with residue formulas and sums of independent spins expressed through the Riemann zeta function, with zeros of the transform located by ball arithmetic. The exponential $e^{2X}$ of the Pólya variable, a size biased variable of Biane, Pitman and Yor, is shown not to be a GGC and to be self decomposable, with hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM) powers $e^{\beta X}$ for $|\beta|\ge5.5791422$ by a computer assisted proof in ball arithmetic. The last sections treat two arithmetic spin chains, Knauf's number theoretical chain (Stern's diatomic sequence, partition function converging to $\zeta(\beta-1)/\zeta(\beta)$ for $\operatorname{Re}\beta>2$) and the Farey chain in a field, with a Perron identity and computed Fisher zeros near the points $1+\rho$, and list open problems. Ferromagnetic approximations of the Pólya law are in Volume II. It also proves that finite sums of independent spins, uniforms and a Gaussian never converge to the Pólya law, that independent ferromagnetic blocks of total weight at most 287 do not either (conditionally for larger weights), and a Möbius identity and a harmonic sum criterion equivalent to RH.

## From the literature

- The Lee and Yang theorem (Lee and Yang 1952), Newman's single spin laws, Hadamard form and cumulants (Newman 1974; 1975), Lieb and Sokal (1981)
- The Griffiths Simon and Ellis, Monroe and Newman classes (Simon and Griffiths 1973; Ellis, Monroe and Newman 1976) and the GHS inequality for the Pólya law (Newman 1991)
- Van Dantzig pairs (Konstantopoulos, Patie and Sarkar 2024)
- Pólya's theorems on real zeros (1918; 1926), de Bruijn's strip theorem (1950), Turán inequalities (Csordas, Norfolk and Varga 1986), Jensen polynomials (Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier 2019)
- The Biane, Pitman and Yor variable (2001), Kent (1978), Fourati (2016)
- Stern's diatomic sequence (Stern 1858) and the Calkin Wilf enumeration of the rationals (Calkin and Wilf 2000)
- Knauf's number theoretical spin chain (Knauf 1993; 1998), its phase transition at $\beta=2$ (Contucci and Knauf 1997) and the Farey fraction chain (Kleban and others)
