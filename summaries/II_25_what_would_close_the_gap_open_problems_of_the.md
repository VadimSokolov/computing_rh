This closing chapter of Volume II sorts the open problems of the computational chapters by what they would accomplish, so that a reader looking for the next step can see which bear on the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) itself and which would sharpen a computation. The sorting follows the path of Volume I: the descent in level of the Thorin density from the basepoint one to the critical line, and the three constraints on any proof (a proof cannot be finite, must use the Euler product, and must not rest on a property that survives a Gaussian tilt). Four groups are distinguished. Problems on the descent itself concern what a proof by deconvolution would still need, whether the backward Cauchy flow is a path at all, the explicit formula read as a deconvolution including a rigorous matrix pencil, the Balazard, Saias and Yor criterion at every level, and where a uniform step of the Thorin phase diagram could come from, the de Bruijn Newman flow being the only known source of an analytic tail below the basepoint one. Problems on windows of the explicit formula concern Weil positivity certified in compact windows to finite support lengths. Problems on approximants concern truncations, friable approximants, extreme value approximants and approximating clocks, which agree with Xi to high accuracy and can have zeros off the line. Problems on cost and certification concern evaluating the level one density in time polynomial in the logarithm of the height and the number of digits, a posteriori bounds for the accelerated flow, and a calibrated Bayesian inversion. The chapter states no new theorem and proves nothing about RH; results using the announced zero free half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ of October 2026 (not refereed) are conditional.
