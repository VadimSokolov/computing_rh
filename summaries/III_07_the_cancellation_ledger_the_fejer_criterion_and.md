# Chapter III.7. The cancellation ledger, the Fejér criterion and the oscillator

What must the primes cancel for the Thorin density of the Riemann xi function to be positive in the critical strip, and how fine must the cancellation be? Following Bondesson's 2 sin squared blocks, the chapter writes the density as an archimedean block, a positive constant at the origin and a 2 sin squared transform of the Chebyshev error psi(y) minus y (the cancellation ledger). It truncates the primes sharply and with Fejér and smooth cosine weights, proves a Fejér criterion equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) by Landau's theorem on Laplace transforms, and computes thresholds on the cutoff. At level one half the criterion becomes an oscillator whose position stays in a band of width 0.0924 exactly when RH holds, extending the tent coefficient criterion of Volume II. The chapter also treats the oscillator of an L function, the set where the density is negative, and Nakamura's characteristic function (never infinitely divisible, but a Thorin representation of log xi).

## From the literature

The Thorin representation and generalised gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992). Positivity of the real part of xi prime over xi to the right of one half under RH (Lagarias 1999; Garunkštis 2002), and the monotonicity form of RH (Sondow and Dumitrescu 2010; Matiyasevich, Saidak and Zvengrowski 2014). Weil's explicit formula and positivity criterion (Weil 1952; Bombieri 2000). Landau's theorem on Laplace transforms (Widder 1941). The test function used for discriminant bounds (Poitou; Odlyzko 1990). Turán's extremal problem for positive definite functions (Siegel; Boas and Kac). The Bohman window (Bohman 1961). Kuo and Murty 2005. The mean square of the Chebyshev error (Cramér 1922), its unboundedness (Littlewood 1914), and limiting distributions (Rubinstein and Sarnak 1994). The limiting law of zeta prime over zeta (Kershner and Wintner 1937; Ihara 2008). Mean values of Dirichlet polynomials (Montgomery and Vaughan 1974). Zero density estimates (Ingham 1940; Huxley 1972; Guth and Maynard 2026). The complete zeta characteristic function (Nakamura 2015; Nakamura and Suzuki 2023) and quasi infinite divisibility (Lindner, Pan and Sato 2018).## New results

New in this book.

- [The ledger as the alpha derivative of the logarithm of the complete zeta characteristic function](../RESULTS.md#ledger_as_alpha_derivative_of_nakamura_factorisation) (Volume III, Proposition 7.8.1, page 153; proved): The arithmetic block, the archimedean block and the Thorin density minus its origin value are the alpha derivatives of the logarithms of the moduli of the zeta factor, the gamma factor and xi, and for alpha above one the characteristic function is quasi infinitely divisible with an explicit quasi Lévy measure. New in this book.
- [Thorin representation of log xi and the extended GGC reading, equivalent to RH](../RESULTS.md#thorin_representation_of_log_xi_and_extended_ggc_reading) (Volume III, Theorem 7.8.3, page 153; proved): For sigma above the largest real part of a zero, log of xi(sigma plus s) over xi(sigma) is the integral of log(1 plus s squared over u squared) against the Thorin density, the archimedean and arithmetic pieces integrate separately, xi(sigma) over xi(sigma plus the absolute value of t) is the characteristic function of a symmetric extended generalised gamma convolution, and the identity at every level is equivalent to RH. New in this book.
