# Chapter III.9. Thorin positivity on every level outside a set of density zero

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume III: Is There a Path? Routes to RH from the Thorin Programme* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 9. What can be proved about the Thorin density of a level without assuming the Riemann Hypothesis? For a level alpha above one half the Thorin density at height r is the real part of the logarithmic derivative of the xi function, v_alpha(r) = Re xi'/xi(alpha + ir), divided by pi, and RH is equivalent to nonnegativity of the compensated density on every level. This chapter rewrites the classical zero density theorems as statements about Thorin measures with explicit constants. A two zero lemma localises negative density to intervals of half width below one half around zeros to the right of the level, and compensated windows of width 9.08 (computed) give positivity of the compensated density outside such windows and at all heights up to 3e12. A mass identity expresses the Thorin mass of a level as the zero count minus twice the count of zeros to the right of the level, with a jump criterion in the level. A resolution proposition says how far a level can see an offending zero, Littlewood's lemma recovers the Bohr and Landau theorem and Carlson's mollifier method is outlined, and the theorem of OpenAI (2026) that zeta has no zero above 7/8 makes the density nonnegative on every level above 7/8. Three problems are posed.

## From the literature

The classical zero density estimates for N(sigma, T) (Bohr and Landau 1914; Carlson 1920; Ingham 1940; Huxley 1972; Guth and Maynard 2026), Hadamard's product for the xi function, Littlewood's lemma (Titchmarsh 1986), verification of RH to height 3e12 (Platt and Trudgian 2021), the explicit zero counting bound (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022), and the zero free half plane above 7/8 as a theorem of OpenAI (2026).

## New results



<!-- results:begin -->
- [Localisation of negative Thorin density near zeros right of a level](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_localisation_offending_zeros) (Volume III, Theorem 9.1.3, page 185; proved): The Thorin density of a level alpha above one half is positive except within a distance below one half of an ordinate of a zero to the right of the level, which gives explicit constants for the classical zero density theorems. New in this book.
- [Compensated Thorin density positive outside windows of width 25 around offending zeros](../RESULTS.md#thorin_compensated_windows_height_139) (Volume III, Theorem 9.2.1, page 185; computed): For a level in (1/2, 1] and height at least 139, the compensated Thorin density is positive unless an offending zero lies within 12.5 of the height; the proof rests on a floating point evaluation of an explicit zero count bound, so it is a computation. New in this book.
- [Compensated Thorin density positive on every level up to height 3e12 (short proof)](../RESULTS.md#thorin_compensated_levels_finite_height) (Volume III, Theorem 9.2.2, page 186; computed): Every compensated level in (1/2, 1] has positive Thorin density for heights from 0 to 3e12 minus 5, by a short proof that uses computed bounds on gaps between zeros (the statement itself is certified elsewhere in the book by ball arithmetic; this entry records the short proof). New in this book.
- [Compensated Thorin density positive outside windows of width 9.08](../RESULTS.md#thorin_compensated_windows_width_908) (Volume III, Corollary 9.2.3, page 187; computed): For a level in (1/2, 1] the set where the compensated Thorin density is not positive has measure at most 9.08 times the count of zeros to the right of the level, and is empty up to 3e12 minus 5. New in this book.
- [Mass identity for the Thorin measure of a level, restated with the remainder bound](../RESULTS.md#thorin_mass_identity_zero_count) (Volume III, Theorem 9.3.1, page 188; proved): The Thorin mass of heights up to R on the level alpha equals the zero count N(R) minus twice the count of zeros to the right of the level, up to an error of order log R. New in this book.
- [Jump criterion for zeros right of the critical line](../RESULTS.md#thorin_mass_jump_criterion) (Volume III, Corollary 9.3.2, page 188; proved): The Thorin mass up to height R, as a function of the level, jumps by 2 at the real part of every zero below height R and is continuous if and only if there is no zero off the line. New in this book.
- [How far a level can see an offending zero](../RESULTS.md#thorin_level_resolution_condition) (Volume III, Proposition 9.5.1, page 190; proved): A zero to the right of a level produces no negative Thorin density on that level when the product of its distances to the level and to the reflected level exceeds a threshold of order 1 over log of the height. New in this book.
- [Thorin density nonnegative on every level above 7/8](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_nonnegative_above_seven_eighths) (Volume III, Theorem 9.6.1, page 191; proved): Using the theorem of OpenAI (2026) that zeta has no zero with real part above 7/8, both the plain and the compensated Thorin density are nonnegative at every height on every level above 7/8. New in this book.
- [Problem on the true resolution of a level by pair correlation](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_pair_correlation_resolution_problem) (Volume III, Problem 9.1, page 192; open): Use the pair correlation of the zeros to decide whether the resolution of a level at height gamma is of order 1 over log gamma or 1 over log squared gamma. New in this book.
- [Problem on Carlson mass defect with explicit constants](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_carlson_mass_defect_problem) (Volume III, Problem 9.2, page 192; open): Carry out Carlson's 1920 argument in Thorin notation to get a mass defect of order R to the 4 alpha (1 minus alpha) with explicit constants. New in this book.
- [Problem on one sided information from positivity of the density](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_one_sided_information_problem) (Volume III, Problem 9.3, page 192; open): Decide whether the positivity of the Thorin density off the defect intervals improves the mean value of log of zeta times a Mobius mollifier beyond the mean square bound. New in this book.
