# Chapter 7. Infinite divisibility and truncations of the reciprocal zeta function

This chapter asks where the normalised reciprocal zeta function $\varphi(s)=\zeta(\frac12)/((1-4s)\zeta(\frac12+\sqrt s))$ sits among infinitely divisible laws, and how far truncations of the Biane Pitman Yor lattice go towards the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). Unconditionally, and certified in interval arithmetic, it proves that the canonical function $w$ is positive, so that $\varphi$ is the Laplace transform of an infinitely divisible law with Lévy density $w(x)/x$, which is not self decomposable, not in the Bondesson class and not a generalized gamma convolution (GGC). It reads the Vere-Jones weights as necklace polynomials, extends the canonical function across the critical strip, and states the Laguerre Pólya class and the Lee and Yang property in Thorin measure terms. For simple zeros $\rho$, zeros of the truncated lattice transforms converge to $\rho/2$ at rate $1/n$: the one sided Conjecture T is false, while the symmetrised Conjecture S is proved above explicit heights for $n\le24$ and $n=32,36$ and only checked numerically below them. It also proves Weil positivity on the Bernstein cone and reads the de Bruijn Newman flow as a path of GGCs.

## From the literature

Thorin measures and generalized gamma convolutions (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992), closed under independent products (Bondesson 2015); unimodality of self decomposable laws (Yamazato 1978); Selberg's central limit theorem for $S(t)$ (Selberg 1946); verification of RH to height 50 (Backlund 1918) and $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021); simplicity of the first $1.5\times10^9$ zeros (van de Lune, te Riele and Winter 1986); an explicit Riemann von Mangoldt bound (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022); necklace polynomials (Metropolis and Rota 1983); Pólya's theory of real zeros (Pólya 1926), Cardon's theorems (Cardon 2004, 2005), zeros of $\xi(s+a)+\xi(s-a)$ for $a\ge\frac12$ (Lagarias 2005), the Lee and Yang theorem (Lee and Yang 1952), Newman's characterization of Lee and Yang spins (Newman 1974); the Biane Pitman Yor identity (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001; Williams 1990); Weil's criterion (Weil 1952; Bombieri 2000); the de Bruijn Newman constant (de Bruijn 1950; Newman 1976; Rodgers and Tao 2020; Polymath 2019; Platt and Trudgian 2021).
