This appendix tabulates landmarks in the computation of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. The 79 zeros below height 200, on which the book measures distances, are the range Backlund verified in 1914, and the verified height $3\times10^{12}$ is behind several unconditional statements. The book's computations do not extend the table.

## From the literature

- Riemann's first zeros, 1859, from notes found by Siegel (Siegel 1932)
- the first 15 zeros, Gram points (Gram 1903)
- all 79 zeros below height 200 (Backlund 1914, 1918)
- all 138 zeros below height 300 (Hutchinson 1925)
- the first 1041 zeros, Riemann Siegel formula (Titchmarsh 1936)
- all 1104 zeros below height 1540, Turing's method (Turing 1953)
- the first 25000 zeros, a close pair near height 7005 (Lehmer 1956)
- the first 3.5 million zeros, Rosser's rule (Rosser, Yohe and Schoenfeld 1969)
- the first 1.5 billion zeros (van de Lune, te Riele and Winter 1986)
- $10^5$ zeros near the $10^{12}$th zero (Odlyzko 1987)
- fast evaluation of zeta (Odlyzko and Schönhage 1988)
- the first $10^{13}$ zeros, not interval certified (Gourdon 2004)
- the Riemann hypothesis verified rigorously below height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
