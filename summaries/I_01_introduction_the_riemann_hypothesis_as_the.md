# Volume I, Chapter 1. Introduction: the Riemann Hypothesis as the positivity of a measure

This chapter states the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) as a positivity condition and sets out the plan of the two volumes. For the completed zeta function $\xi$, put $G(u)=\xi(\frac12+\sqrt{u})/\xi(\frac12)$; the reciprocal $1/G$ is the clock. RH holds exactly when the clock is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution (GGC), whose Thorin measure is then the sum of unit atoms at the squared ordinates of the zeros. This is the Thorin condition. Its equivalent forms are sign conditions: $G'/G$ is a Stieltjes transform, the heat trace of the zeros is completely monotone, the Thorin density $\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Re}(\xi'/\xi)(\frac12+\varepsilon+i\theta)$ is nonnegative at every resolution $\varepsilon>0$ (Lagarias's criterion), Weil's explicit formula functional is positive, and the clock is hyperbolically completely monotone (HCM). The chapter introduces the Hadamard product, the Pólya kernel and density, van Dantzig pairs, the Poisson smoothing of the zeros (the Cauchy flow) and the sine squared lemma, which gives the Thorin density from the primes. It treats GGCs for computation: the Bondesson hierarchy, diagnostics of the Thorin condition and the sinh clock benchmark. It explains why computation can measure but not prove the Thorin condition. The overture describes the division of the material between the theory of Volume I and the computation of Volume II, fixes the status words (proved, computed, certified, conditional, heuristic, open) and one notation for the Thorin density at a basepoint or level, and states the clearest path to RH found in the two volumes: a positivity argument for a zero free half plane that uses the Euler product, with three constraints on any proof. Nothing here proves RH.

## New results

The chapter restates the rigidity and shifted clock theorems of Polson (2026), proves the sine squared lemma of Polson (2017) again, and adds one open question on positivity on a single line. The overture is a statement of the programme and of what any proof along it would need.
