# Volume II, Chapter 4. The de Bruijn Newman flow computed from the Pólya density

The de Bruijn Newman flow reads the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) from a deformation of the Pólya kernel: multiplying the kernel by a Gaussian factor turns $\Xi$ into a family of entire functions $H_t$ that evolve by the backward heat equation, and RH is the statement that no two zeros collide at positive time, equivalently that the de Bruijn Newman constant $\Lambda$ equals zero (Rodgers and Tao proved $\Lambda\ge0$). The Pólya route computes $H_t$ at the cost of $\Xi$. The chapter follows zeros backward to their collisions: the zeros move as unit charges with logarithmic repulsion, a closed form for the velocity at $t=0$ is derived through Hardy's function $Z$, and two collisions are computed from the Pólya kernel. The Hankel (Stieltjes moment) test along the flow is the Thorin positivity of the tilted clock. Collision times are bracketed with Laguerre's quantity, Biane's theorem on free convolution with the semicircle law and a Riccati comparison, and compared with the Lehmer pair lower bounds for $\Lambda$ of Csordas, Smith and Varga. A zero free half plane gives $\Lambda\le2(\alpha-\tfrac12)^2$, conditional for the October 2026 half plane preprints. Later subsections treat the shielding deficit of Newton, the Thorin atoms of the tilted clock and Gaussian smoothing of reciprocal GGC transforms, high order Laguerre expressions along the flow, and forward persistence of real zeros. Results are proved unless marked computed (floating point) or conditional, and none of them settles RH.

## From the literature

- The flow, its real zero property for $t\ge\frac12$ and persistence forward in time (de Bruijn 1950); the constant $\Lambda$ (Newman 1976)
- $\Lambda\ge0$ (Rodgers and Tao 2020); $\Lambda<\frac12$ (Ki, Kim and Lee 2009); $\Lambda\le0.22$ (Polymath 2019); $\Lambda\le0.2$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- Lehmer pairs and lower bounds for $\Lambda$ (Csordas, Smith and Varga 1994)
- Free convolution with the semicircle law and the density of $\mu\boxplus\sigma_t$ (Biane 1997)
- Newman's results on the monotonicity of the kernel logarithm (Newman 1991)

- [Velocity of a zero of the de Bruijn Newman function at time zero through Hardy's function](../RESULTS.md#velocity_of_real_zeros_of_h_zero_through_hardy_function) (Volume II, Proposition 4.1.1, page 48; proved): The velocity at time zero of a simple zero of the de Bruijn Newman function under the backward heat flow is half the sum of the ratio of the second to the first derivative of Hardy's function at the corresponding zero, a rational term and a digamma term, and the last two terms contribute a drift of minus pi over four for high zeros (credited to a note of Nicholas Polson merged into the book). New in this book.
- [Critical point lower bound for the collision time of zeros under the backward heat flow](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_critical_point_collision_bound) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.7, page 60; proved): While all zeros stay real and simple, a gap between two zeros of a real polynomial cannot close under the backward heat flow before the time one over Laguerre's quantity at the critical point of the gap, and z squared minus a squared attains the bound. New in this book.
- [Biane form lower bound for the collision time of zeros under the backward heat flow](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_biane_form_collision_bound) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.9, page 61; proved): While all zeros of a real polynomial stay real and simple, a gap between two adjacent zeros cannot close under the backward heat flow before the time one over the minimum of Laguerre's quantity over the gap, a bound at least as strong as the one from the critical point and attained by z squared minus a squared. New in this book.
- [Local lower bound for the closing of a gap with a correction from the nonreal zeros](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_local_bound_nonreal_zeros) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.12, page 62; proved): For the minimum m of Laguerre's quantity over a gap between real zeros, the upper right Dini derivative is at most one plus an explicit correction, computed from the nonreal zeros, times m squared, which bounds the time at which the gap closes from below. New in this book.
- [Lower bound for the closing of a gap from the quantity delta, with no knowledge of the nonreal zeros](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_delta_collision_bound) (Volume II, Corollary 4.1.13, page 63; proved): A quantity delta computed from the function and its first four derivatives at the minimiser of Laguerre's quantity over a gap between real zeros controls the growth of that minimum under the backward heat flow, and when delta stays at most zero until the collision the gap cannot close before Biane's time, one over the initial minimum of Laguerre's quantity, even when nonreal zeros are present. New in this book.
- [Upper bound (deadline) for the closing time of a gap between real zeros](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_collision_deadline) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.14, page 64; proved): If the share Q of the other zeros and the sum W over the nonreal zeros stay below constants rho and W bar for which 3 rho plus the square root of 9 rho squared plus 6 W bar is less than the initial minimum of Laguerre's quantity over a gap between two real zeros, the gap closes, by a collision of one of its bounding zeros with another zero, no later than an explicit logarithmic time. New in this book.
- [Deadline for the closing of a gap in terms of the quantity kappa](../RESULTS.md#backward_heat_flow_collision_deadline_kappa_form) (Volume II, Corollary 4.1.15, page 64; proved): The deadline for the closing of a gap holds with six times the bound on W replaced by the largest negative part of a quantity kappa, computed from the function at the minimiser and the two zeros bounding the gap, and this form is never weaker. New in this book.
- [Control of the nonreal zeros of the de Bruijn Newman function at negative times](../RESULTS.md#nonreal_zeros_control_along_backward_heat_flow) (Volume II, Problem 4.1, page 67; open): No bound is known, computed from the de Bruijn Newman function at time zero, for how far its nonreal zeros travel at negative times or how close to a gap between real zeros they come, and such control would make the lower and upper bounds for the closing time of a gap unconditional statements about that function. New in this book.
- [Shielding deficit identity for a pair of adjacent real zeros of the de Bruijn Newman function (Newton 2026, Zenodo)](../RESULTS.md#shielding_deficit_identity_for_adjacent_real_zeros_of_de_bruijn_newman_function) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.16, page 69; proved): A telescoping identity (Newton 2026, Zenodo) expresses the shielding deficit of a pair of adjacent real zeros as a sum over the exterior gaps of the relative deviations of those gaps from the pair's gap, when all zeros are real and the exterior zeros tend to infinity. New in this book.
- [Consequences of the shielding deficit identity: lattice, truncated lattice, single enlarged gap, one sided bound and monotonicity of the smallest gap (Newton 2026, Zenodo)](../RESULTS.md#shielding_deficit_consequences_lattice_tail_single_gap_and_smallest_gap_does_not_shrink) (Volume II, Corollary 4.1.17, page 69; proved): Five consequences of the shielding deficit identity: the lattice has zero deficit, a truncated lattice with $R$ exterior zeros per side has deficit $2/(R+1)$, a single enlarged gap contributes $\delta/(j(j+\delta))$, the positive part of the deficit is at most $g^{-1}\sum_j j^{-2}$ times the excess of the exterior gaps over $g$, and a smallest gap of a configuration does not shrink forward in time. New in this book.
- [The de Bruijn Newman function as a Gaussian average and the reciprocal transform of the tilted clock as a Gaussian smoothing of xi](../RESULTS.md#tilted_kernel_gaussian_average_and_reciprocal_tilted_clock_gaussian_smoothing) (Volume II, Proposition 4.1.18, page 70; proved): For $t>0$ the de Bruijn Newman function $H_t$ is the Gaussian average $\mathbb{E}H_0(z+i\sqrt{2t}Z)$ of $H_0$, and $1/R_t(\sigma^2)$, the reciprocal of the Laplace transform of the tilted clock when $t$ is at least the de Bruijn Newman constant, is the Gaussian smoothing of $\xi(\frac12+\sigma)$ with variance $t/2$, normalised at $\sigma=0$. New in this book.
- [The mean of the tilted clock increases with the tilt](../RESULTS.md#mean_of_tilted_clock_increases_along_the_flow) (Volume II, Result 4.1.8, page 71; proved): The mean of the tilted clock increases strictly with the tilt parameter at every tilt at which the clock exists, by the Cauchy Schwarz inequality for the moments of the reweighted kernel (credited to a note of Nicholas Polson merged into the book). New in this book.
- [Large tilt of the clock: the limit is twice the Brownian exit time](../RESULTS.md#large_tilt_limit_of_the_clock_twice_brownian_exit_time) (Volume II, Proposition 4.1.19, page 72; proved): As the tilt $t\to\infty$, the tilted clock divided by the square of the mode $u_t$ of the tilted kernel converges in law to twice the exit time of Brownian motion from $(-1,1)$, its Laplace transform tends to $1/\cosh(2\sqrt s)$, and $u_t$ times the $j$th positive zero of $H_t$ tends to $\pi(j-\frac12)$. New in this book.
- [The tilted clock is not monotone under the common exponential coupling](../RESULTS.md#tilted_clock_not_monotone_under_common_exponential_coupling) (Volume II, Result 4.1.8, page 72; computed): Coupling the tilted clock at different tilts through the same independent exponential variables does not make it almost surely monotone in the tilt on any right neighbourhood of $t=0.2$, because the atoms of the zeros numbered 64 and 92 rise there, a computation in double precision that is not certified, and at $t=0$ the same holds conditionally on RH (credited to a note of Nicholas Polson merged into the book). New in this book.
- [Problem: is the tilted clock stochastically increasing in the tilt](../RESULTS.md#open_stochastic_monotonicity_of_the_tilted_clock) (Volume II, Problem 4.2, page 72; open): The book asks whether the tilted clock is stochastically increasing in the tilt $t$ for $t$ at least the de Bruijn Newman constant, which unconditionally means for $t\ge0.2$, the increasing mean being necessary but not sufficient. New in this book.
- [Gaussian smoothing of a reciprocal GGC transform is again one](../RESULTS.md#gaussian_smoothing_of_reciprocal_ggc_transform_is_again_one) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.20, page 73; proved): Gaussian smoothing in the variable $\sqrt s$ of the reciprocal of the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution (GGC) with discrete Thorin measure and no drift is again the reciprocal of such a transform, the Thorin atoms obey, while they are simple, the motion equation of the de Bruijn Newman flow, and the mean of the GGC satisfies $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\mathbb{E}S_\lambda=(\mathbb{E}S_\lambda)^2-\frac32\operatorname{Var}S_\lambda$. New in this book.
- [Concentration of the Laguerre expressions at the origin along the whole de Bruijn Newman flow, with positivity of $L_{n,t}$ on every window for large $n$ (Newton 2026, Zenodo)](../RESULTS.md#laguerre_expression_central_second_moment_vanishes_uniformly_along_de_bruijn_newman_flow) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.21, page 74; proved): For the probability measures $\mathbb{E}_{n,t}$ defining the Laguerre expressions $L_{n,t}$ of the de Bruijn Newman function, the second moment of the centred variable tends to zero uniformly in $0\le t\le\frac12$, so for every $X$ the expression $L_{n,t}(x)$ is positive on $|x|\le X$ for all large $n$, uniformly in $t$ (Newton 2026, Zenodo); the index is not effective. New in this book.
- [Compact uniform asymptotic of the Laguerre ratio $k_nL_{n+1,t}(x)/L_{n,t}(x)$ along the whole de Bruijn Newman flow (Newton 2026, Zenodo)](../RESULTS.md#uniform_asymptotic_of_laguerre_ratio_one_eighth_log_n_squared_on_every_window) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.23, page 76; proved): For every $X$, uniformly in $0\le t\le\frac12$ and $|x|\le X$, the ratio $k_nL_{n+1,t}(x)/L_{n,t}(x)$ equals $c_n(t)(1+o(1))=\frac18(\log n)^2(1+o(1))$ (Newton 2026, Zenodo), the second equality using the Lambert centring proposition, whose proof in the book is a sketch. New in this book.
- [If the de Bruijn Newman constant is positive, the zeros at that time include a multiple real zero](../RESULTS.md#de_bruijn_newman_constant_positive_implies_a_multiple_real_zero_at_lambda) (Volume II, Corollary 4.1.25, page 77; proved): If the de Bruijn Newman constant Lambda is positive then the function H at time Lambda has a multiple real zero, so RH holds if and only if H_t has only simple zeros for every t in (0,0.2]; the proof uses Polymath Theorem 1.5 and the bound 0.2 of Platt and Trudgian, and is from a note of Nicholas Polson. New in this book.
- [Local Rouché criterion for backward persistence of an adjacent pair of real simple zeros of the de Bruijn Newman function (Newton 2026, Zenodo)](../RESULTS.md#local_rouche_criterion_for_backward_persistence_of_a_pair_of_real_zeros_of_de_bruijn_newman_function) (Volume II, Proposition 4.1.27, page 79; proved): If an explicit integral $\psi_Y(\sigma)$ is smaller than the minimum of $|H_{t_*}|$ on a contour around an adjacent pair of real simple zeros, then the pair stays real and simple for the backward time $\sigma$ and the gap shrinks by at most $2r$ (Newton 2026, Zenodo). New in this book.
- [Obstruction at height: the absolute value Rouché criterion certifies only backward steps of size $O(e^{-\delta a})$ at a real zero $a$ of the de Bruijn Newman function (Newton 2026, Zenodo, mechanism corrected)](../RESULTS.md#no_fixed_step_rouche_criterion_certifies_backward_persistence_at_every_height_for_de_bruijn_newman_function) (Volume II, Proposition 4.1.28, page 79; proved): No criterion of the form $\psi_Y(\sigma)<m$ certifies a fixed backward step $\sigma>0$ at every height, since $\min_\Gamma|H_{t_*}|$ decays like $e^{-\delta a}$ while $\psi_Y(\sigma)\ge\psi_0(\sigma)>0$ does not decay; the step such a criterion can certify at height $a$ is $O(e^{-\delta a})$ (Newton 2026, Zenodo, with the mechanism corrected in the book). New in this book.
