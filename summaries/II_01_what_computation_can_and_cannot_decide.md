# Volume II, Chapter 1. What computation can and cannot decide

What can a computation of the Thorin measure of the reciprocal Xi function establish about the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), and what can a finite computation never decide? The chapter opens Volume II by describing the path to RH as a descent in level: the Thorin measure at level $\theta$ is positive exactly when $\zeta$ has no zero with real part above $\theta$, and the Cauchy deconvolution from level one to the critical line is the computation the volume carries out. It then makes the limits of finite information precise. On the window below height 200 the Pólya route finds 79 zeros and an argument principle count, and the computed minimum of the Thorin density sits at the origin, where it is of order the resolution, so near the critical line positivity is never won by a fixed margin. The sine squared decomposition of the Thorin bracket into drift, archimedean, pole and prime pieces shows how the prime side amplifies errors near the basepoint one. A perturbation theorem replaces two close zeros on the line by a quadruple off it and gives an entire function with the order, type and symmetry of $\xi$ whose heat trace passes the finite tests (Hankel blocks, derivative signs to order $10^{17}$, small time expansion); for each such test the first failing order is computed on windows of low height. A finite computation can refute RH by certifying a zero off the line, can certify that windows are clean, and can measure a margin at a stated resolution and height, but cannot measure a distance to RH. The chapter also gives spacings of zeros against the Gaussian unitary ensemble and a short history of computations of the zeros of $\zeta$ from Riemann, Gram and Backlund to the verification of RH below height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021), with the notation of the book. Results using the unrefereed October 2026 zero free half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ are conditional.

## From the literature

- Verification of RH below height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and the early computations of the zeros (Riemann, Gram 1903, Backlund 1914, Hutchinson 1925, Titchmarsh 1936, Turing 1953, Lehmer 1956, Rosser, Yohe and Schoenfeld 1969, van de Lune, te Riele and Winter 1986, Gourdon 2004)
- Odlyzko's computations near the $10^{12}$th zero and the Odlyzko and Schönhage fast evaluation of $\zeta$ (Odlyzko 1987; Odlyzko and Schönhage 1988)
- The perturbation of $\xi$ that passes the finite tests of the heat trace and the Hankel and derivative sign results (Polson 2017; Polson 2026)
- The sine squared lemma and the canonical measure of $\log\xi$ (Polson 2017; Polson 2026)
- The criterion of Balazard, Saias and Yor (1999) and Lagarias's positivity criterion (Lagarias 1999)

## New results

Results of this chapter, with their status.

- [Four off line zeros that pass the finite heat trace tests](../RESULTS.md#off_line_perturbation_passing_finite_heat_trace_tests) (Volume II, Theorem 1.4.1, page 8; proved): Moving a close pair of zeros of xi on the critical line to four zeros at distance one quarter off the line gives an even entire function whose heat trace still passes the finite tests (infinite divisibility, self decomposability, log convexity, and at large heights positive Hankel blocks and derivative signs up to order 10^17). First stated in Polson (2017, version 24 of 2026), On Hilbert's 8th problem, arXiv:1708.02653.
- [A pair of zeros off the line at the height of a line zero: its contribution to the density at resolution epsilon is positive exactly when epsilon is below d over sqrt 3 or above d](../RESULTS.md#pair_of_zeros_off_the_line_at_the_ordinate_of_a_line_zero_is_positive_for_resolution_below_d_over_sqrt_3_or_above_d) (Volume II, Proposition 1.4.2, page 13; proved): The zeros $\frac12$ and $\frac12\pm d$ at one ordinate contribute to $\pi\rho_\varepsilon$ a rational function that is positive at every $\theta$ if and only if $\varepsilon<d/\sqrt3$ or $\varepsilon>d$; so when every off line zero shares its ordinate with a line zero and the distances have a positive infimum $d_0$, the density is positive on every line with $0<\varepsilon<d_0/\sqrt3$ although zeros lie to the right of it. New in this book.
- [Certified zero of a Mellin transform closes the Bernstein Pick route to RH](../RESULTS.md#bernstein_pick_route_closed_by_certified_zero) (Volume II, Theorem 1.4.3, page 14; proved): A certified simple zero of the meromorphically continued Mellin transform M of the Pólya density near -8.494 + 2.938i gives the canonical interpolant of the moment ratios a nonreal pole, so no Bernstein interpolant is a complete Bernstein function and the Bernstein Pick route to RH is closed. First stated in Polson (2017, version 24 of 2026), On Hilbert's 8th problem, arXiv:1708.02653.
- [Local model for the first failing derivative order of the heat trace near an off line quadruple](../RESULTS.md#first_failing_derivative_order_local_model_off_line_quadruple) (Volume II, Proposition 1.4.4, page 15; proved): To leading order, if two consecutive zeros of xi on the line are replaced by an off line quadruple at distance $\delta$ from the line and height $g$, with $\Delta$ the larger gap to the nearest line zeros and $\delta\ll\Delta\ll g$, the first derivative order at which a sign fails, for the heat trace restricted to the quadruple and its two neighbouring zeros on the line, is $k^*=\omega^*g^2/(2\delta\Delta)$, about $\frac\pi4g^2/(\delta\Delta)$, at a time depending on $\delta$ and $\Delta$ only; the comparison of this time with the seven certified failure times of the book's table, within 0.20 to 2.76 per cent, is a computation. New in this book.
