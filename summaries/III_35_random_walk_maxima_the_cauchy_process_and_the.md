# Chapter III.35. Random walk maxima, the Cauchy process and the Gamma function

This chapter of Volume III reads Stirling's formula, Binet's function and Robbins' bounds as statements about the maximum and ladder heights of a Cauchy random walk, and asks whether a random walk can do for zeta what the Cauchy walk does for Gamma. It proves that Binet's remainder J is the Pollaczek and Spitzer exponent of the Cauchy walk, that discrete versus continuous monitoring of the Cauchy process differs by the Stirling factor, and gives the exact law of the supremum of the Cauchy process. For the Euler product it builds one killed lattice walk per prime whose Wiener and Hopf factor is the Euler factor, computes the negative mass of the signed walk exactly (17/70 at sigma equal to 2, a double pole at sigma equal to 1), and proves, under a hypothesis on the multiplier and partly conditionally, an obstruction to a symmetric step law for zeta. It then studies the Balazard, Saias and Yor Poisson functional: the level profile, a local zero detector, height averages, zero density exponents, a Davenport and Heilbronn control, and windows of half width one for the Thorin density. The chapter finds no path to RH. It is from an unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026).

## From the literature

Stirling's formula, Binet's formulas and Robbins' bounds (Robbins 1955); Spitzer's identity and the Wiener and Hopf factorisation (Spitzer 1956); the Comtet and Majumdar constant (2005); suprema of stable processes (Darling 1956); continuity corrections for monitored Brownian motion (Broadie, Glasserman and Kou 1997); the Balazard, Saias and Yor equivalent of RH (1999) and Bui, Lester and Milinovich (2014); the Davenport and Heilbronn function (1936); Putnam's theorem (1954); Littlewood's lemma (1924); Selberg's zero density estimate (1946); Huxley (1972); Guth and Maynard (2026); the Lagarias criterion (1999); the zero free half plane above 7/8 (OpenAI 2026).

## New results

- [Cauchy random walk, Binet function as Pollaczek and Spitzer exponent](../RESULTS.md#cauchy_walk_ladder_height_is_binet_function) (Volume III, Theorem 35.4.1, page 702; proved): For the Cauchy random walk of scale 2 pi the Pollaczek and Spitzer exponent is Binet's remainder J in Stirling's formula, the ladder height transform is 1 minus e to the minus J, and the maximum has limit law e to the J, so Stirling's correction is a ladder renewal transform. New in this book.
- [Stirling correction factor as a ladder renewal transform of the Cauchy walk, Robbins bounds](../RESULTS.md#stirling_correction_is_ladder_renewal_transform_of_cauchy_walk) (Volume III, Corollary 35.4.2, page 702; proved): The function e to the minus J is a Bernstein function and e to the J is the Laplace transform of the ascending ladder renewal measure of the Cauchy walk, so Stirling's correction factor and Robbins' bounds are statements about this renewal transform. New in this book.
- [Discrete versus continuous monitoring of the Cauchy process, Stirling and Binet](../RESULTS.md#stirling_as_continuous_monitoring_of_cauchy_process) (Volume III, Theorem 35.5.1, page 703; proved): The limiting Laplace transform of the maximum of the Cauchy process monitored at mesh h is sqrt(h) e to the J(lambda h), the continuous monitoring limit is 1 over sqrt(2 pi lambda), and the ratio is Gamma(1+x)(e/x)^x, so Binet's J is the monitoring correction. New in this book.
- [Exact density of the supremum of the standard Cauchy process on [0,1]](../RESULTS.md#exact_density_of_supremum_of_standard_cauchy_process) (Volume III, Theorem 35.5.2, page 704; proved): The supremum of the standard Cauchy process on the unit interval has an explicit decreasing density with an exponential factor between 1 and exp(G/pi), and an explicit small ball expansion (the book notes the shape of a classical result of Darling and claims no priority). New in this book.
- [Moments and growth law of the Stieltjes continued fraction coefficients for symmetric stable walks](../RESULTS.md#continued_fraction_growth_law_for_stable_walks) (Volume III, Proposition 35.6.1, page 706; conditional on Freud asymptotics for the weight w(|k|) = minus log(1 minus exp(minus c |k|^beta)), which has a logarithmic singularity at the origin (only the asymptotic growth statement depends on it; the moments and the Jacobi matrix identification are proved)): For symmetric stable steps the moments of the weight are explicit and the continued fraction coefficients are squared Jacobi entries; their growth like j to the 2 over beta, giving j squared over 16 for Cauchy and j for Gaussian steps, is derived from Freud asymptotics and so is conditional. New in this book.
- [Cauchy walk ladder height has no completely monotone density, e to the minus J is not complete Bernstein](../RESULTS.md#cauchy_ladder_height_not_complete_bernstein) (Volume III, Proposition 35.6.2, page 707; proved): The function e to the minus J, the Laplace exponent of the ladder height of the Cauchy walk of scale 2 pi, is Bernstein but not complete Bernstein, shown by an explicit evaluation at one complex point. New in this book.
- [Ladder height density of the Cauchy walk, values at zero, decrease and power tail](../RESULTS.md#cauchy_ladder_height_density_properties) (Volume III, Proposition 35.6.3, page 708; proved): The ascending ladder height of the Cauchy walk has a decreasing, not log concave density with h(0+) equal to 1/12, h'(0+) equal to minus 1/288 and tail x to the minus 3/2 over sqrt 2, and any symmetric walk with a unimodal step density has a decreasing ladder height density. New in this book.
- [One prime, one walk, a killed lattice walk whose Wiener and Hopf factors are Euler factors](../RESULTS.md#prime_walk_ladder_probability_is_p_to_minus_sigma) (Volume III, Theorem 35.7.1, page 708; proved): A simple symmetric walk on the lattice (log p) times the integers, killed with survival probability sech(sigma log p), has Wiener and Hopf factors equal to the Euler factor 1 minus p to the minus s and its conjugate, and its maximum is log p times a geometric variable, whose sum over primes is the zeta law for sigma above 1. New in this book.
- [Exact negative mass of the quasi probability walk built from the Euler product, wall at sigma equal to 1](../RESULTS.md#negative_mass_of_euler_product_quasi_probability_walk) (Volume III, Theorem 35.7.2, page 710; proved): The signed step measure obtained by multiplying the prime walk symbols has an explicit finite negative mass for sigma above 1, equal to 17/70 at sigma equal to 2 and with a double pole at sigma equal to 1, and the mass is infinite for sigma at most 1. New in this book.
- [Obstruction theorem, no symmetric random walk step law realises zeta under a regularity condition on the multiplier](../RESULTS.md#obstruction_to_a_symmetric_step_law_for_zeta) (Volume III, Theorem 35.8.1, page 713; conditional on For part (b) with 1/2 < sigma <= 1, the twisted mean value theorem for the squared modulus of zeta (the mean of |zeta(sigma+ik)|^2 cos(k log q) tends to the sum over a/b = q of (ab)^(-sigma)), for which the book gives only a sketch): No nondegenerate symmetric step law has Wiener and Hopf symbol equal to a multiplier m(k) times the squared modulus of zeta(sigma+ik) when m is positive on the critical line, or when m has a finite limit at infinity for sigma above 1/2, the second case below sigma equal to 1 depending on a twisted mean value for zeta. New in this book.
