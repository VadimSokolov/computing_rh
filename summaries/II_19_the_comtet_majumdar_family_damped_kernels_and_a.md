# Volume II, Chapter 19. The Comtet Majumdar family: damped kernels and a solved model

Can the Riemann zeta function be reached by explicit probability laws, and can approximants with all zeros real be built from them? The chapter starts from the Comtet and Majumdar identity, in which the Mellin transform of the Bose function equals $\zeta(s)$ over a sine factor, and which gives the constant in the expected maximum of a random walk with symmetric stable steps. It writes the logarithm of the Laplace transform of the uniform law as a regularised integral against Lebesgue minus counting measure, and proves that the reciprocal of an even entire function of order below two is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution (GGC) if and only if all its zeros are real. The $2\sin^2$ lemma turns a signed Lévy measure into a Thorin density, which is nonnegative if and only if a logarithmic derivative inequality holds. For the tilted uniform law the Thorin density is strictly positive for every positive tilt, a Lorentzian smoothing of the lattice $2\pi m$, with Mellin transform the shifted zeta function. The obstructions are proved as well: the shifted $\xi$ function is meromorphic with a pole at $s=3$, so its reciprocal is not a GGC transform, and a kink theorem shows that the Gauss damped Riemann kernel has finitely many real zeros and infinitely many nonreal zeros. Modular constraints on damped kernels in the Laguerre and Pólya class follow, together with a treatment of the de Bruijn Newman family and of what the identity contributes to it. RH itself is not proved, and each route is marked where it stops.

- [A proof by Spitzer's identity and Euler Maclaurin summation that the expected maximum of a symmetric stable walk has the constant of Comtet and Majumdar, zeta of s over a sine factor](../RESULTS.md#expected_maximum_of_a_stable_walk_has_the_zeta_constant_of_comtet_and_majumdar) (Volume II, Proposition 19.1.2, page 581; proved): For a random walk with symmetric stable steps of index $\mu\in(1,2]$ and $s=1/\mu$, $\mathbb EM_n=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{\pi s}n^s+\frac{\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(1-s)}{\pi}+O(n^{s-1})$, so the constant is $\zeta(s)/((2\pi)^s\sin(\pi s/2))$, by Spitzer's identity and Euler Maclaurin summation. - [The logarithm of the Laplace transform of the uniform law is a regularised integral against Lebesgue minus counting measure](../RESULTS.md#logarithm_of_the_uniform_transform_as_a_regularised_signed_levy_integral) (Volume II, Proposition 19.1.3, page 582; proved): For $x>0$, $\ln\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x}=-\lim_{R\to\infty}\{\int_0^R(1-e^{-xt})\frac{dt}t-\sum_{1\le n\le R}\frac{1-e^{-xn}}n\}$, and for $0<\operatorname{Re}z<1$, $\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_0^Rt^{-z}(d\lfloor t\rfloor-dt)=\zeta(z)$. New in this book.

New in this book.
- [The shifted zeta is an explicit series of zeta at s plus k with a tangent factor](../RESULTS.md#exact_expansion_of_the_shifted_zeta_in_zeta_at_integer_translates) (Volume II, Theorem 19.3.2, page 583; proved): For $0<\delta<2\pi$, $\pi I_\delta(s)=\Gamma(1-s)F_\delta(s)$ with $F_\delta(s)=\operatorname{Li}_{1-s}(e^{-\delta})-\Gamma(s)\delta^{-s}=\sum_{k\ge0}\zeta(1-s-k)(-\delta)^k/k!$, and $\zeta_\delta(s)=\sum_{k\ge0}(\delta/2\pi)^k\frac{(s)_k}{k!}\frac{\sin(\pi s/2)}{\sin(\pi(s+k)/2)}\zeta(s+k)=\zeta(s)+\frac\delta{2\pi}s\tan\frac{\pi s}2\zeta(s+1)-\cdots$. New in this book.
- [Obstructions: the shifted xi function has a pole at s equal 3, its zeros drift, and its reciprocal is not a GGC transform](../RESULTS.md#shifted_family_has_poles_drifting_zeros_and_no_ggc_reciprocal) (Volume II, Proposition 19.3.3, page 584; proved): For $0<\delta<2\pi$, $\xi_\delta$ is meromorphic and not entire, with a pole at $s=3$ for every such $\delta$ and poles at $s=-1,-2,\dots$; a pole at $s=2m+1$ ($m\ge2$) occurs exactly when $F_\delta(2m+1)\ne0$ and fails for some $\delta$ (computed values $4.7215$ at $s=5$, $3.0416$ at $s=7$). The zeros move by a first order drift and, when $\xi_\delta(\frac12)\ne0$, $\xi_\delta(\frac12)/\xi_\delta(\frac12+\sqrt w)$ is not a GGC transform nor the Laplace transform of any positive measure. New in this book.
- [A signed L\'evy measure gives a Thorin density, which is nonnegative if and only if a logarithmic derivative inequality holds](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_from_a_signed_levy_measure_with_logarithmic_derivative_condition) (Volume II, Theorem 19.4.1, page 586; proved): If $f'/f(s)-f'/f(\alpha)=\int(e^{-\alpha x}-e^{-sx})M(dx)$ for a signed measure $M$, then $\log\frac{f(\alpha+w)}{f(\alpha)}-w\frac{f'}f(\alpha)=\int_0^\infty\log(1+w^2/y^2)\kappa_\alpha(y)\,dy$ with $\kappa_\alpha(y)=\frac1\pi[\operatorname{Re}\frac{f'}f(\alpha+iy)-\frac{f'}f(\alpha)]$, and if $\kappa_\alpha\ge0$ the compensated reciprocal is a GGC transform. New in this book.
- [Zeros on a vertical line give a Thorin density that is a sum of Lorentzians tending to point masses at the ordinates](../RESULTS.md#zeros_on_one_vertical_line_give_lorentzian_thorin_densities_tending_to_point_masses) (Volume II, Corollary 19.4.2, page 587; proved): If $f'/f(s)=a+\sum_k(s-\rho_k)^{-1}$ with all $\rho_k=\beta_0+it_k$ on one line, $\{t_k\}$ symmetric, $0\notin\{t_k\}$, then at $\alpha=\beta_0+\varepsilon$, $\kappa_\alpha(y)=\frac1\pi\sum_k[\ell_\varepsilon(y-t_k)-\ell_\varepsilon(t_k)]$, and if $\sum t_k^{-2}<\infty$, $\kappa_\alpha(y)\,dy\to\sum_{t_k>0}\delta_{t_k}$ vaguely as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. New in this book.
- [The tilted uniform law has a strictly positive Thorin density for every positive tilt, a Lorentzian smoothing of the lattice 2 pi m](../RESULTS.md#thorin_density_of_the_tilted_uniform_law_is_positive_for_every_tilt) (Volume II, Theorem 19.4.3, page 588; proved): For every $\delta>0$, $\pi\kappa_\delta(y)=\frac{y^2}{\delta(\delta^2+y^2)}-\frac12\coth\frac\delta2\frac{1-\cos y}{\cosh\delta-\cos y}=\sum_{m\ge1}[\ell_\delta(y-2\pi m)+\ell_\delta(y+2\pi m)-2\ell_\delta(2\pi m)]>0$ for $y>0$, so the compensated tilted reciprocal of $\varphi_U(s)=(1-e^{-s})/s$ is a GGC transform, and as $\delta\downarrow0$ the Thorin measure tends to $\sum_{m\ge1}\delta_{2\pi m}$ vaguely. New in this book.
- [The shifted zeta equals (2 pi) to the s times the Mellin transform of the positive Thorin density of the tilted uniform law](../RESULTS.md#shifted_zeta_is_the_mellin_transform_of_the_thorin_density_of_the_tilted_uniform_law) (Volume II, Theorem 19.4.4, page 589; proved): For $1<\operatorname{Re}s<3$ and every $\delta>0$, $\zeta_\delta(s)=(2\pi)^s\int_0^\infty y^{-s}\kappa_\delta(y)\,dy$, and at $\delta=0$ the right side is formally $\zeta(s)$; formally the identity of Comtet and Majumdar is the Mellin transform of the lattice Thorin measure $\sum_m\delta_{2\pi m}$ of the uniform law, divergent for $0<s<1$, and the shift smooths it into a positive density. New in this book.
- [Kink theorem: the Gauss damped Riemann kernel has finitely many real zeros and infinitely many nonreal zeros](../RESULTS.md#gauss_damped_theta_kernel_has_finitely_many_real_zeros_and_no_ggc_reciprocal) (Volume II, Theorem 19.5.1, page 591; proved): For every $\delta>0$, $\Xi_\delta(t)=2\int_0^\infty\Phi_\delta(u)\cos(ut)\,du$ with $\Phi_\delta=2\sum_ne^{-\pi n^2\delta}\Phi_n$ has finitely many real zeros and infinitely many nonreal zeros, because $\Phi_\delta'(0)>0$ gives $t^2\Xi_\delta(t)\to-2\Phi_\delta'(0)<0$; so $\Xi_\delta(0)/\Xi_\delta(i\sqrt w)$ is not a GGC transform. New in this book.
- [Modular constraints: a Laguerre P\'olya damped kernel must have vanishing odd derivatives of the damped kernel at the origin](../RESULTS.md#modular_constraints_on_the_weights_for_a_laguerre_polya_damped_kernel) (Volume II, Corollary 19.5.2, page 592; proved): For real weights $w_n$ with $\sum_n|w_n|n^Ae^{-\pi n^2}<\infty$ for every $A\ge0$, if $\Xi_w\in\mathcal{LP}$ and $\Xi_w$ is not $e^{-at^2}$ times a polynomial, then $\sum_nw_n\Phi_n^{(2j+1)}(0)=0$ for every $j\ge0$. New in this book.
