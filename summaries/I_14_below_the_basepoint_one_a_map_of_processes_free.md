# Volume I, Chapter 14. Below the basepoint one: a map of processes, free probability, almost GGCs and the Lambert law

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 14. What can be said about the Thorin measure of the reciprocal Xi function below the basepoint one, where no zero free half plane is available, and which processes realise the shifted clocks? The centre clock is self decomposable and unimodal with no hypothesis, being the stationary law of an Ornstein Uhlenbeck process driven by a subordinator whose Lévy density is minus the derivative of the heat trace, and the tilted Pólya variable is quasi infinitely divisible above the basepoint one and not at it. A Wiener gamma integral and a Riemann Ornstein Uhlenbeck process, and subordinated Brownian motion run on the clocks, give a map of processes sorted by what each needs: nothing, a zero free half plane, or the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). The Upsilon transform and the Bercovici Pata bijection give the clock a free counterpart, and RH holds exactly when the clock is the Upsilon image of an infinitely divisible law; the semicircle law is a local model for the Jacobi parameters, with Gamma(2), Laguerre polynomials and finite free convolution as exact examples. The almost generalised gamma convolution (almost GGC) theorem uses zero density estimates to show, with no hypothesis, that every shifted clock is a GGC up to a defect carried by the zeros to its right, whose Cauchy kernels carry a vanishing fraction of the mass of the others, with a power saving; the verification of RH to height $3\times10^{12}$ bounds the defect explicitly, and the moments of zeta of Keating and Snaith are read as exponential moments of the Thorin density. The last sections treat Pakes's questions: the solved Lambert law as a Thorin problem, Urbanik's classes (the centre clock lies in $L_2$ and not in $L_3$, with no hypothesis), the absence of a branching process realisation, and a criticality filter along the de Bruijn Newman flow that tests candidate properties implying RH. Computed inequalities are in ball arithmetic.

## From the literature

- Self decomposable laws and GGCs (Sato 1999; Bondesson 1992), driving subordinators (Wolfe 1982; Jurek and Vervaat 1983), unimodality (Yamazato 1978), subexponential tails (Embrechts, Goldie and Veraverbeke 1979)
- Quasi infinite divisibility (Lindner, Pan and Sato 2018), proved above the basepoint one by Nakamura (2015)
- Bessel hitting times (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- The Bercovici Pata bijection (Bercovici and Pata 1999), free regular laws (Arizmendi, Hasebe and Sakuma 2013), free self decomposability (Barndorff-Nielsen and Thorbjørnsen 2002) and its unimodality (Hasebe and Thorbjørnsen 2016), the Upsilon transform (Barndorff-Nielsen and Thorbjørnsen 2004; Barndorff-Nielsen, Maejima and Sato 2006), finite free convolution (Marcus, Spielman and Srivastava 2022)
- Zero density estimates (Ingham 1940; Huxley 1972; Guth and Maynard 2026), verification of RH to $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- Moments of zeta and the Barnes function (Keating and Snaith 2000)
- The Dedekind eta function and the Dirichlet $L$ function of the real character modulo 12
- Pakes's work on size biasing, Steutel's theorem and infinite divisibility (Steutel 1973; Pakes and Khattree 1992; Pakes 2014)
- The Lambert $W$ function as a Thorin problem (Kalugin, Jeffrey and Corless 2011)
- Urbanik's classes (Urbanik) and the de Bruijn Newman constant (Rodgers and Tao 2020)
