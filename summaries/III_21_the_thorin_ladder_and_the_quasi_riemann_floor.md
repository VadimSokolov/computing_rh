# Chapter III.21. The Thorin ladder and the quasi Riemann floor

Chapter III.21 asks what a zero free half plane, the quasi Riemann hypothesis (QRH), gives the ladder of Thorin measures of the reciprocal completed zeta function, and what can be proved below it. The reciprocal xi clock at level alpha is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC) exactly when xi has no zero to the right of alpha, and its Thorin density is the phase density Re xi'/xi, a sum of Cauchy kernels over the zeros. The chapter proves the level criterion, the Poisson form above the floor 7/8, positivity windows to height 3e12, lower bounds from zero counts, a hidden zero bound, and a decomposition of the compensated density into zeta, pole and gamma parts. RH is shown equivalent to compensated positivity at every level, to ratio monotonicity in the level, and, following Zhao (2025), to the sharp one sided Riesz mean bound G(x) at most log 2 pi plus F0 sqrt x, with F0 = 0.0461914 attained under RH. It adds a sawtooth reading over the primes with Gaussian windows valid for all x, the curvature of minus log Xi, the shadow of a zero at one level, a lattice rate for an EM descent, and finds no path through the ladder alone.

## From the literature

- RH as positivity of the phase density, with a correction of the pairing lemma (Lagarias 1999, 2005), extended to the right of any zero free half plane (Garunkstis 2002).
- The zero free half plane Re s above 7/8 (a theorem of OpenAI, 2026).
- RH verified to height 3e12 (Platt and Trudgian 2021); explicit zero counts (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022); N(sigma, T) (Ingham 1940).
- The one sided Riesz converse and oscillation under RH (Zhao 2025); de Bruijn Newman constant (Rodgers and Tao 2020); Davenport and Heilbronn zeros (Spira 1994).
- EM deconvolution (Richardson 1972; Lucy 1974; Vardi and Lee 1993), Nesterov acceleration (Nesterov 1983), inverse first passage (Anulova 1980; Ekstrom and Janson 2016).

- [Lower bound for the phase density from the number of zeros in a window](../RESULTS.md#phase_density_lower_bound_from_zero_count) (Volume III, Theorem 21.2.4, page 333; proved): If no zero lies to the right of the level, the phase density is bounded below by a Cauchy sum over the ordinates alone, hence by a multiple of the number of zeros within a window, which grows like log y. New in this book.
- [Hidden zero bound, a pair to the right of the level hidden by the background](../RESULTS.md#hidden_zero_lower_bound_for_phase_density) (Volume III, Theorem 21.2.5, page 334; proved): If exactly k zero pairs to the right of the level lie within H of the ordinate, each with band radius at least r0, the phase density there is bounded below by the background of the remaining zeros minus the depth of the k bands. New in this book.
- [Decomposition of the compensated phase density into zeta, pole and gamma parts](../RESULTS.md#decomposition_of_compensated_phase_density_zeta_pole_gamma) (Volume III, Proposition 21.3.2, page 336; proved): Inside the strip the compensated phase density of xi is the sum of a signed prime term, a positive term from the pole at one and a positive gamma factor term, and for levels above Theta it is the cosine transform of the Chebyshev error plus that of a positive gamma density. New in this book.
- [Reciprocal xi law as a first passage time plus a GGC](../RESULTS.md#reciprocal_xi_law_stable_factorisation_first_passage) (Volume III, Corollary 21.4.5, page 339; proved): When the level is admissible and compensated positivity holds there, the reciprocal xi law is the convolution of a positive stable law of index one half with a GGC, and RH is equivalent to this decomposition at every level of the strip. New in this book.
- [Tilting identity, one signed Levy measure for all levels](../RESULTS.md#tilting_identity_for_signed_levy_measure_of_xi_all_levels) (Volume III, Theorem 21.5.1, page 340; proved): The compensated phase density of xi at every level above Theta is the cosine transform of one fixed signed measure tilted by an exponential, and that measure is minus the sum of exponentials over the zeros. New in this book.
- [Boundary identity on the critical line and oddness in the level](../RESULTS.md#boundary_identity_on_critical_line_odd_in_level) (Volume III, Proposition 21.5.3, page 341; proved): On the critical line the three parts of the compensated density sum to zero, and the compensated density is odd in the level with first order term equal to the curvature of minus log of the Xi function minus its value at zero. New in this book.
- [The curvature of minus log of the Xi function is smallest at the origin](../RESULTS.md#curvature_of_log_xi_smallest_at_origin) (Volume III, Theorem 21.5.4, page 341; proved): Using the zero free half plane 7/8, the functional equation and prime powers up to 7, the curvature of minus log of Xi exceeds its value at zero by t squared over 10^5 for |t| up to 9 and by 3/500 on regular heights from 9 to 3e12 minus one, and under RH at every height. New in this book.
- [Exact prime side formula for the curvature](../RESULTS.md#exact_prime_side_formula_for_curvature_of_xi) (Volume III, Proposition 21.5.5, page 343; proved): The curvature function K(t) equals minus the analytic continuation to v equal one half of a prime sum with the weight 1 minus cosine, plus an explicit pole term and gamma series. New in this book.
- [Smoothed finite prime sums reproduce the curvature with an explicit error](../RESULTS.md#smoothed_prime_sums_approximate_curvature_with_explicit_error) (Volume III, Proposition 21.5.6, page 343; proved): A smoothed prime sum over n up to e to the 10e reproduces the curvature K(t) to 5e-7 times t squared on |t| up to 4, with a rigorous error bound, but it is an approximation and not a sign certificate. New in this book.

New in this book.

- [Ratio monotonicity of the phase density in the level, and RH](../RESULTS.md#ratio_monotonicity_in_the_level_and_rh) (Volume III, Theorem 21.6.4, page 350; proved): Under RH the phase density divided by a power of the width decreases in the level, conversely monotonicity of such a ratio at every height implies RH, and RH is equivalent to a lower bound by 2 alpha minus 1 times the level one phase density. New in this book.
- [The shadow of a zero at a single level, a zero free band of width about a over log T log log T](../RESULTS.md#shadow_of_a_zero_at_a_single_level) (Volume III, Proposition 21.6.8, page 352; proved): Monotonicity of the compensated density in the level at one level below 7/8, at every height, would give a zero free band about that line whose half width shrinks like 1 over log T log log T, but not a half plane. New in this book.
- [Floor minus ramp, the sawtooth form of the Riesz mean](../RESULTS.md#floor_minus_ramp_identity_for_chebyshev_factorial) (Volume III, Proposition 21.7.1, page 354; proved): The difference between Chebyshev's log of the factorial and the ramp sum is a sawtooth sum over the primes, and G(x) equals gamma x minus that sum minus a boundary term plus half log 2 pi plus O of 1 over x. New in this book.
- [The law of the sawtooth over the primes, an explicit formula with Hurwitz zeta weights](../RESULTS.md#law_of_sawtooth_over_primes_hurwitz_explicit_formula) (Volume III, Theorem 21.7.2, page 355; proved): The distribution of the fractional part x over n with von Mangoldt weights has an explicit formula over the zeros of zeta with weights given by the Hurwitz zeta function at the zero, and a Mellin transform equal to minus zeta prime over zeta times an explicit factor. - [Primes against integers, the sawtooth identity with the divisor error](../RESULTS.md#primes_against_integers_sawtooth_identity) (Volume III, Proposition 21.7.3, page 355; proved): The sum of the sawtooth over the integers equals gamma x plus the divisor error minus one half, and the difference of the prime and integer sawtooth sums equals minus G(x) minus the divisor error minus a boundary term. New in this book.

New in this book.
