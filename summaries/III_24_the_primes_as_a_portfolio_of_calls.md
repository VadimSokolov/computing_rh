# Chapter III.24. The primes as a portfolio of calls

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume III: Is There a Path? Routes to RH from the Thorin Programme* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 24. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/computing_rh/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/computing_rh.

This chapter asks how far probability can take the one sided inequality (M), whose statistic is a convex sum of ramps (x/n - 1)_+ over the prime powers with nonnegative weights. Along geometric Brownian motion this statistic is a submartingale with local time at the prime powers as compensator, and its mean is the Black and Scholes price of a portfolio of calls struck at the prime powers. The price has an explicit formula over the zeros of the Riemann zeta function, each damped by exp(-t rho(1-rho)/2), and Jensen's inequality gives an upper bound for every maturity at an entropy cost x t/2. The chapter derives square root bounds from zeros on the critical line up to a height T_0, compares lognormal, Gamma and band limited kernels, proves a lower bound x/(192 T_0^2) for any stopping rule, prices the portfolio without zeros by the Moebius hedge, and treats completeness of dilates (Salem's criterion on a finite range). It proves neither (M) nor the Riemann hypothesis.

## From the literature

Tanaka's formula (Revuz and Yor 1999); the Black and Scholes price (1973); generalised gamma convolutions (Thorin 1978); the verification of the Riemann hypothesis to height 3 times 10^12 (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and of L functions (Platt 2016), quoted and not checked; bounds for psi from zeros up to a height (Buthe 2016, 2018); Chebyshev's 1850 bounds with the constants 0.92129 and 1.10555; the secant identity (Lai and Vere-Jones 1979); Salem's criterion (1953); the Abel sum for zeta (Hardy and Littlewood 1916); the Weil theorem (1949); Wiener's lemma (Wiener 1932); the Brun and Titchmarsh inequality (Montgomery and Vaughan 1973); the Skorokhod embedding theorem.

## New results

Bounds resting on zeros up to a height are conditional; the explicit ranges use the quoted verifications. Tables and the band limited kernel are computations or outlines.
