# Chapter 25. Extreme values, hitting times and a path to RH

Can the Riemann Hypothesis be reached by approximating the Pólya density of the Riemann Xi function with explicit extreme value laws whose zeros can be controlled, and what does the hitting time of a string say about it? The chapter writes each term of the theta series as a combination of two Gumbel kernels of fractional order and builds three families of approximations: the folded Gumbel kernels, Pólya's two sided kernel and the Dyck chain. It shows that the zeros of Xi are all real if and only if the reciprocal xi law is a generalised gamma convolution, and real and simple if and only if it is the hitting time of a Krein string, recovers the first ordinate from the tail of the hitting time, and gives a Hankel determinant test for real zeros. It reduces RH to a height rule for the folded kernels, with a Rouché certificate in ball arithmetic for the first four levels, studies the shifted Bessel kernels through a Liouville boundary problem and a cotangent law, and tests a detection rule for the first nonreal pair along the de Bruijn and Newman flow. It adds a Pólya frequency characterisation of RH and an expansion of the mean excess function. Each route stops at a known place, and the computed parts are marked as computed.

## From the literature

- Kent's theorem on hitting times of one dimensional diffusions and Krein's inverse spectral theory of strings (Kent 1980, Krein)
- fractional order statistics and the Gumbel limit process (Stigler 1977), and peaks over threshold laws (Pickands 1975, Balkema and de Haan 1974)
- Schoenberg's characterisation of Pólya frequency functions (Schoenberg 1951)
- the de Bruijn and Newman constant, its lower bound and upper bounds (Rodgers and Tao 2020, Polymath 2019, Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- Biane's Liouville string for Pólya's kernel and Devroye's alternating series for densities
- Hurwitz's theorem and Rouché's theorem applied to approximations of the Xi function

## New results

A note of Nicholas Polson adds a computed count of the zeros of the Dirichlet polynomial of the Dyck chain at n = 25600 with -4 < Re s < 6 and 0 < Im s < 60, all left of the critical line, a conjectured lattice bias expansion of that polynomial with its first order consequences for the zeros (a proved phase formula and a computed displacement, the zero near the 64th zero of xi lying to the right of the line), and a one sided approach criterion that implies RH but, conditionally on the conjecture, fails for heights above gamma 64.

No string or hitting time is constructed for the extended system. New in this book.
