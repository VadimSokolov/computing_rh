# Chapter 18. Zhu's compact windows and the Landau Widom horizon

This chapter reads Xuefeng Zhu's 2026 preprint on Weil positivity in compact windows in the language of Thorin measures. The question is how far Weil's criterion for the Riemann Hypothesis, positivity of Weil's quadratic form on test functions supported in every window [-a,a], can be certified by finite computation, and what a window says about the height of the zeros. The objects are the least eigenvalue of the form, the prime comb in its symbol and the Thorin measure of the clock of the Xi function. The methods are a dictionary that makes the form, under RH, a sampling functional of the Thorin measure on Paley Wiener functions, Zhu's test function through a truncated reciprocal Xi transform, the Landau Widom horizon, and damping to a basepoint alpha. It proves the dictionary and a two parameter bound for the least eigenvalue of the damped form (conditional on RH below 1), reproves its coercivity for basepoints above 1 from the Euler product, and computes least eigenvalues and a detection experiment with a hypothetical off line quadruple of zeros. The passage from windows to heights is a heuristic. Four problems are open. Nothing here proves RH.

## From the literature

- Weil's explicit formula and criterion (Weil 1952; Bombieri 2000); positivity on windows with 2a at most log 2 (Yoshida 1992), proved again by other methods (Connes and Consani 2021).
- Zhu (2026): a finite reduction of positivity on a window, a computer assisted certificate on [-0.8, 0.8], the upper bound exp(-a e^a) for the least even eigenvalue under RH for large a, the barrier for pointwise comb certificates and the Landau Widom decay law, a conjecture fitted to computed bounds.
- Positivity on each window as a sampling statement under RH (Zhu 2026), via Littlewood (1924) on gaps between ordinates and Beurling's sampling theorem (Seip 2004).
- Time and band limiting (Slepian and Pollak 1961; Landau and Pollak 1962; Landau and Widom 1980); verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12 (Platt and Trudgian 2021).

## New results

Catalogued: the dictionary, the two parameter bound and four open problems; the tables are reported computations, not catalogued results.

- [Dictionary: Zhu's window form is Yoshida's Weil form and, under RH, a sampling functional of the Thorin measure](../RESULTS.md#zhu_window_form_equals_yoshida_form_and_under_rh_is_thorin_sampling_functional) (Proposition 18.2.1, page 671; conditional on The Riemann Hypothesis, used only for the second set of identities (the sum over the zeros, the integral against the Thorin measure and the sampling reading of the least eigenvalue). The identity $Q(f)=\langle f,f\rangle=\mathcal{Y}(f*f)$ holds with no hypothesis on the zeros): On a window of half length $a$, Zhu's quadratic form equals Yoshida's Weil form of the autocorrelation $f*f$ with no hypothesis, and under RH it equals twice the sum of $|F|^2$ over the positive ordinates, so that the least even eigenvalue is the lower sampling constant of the Thorin measure of the clock for the real even Paley Wiener functions of type $a$. New in this book.
- [Two parameter bound: the damped least eigenvalue is at least the undamped one, and under RH at least $2\xi'(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha)$](../RESULTS.md#two_parameter_bound_for_least_eigenvalue_of_damped_weil_form_on_a_window) (Theorem 18.5.2, page 682; conditional on The Riemann Hypothesis, used for part (ii) when $\alpha<1$, for the lower bound $2b_\alpha\ge4p_1(\alpha-\frac12)-4p_2(\alpha-\frac12)^3$ at every $\alpha>\frac12$ and for the closing maximum. Part (i), the bound $\lambda_{\min}(\alpha,a)\ge2b_\alpha$ for $\alpha\ge1$ and the upper bound $2b_\alpha\le4p_1(\alpha-\frac12)$ hold with no hypothesis on the zeros): The least eigenvalue of the damped Weil form on a window is at least the undamped one for every basepoint $\alpha>\frac12$ with no hypothesis, and under RH (or for $\alpha\ge1$) it is also at least $2\xi'(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha)$, a floor of order $\alpha-\frac12$. New in this book.
- [Open problem: the Landau Widom horizon as a theorem, the largest window on which Weil's form is positive when the zeros are controlled up to a height](../RESULTS.md#open_problem_horizon_as_a_theorem_largest_window_from_zeros_verified_to_height_h) (Problem 18.1, page 684; open): Determine the largest half length $a(H,\delta_0)$ of a window on which Weil's form is positive when all zeros up to height $H$ lie on the critical line and the higher zeros lie within $\delta_0$ of it, and decide whether it is at least $\frac12\log\frac H{2\pi}-C(\delta_0)$ with a constant depending only on $\delta_0$. New in this book.
- [Open problem: derive Zhu's Landau Widom decay law for the least eigenvalue of Weil's form on a window from the Thorin side](../RESULTS.md#open_problem_derive_zhu_decay_law_for_least_eigenvalue_from_thorin_side) (Problem 18.2, page 685; open): Derive Zhu's fitted decay law $-\log\lambda^{\mathrm{even}}_{\min}(\frac12,a)\approx C\,N(T_*)/\log N(T_*)$ with $T_*=2\pi e^{2a}$ for the least even eigenvalue of Weil's form on a window of half length $a$ from his product test function, and explain the constant through the Landau Widom plunge. New in this book.
- [Open problem: certified damped windows for basepoints between one half and one](../RESULTS.md#open_problem_certified_damped_windows_for_basepoint_between_one_half_and_one) (Problem 18.3, page 685; open): Extend Zhu's computer assisted certificate of positivity of Weil's form on a window to the damped forms with basepoint $\alpha\in(\frac12,1)$, where under RH the margin is at least $2\xi'(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha)$ in place of the undamped least eigenvalue, and measure how large a window can be certified. New in this book.
- [Open problem: comb cancellation on average over Paley Wiener functions](../RESULTS.md#open_problem_comb_cancellation_on_average_over_paley_wiener_functions) (Problem 18.4, page 685; open): Determine, as a function of $T$ and $a$, the supremum over Paley Wiener functions $F$ of type $a$ of the average of the prime comb against $|F|^2$ on the frequencies $|t|\ge T$, which is at most the pointwise supremum $A_a=(4+o(1))e^a$. New in this book.
