# Volume II, Chapter 12. The explicit formula as a deconvolution

Is the Guinand and Weil explicit formula the deconvolution that recovers the zeros of the Riemann zeta function from the level one density of the Thorin programme, and how well posed is that problem? The chapter shows that at level one half, under the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), the Cauchy deconvolution written in Fourier variables is the explicit formula: the multiplier $e^{|\omega|/2}$ turns the weights $\Lambda(n)/n$ into $\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n$. For a general measure the problem is exponentially ill posed, but for a sum of atoms a Gaussian tapered transform of the level one density is a sum of damped exponentials, one per zero, so Prony's method and the Hua and Sarkar matrix pencil recover ordinates and real parts. The functional equation makes the problem singular: off line zeros come in pairs at one half plus and minus $\delta$, the data depend on $\delta$ only through $\delta^2$, the real part is resolved at the rate $\tau^{1/2}$, and Bayes factors for the line against the pair grow like $\tau^{-1/2}$. Computations near height 200, at $3\times10^{12}$ (inside the Platt and Trudgian verified range, a demonstration) and at $10^{13}+100$ (above it, with 53 certified zeros on the line but no certified count or real parts) are reported, with five open problems. None of it is a path to RH: the method reads real parts and does not constrain them.

## From the literature

- The explicit formula (Guinand 1948; Weil 1952), pair correlation (Montgomery 1973; Odlyzko 1987)
- The matrix pencil method for damped exponentials (Hua and Sarkar 1990), and Prony's method
- Super resolution by total variation minimisation (Candès and Fernandez Granda 2013, 2014), for positive measures without separation (de Castro and Gamboa 2012; Schiebinger, Robeva and Recht 2018), and its limits (Donoho 1992; Li and Liao 2021)
- Singular mixture models (Chen 1995; Nguyen 2013), verified linear algebra (Rump 2010), sparse ReLU posteriors (Polson and Ročková 2018)
- Verification of RH to height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021), Turing's method (Turing 1953; Trudgian 2011), unrigorous zeros at large height (Gourdon 2004; Odlyzko 1992, 2001), fast zeta evaluation (Hiary 2011)

## New results

Statements are proved unless marked; the large height computations are computed in floating point, and the open problems are open.

- [The explicit formula for every smooth compactly supported test function, read as the deconvolution at level one half](../RESULTS.md#explicit_formula_for_all_compactly_supported_test_functions_as_deconvolution) (Volume II, Theorem 12.1.1, page 247; proved): The Guinand and Weil explicit formula is proved for every test function in C_c^infty, even or not, in the form of the Cauchy deconvolution from level one to level one half, with the multiplier e^{|omega|/2} turning the weights Lambda(n)/n into Lambda(n)/sqrt(n). New in this book.
- [An unresolvable perturbation of the Cauchy deconvolution at width one half](../RESULTS.md#unresolvable_perturbation_of_the_cauchy_deconvolution) (Volume II, Proposition 12.1.2, page 248; proved): Two positive measures at total variation distance one have data P_{1/2} convolved with them within epsilon of one function once 0.72 e^{-Omega/2} is at most 2 epsilon, so the deconvolution is exponentially ill posed for a general measure. New in this book.
- [The Gaussian tapered Fourier transform of the level one density is a sum of damped exponentials, one per zero](../RESULTS.md#tapered_transform_of_the_level_one_density_is_a_sum_of_damped_exponentials) (Volume II, Theorem 12.2.1, page 250; proved): The tapered transform of the level one density equals a sum over the zeros of damped complex exponentials whose poles carry both the ordinate and the real part of each zero, plus an explicit remainder of order e^{-l^2 omega^2/2}; no hypothesis on the real parts is made. New in this book.
- [Posterior concentration for the atoms of the tapered transform model on a window](../RESULTS.md#posterior_concentration_for_zeros_in_a_window_under_the_tapered_model) (Volume II, Theorem 12.4.1, page 254; proved): In the window model of the tapered transform with a spike free prior on the atoms, the posterior concentrates at the true zeros, including their real parts, at the rate of the noise, under hypotheses on bias and aliasing that do not hold at the settings of the chapter's computations, and it decides nothing about the Riemann hypothesis. New in this book.
- [The real part of an off line pair of zeros is resolved only at the rate tau to the one half](../RESULTS.md#rate_tau_to_the_one_half_for_the_real_part_of_a_pair_of_zeros) (Volume II, Theorem 12.5.2, page 257; proved): Because the functional equation forces off line zeros to come in pairs at one half plus and minus delta and the data depend on delta only through delta squared, the real part is resolved at the rate (tau/norm of t_perp)^{1/2} for data accurate to tau, and no test, whatever the method, does better. New in this book.
- [Bayes factors for a zero on the line against a pair at one half plus and minus delta](../RESULTS.md#bayes_factors_for_the_line_against_a_pair_of_zeros) (Volume II, Theorem 12.5.4, page 261; proved): With the spike and slab prior on the pair the Bayes factor for the line against the pair grows like tau to the minus one half, against tau to the minus one for the single zero alternative the functional equation forbids, so the functional equation takes the square root of the evidence. New in this book.
- [The density at a level between one half and one is a signed sum of Lorentzians, and the Riemann hypothesis fixes every damping rate](../RESULTS.md#levels_below_the_zeros_signed_lorentzians_and_poles) (Volume II, Proposition 12.8.1, page 268; proved): At a level theta in (1/2,1] that is not the real part of a zero, the density and its tapered transform are signed sums over the zeros with widths abs(theta minus sigma), and the Riemann hypothesis holds if and only if every damping rate equals theta minus one half. New in this book.
- [Matrix pencil on a window at height 3e12, a demonstration inside the verified range](../RESULTS.md#pencil_at_height_3e12_inside_the_verified_range_demonstration) (Volume II, Result 12.8.6, page 270; computed): A computation by the authors, not rerun for the book, in which the matrix pencil on data at level 0.6 returned 52 components near height 3e12 with real parts within 1.4e-8 of one half, and a sign change certificate puts a zero on the line within 1e-7 of each ordinate; the count and the real parts are not certified, and the window lies inside the range verified by Platt and Trudgian. New in this book.
- [Matrix pencil on a window at height 1e13 plus 100, above the verified range, computed](../RESULTS.md#pencil_components_above_the_verified_range_at_height_1e13) (Volume II, Result 12.8.7, page 272; computed): At height 1e13 plus 100, above the range verified by Platt and Trudgian, the matrix pencil on data at level 0.6 returns 53 components whose real parts lie within 4.7e-9 of one half, a floating point estimate and not a certificate. - [Fifty three certified zeros on the critical line in a window at height 1e13 plus 100, above the verified range](../RESULTS.md#certified_zeros_on_the_critical_line_at_height_1e13_plus_100) (Volume II, Result 12.8.7, page 272; computed): A ball arithmetic scan certifies 53 sign changes of Hardy's function, hence 53 zeros of zeta on the critical line, in a window centred at 1e13 plus 100, above the range verified by Platt and Trudgian; the count of zeros in the window and the real parts are not certified, and nothing is certified off the line.
