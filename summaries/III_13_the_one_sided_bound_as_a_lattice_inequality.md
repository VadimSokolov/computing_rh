# Chapter III.13. The one sided bound as a lattice inequality

Can the one sided bound that is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis be turned into a statement about integers only? The chapter takes the Riesz mean G(y) of the von Mangoldt function, for which RH holds if and only if G(y) is at most C y^(1/2 + epsilon) (Landau's oscillation theorem), and uses convexity of G + y log y (a chord bound), an exact hyperbola identity of Dirichlet type with multiplicity q, and window criteria for zero free half planes. The result is the lattice inequality: with Sigma(n) = sum over j at most n^2 of psi(n^6/j), a Lambda weighted count of lattice points under a hyperbola, RH holds if and only if Sigma(n) is at least n^6 (2 log n + gamma) for all large n, or on windows [N, N^(1+delta)]. Under RH the margin is (1/2 minus F_0) n^4 and sharp. The inequality is verified for 2 at most n at most 1.4 x 10^11 (computed, ball arithmetic above n = 100). A multiplicity threshold at lambda = 1/(2 F_0) separates the members of a family of inequalities that hold under RH (each of which implies RH) from those that fail unconditionally. The inequality itself is not proved and nothing in the chapter proves RH. The chapter compares the lattice inequality with a claimed proof of Alpoge, known only through unverified accounts, and endorses nothing.

## From the literature

Landau's oscillation theorem (Landau 1906); the zero free half plane Re s > 7/8, a theorem of OpenAI (2026); the Brun and Titchmarsh inequality in the form of Montgomery and Vaughan (1973); the bound on sums of Lambda times the sawtooth (Liu, Wu and Yang 2022); explicit bounds for Chebyshev functions (Schoenfeld 1976; Dusart 2010).

## New results

The results are lattice criteria for RH and for zero free half planes, an exact deficit identity, a computed range, a multiplicity threshold and a convexity statement for character twists.
