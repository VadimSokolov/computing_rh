# Volume II, Chapter 17. Truncations: the Biane Pitman Yor lattice, the beta lattice and Hermite coefficients

Can truncations of a lattice of exponential variables decide the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) or the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), and how do the zeros of the truncated transforms move? For $\zeta$, cutting the Biane, Pitman and Yor series $\mathcal S=\sum_k k^{-2}(e_k+e_k')$ after $n$ terms gives a generalised gamma convolution with finitely many Thorin atoms whose Mellin transform is a gamma factor times an exponential polynomial, with zeros converging to $\rho/2$; a condition on these zeros for infinitely many $n$ implies RH (Hurwitz). The truncated zeros move at the first order rate $\kappa(\rho)/n$, and the side of approach is tied to the Riemann Siegel phase, so to Gram's law, which makes the one sided condition (Conjecture T) fail. Symmetrising by the functional equation removes this mechanism (Conjecture S), with a Hurwitz criterion proved, single term and torus bounds for small $n$ and a statement of where they stop. The chapter writes Weil's criterion in Thorin coordinates, shows the Bernstein cone for finite order tests and weak limits to the de Bruijn Newman flow, and proves that no GGC multiplier can manufacture positivity. The same programme is carried out for the Dirichlet $L$ function of the character modulo 4: local zero free conditions equivalent to GRH, an explicit drift law, transient zeros, and symmetrised truncations. The second half computes Hermite coefficients of the Riemann Xi function as a backward heat flow, shows they sample $\Xi$ at a turning point with an error of order $a^4$, locates the first negative coefficient, and counts zeros from sign changes (computed, 379 zeros below height 500 for the modulo 4 function; the sign change count for $\zeta$ up to height 1000 is computed).

## From the literature

- Williams' and Biane, Pitman and Yor's representation of $\xi$ through the lattice $\mathcal S$ (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- Weil's explicit formula criterion (Weil 1952)
- The Hardy function, Gram's law and the Rosser rule (Titchmarsh 1986; Trudgian 2011), and the first $1.5\times10^9$ zeros simple and on the line (van de Lune, te Riele and Winter 1986)
- Fast evaluation of zeta at many points (Odlyzko and Schönhage 1988)
- Incomplete gamma approximants of the Xi function (Haglund 2009)
- The de Bruijn and Newman constant is nonnegative (Rodgers and Tao 2020)
