# Volume I, Chapter 6. Riemann's continuation and the generalised gamma convolution question

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 6. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function*, edition 2, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23891944. This chapter asks how close Riemann's own analytic continuation of 1859 comes to proving that the reciprocal $\xi(\frac12)/\xi(\frac12+\sqrt u)$ of the completed zeta function, the Laplace transform of the Pólya clock, is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution (GGC), the Thorin condition that is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). Riemann's second proof writes $\xi$ as the Mellin transform of a differential operator applied to the theta function, which is a signed mixture of Gamma(2) and exponential densities over the rates $\pi n^2$ and the density of Williams' variable $V=\frac\pi2S_2$. The chapter tests the two facts that suggest a GGC (scale mixtures of Gamma(2) laws, and closure of the class under harmonic means) and finds that neither holds as usually stated: a scale mixture of Gamma(2) laws need not be a GGC, and the natural harmonic mean closure is false (proved). It treats Stefanski's normal scale mixture of the logistic law and the extreme value (Gumbel) law as exponential generalised gamma convolutions, recovers a Thorin density from the heat trace by the sine squared lemma (positive with no hypothesis, but belonging to the positive half of the reciprocal law, not to the clock), and studies the de Bruijn tilt and the tilt by $e^{-|u|}$, whose Fourier transform has finitely many real zeros (proved; a floating point scan on $[0,80]$ finds none, computed). Every step of the continuation is unconditional except the passage from a Mellin transform to the reciprocal of a Laplace transform, a harmonic mean, and making that step a GGC is equivalent to RH. No new route to RH results.

## From the literature

- Riemann's second proof of the functional equation (Riemann 1859; Edwards 1974)
- Williams' representation of $\xi$ as a mixture of Gamma(2) laws and a Bessel hitting time (Williams 1990; Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001)
- generalised gamma convolutions and their closure properties (Thorin 1977; Bondesson 1992)
- the logistic law as a normal scale mixture (Andrews and Mallows 1974; Stefanski 1991)
- the de Bruijn tilt and the strip theorem (de Bruijn 1950)

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Riemann's variable V as a GGC with a signed Gamma mixture density](../RESULTS.md#riemann_variable_ggc_and_signed_gamma_mixture) (Volume I, Proposition 6.1.1, page 72; proved): The variable V with Mellin transform 2 xi(s) is a sum of independent Gamma(2) variables over pi n squared, hence a GGC, its density is a signed mixture of Gamma(2) and exponential densities, and it is not a scale mixture of Gamma laws. New in this book.
- [A GGC with an entire Laplace transform is constant, so X squared and |X| are not GGC](../RESULTS.md#ggc_with_entire_transform_is_constant) (Volume I, Proposition 6.2.1, page 75; proved): A generalised gamma convolution whose Laplace transform extends to an entire function is a constant, so neither the square nor the absolute value of the Pólya variable is a GGC and Bondesson's product theorem does not apply to the arithmetic mean factorisation. New in this book.
- [Positive Thorin density m at the centre with no hypothesis on the zeros](../RESULTS.md#half_line_thorin_density_positive_without_hypothesis) (Volume I, Theorem 6.4.1, page 77; proved): From the heat trace K the function m is positive for every theta with no hypothesis on the zeros and is the Thorin density of the half line law with transform P of the square root, and RH holds exactly when its continuation M is a Stieltjes function. New in this book.
- [The de Bruijn tilt as a normal mixture of Esscher tilts, and the failure of the weight e to the minus |u|](../RESULTS.md#de_bruijn_tilt_as_normal_mixture_of_esscher_tilts) (Volume I, Proposition 6.5.1, page 78; proved): The Gaussian tilts of the Pólya density are normal mixtures of Esscher tilts or horizontal smoothings of Xi, while the exponential weight gives a Fourier transform with only finitely many real zeros. New in this book.
- [Question: is the Laplace transform E sech(sqrt(u) X) a GGC transform](../RESULTS.md#question_arithmetic_mean_sech_is_ggc) (Volume I, Problem 6.1, page 80; open): The book asks whether the arithmetic mean of sech(sqrt(u) X), the Laplace transform of one half X squared times C, is a GGC transform, which would not bear on RH directly. New in this book.
- [Question: a class of mixing laws whose harmonic mean of GGC transforms is a GGC transform](../RESULTS.md#question_harmonic_mean_class_of_mixing_laws) (Volume I, Problem 6.2, page 81; open): The book asks for a class of mixing laws for which the harmonic mean of GGC transforms is again a GGC transform, not already explained by Pólya's theorems on real zeros. New in this book.
- [Question: a quantitative lower bound for Im M(-x - i eta)](../RESULTS.md#question_quantitative_bound_on_continuation_of_m) (Volume I, Problem 6.3, page 81; open): The book asks for a quantitative bound from below on the imaginary part of the continuation M just off the negative axis, in terms of computable data and a zero free region. New in this book.
- [Question: even weights w for which w times Phi has a Fourier transform with only real zeros](../RESULTS.md#question_even_weights_preserving_real_zeros_of_tilted_polya) (Volume I, Problem 6.4, page 81; open): The book asks for which even weights other than mixtures of cosh the tilted Pólya density has a Fourier transform with only real zeros under RH, and whether smoothness at the origin with log w convex suffices. New in this book.
