What follows for the Thorin programme if $\zeta(s)\ne0$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$? This chapter takes as hypothesis ($\mathrm Q_{7/8}$) the half plane announced in October 2026 (OpenAI, unrefereed, with a companion manuscript for $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac{11}{12}$), so every result is conditional unless it says otherwise. Under ($\mathrm Q_{7/8}$): for every basepoint $\alpha\ge\frac78$ the reciprocal $\xi(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha+\sqrt s)$ is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC) with absolutely continuous Thorin density, the clock is strictly increasing and $\operatorname{Re}\xi'/\xi>0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ (the one sided bound of Lagarias type for $\zeta'/\zeta$), the even and odd parts have only imaginary zeros, the de Bruijn Newman constant satisfies $\Lambda\le\frac9{32}$ by de Bruijn's theorem alone, the poles of the reciprocal lie in a thinner parabolic region, the Mertens function is $O(x^{7/8+\varepsilon})$, and density estimates sharpen. Unconditional parts are a window count lemma and a signal proposition. The chapter proves a counting form and a general theorem: if $\zeta$ has no zero with $\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha_0$ then the compensated clock derivative $D_\alpha(r)>0$ for all $\alpha>\alpha_0$, with a computer assisted constant check using ball arithmetic for the first 1700 zeros, and a corollary that a quasi Riemann hypothesis at $\alpha_0$ is equivalent to positivity of the compensated Thorin densities on every line to the right of $\alpha_0$. The basepoints at which the compensated reciprocal is a GGC form a closed half line. Floating point scans of $D_\alpha$ are computed. The strip $\frac12<\alpha<\frac78$ remains open, and Bondesson's closure would need a sequence of basepoints tending to $\frac12$.

## From the literature
