What are the zero free half planes announced in October 2026, who claims them with what status, how far could their arguments be followed here, and what would a half plane do for the Thorin programme? The chapter records the claims: $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ and $\frac{11}{12}$ for every Dirichlet $L$ function, zeta included, and for Hecke $L$ functions over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ (OpenAI 2026, with a Lean formalisation of the $\frac78$ statement), and the constant $0.874957\ldots$ of Liu with later lower constants. The $\frac78$ half plane is a theorem of OpenAI (2026), so every result of the chapter that needs only $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ is proved. The $\frac{11}{12}$ manuscript, Liu's constant and the later constants are unrefereed preprints, and a statement using them is conditional on the hypothesis $(\mathrm Q_\theta)$ that no Dirichlet $L$ function has a zero with real part above $\theta$. The chapter sets out the structure of the argument as far as read: a continuation criterion turning a power saving for smoothed Möbius sums into a half plane (whose short proof is checked), Poisson summation in a sextic twist, Kubota's cubic theta function and a large sieve type recursion, with an exponent bookkeeping that bounds one step at $\frac67$. For every level $\alpha\ge\frac78$ the reciprocal $\xi(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha+\sqrt s)$ is a generalised gamma convolution (GGC), for $\alpha>\frac78$ with absolutely continuous Thorin density and a strictly increasing clock, with $\operatorname{Re}\xi'/\xi>0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$, $\Lambda\le\frac9{32}$ by de Bruijn's theorem, and the Mertens function is $O(x^{7/8+\varepsilon})$ (all proved). Pairing zeros proves $D_\alpha(r)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\alpha+ir)-\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\alpha)>0$ for every $\alpha>\frac78$ and every real $r\ne0$, with a ball arithmetic check over the first 1700 zeros (more generally under any zero free half plane, with a converse), and a counting form with a window count from the bounds of Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong gives explicit heights; the canonical measure is signed, and as a reading the sine squared lemma adds no step. The strip $\frac12<\alpha<\frac78$ stays open: the descent in level is analysed in four readings, none a reduction, and ten open problems are posed. The chapter concludes that the half plane gives no path to the Riemann Hypothesis.

## From the literature

- The zero free half planes $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ (a theorem of OpenAI 2026, Lean formalised) and $\frac{11}{12}$ (OpenAI 2026, unrefereed), and an absolute lower bound for $1-\beta$ at real zeros of real Dirichlet $L$ functions of conductor $q$ in the form $(1-\beta)\log q\ge c$ (OpenAI 2026, unrefereed)
- Improved constants near $0.875$ and a further lowering (Liu 2026; Xu and Sang 2026), unrefereed
- de Bruijn's strip theorem and the bound $\Lambda\le2\Delta^2$ (de Bruijn 1950); Bondesson's closure properties of generalised gamma convolutions (Bondesson 1992); the Mertens function under zero free half planes (Titchmarsh 1986)
- Littlewood's equivalence between bounds for the Mertens function and zero free half planes, in a smoothed form (the smoothed form is OpenAI 2026, Proposition 2.1)
- Kubota's cubic theta function and Poisson summation in twists (Kubota 1969)
- the Thorin programme for reciprocal xi (Polson 2026), the Biane, Pitman and Yor laws, and the verification of RH to height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021)
- the bound on $S(t)$ for the window count (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022)
- a report of the reception in the press (Quanta Magazine, 19 October 2026)

## New results

The results using only $\operatorname{Re}s>\frac78$ are proved; those using the $\frac{11}{12}$ manuscript or Liu's constant are conditional on $(\mathrm Q_\theta)$. The arithmetic checks of the quoted constants and the computed condition numbers are floating point or exact rational computations, not ball arithmetic.
