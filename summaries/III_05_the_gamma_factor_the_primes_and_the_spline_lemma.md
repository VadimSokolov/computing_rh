# Chapter III.5. The gamma factor, the primes and the spline lemma

This chapter reads the gamma factor and the primes of the completed Riemann zeta function xi side by side through Nakamura's signed Lévy measure of log xi. The ledger identity splits the second logarithmic derivative of xi into the von Mangoldt prime comb, the gamma factor density and the contributions of the factors s and s minus 1, and integrates to the Riemann von Mangoldt explicit formula. Euler factors and the gamma factor become Laplace transforms of random variables, and the gamma function gets product forms, including Jackson's q gamma function as a limit of Euler factors at a fake prime. The kernel of the sine squared lemma is the Fourier transform of a B spline: the spline lemma gives signed Lévy patterns with positive Thorin density, and the Fejér Riesz theorem classifies all such patterns on one lattice. The strip form of the ledger writes the compensated Thorin density as a regularised prime term plus nonnegative gamma and pole terms; its nonnegativity at every level of the strip is equivalent to RH, and computed tables show less than one percent of room. Equivalent forms of RH, three programmes and open problems close the chapter.

## From the literature

- Nakamura's Lévy Khintchine criterion for RH (Nakamura 2015) and the explicit formula of Riemann and von Mangoldt.
- Local factors of the completed zeta function (Tate 1967) and regularised products (Deninger 1992; Voros 2010).
- Jackson's q gamma function (Jackson 1904; Gasper and Rahman 2004).
- B splines (Curry and Schoenberg 1966), the Pólya frequency criterion (Schoenberg 1951) and the Fejér Riesz factorisation.
- Positivity of the real part of xi prime over xi (Lagarias 1999; Garunkštis 2002) and the Weil criterion (Bombieri 2000).
- Verification of RH to height 3e12 (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and the zero free half plane Re s > 7/8, a theorem of OpenAI (2026).

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [The ledger identity for the Levy measure of log xi](../RESULTS.md#ledger_identity_for_xi_primes_gamma_factor_and_zeros) (Volume III, Proposition 5.1.2, page 70; proved): The second logarithmic derivative of the completed zeta function is the Laplace transform of the prime comb plus the gamma factor density minus the contributions of the factors s and s minus 1, and this equals minus the sum of the exponentials over the zeros. New in this book.
- [Euler and gamma factors as Laplace transforms of random variables](../RESULTS.md#random_variable_form_of_euler_and_gamma_factors_with_signed_prime_remainder) (Volume III, Proposition 5.2.1, page 71; proved): For sigma above 1 the zeta ratio is the Laplace transform of a sum of independent geometric variables over the primes, the gamma ratio that of a centred sum of independent exponentials, while the prime remainder is a signed measure and not the law of a random variable. New in this book.
- [Measures of the local factors, a comb for each prime and a smooth density at infinity](../RESULTS.md#local_factors_finite_prime_comb_and_infinite_prime_smooth_density) (Volume III, Proposition 5.3.1, page 72; proved): The Euler factor of a prime has poles on a vertical line and contributes a discrete comb at the multiples of log p, while the gamma factor has poles on the negative real axis and contributes a smooth density. New in this book.
- [Gamma function as a limit of Euler factors at a fake prime](../RESULTS.md#gamma_function_as_limit_of_euler_factors_at_a_fake_prime) (Volume III, Proposition 5.4.2, page 74; proved): Jackson's q gamma function is an infinite product of Euler factors of a fake prime P equal to q to the power minus one half along the trivial zero lattice, and its measure tends to the gamma row of the ledger as P decreases to 1. New in this book.
- [B splines with knots at the reciprocal zeros converge to the law of W/2 under RH](../RESULTS.md#bspline_with_two_sided_knots_at_reciprocal_zeros_converges_to_law_of_w_over_2_under_rh) (Volume III, Proposition 5.6.1, page 76; conditional on The Riemann Hypothesis (all nontrivial zeros of zeta have real part one half, so that the ordinates are real)): Under RH the normalised B splines with the 2N plus 1 two sided knots 0 and plus or minus (2N+1)/(2 gamma_n) converge weakly to the law of W/2 as N tends to infinity. New in this book.
- [Spline lemma, positive Thorin density from signed lattice patterns](../RESULTS.md#spline_lemma_positive_thorin_image_of_signed_levy_patterns) (Volume III, Theorem 5.7.1, page 77; proved): For every m the signed combination of m exponentials with the B spline weights a_j equals the integral of log(1 + w^2/z) against an explicit nonnegative density, which extends the sine squared lemma (the case m equal to 1). New in this book.
- [Positive Thorin image of Levy measures built from spline patterns](../RESULTS.md#spline_patterns_give_positive_thorin_image_for_signed_levy_measures) (Volume III, Corollary 5.7.2, page 78; proved): Every Levy measure that is a nonnegative part plus nonnegative mixtures of the spline patterns has a positive Thorin image in the variable w squared, although it may be signed. New in this book.
- [Fejer Riesz classification of signed lattice measures with positive Thorin image](../RESULTS.md#fejer_riesz_classification_of_lattice_patterns_with_positive_thorin_image) (Volume III, Theorem 5.8.1, page 78; proved): A fully compensated signed Levy measure on one lattice has positive Thorin image exactly when a cosine polynomial T is nonnegative, which by the Fejer Riesz theorem holds exactly when T is the squared modulus of (1 minus z) times a real polynomial. New in this book.
- [Strip form of the ledger, decomposition of the compensated density in the critical strip](../RESULTS.md#strip_form_of_the_ledger_decomposition_of_the_compensated_density) (Volume III, Theorem 5.9.1, page 79; proved): Inside the critical strip the compensated density of the Nakamura measure is the regularised prime term plus a nonnegative gamma term and a nonnegative pole term, so positivity at every level is equivalent to a lower bound for the real part of zeta prime over zeta. New in this book.
