# Volume II, Chapter 11. Complexity and acceleration of the backward flow

What does it cost to compute the Thorin measure of the Riemann zeta function at level $\theta$ by the backward Cauchy flow, and can the EM iteration be accelerated? The chapter treats the Cauchy deconvolution of the level one density to the Thorin measure at level $\theta'$ as a positive linear inverse problem solved by the EM algorithm of Vardi and Lee, and splits the cost into three layers: evaluating $\zeta$ on the line $\operatorname{Re}s=1$, the convex inversion and its log concave Bayesian sampling, and verification by Turing's method. It proves log concavity of the Poisson posterior, the precision needed, what EM computes, a rate for EM and local linear convergence (proved), infinite variance of the von Neumann and Ulam estimator, and the heat trace form of the Thorin measure. It gives a positivity theorem for the Thorin density, a mass deficit identity and a level mass function with jumps at off line zeros, and a certificate in ball arithmetic that $u_{3/4}$ and $u_{7/8}$ are positive up to height 1420 with exactly 1000 zeros below 1419.9. The global minimum of the Thorin density at the origin is shown at level 7/8, and uniformly for levels between 7/8 and 1, conditionally on the announced zero free half plane $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ (unrefereed), with a level by level equivalence. Nesterov momentum and SQUAREM are tested against EM, with an a posteriori bound and a smoothed prime sum for $u_1$. The final sections work and list open problems; readings through the Hilbert and Pólya resolvent trace are heuristic.

## From the literature

- Positive linear inverse problems and the EM algorithm for Poisson data (Vardi and Lee 1993; Csiszár and Tusnády 1984)
- Log concave sampling in polynomial time (Applegate and Kannan 1991; Applegate, Kannan and Polson 1990)
- Lagarias's positivity property of the xi function and the Riemann Hypothesis (Lagarias 1999)
- Fast evaluation of the zeta function (Odlyzko and Schönhage 1988; Hiary 2011)
- The Riemann Hypothesis verified to height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian 2021) and explicit zero counting (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022)
- Zero free half plane claims, unrefereed and unchecked here (OpenAI 2026; Liu 2026)

## New results

Results using the announced half plane are conditional; the a posteriori certificate for window masses is the authors' own computation, was not rerun and is not entered.
