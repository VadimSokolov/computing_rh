# Chapter III.22. Stepanov's method and the Riesz mean

From *Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume III: Is There a Path? Routes to RH from the Thorin Programme* by Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 22. Can Stepanov's method for point counts on curves over finite fields help with the one sided Riesz mean bound that is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis? The chapter writes the normalised Riesz mean error of the prime sum as a function Phi, a 2 sin^2 transform of the central Thorin measure of the zeros, and proves criteria for RH in terms of Phi: a doubling criterion, a multiplicity hierarchy D_n and E_n, a family of cosine polynomial criteria that are Weil positivity on staircase test functions, one tower of multiples of u, a computable tower base, complete monotonicity and Hausdorff moment forms, Gamma averages with zero free discs, and Li type and Verblunsky coefficient forms at one rate. Unconditional results include Abel positivity at every rate and a window of positivity of Phi, certified in ball arithmetic from the verified zeros and tail bounds, for u up to 104.86604. The chapter ends with a map of the routes and twelve problems, one of them solved by the computable tower base, and claims no new zero free region.

## From the literature

Stepanov's elementary method (Stepanov 1969), its extension (Schmidt 1976) and the short version with linear dependence on the genus (Bombieri 1974); Weil's quadratic form (Weil 1952); Landau's theorem on one sided bounds (Landau 1905); screw function and compound Poisson criteria with weights m/gamma^2 (Suzuki; Nakamura and Suzuki); Li's criterion (Li 1997; Bombieri and Lagarias 1999; Sekatskii 2014); positivity of Re xi'/xi (Lagarias 1999); verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12 (Platt and Trudgian 2021); explicit zero counting (Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong 2022); explicit zero free regions (Mossinghoff, Trudgian and Yang 2024); the zero free half plane Re s > 7/8, a theorem of OpenAI (2026); limiting distributions (Rubinstein and Sarnak 1994); an off line zero of the Davenport and Heilbronn function (Davenport and Heilbronn 1936); one sided power sums (Turán; Knapowski and Pintz); free convolution powers (Nica and Speicher 2006).

## New results

The doubling, hierarchy, family, tower and staircase criteria, the certified windows and the open problems follow.
