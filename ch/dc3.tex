\section{What can be done and what is open}

\subsection{What can be done}\label{dc:sec:answer}

Partly, and exactly so: the deconvolution of the Thorin density always exists, as Weil's distribution computed from the primes with no hypothesis (Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:weil}), and its positivity to depth $\varepsilon$ is a zero free half plane (Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:damped}), known for $\varepsilon=\frac12$ and announced for $\varepsilon=\frac38$, each rung $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)\ge0$ being a finite problem in the primes up to $e^{2a}$ whose limit, under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}, is a margin $2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ of about $0.092\,\varepsilon$, approached at a rate of order $a^{-2}$.

A rung is finite information in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:ch13:visibility} (Theorem~\ref{thm:ch13:perturb} is the rigorous form of this for the heat trace), so heuristically no finite set of rungs sees every height, and at $\varepsilon=0$ the margin is $0$ and, under RH, the approach to it is faster than any power of $a$ (Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}(c)): this is the deconvolution obstruction, consistent with the theorem of Rodgers and Tao that RH, if true, holds with no slack~\cite{RodgersTao}.

\subsection{Open problems}\label{dc:sec:open}

\begin{problem}[Certified rungs]\label{dc:prob:cert}
Certify $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)>0$ in interval arithmetic for $\varepsilon\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.375\}$ and $a=4$, which by monotonicity in $a$ covers every $a\le4$, and determine how far in $a$ a certificate can be pushed at fixed $\varepsilon$. Each certificate is unconditional; at $\varepsilon=\frac38$ it agrees with the $\lambda_{\min}\ge0$ that the announced half plane gives (Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:damped}) and checks it independently.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[The exact rate]\label{dc:prob:rate}
Prove that, under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}, $a^2(\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)-2b_{1/2+\varepsilon})\to\pi^2v_{1/2+\varepsilon}''(0)/4$, and find the next term. The fit of Section~\ref{dc:sec:numerics} supports the limit to within $0.04$ to $0.2$ percent with a positive correction of order $a^{-1}$; neither is proved.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[The transition at the line]\label{dc:prob:transition}
Describe $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)$ jointly as $a\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to0$. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}, the limit is $2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}$, which is at most $2V_\xi\,\varepsilon$ and asymptotic to it as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, and the rate is algebraic; at $\varepsilon=0$, under RH, the decay is faster than any power. On which curves $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(a)\to0$ does $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)$ stay comparable to $2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}$, and does the crossover occur at $\varepsilon\approx$ the mean spacing of the zeros at height $e^{2a}$?
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Uniformity from structure]\label{dc:prob:uniform}
Find a structural reason, valid for all $a$, why $Q_\varepsilon\ge0$ on $L^2[-a,a]$ for some fixed $\varepsilon<\frac12$, using properties of the archimedean and pole terms that dominate the primes up to $e^{2a}$ uniformly. By Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:damped} any such argument proves a zero free half plane $\re s>\frac12+\varepsilon$. Any such argument must fail for the functions $F_{\beta,\gamma}$ of Theorem~\ref{rg:thm:Fbg}, which have a nonnegative prime measure and real poles, and for the Davenport Heilbronn function, which has zeros with real part greater than $1$ \cite[Section 10.25]{Titchmarsh} and whose form at level $\frac12$ is already indefinite at $L=4$ (Section~\ref{dc:sec:numerics}, computed), so it must use the discreteness of the primes and the Euler product and not only the explicit formula and a functional equation.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Rigorous visibility]\label{dc:prob:visible}
For $b>0$ let $K_b(x,y)=e^{b|x-y|}$ on an interval of length $L$; it has one large positive and one large negative eigenvalue, both about $\pm e^{bL}/(2b)$ when $bL$ is large, and the others are of order $1$ (computed). A pair of zeros off the line with real parts $\frac12\pm(\varepsilon+b)$ adds to the form at level $c=\frac12+\varepsilon$ a term of about $+\frac12\langle h,K_bh\rangle$ at the frequency of the zero, so heuristically a window of length $L$ sees a zero of height $\gamma$ once $e^{bL}\gtrsim2b\log\gamma$, that is, $L\gtrsim\log\log\gamma/b$, and the onset for the Davenport Heilbronn zero at height $85.7$, in $(3.5,4]$, is consistent with it (heuristic, with unknown constants). Prove that positivity of $Q_\varepsilon$ on intervals of length $L$ excludes every zero with $\beta>\frac12+\varepsilon+C\log\log\gamma/L$ at every height $\gamma$, controlling clusters of off line zeros by zero density estimates. The origin of the question is an unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026). Status: open.
\end{problem}
