\section{Nested sampling over heights}\label{cf:sec:ns}

The earlier computations live below height $200$. Since $\xi'/\xi$ costs about $0.07$ seconds even at height $10^7$ (Riemann and Siegel in mpmath, a timing reported by the source), the question is how to spend evaluations at large heights. Nested sampling \cite{Skilling06} concentrates them on the most suspicious heights and, under idealised assumptions (Subsection~\ref{cf:sec:nsalg}), estimates the prior volume of the set of near failures of Thorin positivity. Heights and scores below are recomputed in ball arithmetic; the run parameters, the volumes $\log X$ and the run times are the author's and were not rerun.

\subsection{The score and the algorithm}\label{cf:sec:nsalg}

At height $t$ define the normalised Thorin density
\[
s(t)=\frac{u_{\theta_0}(t)}{\varrho(t)},\qquad \theta_0=0.6,\qquad \varrho(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{qt}{2\pi},
\]
with $q=1$ for $\zeta$ and $q=5$ for the Davenport and Heilbronn function, so that $s$ has mean about one. A negative value of $s(t)$ forces a zero $\rho$ with $\sigma_\rho>\theta_0$ and $\lvert t-\gamma_\rho\rvert<\sqrt{(\sigma_\rho-\theta_0)(\sigma_\rho+\theta_0-1)}<\frac12$, by the pairing in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:signature}(ii). A zero with $\sigma_\rho>\theta_0$ makes $u_\theta$ negative near its height at levels $\theta$ just below $\sigma_\rho$ (Theorem~\ref{cx:thm:positivity}); at the fixed level $\theta_0$ it need not (Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:cancel}, Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline}), and Theorem~\ref{cf:thm:converse}, which integrates over levels, does not bound $s$. For $\zeta$ the positivity of $s$ below height $3\times10^{12}-\frac12$ is Corollary~\ref{cor:dbeta:window}(1) with \cite{PT21}; the computation measures the margin, and the lower tail of $s$ is governed by the largest gaps between consecutive zeros. In unfolded coordinates $x=\varrho t$, and keeping only the zeros near $t$, $s\approx\sum_jP_b(x-x_j)$ with $b=(\theta_0-\frac12)\varrho(t)$.

Take the prior $t\sim U[T_1,T_2]$ and the likelihood $-s(t)$. Nested sampling keeps $N$ live heights. At iteration $k$ it removes the live height with the largest $s$, records the threshold $\ell_k$, and replaces it by a copy of another live height moved by $M$ Metropolis steps for the uniform law on $\{s<\ell_k\}$. Each step is a local Gaussian move of a quarter of the mean spacing with probability $0.9$, or a global uniform proposal with probability $0.1$. Under exact independent replacements and a continuous score distribution, the prior volume $X_k=P(s(t)<\ell_k)$ satisfies that $-\log X_k$ is Gamma distributed with shape $k$ and rate $N$, so $\log X_k=-k/N\pm\sqrt k/N$ in the sense of mean and standard deviation \cite{Skilling06,ChopinRobert10}, and the error of the log volume is about $\sqrt{\log(1/X)/N}$, which does not vanish for rare sets. The implemented clones are not independent, so these statements are the ideal model and not an error certificate. The method is related to adaptive multilevel splitting \cite{CDFG12}; the concentration theorem of Section~\ref{cf:sec:conc} is not shown to cover it, since its thresholds are random and its potentials vanish.

\subsection{Results}

\paragraph{Davenport and Heilbronn} \begin{resultpar}\label{cf:res:nsdh}On $t\in[1000,1100]$, with $N=50$ live heights, $M=4$ moves and $300$ iterations ($1250$ evaluations of $\Lambda'/\Lambda$, twelve minutes), no point of the initial uniform sample had $s<0$. Nested sampling crossed $s=0$ at iteration $239$, so the set of heights with $s<0$ has estimated prior volume $X=e^{-4.78\pm0.31}\approx0.008$, about $0.8$ units of height. Its negative points form two clusters, near $1012.0$ and $1018.8$. Newton's method started there gives two zeros of $f$ off the line, computed in ball arithmetic and certified (one zero in a square of half width $10^{-18}$ around each centre, at $256$ and $384$ bits),
\[
\begin{gathered}
\rho=0.691404808984+1012.019981489088\,i,\\
\rho=0.698097971267+1018.795762313197\,i,
\end{gathered}
\]
together with their reflections. Both zeros are in the table of Balanzario and S\'anchez-Ortiz \cite{BSO07}, and zeros off the line of this function are classical \cite{DH36,Spira94,BSO07}. The argument principle in interval arithmetic, with $f\ne0$ for $\Re s\ge2$ since $\lvert f(s)-1\rvert\le\zeta(2)-1<1$ there, gives $0$ zeros in $[0.6,2]\times[1000,1010]$ and $2$ in $[0.6,2]\times[1010,1020]$ (computed, certified). So nested sampling found every zero with $\beta>0.6$ in $[1000,1020]$. The rest of the band $[1020,1100]$ was not counted.\end{resultpar}

For the most suspicious heights of the bands at $10^6$ and $10^7$ the source reports argument principle counts of zeros with $\beta>0.6$ in $[0.6,2]\times[t-\frac12,t+\frac12]$ equal to $0$ (floating point quadrature, not recomputed), as they must be inside the range verified in \cite{PT21}. At $10^9$ and $10^{10}$ the runs are shorter and the live points collapse into a single gap, so those minima ($0.252$ and $0.292$) are upper bounds on the band minimum. The source reports that the sine kernel predicts the $10^{-4}$ quantile of $s$ at $0.231$ for $10^9$ and the $10^{-3}$ quantile at $0.287$ for $10^{10}$, at the matching $b=0.30$ and $0.34$ (not recomputed).

\paragraph{Checking the volume estimates, and a failure mode} A plain Monte Carlo sample of $5000$ uniform heights in the band at $10^6$ gives $P(s<0.399)=0.130$, $P(s<0.235)=0.0104$ and $P(s<0.2005)=0.0018$. Nested sampling put these levels at $X=0.10$, $0.010$ and $0.0010$. The two agree to within the stated error down to $X\approx10^{-2}$ in this one comparison, and nested sampling underestimates by a factor of about two at $10^{-3}$. The Monte Carlo sample also found a point with $s=0.142$, in a gap of normalised length $2.87$ at height $1004606.8$ (both values recomputed: $s=0.14185$, gap $2.865$), deeper than anything nested sampling reached. The set $\{s<\ell\}$ is a union of thousands of short intervals, one inside each large gap, and local moves cannot travel between them; once global proposals are rarely accepted, the live points collapse into a few intervals. No reliability threshold in the volume is established; beyond about $10^{-2}$ nested sampling is a search heuristic, not an exhaustive search. The remedy, not yet tried, is a gap guided form that proposes new heights at the large gaps directly, located cheaply by sign changes of Hardy's $Z$ function.

\paragraph{Comparison with the sine kernel} The source compares the tails with the prediction of the sine kernel process, modelled by circular unitary matrices of size $600$ at the matching value of $b$, a finite unitary ensemble and not the infinite sine process; the uncertainty of the extreme tail, which comes from few matrices, is not estimated. At volumes $10^{-1}$ and $10^{-2}$ the band at $10^7$ gives $0.394$ against $0.410$ and $0.258$ against $0.259$, and the band at $10^6$ agrees to about $10\%$ (not recomputed). Deeper in the tail the zeros of $\zeta$ give fewer small values of $s$ than the sine kernel model, both in nested sampling and in plain Monte Carlo ($0.0018$ against about $0.004$ at $s<0.2005$). This is the direction of the finite height deficit of large gaps, which Bogomolny, Bohigas, Leboeuf and Monastra describe by a unitary ensemble of effective size $\log(t/2\pi)/\sqrt{12\Lambda_0}$ with $\Lambda_0=1.57314\ldots$ \cite{BBLM06}. The coefficient is $0.2302$ (not $0.2887=1/\sqrt{12}$), and the sizes are $2.76$ at $10^6$, $3.29$ at $10^7$, $4.35$ at $10^9$ and $4.88$ at $10^{10}$ (computed). Their comparison with the finite height data is heuristic and has not been checked quantitatively.

\subsection{What this buys}

Nested sampling does not verify RH at new heights; exhaustive verification by Turing's method already reaches $3\times10^{12}$ \cite{PT21}. It gives a cheap, scale free measure of how close $\zeta$ comes to violating positivity of $u_{\theta_0}$: the smallest margins found are between $0.14$ and $0.29$ of the mean density at heights $10^6$ to $10^{10}$, always in the largest gaps, with a lower tail that agrees with the sine kernel model down to volumes of about $10^{-2}$ and has fewer extreme gaps beyond (computed, heuristic as a statement about the tail). As a heuristic search it found two zeros off the line of the Davenport and Heilbronn function in $[1010,1020]$. Lifting it to a certificate needs the gap guided proposals above and an exhaustive count, which brings it back to Turing's method; the certificates that the book has are in Section~\ref{cx:sec:decide}, Theorem~\ref{cx:thm:certified}, Theorem~\ref{cx:thm:globalmin} and Section~\ref{cx:sec:limits}.

\section{What would close the gap}\label{cf:sec:gap}

With Section~\ref{cf:sec:recap} the results of this chapter place the reciprocal Xi programme as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathfrak U_1$ is unconditionally a positive Cauchy mixture, and $\mathfrak U_{1/2}$ is the counting measure of the critical zeros. The passage between them is one step of the Cauchy semigroup, and RH is exactly the statement that this step can be inverted within positive measures (Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}).
\item Positivity of $\mathfrak U_\tau$ for every $\tau>\sigma$ is $\mathrm{QRH}(\sigma)$, a zero free half plane (Theorem~\ref{cx:thm:positivity}), while positivity at the single level $\sigma$ is necessary and not known to be sufficient (Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline}). There is no slack: the Thorin route to RH is the zero free region problem in another language.
\item Unconditionally, $\mathfrak U_\theta$ is positive up to a fraction $O(T^{-(\theta-1/2)/4})$ of its mass on $(0,T]$ (Theorem~\ref{cf:thm:negmass}, proved with Selberg's density theorem).
\item The Davenport and Heilbronn function shares the framework of this chapter and has zeros off the line (Proposition~\ref{cf:prop:barrier}), so some further property is needed, the Euler product being the natural one. It enters as the inequality of Theorem~\ref{cf:thm:dvp}, which gives positivity within $c/\log t$ of $\theta=1$ (Corollary~\ref{cf:cor:zfr}, with the local form of Corollary~\ref{cf:cor:localpos}). Combining levels as well as heights improves the best single level constant by about $48\%$ (computed, Table~\ref{cf:tab:arith}), but in the class in which every zero contributes nonnegatively no inequality gives more than $1-\beta\ge c/\log\gamma$ with a constant $c<1$ (Proposition~\ref{cf:prop:barrier2}); the ceiling of that class, computed by a discretised linear program and not certified, is about $1/(11.3\log\gamma)$ (Table~\ref{cf:tab:lp}). Pairing each zero with its reflection leaves the class (Proposition~\ref{cf:prop:stechkin}).
\item Every zero off the line forces negative Thorin mass at least its isolation distance over $\log T$ (Theorem~\ref{cf:thm:converse}); for separated zeros the negative mass and the zero count control each other up to a factor $\log T$ when the isolation is at least of order $1/\log T$ and the counts are of order $\log T$ or smaller (Corollary~\ref{cf:cor:twosided}).
\end{enumerate}

The computational statements are local; nested sampling (Section~\ref{cf:sec:ns}) samples heights up to $10^{10}$ and certifies only the windows counted in interval arithmetic. The particle filter recovers the zeros of $\zeta$ in a window to four decimals and bounds their distance from the line by $5\times10^{-4}$ at the $95\%$ level under the Gaussian tolerance model, and it matches the first order Fisher information of the model with free real parts (heuristic; Section~\ref{cf:sec:pf}). The GBC screen scans a window in milliseconds and found all four zeros off the line of the Davenport and Heilbronn function below height $200$, with one boundary false alarm (the window centred at $180$, Table~\ref{cf:tab:scan}) and none for $\zeta$. The contour moments recover a zero off the line to ten digits, computed in ball arithmetic (Table~\ref{cf:tab:dl}). None of this extends past the verified height $3\times10^{12}$, and none of it is a proof.

A proof along these lines needs one of two things. The first is an arithmetic input, coming from the Euler product, that controls $u_\theta$ uniformly in $t$ for some $\theta<1$ beyond the Vinogradov and Korobov boundary. Section~\ref{cf:sec:arith} shows that positivity inequalities of de la Vall\'ee Poussin type, in which every zero contributes nonnegatively, cannot supply it: they are capped at $c/\log t$ with $c<1$, and in the computed basic form at about $1/(11.3\log t)$. The input could instead be a growth bound for $\zeta$ near $\theta=1$, as in Vinogradov and Korobov, but uniform (heuristic). The second is a density estimate strong enough to force the negative Thorin mass to vanish. Theorem~\ref{cf:thm:converse} shows this direction is not lost to clustering: every zero off the line pays negative mass in proportion to its isolation. But the density estimate needed is itself equivalent to the absence of such zeros. Both routes are open.
