\section{Almost generalised gamma convolutions}\label{ch:almost}

Below the basepoint one, as Section~\ref{bp:sec:compare} concluded, every positive object built so far from the shifted clock $T_\alpha$, whose Laplace transform is $\varphi_\alpha(u)=\xi(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha+\sqrt u)$, is equivalent, up to the boundary case, to the absence of zeros in the half plane $\re s>\alpha$. So are Salem's equation, the dual form of completeness (Corollary~\ref{wr:cor:salem}), and Riesz's growth criterion with exponent $\alpha/2$ (Sections~\ref{wr:sec:riesz} and \ref{ch:riesz}). These equivalences are restatements rather than reductions; their value is that several of them are computable and that they suggest the right intermediate targets. Since no zero free half plane was known before 2026, and none is known to the left of the line $\re s=\frac78$ (Section~\ref{sec:ch25:open}), the question that remains is quantitative: how negative can the Thorin density be below the basepoint one? Zero density estimates answer it with no hypothesis. The negative part of the density is a sum of Cauchy kernels, one for each zero to the right of $\alpha$, so its mass is controlled by the number of such zeros, while the positive part grows like the zero count. With the estimates of Ingham, of Guth and Maynard and of Huxley, every $T_\alpha$ with $\alpha>\frac12$ is a GGC up to a defect carried by the zeros to the right of $\alpha$, whose Cauchy kernels carry a vanishing fraction of the mass of the others, with a power saving; we call this property almost GGC.

Section~\ref{ag:sec:almost} proves this (Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost}). Section~\ref{ag:sec:factor} factorises $\varphi_\alpha$ exactly into the Laplace transform of a GGC and an explicit defect that carries every zero to the right of $\alpha$, and bounds the defect with no hypothesis from the verification of RH to the height $T_0=3\times10^{12}$ \cite{PT21}. Section~\ref{ag:sec:cancel} shows by a cancellation lemma where negativity of the density can come from, Section~\ref{ag:sec:num} inserts hypothetical off line zeros into the computed density, and Section~\ref{ag:sec:margins} compares the positivity margins of the density with the circular unitary ensemble. Section~\ref{ag:sec:moments} turns to the moments of $\zeta$ on the critical line. Their random matrix factor is given by the Barnes function, which Nikeghbali and Yor express through GGC variables, and they are exponential moments of the Thorin density integrated across the basepoints; through zero density they bear on the strength of the almost GGC theorem, but not on exact positivity. Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly} explains why no count of zeros turns ``almost'' into ``exactly'', which is where Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusions} takes up the path. With $\xi$ again in place of $\xi(\cdot,\chi_{12})$, we keep the notation of Sections~\ref{ch:basepoint} and \ref{ch:sato}: $v_\alpha(\theta)=\re(\xi'/\xi)(\alpha+i\theta)$, and the Thorin density at the basepoint $\alpha$ in the chart $\theta=\sqrt z$ is $v_\alpha/\pi$, the density $\rho_{\alpha-1/2}$ of \eqref{eq:rho} at the resolution $\alpha-\frac12$; in this section the basepoint, not the resolution, is the index. $P_w(x)=w/(\pi(w^2+x^2))$ is the Poisson kernel of Section~\ref{ch:clock}, $P_{-w}=-P_w$, and $P_0$ is the unit point mass, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:signature}. A zero is $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$; $N(T)$ counts the zeros with $0<\gamma\le T$, and $N(\sigma,T)$ those among them with $\beta\ge\sigma$, so that $N(\sigma,T)\ge\nu(\sigma,T)$ in the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:zerodensity}. The scripts are in the folder \texttt{riesz\_program/code} of the archive and their outputs in \texttt{riesz\_program/data}; we name them at each table and figure.

\subsection{Mass bookkeeping and the almost GGC theorem}\label{ag:sec:almost}

The Poisson form of Proposition~\ref{prop:struct} splits according to the side of the line $\re s=\alpha$ on which each zero lies, $v_\alpha=v_\alpha^+-v_\alpha^-$, with
\begin{equation}\label{ag:eq:split}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{v_\alpha^+(\theta)}\pi&=\sum_{\beta\le\alpha,\ \gamma>0}\big[P_{\alpha-\beta}(\theta-\gamma)+P_{\alpha-\beta}(\theta+\gamma)\big],\\
\frac{v_\alpha^-(\theta)}\pi&=\sum_{\beta>\alpha,\ \gamma>0}\big[P_{\beta-\alpha}(\theta-\gamma)+P_{\beta-\alpha}(\theta+\gamma)\big].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both parts are positive, and a zero on the line $\re s=\alpha$, if there is one, contributes a unit atom at its ordinate to the positive part. Each pair of kernels has mass one on $(0,\infty)$. A zero with $\beta>\alpha$ has the partner $1-\bar\rho=1-\beta+i\gamma$, whose real part $1-\beta<\frac12<\alpha$ puts it in the positive part. Write $m_\alpha^\pm(T)=\pi^{-1}\int_0^Tv_\alpha^\pm(\theta)\,d\theta$ for the positive and negative masses on $[0,T]$.

\begin{lemma}[Mass bookkeeping]\label{ag:lem:mass}
Let $\alpha>\frac12$, and suppose that $N(\alpha,t)\le t^{e+o(1)}$ as $t\to\infty$ for some $e<1$. Then
\[
m_\alpha^-(T)\le N(\alpha,2T)+O\big(T^{e-1+o(1)}\big),\qquad m_\alpha^+(T)=\frac T{2\pi}\log\frac T{2\pi}+O(T).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A zero with $\beta>\alpha$ and $\gamma\le2T$ contributes at most its total mass one. For a zero with $\gamma>2T$ put $w=\beta-\alpha$, so that $0<w<\frac12$. Since $\int_0^TP_w(\theta+\gamma)\,d\theta=\int_{-T}^0P_w(\theta-\gamma)\,d\theta$, its pair of kernels has mass on $[0,T]$ at most
\[
\int_{-\infty}^TP_w(\theta-\gamma)\,d\theta\le\frac{w}{\pi(\gamma-T)}\le\frac1{\pi\gamma},
\]
and $\sum_{\beta>\alpha,\ \gamma>2T}\gamma^{-1}=\int_{2T}^\infty t^{-1}dN(\alpha,t)\le\int_{2T}^\infty N(\alpha,t)t^{-2}dt\ll T^{e-1+o(1)}$. The positive part contains the kernels of all zeros with $\beta\le\alpha$, among them every zero on the critical line and every partner $1-\bar\rho$. Its mass on $[0,T]$ is therefore $N(T)$ up to the $N(\alpha,T)=O(T^{e+o(1)})$ zeros to the right of $\alpha$, whose kernels it lacks, and up to the tails of the kernels of the zeros near height $T$, which change it by $O(\log^2T)$. Since $N(T)=\frac T{2\pi}\log\frac T{2\pi}-\frac T{2\pi}+O(\log T)$, the second claim follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Almost GGC]\label{ag:thm:almost}
For every $\frac12<\alpha<1$, with no hypothesis,
\[
\frac{m_\alpha^-(T)}{m_\alpha^+(T)}\le T^{e(\alpha)-1+o(1)},\qquad e(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{3(1-\alpha)}{2-\alpha},&\frac12<\alpha<\frac7{10},\\[2ex]\dfrac{15(1-\alpha)}{3+5\alpha},&\frac7{10}\le\alpha<1,\end{cases}
\]
and $e(\alpha)<1$ throughout, so the negative part $v_\alpha^-/\pi$ of \eqref{ag:eq:split}, the Cauchy kernels of the zeros to the right of $\alpha$, carries on $[0,T]$ a vanishing fraction of the mass of the positive part, with a power saving. For $\alpha\ge1$ no zero lies to the right of $\alpha$, and $m_\alpha^-=0$. Under the density hypothesis $e(\alpha)=2(1-\alpha)$. RH holds if and only if $v_\alpha^-$ vanishes identically for every $\alpha>\frac12$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:mass} with three zero density estimates: Ingham's $N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{3(1-\sigma)/(2-\sigma)}\log^5T$ \cite{Ingham40}; Guth and Maynard's $N(\sigma,T)\le T^{15(1-\sigma)/(3+5\sigma)+o(1)}$ for $\frac7{10}\le\sigma\le\frac45$ \cite[Theorem 1.2]{GM26}; and Huxley's $N(\sigma,T)\le T^{3(1-\sigma)/(3\sigma-1)+o(1)}$ for $\sigma\ge\frac34$ \cite{Huxley72}, whose exponent is at most $15(1-\sigma)/(3+5\sigma)$ when $\sigma\ge\frac45$. The first two exponents agree at $\sigma=\frac7{10}$, and together they give the uniform bound $N(\sigma,T)\le T^{30(1-\sigma)/13+o(1)}$ of Guth and Maynard. Since $3(1-\alpha)<2-\alpha$ exactly when $\alpha>\frac12$, and $15(1-\alpha)<3+5\alpha$ exactly when $\alpha>\frac35$, the exponent is below one for every $\frac12<\alpha<1$. Finally, $v_\alpha^-$ vanishes identically exactly when no zero has $\beta>\alpha$, which is the GGC criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson}.
\end{proof}

Even the classical estimate of Bohr and Landau \cite{BL14}, $N(\sigma,T)=O(T)$ for fixed $\sigma>\frac12$, gives a vanishing fraction $O(1/\log T)$; the zero density estimates improve this to a power of $T$. Two remarks sharpen the statement. First, the theorem bounds the decomposition mass $m_\alpha^-(T)$, which overcounts the negative part $\int_0^T\max(-v_\alpha/\pi,0)\,d\theta$ of the density itself, because the positive kernels of nearby zeros cancel much of each negative kernel. The partner of a zero already cancels part of it: with $\varepsilon=\alpha-\frac12$ and $\delta=\beta-\frac12$, twice the mass in Proposition~\ref{prop:signature}(iii) gives the negative part of $P_{\alpha+\beta-1}-P_{\beta-\alpha}=P_{\varepsilon+\delta}+P_{\varepsilon-\delta}$ the mass $\frac4\pi\arctan\sqrt{(\delta+\varepsilon)/(\delta-\varepsilon)}-1=\frac2\pi\arcsin\frac\varepsilon\delta$, less than the mass one that the decomposition counts, and Section~\ref{ag:sec:num} shows the effect of the other zeros. Second, on the verified range the negative mass is essentially zero. All zeros with $0<\gamma\le T_0$ lie on the critical line \cite{PT21}, so for $T\le T_0/2$ the only negative contributions come from zeros above $T_0$, and by the proof of Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:mass} their total mass on $[0,T]$ is $O(T_0^{e(\alpha)-1+o(1)})$. Beyond the verified range Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost} takes over.

Table~\ref{ag:tab:exp}, computed by \texttt{almost.py}, lists the exponents, and the left panel of Figure~\ref{ag:fig:almost} draws them. The saving $1-e(\alpha)$ is small near the centre, where Ingham's exponent gives $(2\alpha-1)/(2-\alpha)$, $0.069$ at $\alpha=0.55$, and it grows to $0.8$ at $\alpha=0.9$. The density hypothesis would give $2\alpha-1$; the difference from the saving of the theorem is largest in the middle of the strip, $0.092$ at $\alpha=0.7$, and vanishes at $\alpha=0.9$. Sharper exponents are known near $\alpha=1$: the density hypothesis itself holds for $\alpha>\frac{25}{32}$ \cite[p.~133]{Bourgain00}, and Huxley's exponent \cite{Huxley72} is $0.176$ at $\alpha=0.9$; those of the theorem suffice for what follows.

\subsection{The canonical factorisation and explicit unconditional bounds}\label{ag:sec:factor}

The splitting \eqref{ag:eq:split} does more than bookkeeping: it factorises the Laplace transform of the clock. The zeros with $\beta\le\alpha$ give a generalised gamma convolution, and those with $\beta>\alpha$ an explicit factor, which is identically one exactly when there are none.

\begin{proposition}[Canonical factorisation]\label{ag:prop:factor}
For every $\alpha>\frac12$ and $u\ge0$, $\varphi_\alpha(u)=\varphi_\alpha^+(u)\,D_\alpha(u)$, where
\begin{gather*}
\varphi_\alpha^+(u)=\exp\Big\{-\int_0^\infty\log\Big(1+\frac u{\theta^2}\Big)\frac{v_\alpha^+(\theta)}\pi\,d\theta\Big\},\\
D_\alpha(u)=\prod_{\beta>\alpha,\ \gamma>0}\frac{(\beta-\alpha)^2+\gamma^2}{(\sqrt u-\beta+\alpha)^2+\gamma^2};
\end{gather*}
here $\varphi_\alpha^+$ is the Laplace transform of a GGC $T_\alpha^+$ with Thorin density $v_\alpha^+/\pi$ in the chart $\theta=\sqrt z$, and the product defining the defect $D_\alpha$ runs over the zeros to the right of the line $\re s=\alpha$ and converges absolutely. The defect is identically one if and only if $\zeta$ has no zeros with $\re s>\alpha$, and then $T_\alpha\stackrel d=T_\alpha^+$. Otherwise $D_\alpha(u)\to0$ as $u\to\infty$, and $T_\alpha^+$ is not the sum of $T_\alpha$ and an independent nonnegative variable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Grouping the Hadamard product of $\xi$ in conjugate pairs, as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:struct}, gives for $u\ge0$
\[
\varphi_\alpha(u)=\prod_{\gamma>0}\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}{(\alpha-\beta+\sqrt u)^2+\gamma^2},
\]
over the zeros with $\gamma>0$, counted with multiplicity; each factor is $1+O((u+\sqrt u)\gamma^{-2})$ and $\sum\gamma^{-2}<\infty$, so the product converges absolutely. For $\beta<\alpha$, with $w=\alpha-\beta>0$, the factor is $(w^2+\gamma^2)/((w+\sqrt u)^2+\gamma^2)=\exp\{-\int_0^\infty\log(1+u/\theta^2)[P_w(\theta-\gamma)+P_w(\theta+\gamma)]\,d\theta\}$, the Laplace transform of a GGC whose Thorin density is the pair of Cauchy kernels of width $w$ at $\pm\gamma$ (the computation after Theorem~\ref{sm:thm:thorin}); for $\beta=\alpha$ it is $\gamma^2/(\gamma^2+u)$, an exponential law, whose Thorin measure is the atom at $\gamma$. The partial products over the zeros with $\beta\le\alpha$ are therefore Laplace transforms of GGCs, they converge for every $u\ge0$ to a limit continuous at the origin, and by the closure theorem of Section~\ref{sec:weak:limits} the limit is the Laplace transform of a GGC, with Thorin density $v_\alpha^+/\pi$. The remaining factors form $D_\alpha$. If a zero lies to the right of $\alpha$, put $w=\beta-\alpha\in(0,\frac12)$; its factor tends to zero as $u\to\infty$, and every factor of $D_\alpha$ is at most one for $u\ge1$, because $(\sqrt u-w)^2\ge w^2$ when $\sqrt u\ge2w$. So $D_\alpha(u)\to0$. If $T_\alpha^+$ were $T_\alpha+S$ with $S\ge0$ independent of $T_\alpha$, then $\E e^{-uS}=\varphi_\alpha^+(u)/\varphi_\alpha(u)=1/D_\alpha(u)$ would be unbounded, which is impossible.
\end{proof}

The proposition also says what the signed density $v_\alpha/\pi$ is. When every zero lies to the left of the line $\re s=\alpha$ it is the Thorin density of $T_\alpha$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson}); a zero on the line adds to the Thorin measure an atom at its ordinate, the term $P_0$ of \eqref{ag:eq:split}, which the function $v_\alpha$ does not show. When a zero lies to the right of the line, $T_\alpha$ is not a GGC and $v_\alpha/\pi$ is not a signed Thorin density for it in any sense: the negative kernel $-P_w$ of such a zero integrates to the factor $((w+\sqrt u)^2+\gamma^2)/(w^2+\gamma^2)$, while the zero contributes $(w^2+\gamma^2)/((\sqrt u-w)^2+\gamma^2)$ to $\varphi_\alpha$. So $\varphi_\alpha\ne\varphi_\alpha^+/\varphi_\alpha^-$ for the GGC transform $\varphi_\alpha^-$ with Thorin density $v_\alpha^-/\pi$, and by Proposition~\ref{ag:prop:factor} the clock is not $T_\alpha^+$ minus any independent nonnegative variable. It is a GGC times the defect $D_\alpha$, which carries all possible failure of RH, and Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost} says that the zeros entering the defect are sparse in a precise sense: their kernels carry a vanishing, power saving fraction of the mass. The same bookkeeping applies to the other reformulations in Chapters~\ref{chap:basepoint} to \ref{chap:almost}. In the normal scale mixture of Theorem~\ref{sm:thm:strip} it bounds the negative part of the candidate EGGC density $v_\alpha/\pi$; in Wiener's theorem (Proposition~\ref{wr:prop:wiener}) it bounds the proportion of frequencies at which the Fourier transform of the tilted P\'olya kernel can vanish; and in Riesz's criterion (Section~\ref{wr:sec:riesz}) it bounds the number of Mellin poles to the right of $\alpha/2$. Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:explicit} shows that, with no hypothesis, the defect is extremely close to one at every fixed $u$.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit bounds from verification]\label{ag:thm:explicit}
Let $T_0=3\times10^{12}$, below which all zeros lie on the critical line \cite{PT21}, and let $\Sigma_0=\sum_{\gamma>T_0}\gamma^{-2}$, the sum over all zeros above $T_0$, counted with multiplicity. By the Riemann von Mangoldt formula with the explicit error term of Hasanalizade, Shen and Wong \cite{HSW22}, recalled in the proof of Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:v1min}, $\Sigma_0$ differs from $\frac{\log(T_0/2\pi)+1}{2\pi T_0}=1.4797\times10^{-12}$ by less than $2\times10^{-24}$, so $\Sigma_0<1.48\times10^{-12}$. Then for every $\alpha>\frac12$ and every $u\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $|\log D_\alpha(u)|\le(u+\sqrt u)\,\Sigma_0$, so that
\[
e^{-1.5\times10^{-12}(u+\sqrt u)}\,\varphi_\alpha^+(u)\le\E e^{-uT_\alpha}\le e^{1.5\times10^{-12}(u+\sqrt u)}\,\varphi_\alpha^+(u);
\]
\item the negative part of the density satisfies $v_\alpha^-(\theta)/\pi\le2\Sigma_0/\pi<9.43\times10^{-13}$ for $0\le\theta\le T_0/2$;
\item consequently $v_\alpha(\theta)>0$ for all $0\le\theta\le T_0/2$ whenever $\alpha-\frac12>6\times10^{-10}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every zero to the right of $\alpha$ has $\gamma>T_0$ and $\beta>\frac12$. The zeros above $T_0$ off the critical line come in pairs $\beta+i\gamma$, $1-\beta+i\gamma$ of equal multiplicity, by $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ and $\xi(\bar s)=\overline{\xi(s)}$, and at most one member of each pair lies to the right of $\alpha$, so $\sum_{\beta>\alpha,\ \gamma>0}\gamma^{-2}\le\Sigma_0/2$. (1) For a zero to the right of $\alpha$ put $w=\beta-\alpha\in(0,\frac12)$ and $x=(u-2w\sqrt u)/(w^2+\gamma^2)$, so that its factor in $D_\alpha$ is $1/(1+x)$. Since $u-2w\sqrt u\ge-w^2$, we have $x\ge-w^2/\gamma^2>-\frac12$, and $|x|\le(u+\sqrt u)/\gamma^2$; for $x\ge-\frac12$, $|\log(1+x)|\le2|x|$. Hence the logarithm of its factor is at most $2(u+\sqrt u)/\gamma^2$ in absolute value, and summing gives $|\log D_\alpha(u)|\le(u+\sqrt u)\Sigma_0$; the inequalities for $\E e^{-uT_\alpha}=\varphi_\alpha^+(u)D_\alpha(u)$ follow. (2) For $\theta\le T_0/2$ and $\gamma>T_0$, $P_w(\theta-\gamma)+P_w(\theta+\gamma)\le\frac{2w}{\pi(\gamma-\theta)^2}\le\frac4{\pi\gamma^2}$, and summing over the zeros to the right of $\alpha$ gives at most $\frac4\pi\cdot\frac{\Sigma_0}2$. (3) Write $a=\alpha-\frac12$. If $a\ge\frac12$, then $\alpha\ge1$, no zero lies to the right of $\alpha$, and $v_\alpha=v_\alpha^+>0$; so let $6\times10^{-10}<a<\frac12$. The zeros below $T_0$ lie on the line and contribute to $v_\alpha^+/\pi$ the kernels $P_a(\theta-\gamma)$, and all other kernels of $v_\alpha^+$ are positive, so $v_\alpha^+(\theta)/\pi\ge\frac a{\pi(a^2+\gamma_1^2)}$ at every $\theta$ within $\gamma_1$ of an ordinate below $T_0$. Every $\theta\in[0,T_0/2]$ is such a point. On $[0,\gamma_1]$ the ordinate $\gamma_1$ serves. On $[\gamma_1,2000]$ one of the first $1800$ ordinates serves: they are the certified zeros of the proof of Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:v1min}, they reach height $2304.3$, and consecutive ones differ by less than $6.8874<2\gamma_1$. For $2000\le\theta\le T_0/2$, the count of that proof, with $H=14$ in place of $15$, shows that the window $(\theta-14,\theta+14]$ holds at least $n(\theta)=\frac{14}\pi\log\frac{\theta-14}{2\pi}-E(\theta-14)-E(\theta+14)$ zeros; while $\theta+14\le X_0$, $n$ increases and $n(2000)>20$, and for $\theta+14>X_0$, $n(\theta)>\frac{14}\pi\log\frac{\theta-14}{2\pi}-0.6\log(\theta+14)>80$, so the window holds a zero, which lies within $14<\gamma_1$ of $\theta$ and, since $\theta+14<T_0$, on the line. The script \texttt{explicit\_bounds.py}, with output \texttt{explicit\_bounds.json}, recomputes the $1800$ zeros and checks these inequalities in ball arithmetic. Finally, since $a<\frac12$,
\[
\frac a{\pi(a^2+\gamma_1^2)}>\frac a{\pi(\frac14+\gamma_1^2)}>\frac{6\times10^{-10}}{\pi(\frac14+\gamma_1^2)}>9.5\times10^{-13},
\]
which is more than the bound $2\Sigma_0/\pi<9.43\times10^{-13}$ of (2) on $v_\alpha^-/\pi$, so $v_\alpha=v_\alpha^+-v_\alpha^->0$ on $[0,T_0/2]$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:explicit} restates the verification of RH to height $3\times10^{12}$ in Thorin form: at every basepoint, however close to the centre, the clock is a GGC up to a Laplace transform factor within $e^{\pm1.5\times10^{-12}(u+\sqrt u)}$, and for $\alpha-\frac12>6\times10^{-10}$ the density $v_\alpha/\pi$ is pointwise positive on the verified window. Part (3) of Theorem~\ref{sm:thm:strip} is the same statement for the characteristic function of the normal scale mixture, with a remainder of relative size $\log T_0/T_0$, and the paragraph after its proof bounds the negative part of $v_\alpha/\pi$ for $\theta\le10^9$ in the same way as part (2).

Part (3) of Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:explicit} treats every zero above $T_0$ as if its kernel could be negative wherever the density is evaluated. As the first author's note on the Dirichlet beta function observes \cite{P26beta}, grouping each such zero with its reflection in the critical line does better, because the sum of the two kernels of an off line pair is negative only near its ordinate.

\begin{corollary}[Positivity on the verified window]\label{cor:dbeta:window}
Let $T_0=3\times10^{12}$ and $V_\xi=(\log\xi)''(\frac12)=0.04620998623$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\alpha>\frac12$, $v_\alpha(\theta)>0$ for $|\theta|\le T_0-\frac12$.
\item For every $\alpha\in(\frac12,7.35]$, $v_\alpha(\theta)>v_\alpha(0)$ for $0<|\theta|\le T_0-\frac{1+\sqrt2}2$, and $0<v_\alpha(0)\le(\alpha-\frac12)V_\xi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(1) Put $\varepsilon=\alpha-\frac12$. By the Hadamard product, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:struct}, $v_\alpha(\theta)$ is the absolutely convergent sum over the zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, counted with multiplicity, of $(\alpha-\beta)/((\alpha-\beta)^2+(\theta-\gamma)^2)$. A zero on the critical line contributes $\pi P_\varepsilon(\theta-\gamma)>0$. The zeros off the line come in pairs $\frac12\pm\delta+i\gamma$ with $0<\delta<\frac12$ and equal multiplicities, and a pair contributes $\pi(P_{\varepsilon+\delta}+P_{\varepsilon-\delta})(\theta-\gamma)$, which is nonnegative if $\delta\le\varepsilon$ and, if $\delta>\varepsilon$, negative exactly where $|\theta-\gamma|<\sqrt{\delta^2-\varepsilon^2}$, by the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:signature}(ii); and $\sqrt{\delta^2-\varepsilon^2}<\delta<\frac12$. Every zero off the line has $|\gamma|>T_0$ \cite{PT21}, so for $|\theta|\le T_0-\frac12$ every pair has $|\theta-\gamma|>\frac12$ and contributes a nonnegative term, while the zero $\frac12+i\gamma_1$ contributes a positive one.

(2) This is Theorem~\ref{thm:dbeta:minall}(i) and (ii) for $\xi$, with $\alpha_{\max}=7.35$ and $H_0=T_0$ (Table~\ref{tab:dbeta:minall}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:dbeta:window}
Part (1) improves part (3) of Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:explicit} in both respects: it holds for every $\alpha>\frac12$, not only for $\alpha-\frac12>6\times10^{-10}$, and on $[0,T_0-\frac12]$ rather than on $[0,T_0/2]$. Likewise the bound after the proof of Theorem~\ref{sm:thm:strip} on what the zeros above $T_0$ could subtract from $v_\alpha/\pi$ is not needed below $T_0-\frac12$, where every pair contributes a nonnegative term. So on the verified window the positivity of the density adds nothing to the verification. The corollary does not touch the defect of part (1) of Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:explicit}: a zero above $T_0$ to the right of the line $\re s=\alpha$ stops $T_\alpha$ from being a GGC whatever $v_\alpha$ does on the window (Proposition~\ref{ag:prop:factor}), and near its own ordinate it can make $v_\alpha$ negative (Section~\ref{ag:sec:cancel}). Part (2), which comes from the comparison across vertical lines of Section~\ref{sec:dbeta:comp}, locates the minimum: for every $\alpha\in(\frac12,7.35]$ the density $v_\alpha/\pi$ is smallest at the origin, and only there, for $0<|\theta|\le T_0-\frac{1+\sqrt2}2$, as the next paragraph finds on $[0,200]$ at $\alpha=0.6$. Its proof uses of the zeros only the first ordinate and the verification height.
\end{remark}

On the computed range the numbers bear this out. By \texttt{more2.py}, the minimum over $[0,2100]$ of the Poisson sum over the first $1700$ ordinates, taken at the midpoints between them and at the origin, is $1.44\times10^{-3}$, $1.44\times10^{-5}$, $1.44\times10^{-8}$ and $1.44\times10^{-10}$ at $\alpha-\frac12=10^{-1}$, $10^{-3}$, $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-8}$. It is attained at $\theta=0$, it is proportional to $\alpha-\frac12$, it lies above the lower bound $a/(\pi(a^2+\gamma_1^2))$ of the proof, and it is far above $9.4\times10^{-13}$. The complete density is larger by the contribution of the zeros above height $2198$: at $\alpha=0.6$ its minimum on $[0,200]$ is the Cauchy floor $b_\alpha/\pi=0.0014709$, attained at the origin (Section~\ref{sm:sec:strip}), in agreement with the margin $0.0147\,\varepsilon$ of Section~\ref{ch:motivation}. The largest gap between consecutive ordinates below height $2198$ is $6.89$, between $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$.

The defect is the quantity to track as verification and zero density estimates improve. The proof of part (1) gives, with no hypothesis, $|\log D_\alpha(u)|\le2(u+\sqrt u)\sum_{\beta>\alpha,\ \gamma>0}\gamma^{-2}$. With the verification height and the trivial count the sum is at most $\Sigma_0/2$, with $\Sigma_0<1.48\times10^{-12}$. The zero density estimates of Section~\ref{ag:sec:almost} \cite{Ingham40,GM26,Huxley72} improve this to $\sum_{\beta>\alpha}\gamma^{-2}\le\int_{T_0}^\infty t^{-2}dN(\alpha,t)\ll T_0^{e(\alpha)-2+o(1)}$, smaller by a further power of $T_0$ since $e(\alpha)<1$. What is wanted is a table of explicit defects as functions of $\alpha$ and $T_0$, with explicit zero density estimates in place of the $o(1)$ in the exponents, updated as verification heights and density exponents improve. Such a table would measure, in one number per basepoint, how close the programme is to its target.

\subsection{Where negativity can come from: a cancellation lemma}\label{ag:sec:cancel}

The counting bound treats every zero to the right of $\alpha$ as if its kernel were entirely negative. Pointwise, a negative kernel is cancelled wherever the rest of the density exceeds it, and the kernel of width $w$ has height at most $1/(\pi w)$.

\begin{lemma}[Cancellation]\label{ag:lem:cancel}
Let $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ be a zero with $\beta>\alpha$, and let $m>0$ be a lower bound on an interval $I$ for the rest of the density, $v_\alpha/\pi+P_{\beta-\alpha}(\cdot-\gamma)$. If $\beta-\alpha\ge1/(\pi m)$, the kernel of $\rho$ cannot make $v_\alpha$ negative on $I$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $I$, $v_\alpha/\pi\ge m-\max_xP_{\beta-\alpha}(x)=m-1/(\pi(\beta-\alpha))\ge0$.
\end{proof}

The lemma has a heuristic consequence. At height $\gamma$ the density $v_\alpha/\pi$ averages $\frac1{2\pi}\log\frac\gamma{2\pi}$, the density of the ordinates, and when $\alpha-\frac12$ is large compared with the mean spacing $2\pi/\log\gamma$ it stays close to that average. Taking this average for $m$ in the lemma, a zero to the right of $\alpha$ can then produce negativity only if
\[
\beta-\alpha<\frac{2\pi}{\pi\log(\gamma/2\pi)}=\frac2{\log(\gamma/2\pi)} .
\]
Heuristically, then, positivity of the Thorin density at the basepoint $\alpha$ can be broken at height $\gamma$ only by zeros in the thin strip $\alpha<\beta<\alpha+2/\log(\gamma/2\pi)$; zeros farther to the right are absorbed by the positive mass of the zeros on the line, and the dangerous strip narrows as the height grows. The local visibility condition of Section~\ref{sec:ch13:visibility}, which concerns small resolutions, has the same scale $1/\log\gamma$. The heuristic proves nothing, because a rigorous lower bound for the background near an arbitrary height would itself control $\zeta'/\zeta$ in the strip. It does identify where a counterexample would have to live, and it suggests combining zero density estimates for thin strips with lower bounds for $\re\xi'/\xi$ on average.

We tested the lemma at the heights $300$ and $10^5$ with \texttt{high.py}. The script computes the background $v_{0.6}/\pi$ from $\xi'/\xi$, through $\zeta'/\zeta$ and the gamma factor, on a window of width $8$ with step $0.01$, and adds the kernels of a single hypothetical pair of zeros at $\beta+i\gamma_0$ and $1-\beta+i\gamma_0$, with $\gamma_0$ the centre of the window (Figure~\ref{ag:fig:cancel}, middle and right). At height $300$ the background has mean $0.51$ and minimum $0.069$; at height $10^5$ it has mean $1.59$, close to the density of ordinates $\frac1{2\pi}\log\frac{10^5}{2\pi}=1.54$, and minimum $0.34$. Table~\ref{ag:tab:cancel} shows the result. At both heights zeros with $\beta-\alpha\le0.2$ produce negativity and zeros with $\beta-\alpha\ge0.3$ are absorbed. At height $10^5$ the heuristic threshold $2/\log(10^5/2\pi)=0.21$ is close to the observed one, while the rigorous guarantee of Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:cancel} with the window minimum, $1/(\pi m)=0.93$, is conservative. For a fixed width the negative dip is deeper at the greater height, because the kernel is the same while the local minimum of the background near the hypothetical zero differs.

\subsection{Positivity margins and the circular unitary ensemble}\label{ag:sec:margins}

By Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:cancel}, a zero to the right of $\alpha$ at height $\gamma$ can make the density negative only if its distance to the line $\re s=\alpha$ is below $1/(\pi m)$, where $m$ is the local minimum of the rest of the density. The distribution of the local minima, the positivity margins, therefore determines how much room RH has at each height. The minima occur between consecutive ordinates. After unfolding the ordinates to unit mean spacing, $x_n=\vartheta(\gamma_n)/\pi+1$ with $\vartheta$ the Riemann Siegel theta function, as in Section~\ref{sec:ch15:spacings}, the density at the resolution of $\kappa$ mean spacings is
\[
\bar\rho_\kappa(x)=\sum_nP_\kappa(x-x_n)=\sum_n\frac{\kappa}{\pi(\kappa^2+(x-x_n)^2)},
\]
whose mean is one. Section~\ref{sec:ch15:spacings} compared the spacings of the zeros with the random matrix prediction; here the object of comparison is the density itself at the points where it is smallest.

The script \texttt{sens.py} evaluates $\bar\rho_\kappa$ at the $1600$ midpoints between consecutive unfolded ordinates below height $2198$, leaving out $50$ mean spacings at each end, and the same statistic at about $3600$ midpoints between the eigenvalues of twelve Haar distributed unitary matrices of size $400$, unfolded and extended periodically. The circular unitary ensemble (CUE) of these matrices models the local statistics of the zeros. Table~\ref{ag:tab:margin} and Figure~\ref{ag:fig:margins} show the result. The medians agree to $1\%$ at every resolution. In the lower tail the zeros have slightly larger margins than the CUE, $0.1177$ against $0.1095$ at the $1\%$ quantile for $\kappa=0.1$, a finite height effect in the expected direction, since the low zeros are more rigid than the limit of large matrices.

\subsection{Moments of zeta, the Barnes function and zero density}\label{ag:sec:moments}

The strength of Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost} is set by the zero density exponents, which rest on mean value estimates on the critical line, Ingham's on the fourth moment of $\zeta$. The moments of $\zeta$ are also where probabilistic structure of the GGC kind meets the zeros, through random matrix theory. Keating and Snaith \cite{KS00} modelled $\zeta(\frac12+it)$ by the characteristic polynomial $Z_N(\theta)=\det(I-Ae^{-i\theta})$ of a Haar distributed unitary matrix $A$ of size $N$, a matrix of the circular unitary ensemble of Section~\ref{ag:sec:margins}, with $N$ in the role of $\log(T/2\pi)$. By analogy with the exact formula
\[
\E|Z_N(\theta)|^{2k}=\prod_{j=1}^N\frac{\Gamma(j)\Gamma(j+2k)}{\Gamma(j+k)^2}\sim\frac{\mathsf G(1+k)^2}{\mathsf G(1+2k)}\,N^{k^2},\qquad N\to\infty,
\]
they conjectured that the moments of $\zeta$ grow in the same way, with $\log(T/2\pi)$ in place of $N$ and an arithmetic factor $a_k$ in front (Section~\ref{ag:sec:ks}). Here $\mathsf G$ is the Barnes $G$ function, $\mathsf G(1+z)=\Gamma(z)\mathsf G(z)$ with $\mathsf G(1)=1$, written in sans serif to keep $G$ for the clock of Section~\ref{ch:clock}; $Z_N$ is unrelated to the normal variable $Z$ of Section~\ref{ch:strip}. Nikeghbali and Yor \cite{NY09} gave the random matrix factor a probabilistic meaning in GGC language: the Mellin transform of $|Z_N|$ and the Barnes function are expressed through products and sums of independent gamma, beta and log gamma variables, and the reciprocal of $\mathsf G$ has a L\'evy Khintchine type representation. The source of these representations is the decomposition of Bourgade, Hughes, Nikeghbali and Yor \cite{BHNY08},
\[
Z_N\stackrel d=\prod_{j=1}^N\Big(1+e^{i\omega_j}\sqrt{B_{1,j-1}}\Big),
\]
with independent angles $\omega_j$ uniform on $[0,2\pi)$ and independent beta variables $B_{1,j-1}$ with parameters $1$ and $j-1$, where $B_{1,0}=1$.

Table~\ref{ag:tab:ks}, computed by \texttt{killing2.py}, checks the exact moments against direct simulation of Haar unitary matrices and against the beta decomposition; the $\pm$ are one standard error. The exact values, computed by \texttt{rmt\_ratios.py}, show how slowly $\E|Z_N|^{2k}/N^{k^2}$ approaches the Barnes constant: for $k=2$ the ratio is $0.1716$, $0.0902$, $0.0840$ and $0.0834$ at $N=10$, $10^2$, $10^3$ and $10^4$, against $\mathsf G(3)^2/\mathsf G(5)=\frac1{12}$, and for $k=3$ it is $1.18\times10^{-3}$, $1.51\times10^{-4}$, $1.189\times10^{-4}$ and $1.1605\times10^{-4}$, against $\mathsf G(4)^2/\mathsf G(7)=1.1574\times10^{-4}$. The Haar estimates of the fourth moment are unreliable at these sample sizes, $1330\pm103$ against $1716$ at $N=10$, because $|Z_N|^4$ is heavy tailed. The beta decomposition samples the same law more cheaply, so ten times as many samples are drawn, and it agrees with the exact values.

\subsubsection{The Keating and Snaith moments}\label{ag:sec:ks}

Write $I_k(T)=\frac1T\int_0^T|\zeta(\frac12+it)|^{2k}dt$. The conjecture of Keating and Snaith \cite{KS00}, in the form of Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein and Snaith \cite{CFKRS05}, is
\[
I_k(T)\sim a_k\,\frac{\mathsf G(1+k)^2}{\mathsf G(1+2k)}\Big(\log\frac T{2\pi}\Big)^{k^2},\qquad a_k=\prod_p\Big(1-\frac1p\Big)^{k^2}\sum_{m\ge0}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(m+k)}{m!\,\Gamma(k)}\Big)^2p^{-m},
\]
the product of an arithmetic factor from the primes and the random matrix factor. It is a theorem for $k=1$ and $k=2$ and open for $k\ge3$. For $k=1$ Hardy and Littlewood \cite{HL16} proved the leading term, and Ingham \cite{Ingham26} the mean square with its secondary term, $I_1(T)=\log\frac T{2\pi}+2\gamma_E-1+o(1)$; for $k=2$ Ingham \cite{Ingham26} proved the asymptotic with the leading coefficient $\frac1{2\pi^2}$. The local factor of $a_k$ is $(1-\frac1p)^{k^2}{}_2F_1(k,k;1;\frac1p)$. Since ${}_2F_1(1,1;1;x)=(1-x)^{-1}$ and ${}_2F_1(2,2;1;x)=(1+x)(1-x)^{-3}$, it equals $1$ for $k=1$ and $1-p^{-2}$ for $k=2$, so that $a_1=1$ and $a_2=\prod_p(1-p^{-2})=6/\pi^2$ exactly. The Euler product over the primes below $2\times10^5$, with each local factor written through ${}_2F_1$, reproduces these: it gives $a_1=1$ and $a_2=0.6079273$, which agrees with $6/\pi^2=0.6079271$ to six digits, the truncation changing it by a relative $3.8\times10^{-7}$. It gives $a_3=0.0493218$; since the local factor for $k=3$ is $1-9p^{-2}+O(p^{-3})$, the omitted primes lower it by a relative $9\sum_{p>2\times10^5}p^{-2}\approx4\times10^{-6}$, to about $0.0493217$. The script \texttt{ks.py} hard codes these three values, which \texttt{repro/ks\_ak.py} recomputes from the product. With $\mathsf G(3)^2/\mathsf G(5)=\frac1{12}$ and $\mathsf G(4)^2/\mathsf G(7)=\frac1{8640}$ the leading coefficients are $1$, $0.0506606=\frac1{2\pi^2}$, which is Ingham's constant, and $5.709\times10^{-6}$, the value $42a_3/9!$ of \cite{CFKRS05}.

\subsubsection{The moments as exponential moments of the Thorin density}

The densities $v_\sigma$ are the derivatives of $\log|\xi|$ across the basepoints: $\frac\partial{\partial\sigma}\log|\xi(\sigma+it)|=\re\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\sigma+it)=v_\sigma(t)$ wherever $\xi(\sigma+it)\ne0$. Integrating from the critical line to the basepoint one, for every $t$ such that $\xi$ has no zero on the segment from $\frac12+it$ to $1+it$, which excludes only countably many $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{ag:eq:logxi}
\log|\xi(\tfrac12+it)|=\log|\xi(1+it)|-\int_{1/2}^1v_a(t)\,da .
\end{equation}
The moments concern $\zeta$, not $\xi$. With the gamma factor $h(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\frac s2)$, so that $\xi=h\zeta$, the identity becomes
\[
|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^{2k}=|\zeta(1+it)|^{2k}\,\Big|\frac{h(1+it)}{h(\frac12+it)}\Big|^{2k}\exp\Big\{-2k\int_{1/2}^1v_a(t)\,da\Big\},
\]
and Stirling's formula gives $|h(1+it)/h(\frac12+it)|\sim(t/2\pi)^{1/4}$ as $t\to\infty$. So the Keating and Snaith moments are exponential moments, over $t$ uniform on $[0,T]$, of the density $v_a(t)$ integrated across the basepoints from $\frac12$ to $1$, with the weight $|\zeta(1+it)|^{2k}|h(1+it)/h(\frac12+it)|^{2k}$, which involves no zero of $\zeta$. At every such basepoint $v_a/\pi$ is the Thorin density of $T_a$ exactly when RH holds (Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson} and Proposition~\ref{ag:prop:factor}). In the same terms, RH, which is the positivity of $v_a$ for every $a>\frac12$ (Lagarias's criterion, Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline}), is the statement that $|\xi(\sigma+it)|$ increases with $\sigma\ge\frac12$ for every $t$, a reformulation due to Sondow and Dumitrescu \cite{SD10}.

We checked \eqref{ag:eq:logxi} with \texttt{ks.py} at $t=10$, $20$ and $100$, where both sides agree to all fifteen digits computed, $\log|\xi(\frac12+it)|$ being $-3.2710155$, $-10.2139490$ and $-69.3773014$. At $t=14.134725$, the first ordinate to six decimals, the right side is $-22.353$, the logarithm of $|\xi(\frac12+it)|\approx2\times10^{-10}$ at a point within $1.5\times10^{-7}$ of the zero; the script records the left side there as $-\infty$, its value at $\gamma_1$ itself, instead of evaluating it. On the grid $\sigma=\frac12,0.55,\dots,1$ the modulus $|\xi(\sigma+it)|$ increases at all four heights.

In the language of \eqref{ag:eq:logxi} the conjecture of Keating and Snaith is a large deviation statement for the integrated Thorin density. Selberg's central limit theorem \cite{Selberg46}, which Section~\ref{ch:rz4} uses for the argument $S(t)$, says that for $t$ uniform on $[T,2T]$ the variable $\log|\zeta(\frac12+it)|$ is approximately Gaussian with variance $\frac12\log\log T$. Gaussian exponential moments would give $(\log T)^{k^2}$, and the constant $a_k\mathsf G(1+k)^2/\mathsf G(1+2k)$ is the correction to them. On $[500,1000]$ the sample variance of $\log|\zeta(\frac12+it)|$ is $1.53$, against $\frac12\log\log750=0.95$. The difference is a constant term, not slow convergence: the random matrix model predicts the variance $\frac12\log\log\frac t{2\pi}+c+o(1)$ with $c=\frac12\big(\gamma_E+1-\sum_p\sum_{m\ge2}(\frac1m-\frac1{m^2})p^{-m}\big)=0.70$ \cite{KS00}, a conjecture, and this gives $1.48$ on $[500,1000]$, close to the sample value.

\subsubsection{What the moments give}

Moments of $\zeta$ on the critical line control zero density through mean value theorems, and zero density controls the almost GGC property. Ingham's exponent $3(1-\sigma)/(2-\sigma)$, which gives Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost} its saving $(2\alpha-1)/(2-\alpha)$ for $\frac12<\alpha<\frac7{10}$, rests on the fourth moment, $k=2$, proved by Ingham \cite{Ingham26}. Bounds for all moments, that is the Lindel\"of hypothesis in the form $\int_0^T|\zeta(\frac12+it)|^{2k}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}$ for every $k$, imply the density hypothesis $N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{2(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}$, which would give the saving $2\alpha-1$, larger than that of Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost} for $\frac12<\alpha<\frac9{10}$ (Table~\ref{ag:tab:exp}). The Keating and Snaith asymptotics would give this and much more. So the line through the Barnes function is the one place where probabilistic structure of the GGC type bears directly on the strength of the almost GGC theorem.

Two limits should be stated. The GGC structure of Nikeghbali and Yor belongs to the random matrix model, not to $\zeta$: it explains the conjecture without proving it, and only the second and fourth moments of $\zeta$ are known asymptotically. And moments, even the Lindel\"of hypothesis, bound the number of zeros off the line but do not exclude them. They improve ``almost GGC'' without reaching ``GGC''.

\subsection{From almost to exactly}\label{ag:sec:exactly}

Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost} is the strongest statement that the Thorin programme yields with no hypothesis, and in form it is stronger than the positivity at $\alpha\ge1$: it covers every basepoint above the centre. It cannot be upgraded to exact positivity by improving the exponents, or by the moment bounds of Section~\ref{ag:sec:moments}. Even the density hypothesis, or the Lindel\"of hypothesis which implies it, leaves room for a sparse set of zeros off the line, and a single off line zero $\beta+i\gamma$ stops $T_\alpha$ from being a GGC for every $\alpha$ in $[\frac12,\beta)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson}) and makes $v_\alpha$ negative near $\gamma$ for every $\alpha$ close enough to $\beta$ (Section~\ref{ag:sec:cancel}). The step from ``almost'' to ``exactly'' is a step from counting zeros to excluding them.

Four facts about the programme say that this step needs input from outside it. The smoothing runs the wrong way: the Cauchy kernels form a semigroup, $P_a*P_b=P_{a+b}$ for $a,b>0$, so when no zero lies to the right of $\alpha$ the density at a basepoint $\alpha'>\alpha$ is the Poisson smoothing of the density at $\alpha$ by the kernel of width $\alpha'-\alpha$, and positivity propagates to the right; RH asks to propagate it to the left, which is deconvolution, and deconvolution does not preserve positivity. Arithmetic domination requires a zero free region: below the basepoint one the compensated L\'evy form, which the sine squared lemma converts into the density, holds if $\psi(x)-x=O(x^{\alpha'})$ for some $\alpha'<\alpha$, and only if $\psi(x)-x=O(x^{\alpha+\delta})$ for every $\delta>0$, where $\psi$ is Chebyshev's function (Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:below}). Either condition is a zero free half plane, none was known for any $\alpha<1$ before the half plane $\re s>\frac78$ announced in October 2026, which came from outside the programme (Section~\ref{sec:ch25:open}), and removing the pole does not help: for $L(s,\chi_{12})$ the prime sums need no compensation, and the boundary is the same (Section~\ref{bp:eta:sec:below}). There is no margin: along the de Bruijn Newman flow the reciprocal is a GGC for $t\ge0.2$ \cite{PT21}, which improves $t\ge0.22$ \cite{Polymath} (Theorem~\ref{rz:thm:dbn} and Section~\ref{ch:dbn}), RH is the statement at $t=0$, and the de Bruijn Newman constant is nonnegative \cite{RodgersTao}, so if RH is true it holds with no slack and a proof must establish a closed condition at the boundary itself. And stronger positivity conditions can be false: Conrey and Li showed that a positivity condition in de Branges' approach to RH fails \cite{CL00}, so any positivity statement stronger than RH should be tested numerically before it is pursued (Section~\ref{st:sec:wp5}).

In the Stieltjes form (iv) of Theorem~\ref{bh:thm:main} the difference between counting zeros and excluding them is explicit. Split $G'/G=S_{\rm on}+R_{\rm off}$, where $S_{\rm on}(u)=\sum(u+\gamma^2)^{-1}$ runs over the zeros $\frac12+i\gamma$ on the line with $\gamma>0$ and is a Stieltjes function with measure $U_{\rm line}$, and $R_{\rm off}(u)=\sum(u-(\rho-\frac12)^2)^{-1}$ runs over the zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ off the line with $\gamma>0$. By Theorem~\ref{bs:thm:local}, all the poles of $R_{\rm off}$ lie in a parabolic sliver $\mathcal E_{T_0}$ beside the negative axis, which begins at $-T_0^2+T_0+\frac14$, and in the upper half plane outside it $\im R_{\rm off}\le0$ and $\im(G'/G)<0$. The size of $R_{\rm off}$ away from the sliver needs no count of zeros: if $|u|\le T_0^2/2$, a disc that misses $\mathcal E_{T_0}$, then every term has $|u-(\rho-\frac12)^2|\ge|\rho-\frac12|^2-|u|>\gamma^2/2$, since $\gamma>T_0$, so $|R_{\rm off}(u)|\le2\Sigma_0<3\times10^{-12}$ by Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:explicit}. Only $\sum\gamma^{-2}<\infty$ and the verified height enter.

The number of terms of $R_{\rm off}$, on the other hand, is not known to be small. Zero density estimates bound the number of those with $|\beta-\frac12|\ge\sigma-\frac12$ and $\gamma\le T$ by $T^{e(\sigma)+o(1)}$ with $e(\sigma)<1$, for each fixed $\sigma>\frac12$ (Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost}), which is $o(T)$; but zeros off the line may approach the line as $\gamma$ grows, and for all of them together only a proportion is known. At most $(1-\kappa+o(1))N(T)$ of the zeros up to height $T$ lie off the line, with $\kappa>\frac5{12}$ in the last published record \cite{PRZZ20} and $\kappa>0.6725$, counting only simple zeros on the line, in two preprints of 2026 \cite{AF26,Lam26}; that is not $o(T)$, nor even $o(N(T))$. No count, however strong, touches the Thorin condition, which requires $R_{\rm off}\equiv0$, because a single pair puts a pole of $G'/G$ off the cut. Section~\ref{pk:sec:filter} gives a test for arguments that claim to exclude such poles: since the de Bruijn Newman flow has nonreal zeros at every $t<0$ \cite{RodgersTao}, no property that the kernel $e^{tv^2/4}\Phi(v)$ keeps for some $t<0$ can exclude them, so a proof must use one that fails for every $t<0$, and the Euler product and the explicit formula are the natural candidates.

The forms that the new input could take are those of Section~\ref{ch:path}: a positive object built from arithmetic, such as a Krein string or a diffusion whose hitting time is the clock, which is the idea of Hilbert and P\'olya in probabilistic form, with Williams' Bessel process (Section~\ref{ch:boundaries}) as the model; positive approximants, proved positive independently, converging under the closure theorem; or a direct variogram inequality at the centre. The first new theorem any of them would give is the GGC property of $T_\alpha$ at a single basepoint $\alpha<\frac78$, that is $v_\alpha^-\equiv0$, a zero free half plane beyond the one announced in 2026 (Section~\ref{sec:ch25:open}); positivity of $v_\alpha$ on the single line $\re s=\alpha$ is not known to suffice (Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline}).

Sections~\ref{ag:sec:factor} and \ref{ag:sec:cancel} make the remaining problem concrete. The growth of the defect is not part of it. For $u\ge1$ every factor of $D_\alpha$ is at most one, and a factor with $\gamma\le\sqrt u$ is at least $\gamma^2/(u+\gamma^2)\ge\gamma^2/(2u)\ge1/u$, since $\gamma\ge\gamma_1>\sqrt2$. Bounding the factors with $\gamma>\sqrt u$ as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:explicit} therefore gives, for $u\ge1$,
\begin{align*}
|\log D_\alpha(u)|&\le N(\alpha,\sqrt u)\log u+2(u+\sqrt u)\sum_{\beta>\alpha,\ \gamma>\sqrt u}\gamma^{-2}\\
&\le u^{e(\alpha)/2+o(1)},\qquad u\to\infty,
\end{align*}
by the exponents of Theorem~\ref{ag:thm:almost}. So bounds on the growth of the defect follow from zero density estimates alone, and what remains open is whether it is identically one. Four questions would each be a genuine step.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Cancellation on average: show that for fixed $\alpha>\frac12$ the background density $v_\alpha/\pi$ is bounded below by $c\log\gamma$ outside a set of heights of density zero. With Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:cancel} this would confine any failure of positivity to zeros in the strips $\alpha<\beta<\alpha+O(1/\log\gamma)$. Remark~\ref{rem:dbeta:average} proves the lower bound for $v_\alpha/\pi$ itself, but its exceptional set contains a neighbourhood of the ordinate of every zero to the right of $\alpha$, which is where Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:cancel} would need it.
\item Thin strip density: bound the number of zeros with $\alpha<\beta<\alpha+2/\log\gamma$ and $\gamma\le T$ by $o(T^{1-\delta})$ for some $\delta>0$, uniformly in $\alpha$. Combined with (1) this would give a pointwise almost positivity theorem.
\item Explicit defect: bound $\log D_\alpha(u)$ explicitly, with constants depending on $\alpha$ and the verified height, through explicit zero density estimates, as described at the end of Section~\ref{ag:sec:factor}.
\item Positivity at one basepoint: prove $D_\alpha\equiv1$, that is $v_\alpha^-\equiv0$, for a single $\alpha<\frac78$, which is a zero free half plane beyond the one announced in 2026 (Section~\ref{sec:ch25:open}).
\end{enumerate}
What the Thorin framework contributes is a precise target, the GGC property of one explicit clock $T_\alpha$ at one basepoint below $\frac78$, together with computational tools that test candidate arguments against it: certified densities on windows, positivity margins calibrated against random matrix theory, and the explicit defect. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusions} takes stock of the whole path, beginning with Section~\ref{ch:path}.
