\chapter{Introduction and foundations}\label{chap:foundations}

The book rests on one equivalence, between the Riemann Hypothesis and the positivity of the Thorin measure of a function built from the completed zeta function. Section~\ref{ch:intro} states it as the Thorin condition, explains why computation can measure and calibrate that positivity but cannot prove it, and gives the plan of the book, its principal results with their status, and its conventions. Sections~\ref{ch:clock} and \ref{ch:ggc} set up the objects and the language on which the later chapters build. The first introduces the P\'olya density, the clock and its heat trace, and the Thorin density at the basepoint $\frac12+\varepsilon$; the second treats, from a computational point of view, the generalised gamma convolutions, the class of laws that the Thorin condition asks the clock to enter.
