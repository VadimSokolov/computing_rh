\section{The ladder and the quasi Riemann abscissa}\label{mi:sec:quasi}

The ladder $F_b$ of Section~\ref{mi:sec:ladder} is the opposite symmetrisation: $F_b=\cos(b\partial)\Xi$ while $g_b=\sec(b\partial)\Xi$, and in the Mellin variable $F_b(z)=\frac14\E[\Upsilon^s(\Upsilon^b+\Upsilon^{-b})]$ with $s=\frac12+iz$, the Rademacher Esscher weight in place of the logistic coin. On $\R$, $F_b(x)=\re\xi(\alpha+ix)$ with $\alpha=\frac12+b$. Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:ladder} is due to de Bruijn and Lagarias, who also showed that on $\RH$ all rungs $b>0$ are real rooted (Theorem~\ref{pol:thm:cosh}). Let $\sigma_{\max}=\sup\{\re\rho\}$ over the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$, and let $\gamma_k(b)$ be as in Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:ladder}.

\begin{proposition}\label{mi:prop:BTheta}
$B_{\cosh}\le\sigma_{\max}-\frac12$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The zeros of $\Xi$ satisfy $|\im t|\le\Delta=\sigma_{\max}-\frac12$. De Bruijn's strip theorem with $\Delta$ in place of $\frac12$ in the proof of Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:ladder} shows that the zeros of $F_b$ satisfy $|\im z|\le(\max(\Delta^2-b^2,0))^{1/2}$, so $F_b$ is real rooted for $b\ge\Delta$ (Theorem~\ref{pol:thm:cosh}(ii)).
\end{proof}

Numerically, below height $100$ the number of real zeros of $\re\xi(\alpha+it)$ is $29=N(100)$ for every $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.55,0.6,0.7,0.8,1,1.25,1.5\}$ tested, and the first twelve Thorin atoms $\gamma_k(b)$, which move continuously where the zeros are simple, decrease strictly in $b$ on the grid $\alpha=0.50,0.51,\dots,1.00$ (Figure~\ref{mi:fig:path}); the smallest gap $\gamma_{k+1}-\gamma_k$ is $1.77$.

Conversely, at height $x$ and $z=x+iy$ with $y>0$, Stirling and the functional equation give
\[
\frac{|\Xi(z+ib)|}{|\Xi(z-ib)|}\approx\Big(\frac x{2\pi}\Big)^{y}\frac{|\zeta(\alpha+y+ix)|}{|\zeta(\alpha-y+ix)|},
\]
so the two terms of $F_b$ can cancel off the axis only near zeros of $\zeta$ with $\beta\approx\alpha+y$, and, heuristically, Rouch\'e near a simple zero with $\beta>\alpha$ produces a zero of $F_b$ near $\gamma+i(\beta-\alpha)$. The cosh shift moves the effective abscissa by the fixed amount $b$; the heat flow, in the Rodgers Tao approximation, moves it by $\lambda\log(x/2\pi)$, which clears every zero at large height and is why Ki, Kim and Lee \cite{KKL09} have only finitely many nonreal zeros for every $\lambda>0$ and the Polymath bound \cite{Polymath} is reachable.

\begin{conjecture}\label{mi:conj:quasi}
Let $\sigma_{\max}^\infty=\limsup_{\gamma\to\infty}\beta$. Then $\sigma_{\max}^\infty-\frac12\le B_{\cosh}\le\sigma_{\max}-\frac12$. The upper bound is Proposition~\ref{mi:prop:BTheta}; the lower bound is conjectured. Under the conjecture, lowering the unconditional bound $B_{\cosh}\le\frac12$ is equivalent to a quasi Riemann hypothesis whenever $\sigma_{\max}$ is approached at large height.
\end{conjecture}

We test the mechanism on a function with genuine off line zeros. Let $\chi$ be the character modulo $5$ with $\chi(2)=i$ and $\kappa=(\sqrt{10-2\sqrt5}-2)/(\sqrt5-1)=0.2840790438$. The Davenport Heilbronn function \cite{DH36} is $f(s)=\frac{1-i\kappa}2L(s,\chi)+\frac{1+i\kappa}2L(s,\bar\chi)$, completed as $\Lambda(s)=(5/\pi)^{s/2}\Gamma(\frac{s+1}2)f(s)$; it satisfies $\Lambda(s)=\Lambda(1-s)$ (to $2.4\times10^{-29}$ at $30$ digits) and is real on the critical line, and its first zero off the line is $0.80851718246+85.69934848538i$. The ladder built from $\Lambda$ has a nonreal pair above this zero for $\alpha$ up to about $0.798$, slightly below $\beta$. The imaginary part of the pair is $0.2893$, $0.2244$, $0.1019$, $0.0685$, $0.0425$ at $\alpha=0.6$, $0.7$, $0.78$, $0.79$, $0.795$, and its square falls almost linearly near the collision (the slopes of the square are $-0.33$, $-0.50$, $-0.57$, $-0.57$ between consecutive values). The pair collides on the axis at $\alpha=0.798095$, $x=85.6255$, and is real for $\alpha\ge0.80$, with the total count of zeros in the box $[83,88.5]\times[-0.9,0.9]$ conserved at $4$. The small early collision is a finite height effect, since $(x/2\pi)^y$ is only $13.6^y$ at height $85$; it is the reason for $\sigma_{\max}^\infty$ rather than $\sigma_{\max}$ in the lower bound.

\subsection{An exact paired form}\label{mi:sec:paired}

The ladder uses only the real part of $\xi$ on the vertical line; pairing it with the imaginary part gives an exact statement. For $\alpha>\frac12$ put $E_\alpha(z)=\xi(\alpha-iz)$, $A_\alpha=\frac12(E_\alpha+E_\alpha^\#)$ and $B_\alpha=\frac i2(E_\alpha-E_\alpha^\#)$, with $E^\#(z)=\overline{E(\bar z)}$, so that $A_\alpha(t)=\re\xi(\alpha+it)=F_{\alpha-1/2}(t)$ and $B_\alpha(t)=\im\xi(\alpha+it)$ for real $t$, and $E_\alpha=A_\alpha-iB_\alpha$. Here $A_\alpha$ is the even part of Section~\ref{pol:sec:cosh} evaluated at $iz$.

\begin{theorem}\label{mi:thm:paired}
For $\alpha>\frac12$ the following are equivalent: (i) $\zeta(s)\ne0$ for $\re s\ge\alpha$; (ii) $E_\alpha$ is Hermite Biehler, that is $|E_\alpha(z)|>|E_\alpha^\#(z)|$ for $\im z>0$; (iii) $\re\xi(\alpha+it)$ and $\im\xi(\alpha+it)$, as entire functions of $t$, have only real simple zeros, and these strictly interlace. Hence the paired bottom rung, the infimum of the $\alpha-\frac12$ for which these hold, is exactly $\sigma_{\max}-\frac12$, and $\RH$ holds if and only if (iii) holds for every $\alpha>\frac12$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): for $z=x+iy$ with $y>0$ we have $E_\alpha(z)=\xi(\alpha+y-ix)$ and $E_\alpha^\#(z)=\xi(\alpha-y+ix)$. Let $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ run over the zeros of $\xi$, a set symmetric under $\rho\mapsto\bar\rho$. By the Hadamard product, pairing $\rho$ with $\bar\rho$,
\[
\frac{|E_\alpha(z)|^2}{|E_\alpha^\#(z)|^2}=\prod_\rho\frac{(\alpha+y-\beta)^2+(x-\gamma)^2}{(\alpha-y-\beta)^2+(x-\gamma)^2}=\prod_\rho\Big(1+\frac{4y(\alpha-\beta)}{|\alpha-y+ix-\rho|^2}\Big),
\]
and every factor exceeds $1$ when $\beta<\alpha$. The product converges absolutely because $\sum\gamma^{-2}<\infty$, and $E_\alpha$ has no real zeros since no $\beta$ equals $\alpha$. Hence $|E_\alpha|>|E_\alpha^\#|$ on the upper half plane. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii) is the Hermite Biehler theorem in de Branges's form: if $E=A-iB$ is Hermite Biehler, then $A$ and $B$ are real entire with only real simple zeros that strictly interlace. (iii)$\Rightarrow$(i): $A_\alpha$ and $B_\alpha$ are real entire of order one with only real zeros, hence of genus at most one; $A_\alpha$ is even and $B_\alpha$ is odd, so their Hadamard products carry no factor $e^{az}$: $A_\alpha(z)=\xi(\alpha)\prod_k(1-z^2/a_k^2)$ and $B_\alpha(z)=\xi'(\alpha)z\prod_k(1-z^2/b_k^2)$ (without the parity the step fails, since interlacing alone allows an extra factor $e^{az}$). For strictly interlacing zeros the quotient of these products is, up to sign, a Pick function by Chebotarev's theorem \cite{Chebotarev28}. Since $B_\alpha/A_\alpha\sim(\xi'(\alpha)/\xi(\alpha))z$ at $0$, with $\xi(\alpha)>0$ and $\xi'(\alpha)>0$ ($\xi'(\alpha)>0$ holds unconditionally for $\alpha>\frac12$, because $\xi'/\xi(\alpha)=\sum_\rho(\alpha-\rho)^{-1}$ is real and each pair $\rho$, $1-\rho$ contributes $\re\big[(\alpha-\rho)^{-1}+(\alpha-1+\rho)^{-1}\big]=(2\alpha-1)(xy+\gamma^2)/((x^2+\gamma^2)(y^2+\gamma^2))>0$, with $x=\alpha-\beta$, $y=\alpha-1+\beta$ and $xy>-\frac14>-\gamma^2$), the sign is $+$: $B_\alpha/A_\alpha$ is a Pick function and $\im(B_\alpha/A_\alpha)>0$ on the upper half plane. The identity $|E_\alpha^\#|^2-|E_\alpha|^2=-4|A_\alpha|^2\im(B_\alpha/A_\alpha)$ then gives $|E_\alpha|>|E_\alpha^\#|\ge0$ on the upper half plane, and $E_\alpha\ne0$ on $\R$ because the zeros of $A_\alpha$ and $B_\alpha$ are disjoint. So $E_\alpha(z)=\xi(\alpha-iz)$ has no zeros in the closed upper half plane; since $E_\alpha(z)=0$ at $z=-\gamma+i(\beta-\alpha)$, this says $\beta<\alpha$ for every zero. (The other sign is excluded by Hardy: $E_\alpha^\#(z)=\xi(\alpha+iz)$ vanishes at $\gamma+i(\alpha-\frac12)$ for every zero on the line, so it is not Hermite Biehler.) The last two statements follow.
\end{proof}

Its Thorin form is the following. Since $A_\alpha$ is even with $A_\alpha(0)=\xi(\alpha)>0$ and $B_\alpha$ is odd with $B_\alpha'(0)=\xi'(\alpha)>0$,
\[
\frac{2\xi(\alpha)}{\xi(\alpha+\sqrt s)+\xi(\alpha-\sqrt s)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2\sqrt s\,\xi'(\alpha)}{\xi(\alpha+\sqrt s)-\xi(\alpha-\sqrt s)}
\]
(these are $\xi(\alpha)/A_\alpha(i\sqrt s)$ and $B_\alpha'(0)/(B_\alpha(z)/z)$ at $z=i\sqrt s$) are Laplace transforms of generalised gamma convolutions with strictly interlacing Thorin atoms exactly when $\zeta$ has no zeros with $\re s\ge\alpha$. Call two such generalised gamma convolutions, whose Thorin atoms $a_k^2$ and $b_k^2$ strictly interlace, a Thorin pair: the even and odd parts of $\xi$ about $\alpha$ form a Thorin pair precisely to the right of every zero. For $\zeta$ the interlacing holds on $[0,100]$ at $\alpha=0.55$, $0.6$, $0.7$, $1$ (and $0.8$, $1.25$, $1.5$), with $29$ positive zeros of each function and smallest distance between a zero of $A_\alpha$ and one of $B_\alpha$ equal to $0.50$, $0.51$, $0.54$, $0.69$ for $\alpha=0.55$, $0.6$, $0.7$, $1$. For Davenport Heilbronn the criterion is sharp where the ladder alone is not: at $\alpha=0.800$ and $0.805$ the real part already has only real zeros on $(83,88.5)$, yet the zeros of the real (A) and imaginary (B) parts come in the order ABAABBAB, while at $\alpha=0.82$ and $0.85$ the order is ABABABAB.

\subsection{Density one by phase counting}\label{mi:sec:density}

Let $\theta_\alpha(t)=\arg\xi(\alpha+it)$; for $\alpha>\sigma_{\max}$ this is $\pi$ times the clock $U_\alpha$ of Proposition~\ref{pol:prop:round}. Since $\re(\xi'/\xi)(s)=\sum_\rho\re(s-\rho)^{-1}$ (Hadamard's product; the same identity is used in the proof of Proposition~\ref{pol:prop:round}),
\[
\theta_\alpha'(t)=\sum_\rho\frac{\alpha-\beta}{(\alpha-\beta)^2+(t-\gamma)^2},
\]
a sum of Cauchy bumps of mass $+\pi$ for $\beta<\alpha$ and $-\pi$ for $\beta>\alpha$. Write $N^\pm(T)$ for the number of zeros with $|\gamma|\le T$ (so $N^\pm=2N$ with the usual $N$ counting $0<\gamma\le T$) and $N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T)$ for those with $\beta>\alpha$.

\begin{theorem}\label{mi:thm:density}
Let $\alpha>\frac12$ with $\beta\ne\alpha$ for every zero. Then $\theta_\alpha(T)-\theta_\alpha(-T)=\pi(N^\pm(T)-2N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T))+O(\log T)$. Hence $\re\xi(\alpha+it)$ and $\im\xi(\alpha+it)$ each have at least $N^\pm(T)-2N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T)-O(\log T)$ real zeros in $[-T,T]$. If moreover $F_b$, $b=\alpha-\frac12$, satisfies the Riemann von Mangoldt formula with error $O(\log T)$, its nonreal zeros with $|\re z|\le T$ number at most $2N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T)+O(\log T)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Integrating $\theta_\alpha'$ over $[-T,T]$, a zero $\rho$ with $|\gamma|\le T$ contributes $\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha-\beta)\,(\arctan\frac{T-\gamma}{|\alpha-\beta|}+\arctan\frac{T+\gamma}{|\alpha-\beta|})$, which is $\pm\pi$ up to a remainder of size $|\alpha-\beta|/(T-|\gamma|)$, and a zero with $|\gamma|>T$ contributes a remainder of size $|\alpha-\beta|/(|\gamma|-T)$, of the opposite sign. Zeros within distance $1$ of $\pm T$ number $O(\log T)$ and contribute $O(\log T)$ in all. The remaining remainders sum to $O(\log^2T)$ if estimated separately; pair $\rho$ with $1-\rho$, which has the same ordinate and combined weight $(\alpha-\beta)+(\alpha-1+\beta)=2\alpha-1$, independent of $\beta$. Up to $O(\log T)$ the remainders are then $(2\alpha-1)\sum_\gamma\operatorname{sgn}(|\gamma|-T)\big(\frac1{||\gamma|-T|}-\frac1{|\gamma|+T}\big)$, and partial summation with $N^\pm(t)=\frac t\pi\log\frac t{2\pi e}+O(\log t)$ shows that the contributions from below and from above $T$ cancel to $O(\log T)$. This proves the first formula. A continuous phase $\theta_\alpha$ with total change $\Delta$ crosses the lattice $\pi\mathbb Z$ (zeros of $\im\xi(\alpha+it)$) and the lattice $\pi\mathbb Z+\frac\pi2$ (zeros of $\re\xi(\alpha+it)$) at least $|\Delta|/\pi-1$ times each, with $\Delta=\theta_\alpha(T)-\theta_\alpha(-T)$. Under the Riemann von Mangoldt formula $F_b$ has $N^\pm(T)+O(\log T)$ zeros with $|\re z|\le T$, of which at least $N^\pm(T)-2N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T)-O(\log T)$ are real.
\end{proof}

With Ingham's estimate \cite{Ingham40}, $N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T)=O(T^{3(1-\alpha)/(2-\alpha)}\log^5T)=o(T)$ for every $\alpha=\frac12+b>\frac12$, since $3(1-\alpha)/(2-\alpha)<1$. So, unconditionally, $\re\xi(\alpha+it)$ has at least $N^\pm(T)-o(T)$ real zeros in $[-T,T]$, against $N^\pm(T)\sim\frac T\pi\log\frac T{2\pi}$. If the rung $F_b$ satisfies the Riemann von Mangoldt formula with error $O(\log T)$, which is open (Problem~\ref{mi:prob:rvm}), almost all zeros of $\frac12(\xi(s+b)+\xi(s-b))$ lie on the critical line, and as a count the number of conjugate pairs of nonreal zeros up to height $T$ is at most $N^\pm_{>\alpha}(T)+O(\log T)$. For the Davenport Heilbronn function, whose zeros off the line below height $200$ have $\beta=0.8085$, $0.6508$, $0.5744$, $0.7243$ (computed in $0.52\le\re s\le1.5$, $0.1\le\im s\le200$, at heights $85.70$, $114.16$, $166.48$, $176.70$), the real part of $\Lambda(\alpha+it)$ has $130$, $130$, $126$, $124$ real zeros on $(0.5,200)$ at $\alpha=0.95$, $0.85$, $0.7$, $0.6$, where $0$, $0$, $2$, $3$ of those zeros lie to the right of $\alpha$: the deficits $4$ and $6$ are two real zeros per zero to the right, as the count predicts. The nonreal zeros of $F_b$ were not counted directly, so that each such zero costs one conjugate pair is established only at the level of these counts. For $\zeta$ itself, the error $\theta_\alpha(T)-\theta_\alpha(-T)-\pi N^\pm(T)$ at $T=20,40,60,80,100$ stays below $1.6$ for $\alpha=0.6$, $0.75$, $1$ (computed).

\subsection{Interlacing across rungs}\label{mi:sec:interlace}

This subsection is based on \cite{P26pg10}. Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:paired} interlaces $A_\alpha$ with $B_\alpha$ on one rung; for $\alpha>1$ the Euler product compares different rungs. Normalise $\theta_\alpha(0)=0$, write $M(\sigma)=-\zeta'(\sigma)/\zeta(\sigma)=\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}$ for $\sigma>1$, so that $|(\zeta'/\zeta)(\sigma+it)|\le M(\sigma)$, and take $\arg\zeta(\sigma+it)=\im\log\zeta(\sigma+it)$ with $\log\zeta(s)=\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-s}/\log n$.

\begin{proposition}\label{mi:prop:pairs}
Let $1<\alpha<\alpha'$, $h=\alpha'-\alpha$, $D=\theta_{\alpha'}-\theta_\alpha$,
\[
\mathcal I=\int_\alpha^{\alpha'}M(\sigma)\,d\sigma=\log\frac{\zeta(\alpha)}{\zeta(\alpha')},\qquad J=\sum_p\big(\arctan p^{-\alpha}-\arctan p^{-\alpha'}\big)\le\mathcal I .
\]
Then $D(t)=\frac{h\pi}4+\arg\zeta(\alpha'+it)-\arg\zeta(\alpha+it)+O(1/t)$, so $|D(t)-\frac{h\pi}4|\le\mathcal I+O(1/t)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $[\frac{h\pi}4-\mathcal I,\frac{h\pi}4+\mathcal I]\subset(k\pi,(k+1)\pi)$ for an integer $k$, the zeros of $A_\alpha$ and $A_{\alpha'}$ alternate for large $t$, and so do those of $B_\alpha$ and $B_{\alpha'}$.
\item[(ii)] If $[\frac{h\pi}4-\mathcal I,\frac{h\pi}4+\mathcal I]\subset(k\pi-\frac\pi2,k\pi+\frac\pi2)$ for an integer $k$, the zeros of $A_\alpha$ and $B_{\alpha'}$ alternate for large $t$, and so do those of $B_\alpha$ and $A_{\alpha'}$.
\item[(iii)] If a multiple of $\pi$ lies in $(\frac{h\pi}4-J,\frac{h\pi}4+J)$, the zeros of $A_\alpha$ and $A_{\alpha'}$ fail to alternate at arbitrarily large $t$; if an odd multiple of $\frac\pi2$ does, so do those of $A_\alpha$ and $B_{\alpha'}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $\alpha>1$, $\xi(\alpha+it)\ne0$ and $\theta_\alpha'>0$ by the display before Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:density}, so $A_\alpha=|\xi|\cos\theta_\alpha$ and $B_\alpha=|\xi|\sin\theta_\alpha$ vanish exactly where $\theta_\alpha$ crosses $\frac\pi2+\pi\mathbb Z$ and $\pi\mathbb Z$. $D(t)$ is the integral of $\im(\xi'/\xi)(\sigma+it)$ over $\alpha\le\sigma\le\alpha'$; by Stirling's formula the factor $\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\frac s2)$ contributes $\frac{h\pi}4+O(1/t)$, and $|\int_\alpha^{\alpha'}\im(\zeta'/\zeta)(\sigma+it)\,d\sigma|\le\mathcal I$. Also $J\le\mathcal I$ termwise: $\arctan x-\arctan y\le\log\frac{1-y}{1-x}$ for $0\le y\le x<1$, since $\frac1{1+u^2}\le\frac1{1-u}$, and with $x=p^{-\alpha}$, $y=p^{-\alpha'}$ the right sides sum to $\mathcal I$. Let $n_1$ and $n_2$ count the zeros in $(0,t]$ of the two functions compared, with phases $\phi_1=\theta_\alpha$ and $\phi_2=\theta_{\alpha'}$ in (i), $\phi_2=\theta_{\alpha'}-\frac\pi2$ in (ii). Under (i) or (ii), $\phi_2-\phi_1$ stays in one interval $(k'\pi,(k'+1)\pi)$ for $t\ge t_0$, so $n_2-n_1$ takes only two adjacent values there, which is alternation. For (iii), $\arg\zeta(\alpha'+it)-\arg\zeta(\alpha+it)=\sum_p\{\arg(1-p^{-\alpha}w_p)-\arg(1-p^{-\alpha'}w_p)\}$ with $w_p=p^{-it}$, a sum that converges absolutely and uniformly. By Kronecker's theorem \cite[Chapter 23]{boHardyWright} for the numbers $\log p$ it comes within any $\varepsilon>0$ of its value $J$ at $w_p=-i$ for all $p$, and of its value $-J$ at $w_p=i$, at arbitrarily large $t$, and $D$ is uniformly continuous, since $|D'|\le M(\alpha)+M(\alpha')+o(1)$. So at arbitrarily large $t$ there are intervals of a fixed length on which $\phi_2-\phi_1$ lies below the critical multiple of $\pi$ by $\varepsilon$, followed by intervals on which it lies above it by $\varepsilon$. Since $\phi_1$ advances by about $\frac12\log\frac t{2\pi}$ per unit of $t$, $n_2-n_1$ takes some value $m-1$ on the first and $m+1$ on the second; between the last visit to $m-1$ and the first visit to $m+1$ come two zeros of one function with no zero of the other between them. The pairs $(B_\alpha,B_{\alpha'})$ and $(B_\alpha,A_{\alpha'})$ are treated in the same way, with the phases $\theta_\alpha-\frac\pi2$ and $\theta_{\alpha'}-\frac\pi2$ or $\theta_{\alpha'}$.
\end{proof}

For $\alpha=5$, $\alpha'=11$, $\frac{h\pi}4=\frac{3\pi}2$ and $\mathcal I=0.035768<\frac\pi2$, so $A_5$ and $A_{11}$ alternate for large $t$ by (i), while $A_5$ and $B_{11}$ fail to alternate at arbitrarily large $t$ by (iii), which applies because $\frac{h\pi}4=\frac{3\pi}2$ is itself an odd multiple of $\frac\pi2$ and $J>0$; no failure occurs below $t=1000$ (computed). For $\alpha=2$, $\alpha'=3.9$, $\frac{h\pi}4=\frac\pi2-\frac\pi{40}$ and $J\ge\arctan\frac14-\arctan2^{-3.9}=0.17809>\frac\pi{40}$, so $A_2$ and $B_{3.9}$ fail to alternate at arbitrarily large $t$; in $(0,100]$ they have $29$ and $29$ zeros, which alternate up to height $71.518$, and the first failure is the zero $74.163$ of $B_{3.9}$, the second of two consecutive zeros of $B_{3.9}$ with no zero of $A_2$ between them (computed on a grid, not certified). For $\alpha=2$, (i) applies for $0<h<3.4004$ with $k=0$ and for $4.6198<h<7.3682$ with $k=1$, and (ii) for $0<h<1.5161$ with $k=0$ and for $2.5695<h<5.3744$ with $k=1$ (certified). The lower condition of (i) with $k=0$, $\mathcal I<\frac{h\pi}4$, holds for every $h$ once $\alpha\ge\sigma_1=1.80732$, where $M(\sigma_1)=\frac\pi4$.

\begin{proposition}\label{mi:prop:noninter}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For $\alpha>1$, $A_\alpha$ has $N(T)-S(T)+O_\alpha(1)$ zeros in $(0,T]$, where $N(T)=\frac{\vartheta(T)}\pi+1+S(T)$ counts the zeros of $\Xi$ with $0<\gamma\le T$. Hence the zeros of $A_\alpha$ do not alternate with the real zeros of $\Xi$ for large $t$.
\item[(ii)] For $a>0$, $\re K_{\frac94+\frac{it}2}(a)$ has $\frac T{2\pi}(\log\frac Ta-1)+O_a(1)$ zeros in $(0,T]$. Hence two of these functions with different $a$ do not alternate for large $t$, and none alternates with the real zeros of $\Xi$ for large $t$ if $a\le2\pi$, or, under the Riemann hypothesis, if $a>2\pi$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Zero sets that alternate for $t\ge t_0$ have counting functions that differ by $O(1)$. (i) By the proof of Proposition~\ref{mi:prop:pairs}, $\theta_\alpha(t)=\vartheta(t)+\pi+(\alpha-\frac12)\frac\pi4+\arg\zeta(\alpha+it)+O(1/t)$ is increasing, $|\arg\zeta(\alpha+it)|\le\log\zeta(\alpha)$, and $A_\alpha$ vanishes where $\theta_\alpha$ crosses $\frac\pi2+\pi\mathbb Z$; this gives the count. Alternation with the real zeros of $\Xi$, at most $N(T)$ in number, would give $S(T)\ge-O(1)$, but $S$ is unbounded in both directions \cite{Selberg46}. (ii) For $\nu=\frac94+\frac{it}2$, $K_\nu(a)=\frac\pi2(I_{-\nu}(a)-I_\nu(a))/\sin\nu\pi=\frac12\Gamma(\nu)(\frac a2)^{-\nu}(1+O_a(1/t))$, so, as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{pol:prop:gram}, the zeros for large $t$ are the crossings of $\frac\pi2+\pi\mathbb Z$ by an increasing function $\arg\Gamma(\nu)-\frac t2\log\frac a2+O_a(1/t)=\frac t2\log\frac ta-\frac t2+\frac{7\pi}8+O_a(1/t)$. The counts of two members differ by $\frac T{2\pi}\log\frac{a'}a+O(1)$. Against $\Xi$: for $a<2\pi$ the count exceeds $N(T)$ by $\frac T{2\pi}\log\frac{2\pi}a+O(\log T)$; for $a=2\pi$ it is $N(T)-S(T)+O(1)$ and the argument of (i) applies; for $a>2\pi$ it falls short of $N(T)$ by $\frac T{2\pi}\log\frac a{2\pi}+O(\log T)$, which excludes alternation when all zeros of $\Xi$ are real.
\end{proof}

For $a=2\pi$ the function is $\Xi^*/(8\pi^2)$ of Proposition~\ref{pol:prop:bessel}, whose zeros follow the half Gram points of Proposition~\ref{pol:prop:gram}.

\subsection{Universal factors as vertical averages}\label{mi:sec:vertical}

Every universal factor acts as a vertical average, $\int_\R\Phi(u)\E\cosh(uX)e^{izu}\,du=\E\,\Xi(z+iX)$: the ladder is $X=\pm b$ and the de Bruijn Newman flow is $X=\sqrt{2\lambda}\,\mathcal N$ with $\mathcal N$ standard normal. Since $|\Xi(x+iu)|$ grows like $(x/2\pi)^{|u|/2}$, the average is dominated near the Esscher tilt location
\[
u^*(L)=\arg\max_u\big[\tfrac L2u+\log p_X(u)\big],\qquad L=\log\frac x{2\pi},
\]
which is $\lambda L$ for the Gaussian, Rodgers and Tao's effective abscissa, and $\operatorname{ess\,sup}X$ for bounded $X$. Below the asymptotic regime $\lambda L^2\gg1$ both flows obey the local model of a pair at $\pm i\eta$, $\eta=\beta-\frac12$: on $z^2+\eta^2$, $\cos(b\partial)$ gives $z^2+\eta^2-b^2$ and $e^{-\lambda\partial^2}$ gives $z^2+\eta^2-2\lambda$, so the model predicts $b_c=\eta$ and $\lambda_c=\eta^2/2$. \begin{remark}[Mixtures of rungs]\label{mi:rem:mixtures}
For symmetric $X$, the vertical average $\E\,\Xi(z+iX)=\E F_{|X|}(z)$ is a positive mixture of rungs, and when $\E\cosh(uX)=\prod_k\cosh(\lambda_ku)$, so that $\E X^2=\sum_k\lambda_k^2$, Proposition~\ref{fm:prop:bruijn}, de Bruijn's theorem, puts its zeros in $|\im z|\le(\max(\frac14-\E X^2,0))^{1/2}$. Following \cite{P26pg10}, each of the following therefore has only real zeros, unconditionally: $\frac12(\Xi+F_b)$ for $b\ge2^{-1/2}$, from $X=\frac b2(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)$ with independent signs $\varepsilon_k$; $\frac34F_b+\frac14F_{3b}$ for $b\ge\frac1{2\sqrt3}$, from $X=b(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3)$; $\frac12(F_{b_1}+F_{b_2})$ for $b_1^2+b_2^2\ge\frac12$, from $\frac12(\cosh b_1u+\cosh b_2u)=\cosh\frac{(b_1+b_2)u}2\cosh\frac{(b_2-b_1)u}2$; and $\frac1{b_2-b_1}\int_{b_1}^{b_2}F_b\,db$ for $b_1^2+b_1b_2+b_2^2\ge\frac34$, from $\frac{\sinh x}x=\prod_{k\ge1}\cosh(2^{-k}x)$. Conversely $\Xi$ is no mixture $\int F_b\,\mu(db)$ with $\mu\ge0$ other than $\mu=\delta_0$: on the kernel side $\int\cosh(bu)\,\mu(db)\equiv1$ forces $\mu=\delta_0$. It is instead the hyperbolic secant smoothing of every rung, $\Xi=p_b*F_b$ with $p_b(x)=\frac1{2b}\sech\frac{\pi x}{2b}$, whose characteristic function is $\sech(bu)$; the same smoothing takes $\Xi$ to $g_b$ (Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:levyarea}). Beyond products of $\cosh$ the variance does not decide. For $\Pr(X=0)=\frac14$ and $\Pr(X=\pm2/\sqrt3)=\frac38$, $\E X^2=1$ and $\E\cosh(uX)=\frac14+\frac34\cosh\frac{2u}{\sqrt3}$ is a universal factor, by P\'olya's theorem on universal factors \cite[p.~197]{deBruijn}, since $\frac14+\frac34\cos\frac{2w}{\sqrt3}$ has only real zeros; yet for $p(z)=(z^2+1)^3$, with zeros at height $1$,
\[
\E\,p(z+iX)=z^6-12z^4+5z^2+\tfrac29
\]
has two zeros on the imaginary axis, while the two laws of variance one in the local model above give $\cos(\partial)p=z^4(z^2-12)$ and $e^{-\partial^2/2}p=z^6-12z^4+30z^2-8$, both with only real zeros.
\end{remark}

The local model predicts the collision of a pair at $\pm i\eta$ from the variance alone. To the next order the shape of the law enters; \cite{P26pg10} tests this numerically, and the following statement makes the test precise.

\begin{proposition}[One pair]\label{mi:prop:collision}
Let $G$ be a real entire function of order less than two, $a\in\R$ with $G(a)\ne0$, $\tau_j=G^{(j)}(a)/G(a)$ and $F_\eta(z)=((z-a)^2+\eta^2)G(z)$. Let $\ell$ be symmetric, bounded or standard normal, with $\E\ell^2=1$ and $m_4=\E\ell^4$, and put $d_c=\tau_1^2-\frac{m_4-1}2\tau_2$. For every $K>|d_c|$ there are $r>0$ and $\eta_0>0$ such that for $0<\eta<\eta_0$ and $|s^2-\eta^2|\le K\eta^4$ the function $\E F_\eta(z+is\ell)$ has exactly two zeros in $|z-a|<r$, and there is $s_c$ with
\[
s_c^2=\eta^2-d_c\,\eta^4+O(\eta^6)
\]
such that the two zeros are a nonreal conjugate pair for $s<s_c$, a double real zero at $a+\tau_1\eta^2+O(\eta^4)$ for $s=s_c$, and real and distinct for $s>s_c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $z=a+\eta^2x$ and $s^2=\eta^2-\eta^4d$. Since $\ell$ is symmetric, $\E F_\eta(z+is\ell)$ is an analytic function of $z$ and $s^2$, and expanding $G(z+is\ell)=G(a)\sum_j\tau_j(z-a+is\ell)^j/j!$ with $\E\ell^2=1$ and $\E\ell^4=m_4$ gives
\[
\frac{\E F_\eta(z+is\ell)}{G(a)\,\eta^4}=(x-\tau_1)^2+d-d_c+\eta^2R(x,d,\eta),
\]
with $R$ analytic and bounded for bounded $x$, $d$ and small $\eta$; the expectation converges because $G$ has order less than two. As $\eta\to0$ the function tends to $(z-a)^2G(z)$ uniformly near $a$, so by Hurwitz's theorem it has exactly two zeros in a fixed disc about $a$, and they lie at bounded $x$, by Rouch\'e's theorem against $(x-\tau_1)^2$ on $|x-\tau_1|=C$. At $\eta=0$ the system $P=\partial_xP=0$ for the right side has the nondegenerate solution $x=\tau_1$, $d=d_c$, which the implicit function theorem continues to small $\eta$. The function is real on $\R$, so the two zeros are real or conjugate according to the sign of the discriminant; $(x_+-x_-)^2=4(d_c-d)+O(\eta^2)$ is analytic in $d$ with $d$-derivative $-4+O(\eta^2)$, so its only zero is $d_c(\eta)=d_c+O(\eta^2)$ (this is where $K>|d_c|$ is used), and its sign is that of $d_c(\eta)-d$.
\end{proof}

With $\ell=(\varepsilon_1+\dots+\varepsilon_n)/\sqrt n$, the ladder of $n$ independent $\cosh$ steps, $m_4=3-\frac2n$, that is $1$, $2$ and $\frac73$ for one, two and three steps, and $m_4=3$ for the Gaussian, the de Bruijn Newman flow. So the variance decides the collision to first order, as in the local model, and the law enters at order $\eta^4$ through its kurtosis. Table~\ref{mi:tab:collide} tests the expansion on $\Xi$ itself, with its first zero moved to $\gamma_1\pm i\eta$ and its mirror to $-\gamma_1\pm i\eta$, so that $a=\gamma_1$ and $G(z)=\Xi(z)((z+\gamma_1)^2+\eta^2)/(z^2-\gamma_1^2)$, which is $\Xi(z)(z+\gamma_1)/(z-\gamma_1)$ times $1+O(\eta^2)$ near $\gamma_1$; this factor changes the expansion in the proof of Proposition~\ref{mi:prop:collision} only at order $\eta^6$, so the proposition holds with the $\tau_j$ of $\Xi(z)(z+\gamma_1)/(z-\gamma_1)$, $\tau_1=-0.54426$ and $\tau_2=0.22728$ (computed).

\section{Open problems}\label{mi:sec:open}

\begin{problem}
Decide whether the heuristic of Remark~\ref{mi:prop:zerocount} holds with a bounded error.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{mi:prob:nuP}
Assuming $\RH$ for $\Xi$, decide whether $\nu_P\ge0$ on all of $(0,\infty)$ for every $P$. The angle $y_0/x_0\sim(\pi/2)/\log x_0$ of the departed chain tends to zero, which suggests yes; the computed range is $[0.02,3]$.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Prove Conjecture~\ref{mi:conj:survey}, or at least $|R_P-N(\tau_P^*)|=O(\log q)$ under $\RH$ and a lower bound for the peaks of Hardy's function $Z$ on negative humps.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Find a computable Thorin criterion that detects the departed zeros, for instance the failure of positivity of Hankel or moment matrices built from $\nu_P$ beyond a certain order, and use the friable approximants to calibrate it.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Prove that all zeros of $g^{(2)}$ lie in $|\im w|<2$, and deduce from de Bruijn's theorem that every zero of Miyatake's $\xi^{(2)}$ is real.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{mi:prob:cardon}
Prove that no factorisation $\Phi=U\chi$ with $U=\E\cosh(tX)$ a nontrivial universal factor has $\hat\chi$ in the Laguerre P\'olya class, which would extend the theorem of Rodgers and Tao from Gaussian factors to Cardon's factors.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{mi:prob:quasi}
Make the local mechanism of Section~\ref{mi:sec:quasi} rigorous at all large heights, proving $B_{\cosh}\ge\sigma_{\max}^\infty-\frac12$ in Conjecture~\ref{mi:conj:quasi}; Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:paired} already settles the paired version. Proposition~\ref{mi:prop:collision} proves the mechanism for one pair as $\eta\to0$ with the other zeros fixed; what is missing is uniformity in the height and in the other zeros.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{mi:prob:rvm}
Prove the Riemann von Mangoldt formula for the rungs $F_b$ with error $O(\log T)$, which makes the density one statement after Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:density} unconditional.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Prove a Ki, Kim and Lee theorem for the vertical average $\E\,\Xi(z+iX)$ whenever $X$ is unbounded, and show that for bounded $X$ the nonreal zeros at large height sit above zeros of $\zeta$ with $\beta>\frac12+\operatorname{ess\,sup}X$.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{mi:prob:escape}
Make the escape law \eqref{mi:eq:escape} rigorous for $b>2$, $C_b\ne0$.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{mi:prob:b0}
Certify, by interval arithmetic, that $C_b$ has exactly one zero $b_0$ on $(2,\infty)$: an interval Newton enclosure of $b_0=2.4588641657$, the sign of $\Phi(i\pi/(2b))$ on $(2,20]$ by subdivision with a rigorous tail for the series over $n$, Theorem~\ref{mi:thm:cusp} with an explicit error near $b=2$, and a proof that $\Phi(iy)>0$ for $0<y\le\pi/40$ to cover $b>20$.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{mi:prob:E}
Decide whether $\mathcal E_{2\pi/9}$ of Proposition~\ref{mi:prop:rigid}, equivalently $q_{\pi/9}*\Xi^{\mathcal U}$ of Proposition~\ref{mi:prop:meixner}, is in the Laguerre P\'olya class, for $a_0=2\pi$ or for every $a_0>0$. That $q_{\pi/9}$ is a P\'olya frequency function does not suffice: convolution with a symmetric probability density of variance $v$ sends $z^2-r^2$ to $z^2-r^2+v$, which has nonreal zeros for $r^2<v$, and the same convolution gives nonreal zeros for $\Xi$ (Section~\ref{mi:sec:mixture}). A proof has to use more of $\Xi^{\mathcal U}$, for instance the spacing of its zeros.
\end{problem}
