\section{Where the uniform step could come from}\label{cp:sec:uniform}

Section~\ref{sec:ch25:open} names three possible sources of the missing uniform step: a positive approximating family whose positivity is proved independently, a Hilbert P\'olya operator, or a direct Weil inequality.

\subsection{The Weil Gram matrix on compactly supported test functions}\label{cp:sec:weil}

For $h$ smooth enough, even or not (each term depends on $h$ only through its even part $h_e$, defined below), with $\hat h(r)=\int h(x)e^{irx}\,dx$, the explicit formula reads
\[\begin{aligned}
\sum_\rho\hat h(\gamma_\rho)&=\hat h(\tfrac i2)+\hat h(-\tfrac i2)-\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\big(h(\log n)+h(-\log n)\big)\\&\qquad+\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R\hat h(r)\,\re\psi\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2\big)\,dr-h(0)\log\pi,
\end{aligned}\]
with $\rho=\frac12+i\gamma_\rho$ and $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$. For $h=g*\tilde g$ with $\tilde g(x)=\overline{g(-x)}$ and $g$ supported in $[-L/2,L/2]$, the left side is $\sum_\rho|\hat g(\gamma_\rho)|^2\ge0$ under RH, which is Weil's criterion \cite{Weil52}, $h$ is supported in $[-L,L]$, and the prime sum runs over prime powers $n\le e^L$. The archimedean term was evaluated in $x$ space,
\[\begin{gathered}
\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat h(r)\,\re\psi\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2\big)\,dr=\int_0^\infty\Big[\frac{h(0)e^{-2x}}x-\frac{2h_e(x)e^{-x/2}}{1-e^{-2x}}\Big]dx,\\ h_e(x)=\tfrac12\big(h(x)+h(-x)\big),
\end{gathered}\]
which is far better conditioned than the $r$ integral for compactly supported $h$: truncated at $r=400$, the $r$ integral carries an error of $6\times10^{-6}$ in the archimedean term of the diagonal entry at $w=0.1$, more than two thousand times the smallest eigenvalue $2.5\times10^{-9}$ of Table~\ref{cp:tab:weil}. With hat functions $g_j(x)=\max(0,1-|x-c_j|/w)$ at nodes spaced $w$ apart inside $[-L/2,L/2]$, $N=L/w-1$ functions in all, $h_{jk}=g_j*\tilde g_k$ is an explicit piecewise cubic, and the Gram matrix $G_{jk}=W(h_{jk})$ is Hermitian, with $W(g*\tilde g)\ge0$ on the span exactly when $G$ is positive semidefinite.

Three findings (Tables~\ref{cp:tab:weil} and~\ref{cp:tab:weilcheck}, Figure~\ref{cp:fig:weil}). First, $\lambda_{\min}>0$ throughout and it falls roughly geometrically in $L$, by a factor between about $2.5$ and $20$ per unit of $L$ up to $L=6$, as it must, since the nodes are nested (for the values of $L$ used), so that the matrix for a shorter support is a principal submatrix of the matrix for a longer one, and the smallest eigenvalue of the Weil form on $[-L/2,L/2]$ tends to zero as $L$ grows: a long enough support can make $\hat g$ small at every low ordinate. Second, the two sides of Weil's identity agree to a relative $2\times10^{-4}$ at every $(w,L)$, down to $\lambda_{\min}=2.5\times10^{-9}$; and the margin is carried by a handful of zeros, zero $41$ at height $124.3$ carrying $32\%$ at $w=0.1$, $L=6$. Third, at $w=0.05$ a pair displaced at height $g\approx17.6$ makes $\lambda_{\min}$ negative at every $L\le4$, one at $g\approx54.7$ only at $L=4$ and one at $g\approx146.7$ at no $L\le4$, an instance of the finite information principle of Section~\ref{cp:sec:finiteinfo}. How fine $w$ must be to see a pair at height $g$ is not fixed by one resolution: at $w=0.2$ the pair at $g\approx17.6$ is already seen at $L=2$, with $wg\approx3.5$.

The sign structure is the reverse of the L\'evy picture of Section~\ref{ch:basepoint}, where the pole of $\zeta$ is a negative input to $\log\xi$; in the Weil form the pole, on the minimising vectors of Table~\ref{cp:tab:weil}, contributes positively. A certificate for the Weil form would therefore have to show, for every $L$, that the three terms together are nonnegative on the whole span, although the pole term alone is not (for real odd $g$ it is $-2G(\frac12)^2$, with $G(a)=\int g(x)e^{ax}\,dx$), and on the minimising vectors the pole term outweighs the prime and archimedean terms only by a margin that tends to zero. No uniformly positive quadratic form can do that. What could is a factorisation $W(g*\tilde g)=\|Ag\|^2$ with $A$ built from the arithmetic and exact positivity, the shape of the Hilbert P\'olya mechanism, and Problem~\ref{cp:prob:chol} asks for the first experiment in that direction.

\subsection{The cost of the flow route}\label{cp:sec:cost}

The published points of the phase diagram give the cost of descending along it (Table~\ref{tab:ch21:hist} lists the bounds on $\Lambda$). Polymath15 proved $\Lambda\le0.22$ with the barrier $X\approx6\times10^{10}$, and Table~1 of their paper lists, for each bound from $\Lambda\le0.21$ down to $\Lambda\le0.1$, the barrier $X$ that the same method needs, with RH verified to height $X/2$ \cite{Polymath}: $X\approx2\times10^{12}$ for $0.21$, $5\times10^{12}$ for $0.2$, $2\times10^{13}$ for $0.19$, $7\times10^{15}$ for $0.15$ and $9\times10^{21}$ for $0.1$. Platt and Trudgian obtained $\Lambda\le0.2$ from the second row, which by their account needs verification to $2.51\times10^{12}$, and the third row, $0.19$, requires verification slightly above $10^{13}$ \cite{PT21}. The product $\Lambda\log X$ is $5.46$ at $0.22$ and falls along the table from $5.95$ at $0.21$ to $5.06$ at $0.1$.

\begin{proposition}[Computed]\label{cp:prop:cost}
The least squares fit of $\Lambda=c/\log X$ in $1/\log X$ to the bound $\Lambda\le0.22$ and the twelve rows of the table gives $c=5.59$, with residuals below $0.013$ in $\Lambda$, which along the table are errors of up to a factor of about $200$ in $X$; on this law a bound $\Lambda\le\delta$ requires a barrier $X$ of order $e^{c/\delta}$, that is verification to height $X/2$: $X=10^{48.6}$ for $\delta=0.05$ and $10^{242.9}$ for $\delta=0.01$. Since $\Lambda\log X$ falls along the table, the law $\log X=a/\Lambda+b$ fits its twelve rows better, with $a=4.24$, $b=8.28$ and residuals below $0.003$ in $\Lambda$, within a factor $1.6$ in $X$, and gives $X=10^{40.4}$ for $\delta=0.05$ and $10^{187.7}$ for $\delta=0.01$.
\end{proposition}

Beyond the table the two laws are orders of magnitude. Their form agrees with the heuristic that the flow at time $t$ makes the zeros near height $x$ real once $t\log x$ exceeds a constant, which is how the Riemann Siegel type approximation of $H_t$ enters \cite{Polymath}. Along the table, halving the bound from $0.2$ to $0.1$ raises the required barrier from $5\times10^{12}$ to $9\times10^{21}$, to about its power $1.7$, and the power is $2$ at every bound on the law $c/\log X$ and tends to $2$ as the bound decreases on the law $\log X=a/\Lambda+b$. By the table itself the bound $\Lambda\le0.15$ needs verification to a height of about $3.5\times10^{15}$, some $1200$ times the record $3\times10^{12}$ \cite{PT21}, and $\Lambda\le0.1$ about $4.5\times10^{21}$; no conceivable computation reaches $0.05$. The flow route produces a theorem at every stage, a monotone sequence of upper bounds for $\Lambda$, which is $0$ exactly when RH holds \cite{RodgersTao}, as no other route does, and it cannot reach the corner of the diagram by computation.

\paragraph{The best point on a level set.} An unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026) measures how far the verification already available reaches when $(t_0,y_0)$ is chosen along the level set rather than with $t_0$ close to the bound sought. Hypothesis (ii) of Theorem~\ref{barrier:thm:pm} is replaced by a sufficient condition for $f_{t_0}(x+iy)\ne0$, where $f_t$ is the main term of the effective approximation of $H_t$ in \cite{Polymath}: the sums of $f_t$ are multiplied by the Euler mollifier $\prod_{p\le5}(1-b_p^tp^{-s})$, with $b_n^t=e^{(t/4)\log^2n}$ and $s=\frac{1+y-ix}2$, and bounded by the triangle inequality, and the error terms of the approximation, of order $(x/4\pi)^{-1/4}\approx1.2\times10^{-3}$ at $x=6\times10^{12}$, are dropped. At the barrier height $X\approx6\times10^{12}$, which the verification of Platt and Trudgian to $3\times10^{12}$ covers \cite{PT21}, the smallest $y_0$ for which the condition holds at $x=X$ for every $y\in[y_0,\sqrt{1-2t_0}]$ (monotonicity in $y$ was checked only at $t_0=0.17$) is $0.152$, $0.181$, $0.218$ and $0.318$ at $t_0=0.19$, $0.18$, $0.17$ and $0.15$, so that $t_0+\frac12y_0^2$ is $0.2016$, $0.1964$, $0.1938$ and $0.2006$, with least value about $0.1936$ near $t_0=0.1675$, $y_0=0.229$ (computed in double precision and recomputed independently; at $(t_0,y_0)=(0.17,0.22)$ the margin of the condition is $0.014$ at $x=X$, $0.11$ at $1.5X$ and $0.18$ at $2X$). The gain does not come from a sharper estimate: at the parameters of the rows $0.20$ and $0.19$ of Table~1 of \cite{Polymath}, $(t_0,y_0)=(0.186,0.167)$ and $(0.180,0.141)$, the condition fails where the lower bound of \cite{Polymath} succeeds, yet optimised over $(t_0,y_0)$ it gives about $0.1955$ at $X=5\times10^{12}$ and $0.184$ at $X=2\times10^{13}$, against $0.20$ and $0.19$ in the table. Heuristic: with the verification to $3\times10^{12}$, the method of \cite{Polymath} with $(t_0,y_0)$ near $(0.17,0.23)$ should give $\Lambda\le0.197$ (a margin choice; the point $(0.17,0.22)$ above, with margin $0.014$, gives $0.1942$) and the point $(0.17,0.615)$ of $\mathcal G$; at time $0.17$ the best rigorous point is de Bruijn's $\alpha=\frac12+\frac12\sqrt{1-2t}=0.906$, since the points of Corollary~\ref{cp:cor:known}(b) and (c) have $t\ge0.186$ and Theorem~\ref{cp:thm:phase}(i) carries points only forward in time. A theorem needs rigorous bounds for the error terms, a lower bound for (ii) uniform in $x\ge X$ (the condition is not monotone in $x$, since the length of the sums jumps), and a certified barrier (iii); none of these has been done.

\subsection{A programme}\label{cp:sec:programme}

The phase diagram suggests five steps; the first three are engineering.
\begin{enumerate}
\item An evaluator: a Riemann Siegel type form of $\xi_t$ and $\xi_t'$, such as the effective approximation of $H_t$ in \cite{Polymath}, in ball arithmetic, at a cost of about $\sqrt{\theta/2\pi}$ terms per point (Table~\ref{tab:compare}), replacing the P\'olya integral, whose required precision grows like $0.341\,\theta$ digits (Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}); see Section~\ref{st:sec:wp2} for $t=0$. Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:shift} gives another evaluator: at $s=\frac12+i\theta$, $\xi_t(s)$ is $g(s)e^{t\ell(s)^2/4}$ times a Gaussian average of $\zeta e^{r}$ centred at $s+\frac t2\ell(s)$, close to $\frac12+\frac t4\log\frac\theta{2\pi}+i(\theta+\frac{\pi t}8)$, with variance $\frac t2$ in the real direction, which Gauss Hermite quadrature computes from ordinary evaluations of $\zeta$ and $\Gamma$; at $t=0.2$ the relative differences between the rules with $40$ and $60$ nodes are $6.8\times10^{-56}$ at $\theta=1000.3$ and $2.4\times10^{-47}$ at $\theta=4000.3$, and an error bound for the quadrature is open.
\item Certificates in Thorin form: Theorem~\ref{cp:thm:cert} or Proposition~\ref{cp:prop:thorincert} at a target $(t_0,\alpha_0)$ up to the barrier.
\item The tail in the same framework: the barrier and the analytic region beyond it \cite{Polymath}, so that the points of Corollary~\ref{cp:cor:known}(b) and (c) are certified end to end and stated as GGC theorems.
\item A measurement: the optimal $(t_0,\alpha_0)$ on each level set as a function of the verification height, and the constant $c$ of Proposition~\ref{cp:prop:cost} as a function of the height; a first, heuristic, measurement at $X=6\times10^{12}$ is in Section~\ref{cp:sec:cost}.
\item The structural step: a tail at $t=0$ below the basepoint one (below $\frac78$ if the announced half plane is confirmed). The step is the existence of the tail, and it must come from one of the mechanisms of Section~\ref{sec:ch25:open}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Open problems}\label{cp:sec:open}

\begin{problem}\label{cp:prob:high}
Compute $v_t(\alpha,\theta)$ at $t=0$ and $t=0.2$ near the Polymath barrier, heights near $3\times10^{10}$, with a Riemann Siegel type evaluator of $H_{0.2}$ such as the effective approximation of \cite{Polymath} (Section~\ref{ch:classical} gives the one for $\zeta$), and measure how the difference between the functions $v_{0.2}/\pi$ and $v_0/\pi$ grows with the height. Proposition~\ref{cp:prop:see}(iii) measures it below $100$. For $t=0$ see work package~2 of Section~\ref{st:sec:wp2} and Section~\ref{sec:ch25:contrib}.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{cp:prob:boundary}
Describe the boundary $\alpha^*(t)$ of $\mathcal G$ for $0\le t\le0.2$ as far as computation and the known zero free regions allow. Is $\alpha^*$ continuous, and is $(\alpha^*(t)-\frac12)^2+\frac t2$ strictly decreasing in $t$ where $\alpha^*(t)>\frac12$? Theorem~\ref{cp:thm:phase}(i) shows that it is nonincreasing there. Problem~\ref{mw:prob:flow} asks the neighbouring question for the flow clock $T_t$, $0<t<0.2$.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{cp:prob:trunc}
Give the symmetrised truncations of Section~\ref{ch:rz4} a coordinate on the phase diagram: for some $(t,\alpha)$ outside the region of Corollary~\ref{cp:cor:known}, is there a family of explicit truncations of $\xi_t$, built from finitely many terms of the P\'olya integral or of a Dirichlet series and making no use of the zeros, as the symmetrised lattice truncations $K_n$ of Subsection~\ref{rz:sec:finite} are, that converges to $\xi_t$ on compact sets, together with heights $h_n\to\infty$ such that for infinitely many $n$ the truncation has no zero with $\re s>\alpha$ and $|\im s|<h_n$? Hurwitz's theorem then makes $(t,\alpha)$ a new point of $\mathcal G$, as in Corollary~\ref{mw:cor:hurwitz}(c); at $t=0$ and $\alpha=\frac12$ the question for the $K_n$ is Conjecture~S (Theorem~\ref{rz:thm:symhurwitz}), and for the stages of Chapter~\ref{chap:maxwald} it is Problem~\ref{mw:prob:hurwitz}.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{cp:prob:chol}
Compute the Cholesky factor of the Weil Gram matrix of Section~\ref{cp:sec:weil} for decreasing $w$ and increasing $L$, and decide whether its entries converge to an object built from the arithmetic, the first test of a factorisation $W(g*\tilde g)=\|Ag\|^2$.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{cp:prob:gas}
Find a global functional of the zeros of $H_t$, insensitive to individual close pairs, that is monotone along the flow and whose positivity at $t=0$ excludes collisions at positive time. Biane's gap functional of Section~\ref{sec:wigbiane:biane} is local, the minimum over one gap of Laguerre's quantity, and Problems~\ref{prob:wigbiane:control} and~\ref{prob:wigfree:collide} ask for the control of the nonreal zeros that would make its brackets unconditional; the functional asked for here should instead be one quantity for all the zeros.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{cp:prob:rvm}
(a) Prove that $v_{0.2}(10,\theta)\ge\frac{19}2V_{0.2}$ for every $\theta\ge\theta_1(0.2)$, which by Corollary~\ref{cp:cor:minflow} makes $\tilde\varphi_{0.2,\alpha}$ a GGC transform for every $\alpha\in[\frac12,10]$. By Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:shift} and Cauchy's estimate it suffices to bound $|\zeta_{0.2}-1|$ on the discs of radius $5$ about $10+i\theta$ by a small constant uniform in $\theta$; on these discs the shifted Gaussian average of the lemma reaches $\re s\le2$ with probability below $10^{-20}$ at every height, and at the four heights sampled the largest of $256$ sampled values of $|\zeta_{0.2}-1|$ on each boundary circle is at most $0.0326$ (computed, not a bound on the discs). (b) For $0<t\le0.2$ prove an explicit bound $|S_t(T)|\le c_1\log T+c_2$ for $T\ge T_1$, for instance by proving the two hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{cp:prop:count}(iii) with an explicit $M$, from Lemma~\ref{cp:lem:shift}, Rademacher's convexity bound for $-\frac12\le\re s\le\frac32$ \cite{Rademacher59} and the functional equation, with its Gamma factor kept, for $\re s<-\frac12$. The minimum of $\re\zeta_{0.2}(2+iy)$ over $0\le y\le200$ is $0.69006$, near $y=14.015$ (computed). At $t=0$ much sharper explicit bounds are known \cite{HSW22}.
\end{problem}
