\chapter{Code and reproducibility}\label{app:code}

A number in this book can be checked once three things are known: the evaluator that produced it, the precision at which it ran, and the script that turned its output into a table or a figure. The book's archive of scripts and data files is in its repository, \url{https://github.com/VadimSokolov/computing_rh}, beside the \LaTeX\ source of the book. The scripts of Chapters~\ref{chap:foundations} to \ref{chap:heat} and \ref{chap:probability} are in the folder \texttt{code/} of the book's archive, and the data files they write are in \texttt{data/}. The interval enclosures, spot checks and other scripts of Chapters~\ref{chap:rzeta} and \ref{chap:rzid} are in \texttt{verification\_reciprocal\_zeta/}; the verification script of Section~\ref{ch:boschB} is in \texttt{code\_bosch/}, with an identical copy beside the authors' notes in \texttt{bosch\_hcm\_xi/}; the scripts of Chapters~\ref{chap:basepoint} to \ref{chap:almost} are in \texttt{basepoint\_one/}, \texttt{sato\_bondesson/} and \texttt{riesz\_program/}; and the reruns and reconstructions made for the book are in \texttt{repro/}. Section~\ref{app:code:eval} prints the two P\'olya evaluators of Section~\ref{ch:polya} and states what their ball radii certify. Section~\ref{app:code:scripts} gives the software the scripts use and the order in which they run, names the scripts and data files behind the tables and figures of Chapters~\ref{chap:foundations} to \ref{chap:probability}, and collects the constants that recur in several sections. Section~\ref{app:code:partVI} does the same for Chapters~\ref{chap:basepoint} to \ref{chap:almost}, and Section~\ref{app:code:repro} reports what a rerun of the whole archive reproduced and which computations still have no script.

\section{The P\'olya evaluators}\label{app:code:eval}

The first listing is \texttt{code/polya\_flint.py}, the evaluator of Section~\ref{ch:polya} on the real $u$ axis. Its function \texttt{setup} computes the node values $\Phi_j=\Phi(jh)$, $0\le j\le J$, from the theta series \eqref{eq:Phi} in ball arithmetic and stores the polynomials $P$ and $Q$ of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid} as \texttt{PA} and \texttt{PB}; \texttt{xi\_pair} returns $P(E)+P(E^{-1})$ and $Q(E)-Q(E^{-1})$ with $E=e^{sh}$, the trapezoid approximations to $\xi(\frac12+s)$ and $\xi'(\frac12+s)$. The defaults are $240$ bits, $h=1/200$ and $|u|\le3$, so that $J=600$. Most runs call \texttt{setup(420, 200, 3)}, the parameters of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}; the Keiper Li coefficients of Section~\ref{ch:li} use $600$ bits, and the Selberg computation of Section~\ref{ch:sin2more} uses $300$.

\lstinputlisting[language=Python,showstringspaces=false,title={\texttt{code/polya\_flint.py}}]{code/polya_flint.py}

The second listing is \texttt{code/tilted.py}, which implements Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt}. With $\eta=\pi/4-\delta$ and nodes $u_j=x_j+i\eta$, $x_j=jh$, $|j|\le J$, on the line $\im u=\eta$, where $J=\lceil x_{\max}/h\rceil$ for the cut $|x|\le x_{\max}$, so that there are $2J+1$ nodes, it returns $e^{i\eta s}h\sum_j\Phi(u_j)e^{sx_j}$ and $e^{i\eta s}h\sum_ju_j\Phi(u_j)e^{sx_j}$, the trapezoid approximations to $\xi(\frac12+s)$ and $\xi'(\frac12+s)$ on the tilted line; \texttt{Phi\_c} sums the theta series at complex $u$, using the evenness of $\Phi$ when $\re u<0$. Of the two evaluators in the file, \texttt{xi\_pair} applies Horner's rule and is called only by \texttt{ttest.py}, which exhibits the growth of the ball radii described in Section~\ref{sec:ch4:balls}; the production scripts evaluate each exponential directly, through \texttt{xi\_pair\_direct} or an equivalent loop. The Lehmer pair and its de Bruijn Newman flow use $\delta=0.01$, $256$ bits, $h=6\times10^{-4}$ and $|x|\le3.9$; the count at height $1000$ in Section~\ref{sec:ch5:count} uses $\delta=0.1$, $200$ bits, $h=0.004$ and $|x|\le4.2$; the module \texttt{clock.py}, which evaluates the Laplace transform of the clock for Sections~\ref{ch:clocklaw} and \ref{ch:boundaries}, switches to the tilted evaluator with $\delta=0.1$, $320$ bits, $h=0.003$ and $|x|\le4.5$ when $|\im s|\ge120$; and the runs of Table~\ref{tab:tilt} take $\delta$, the precision, $h$, the cut and the heights from the command line of \texttt{ttest2.py}. The command lines, recorded at the top of the two test scripts, are \texttt{python3 ttest2.py 0.10 200 0.004 4 200 500 1000}, with $2001$ nodes, for the rows with $\delta=0.1$, \texttt{python3 ttest2.py 0.02 220 0.001 4.2 1000 2000 5000}, with $8401$ nodes, for the rows with $\delta=0.02$, and \texttt{python3 ttest.py 0.1 200 0.004 4}, which prints \texttt{nan} at the heights $200$ to $2000$, where the Horner ball of $\xi$ contains zero.

\lstinputlisting[language=Python,showstringspaces=false,title={\texttt{code/tilted.py}}]{code/tilted.py}

In both evaluators the radii of the returned balls bound the rounding errors of the finite sums, and nothing else. Three errors lie outside them: the discretisation error of the trapezoid rule, bounded in Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}; the cut of the axis at $|u|\le3$, where $\Phi<10^{-500}$, or at $|x|\le x_{\max}$ on the tilted line; and the truncation of the theta series, whose loop stops at the first term with midpoint below $2^{-b-20}$ at working precision $b$ and does not add the tail to the radius. A certified enclosure of $\xi$ adds bounds for these three errors to the radii. The stopping test compares double precision numbers, so for $b\ge1055$ its threshold underflows to zero and the test is never met. The untilted route needs more than $1054$ bits for thirty digits above height about $840$ (Table~\ref{tab:ch4:budget}), and there the test has to be made in ball arithmetic. The runs in this book use at most $600$ bits and are not affected.

\section{Scripts, data files and reference constants}\label{app:code:scripts}

Ball arithmetic comes from \texttt{flint}, the Python interface to FLINT and Arb \cite{Arb}, which also gives the seventy digit ordinates of the first $1700$ zeros used by \texttt{arith.py}. Reference values of $\zeta$, $\Gamma$ and $\xi$, the other ordinates of the zeros, the interval arithmetic of Chapters~\ref{chap:rzeta} and \ref{chap:rzid} and the Talbot inversion of Laplace transforms \cite{Talbot79} come from \texttt{mpmath} \cite{mpmath}; \texttt{numpy} and \texttt{scipy} supply arrays, quadrature, interpolation, root finding, differential equations and nonnegative least squares, \texttt{sympy} the primes and the M\"obius function, and \texttt{matplotlib} the figures.

The scripts run from \texttt{code/} and read and write their data files in the working directory; the data files named in Tables~\ref{tab:app:scripts} and \ref{tab:app:scriptsb} are in \texttt{data/}, except the reports and JSON files of Chapters~\ref{chap:rzeta} and \ref{chap:rzid}, which are in \texttt{verification\_reciprocal\_zeta/} and its subfolder \texttt{torus/}. The P\'olya evaluators use no zeros. Ten scripts, \texttt{analyse.py}, \texttt{deconv.py}, \texttt{deconvplot.py}, \texttt{hankel.py}, \texttt{kenttail.py}, \texttt{kenttail2.py}, \texttt{kmono.py}, \texttt{perturb.py}, \texttt{plots3.py} and \texttt{selberg.py}, load the first $1700$ ordinates of the zeros from \texttt{g\_1\_400.npy}, \texttt{g\_401\_1000.npy} and \texttt{g\_1001\_1700.npy}. These lists are not in \texttt{code/}: \texttt{repro/zeros.py} computes them with \texttt{mpmath.zetazero} at thirty digits, and identical copies are in \texttt{riesz\_program/zeros/} and in several of the folders that Section~\ref{app:code:partVI} describes. \texttt{spacing.py} reads the lists when they are present and otherwise computes and saves them, and \texttt{zeros\_hp.py}, which writes the seventy digit ordinates of \texttt{zeros\_1700.txt} that \texttt{arith.py}, \texttt{cm\_orders.py}, \texttt{firstfail.py}, \texttt{firstfail\_arb.py}, \texttt{gaussweil.py}, \texttt{phasefree.py} and \texttt{vdpair.py} read, uses the lists and \texttt{part1.json} only to check its output. Two scripts come first, because others read their output. \texttt{part1.py} finds the sign changes of $\Xi$ below height $200$ on a grid of step $0.05$, refines the zeros by Newton's method and computes $N(200)$ by the argument principle. \texttt{part2.py} evaluates $\pi\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)=\re(\xi'/\xi)(\frac12+\varepsilon+i\theta)$ and the continuous argument of $\xi(\frac12+\varepsilon+i\theta)$ for $0\le\theta\le200$, at each resolution $\varepsilon$ given on its command line, and saves them in the files \texttt{eps\_*.npz} with the largest argument increment, which it also prints. The book uses the eight resolutions $1$, $0.5$, $0.25$, $0.1$, $0.05$, $0.02$, $0.01$ and $0.005$, and the largest increment over all of them, $0.29$ at $\varepsilon=1$, is the one quoted in Section~\ref{sec:ch4:height200}. The distances of Section~\ref{ch:distances} (\texttt{analyse.py}), the zero density identity of Section~\ref{ch:turing} (\texttt{turing\_ig.py}) and the deconvolution of Section~\ref{ch:deconv} (\texttt{deconv.py} and \texttt{deconvplot.py}) start from these files, and \texttt{analyse.py} writes \texttt{plotcurves.npz}, which \texttt{plots.py}, \texttt{plots3.py} and \texttt{plots4.py} read; the \texttt{.npz} files are not in the archive. \texttt{analyse.py}, \texttt{clockgrid.py}, \texttt{hazard\_spec.py} and \texttt{dbn.py} read \texttt{part1.json}, and the boundary computations of Section~\ref{ch:boundaries} start from \texttt{hazard\_spec.json}. \texttt{hcm\_arb.py}, which encloses in ball arithmetic the $216$ values that \texttt{hcm.py} computes, reads \texttt{hcm.json} to compare them. Likewise \texttt{firstfail\_arb.py}, which certifies in ball arithmetic the negative values that \texttt{firstfail.py} finds, reads \texttt{firstfail.json}, and so do \texttt{gsmooth\_arb.py} and \texttt{phasefree\_arb.py}, for the pairs and their heights; these two compute the zeros they need with Arb instead of reading them.

The plotting scripts draw the figures from the saved data. The figures are in colour in the digital edition, the PDF at the DOI on the copyright page, and in grey in the paperback; where a figure tells its curves apart by colour alone, the digital edition is the one to read. \texttt{plots.py} takes no arguments; it reads \texttt{plotcurves.npz}, \texttt{analysis.json}, \texttt{hcm.json} and \texttt{results\_prime.json}, and besides its figures it writes \texttt{extra.json} and \texttt{sens2.json}. \texttt{dbnplot.py} reads \texttt{dbn.json}. \texttt{plots.py}, \texttt{plots2.py}, \texttt{perturb.py} and \texttt{dbnplot.py} save their figures in \texttt{fig/} below the working directory, and \texttt{plots3.py}, \texttt{plots4.py}, \texttt{kmono.py}, \texttt{deconvplot.py}, \texttt{lplots.py} and \texttt{lehmerflowplot.py} in \texttt{book/fig/}; of these only \texttt{perturb.py} and \texttt{dbnplot.py} create their folders. Four scripts of \texttt{code/} are reconstructions, written for the book where the archive held a data file or a module but no script; the header of each says so and gives its command line, its inputs and what it reproduces. \texttt{hankel.py} and \texttt{spacing.py} reproduce \texttt{hankel.json} and \texttt{spacing.json} byte for byte, and \texttt{spacing.py} also draws \texttt{spacing.pdf} in the working directory. \texttt{sinh\_bench.py} reproduces \texttt{sinh\_bench.json}, whose lattice sums are cut at $|k|\le400000$, to a relative $1.1\times10^{-12}$, and \texttt{python3 sinh\_bench.py closed} writes the closed forms, which Sections~\ref{ch:ggc} and \ref{ch:spacing} quote, to \texttt{sinh\_bench\_closed.json}. \texttt{polya.py} wraps \texttt{setup(420, 200, 3)} and \texttt{xi\_pair} of \texttt{polya\_flint.py} for \texttt{primeside.py}, which imports it.

In \texttt{verification\_reciprocal\_zeta/}, \texttt{enclosures.py} computes the enclosures E1 to E6 of Chapters~\ref{chap:rzeta} and \ref{chap:rzid} in outward rounded interval arithmetic and writes \texttt{enclosures\_report.md}. \texttt{certify\_k9\_k2.py} encloses in ball arithmetic the value of $\Re\kappa(\rho_{213})$ used in Corollary~\ref{rz:cor:Tfalse} (both from its definition and from the formula of Theorem~\ref{rz:thm:kappaexact}), the sign of $\Re\kappa$ at the first $460$ zeros, and the values of $S$ quoted in and after the proof of Theorem~\ref{rz:thm:class}, and writes \texttt{certify\_k9\_k2.json}; it runs in a few seconds. \texttt{spot\_checks.py} recomputes the constants of these chapters that \texttt{mpmath} and the first few hundred zeros can settle and prints each beside the value in the text. It writes no file: its report is its output, \texttt{python3 spot\_checks.py > spot\_checks\_report.md}, which takes about eight minutes, and it checks the nine rows of Table~\ref{rz:tab:constants} and four of the twenty five rows of Table~\ref{rz:tab:num}. Two scripts compute numbers quoted in Section~\ref{ch:rz3}, each in about a minute. \texttt{sawtooth\_truncation.py} computes the root mean square of $S$ and of the errors of its truncated sawtooth and classical series on uniform grids of steps $10^{-3}$, $5\times10^{-4}$ and $2.5\times10^{-4}$ in $[10,50]$, and the sums $\frac12\sum_{N\le N_0}|B_N|$, quoted after Proposition~\ref{rz:prop:sawtrunc}, and writes \texttt{sawtooth\_truncation.json}. \texttt{harmonic\_decay.py} computes the maxima of $\log|\hat f_{\kappa^*}(v)|/v$ over the windows $[0.95V,1.05V]$ quoted after Proposition~\ref{rz:prop:harmonic}, by root finding in each interval between consecutive zeros of $\hat f_{\kappa^*}$, where the quotient has a single maximum, rather than on a grid, and writes \texttt{harmonic\_decay.json}. The subfolder \texttt{torus/} holds the branch and bound of Remark~\ref{rz:rem:torus}, which proves in ball arithmetic the bounds and thresholds of Tables~\ref{rz:tab:torus} and \ref{rz:tab:torus1} and the second condition of Theorem~\ref{rz:thm:torus}. It ran as arrays of tasks on a cluster, and its proofs in ball arithmetic took about twenty CPU hours; the folder holds the programs and their aggregated JSON outputs, and \texttt{torus/README.md} says what each script does and how to rerun it. Tables~\ref{tab:app:scripts} and \ref{tab:app:scriptsb} list the scripts section by section. Tables that collect statements, or evaluate the formulas given in their captions, need no script; Section~\ref{app:code:repro} names the other tables and figures of Chapters~\ref{chap:foundations} to \ref{chap:probability} that the two tables do not list.

\section{The scripts for the basepoint one and the critical strip}\label{app:code:partVI}

The scripts of Chapters~\ref{chap:basepoint} to \ref{chap:almost} stay in the folders of the authors' notes, and each reads and writes its data files in its own folder; the exceptions are Sections~\ref{sec:dbeta:comp}, \ref{sec:sqfree}, \ref{sec:wigfree} and \ref{sec:pakes}, whose scripts are in \texttt{code/} and whose data files are in \texttt{data/}, and Section~\ref{sec:biane:cm}, computed by the scripts of Section~\ref{ch:biane} in Table~\ref{tab:app:scriptsb}. In \texttt{basepoint\_one/}, the folder \texttt{xi} holds the scripts for $\xi$ of Chapter~\ref{chap:basepoint}, and \texttt{eta} those for $L(s,\chi_{12})$ of Section~\ref{ch:bpeta}; \texttt{scale\_mixture} and \texttt{wiener} hold those of the two halves of Section~\ref{ch:strip}, and \texttt{process\_map} those of the map of processes of Section~\ref{ch:sato} and of the boundaries across the basepoints of Section~\ref{bp:sec:across}. The folder \texttt{sato\_bondesson/} holds the other scripts of Section~\ref{ch:sato}, and \texttt{riesz\_program/code/} those of Sections~\ref{ch:riesz} and \ref{ch:almost}, whose data files are in \texttt{riesz\_program/data/}; the script \texttt{setup.sh} links these files and the zero lists of \texttt{riesz\_program/zeros/} into \texttt{code/}, from which the scripts run. Tables~\ref{tab:app:partVI} and \ref{tab:app:partVIb} give the scripts behind each table and figure. Several names recur across the folders, so the tables give the folder of every script: \texttt{comp.py} is in \texttt{scale\_mixture}, \texttt{process\_map} and \texttt{sato\_bondesson}, \texttt{figs.py} in \texttt{xi}, \texttt{eta}, \texttt{process\_map} and \texttt{sato\_bondesson}, \texttt{improve.py} and \texttt{figs2.py} in \texttt{xi} and \texttt{scale\_mixture}, and \texttt{salem\_riesz.py} in \texttt{wiener} and \texttt{riesz\_program/code}, while \texttt{mc.py} of \texttt{xi} and \texttt{arith.py} of \texttt{eta} are not the scripts of those names in \texttt{code/}. The scripts take no arguments, with three exceptions: \texttt{bnd\_alpha.py} takes the basepoint, $0.6$ or $0.75$; \texttt{weil.py} takes the weights $\log p$ in double precision, or at thirty digits when called as \texttt{python3 weil.py mp}; and \texttt{riesz\_large.py} takes $x$, the truncation $K$ and the twist $h$, as in \texttt{python3 riesz\_large.py 1e10 200 50}.

In \texttt{xi}, \texttt{compute.py} writes the Thorin density at the basepoint one, the sine squared check with the primes up to $10^7$ and the values of Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:nogo} to \texttt{alpha1.json}, and each other script that computes writes the data file of its own name, except \texttt{rs1c.py}, which adds the tangent boundary to \texttt{rs1.json}. \texttt{mc.py} also writes \texttt{surv.json} and a sample \texttt{X\_sample.npy}, and \texttt{rs1d.py} and \texttt{errformula.py} read \texttt{rs1.json} and \texttt{below.json}. The sample is not in the archive, so \texttt{figs.py}, which draws Figure~\ref{bp:fig:mc} from it, runs only after \texttt{mc.py}. The script \texttt{heat\_primes.py} was added for the book: it reads no file, computes the thresholds of Table~\ref{bp:tab:heat} in floating point and certifies Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:heatcert} in ball arithmetic with \texttt{python-flint}, in about a minute and a half on $32$ cores. In \texttt{eta}, \texttt{zeros12.py} computes the $297$ zeros of $L(s,\chi_{12})$ below height $340$ and saves them in \texttt{g12.npy}, which \texttt{base.py}, \texttt{arith.py} and \texttt{hit.py} read; \texttt{hit2.py} adds to \texttt{hit.json}, and \texttt{figs.py} reads \texttt{alpha1.json} of \texttt{xi} as well as the files of its own folder.

In \texttt{scale\_mixture}, \texttt{dens2.py} adds the densities of $Y_\alpha$ to \texttt{strip.json}, and \texttt{figs2.py} draws Figure~\ref{sm:fig:strip} from \texttt{strip.json} and \texttt{comp.json}. In \texttt{wiener}, two scripts were added for the book: \texttt{figs\_wiener.py}, which draws Figures~\ref{wr:fig:wiener} and \ref{wr:fig:salem}, whose drawing script the authors' package did not contain, and \texttt{riesz\_explicit.py}, a reconstruction that writes \texttt{riesz\_explicit.json} from \texttt{salem\_riesz.json}, byte for byte as archived. In \texttt{process\_map}, \texttt{comp.py} and \texttt{comp2.py} read a copy of \texttt{alpha1.json}, and \texttt{figs.py} draws Figure~\ref{pm:fig:paths} from \texttt{ou.json}, the files \texttt{bnd\_0.6.json} and \texttt{bnd\_0.75.json}, and copies of \texttt{rs1.json} from \texttt{xi} and of \texttt{volterra.json} from \texttt{data/}. In \texttt{sato\_bondesson/}, \texttt{comp.py} reads \texttt{alpha1.json}, \texttt{rs1.json} and \texttt{improve.json} of \texttt{basepoint\_one/xi} and \texttt{clockgrid.json} and \texttt{hazard\_spec.json} of \texttt{data/}, and \texttt{weil.py} prints its results and writes no file; \texttt{figs.py} holds as constants the five values of the explicit formula that Figure~\ref{sb:fig:tail} shows as dots.

In \texttt{riesz\_program/code/}, \texttt{killing2.py} adds to \texttt{killing.json}, which \texttt{rmt\_ratios.py} reads, \texttt{more2.py} reads \texttt{almost.json}, and \texttt{figs\_riesz.py} reads \texttt{riesz\_thorin.json}, \texttt{salem\_riesz.json} and \texttt{riesz\_explicit.json}, the last two identical to the files of \texttt{basepoint\_one/wiener}. Seven scripts of the folder were added for the book: \texttt{v1min.py}, which certifies Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:v1min}; \texttt{explicit\_bounds.py}, which certifies in ball arithmetic the numbers used in the proofs of Theorems~\ref{sm:thm:strip}(3) and \ref{ag:thm:explicit}; \texttt{almost\_dip.py}, \texttt{check\_large\_twisted.py}, \texttt{ggc\_offset.py} and \texttt{rmt\_ratios.py}, which compute numbers of Sections~\ref{ch:riesz} and \ref{ch:almost} that had no archived output; and \texttt{figs\_almost.py}, which redraws Figures~\ref{ag:fig:almost} and \ref{ag:fig:cancel} in the notation of the book. Two checks were added in \texttt{repro/}: \texttt{ks\_ak.py} recomputes the arithmetic factors that \texttt{ks.py} holds as constants, and \texttt{zeros\_mp.py} and \texttt{v1\_grid\_mp.py}, run as arrays of tasks on a cluster and collected by \texttt{v1\_collect.py} in \texttt{v1\_check.json}, check Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:v1min} independently with \texttt{mpmath}. The figures of Chapters~\ref{chap:basepoint} to \ref{chap:almost} are copies, renamed in \texttt{fig/}, of the PDF files that these scripts write: \texttt{bp1\_*.pdf} from \texttt{xi}, \texttt{scale\_mixture}, \texttt{wiener} and \texttt{process\_map}, \texttt{bp1eta\_*.pdf} from \texttt{eta}, \texttt{sb\_*.pdf} from \texttt{sato\_bondesson}, and \texttt{ri\_*.pdf} and \texttt{ag\_*.pdf} from \texttt{riesz\_program/code}, where \texttt{figs\_strategy.py} writes the panels of Figures~\ref{ri:fig:filters} and \ref{ag:fig:margins}. The labels inside the other plots keep the notation of the notes.

\section{The scripts of the ferromagnetic chapter}\label{app:ferro}

The scripts of Chapter~\ref{chap:ferro} are in the folder \texttt{ferro/}, each with the data file of its own name and the log of its run on the Hopper cluster. They work in the units of \eqref{eq:ch2:Phi}, and those that need the zeros read the first $1700$ ordinates, to $70$ digits, from a copy of \texttt{data/zeros\_1700.txt} in the working folder. \texttt{fm\_hermite.py} runs in two modes: with a list of values of $K$ it brackets the thresholds $v^*(K)$ of Table~\ref{fm:tab:fK} and writes \texttt{fm\_hermite.json}, and with the arguments \texttt{all 200} it checks $f_K$ itself for every $K\le200$ and writes \texttt{fm\_hermite\_all.json}. \texttt{fm\_maxwell.py} takes the number of draws, $10^7$ for the book; \texttt{fm\_maxwell\_1e6.json} is an earlier run with $10^6$ draws. \texttt{fm\_hcm.py} computes in ball arithmetic with python-flint \cite{Arb}, and its output is the certificate of Proposition~\ref{fm:prop:hcm}, in the variable $t/2$. \texttt{fm\_cutoff.py} also works in ball arithmetic, with the zeros isolated by Arb instead of read from the file, and so do \texttt{fm\_qcert.py} and \texttt{fm\_tvcert.py}: in the mode \texttt{zeros 800 9600}, \texttt{fm\_qcert.py} writes $\operatorname{Var}X$ and the first $800$ ordinates, computed by Arb at $9600$ bits, to \texttt{fm\_zeros\_hp.txt} (not in the archive), which both scripts then read; its modes \texttt{table}, \texttt{all 200} and \texttt{minq} give the brackets of $v^*(K)$, the positivity of $f_K$ for every $K\le200$ and the minima of $Q_{K,2\beta_K}$. Table~\ref{tab:app:ferro} lists the scripts and what they produce.

\section{Reruns and gaps}\label{app:code:repro}

In September 2026 every script of the archive was run again from a fresh copy on the Hopper cluster of George Mason University, and every data file it wrote was compared with the archived one; the reports are \texttt{compare\_report.md}, \texttt{compare\_part6\_report.md} and \texttt{compare\_riesz\_report.md} in \texttt{repro/}. Apart from the running times that four of the files record, the rerun reproduced every archived data file that a script writes to a relative $10^{-9}$, most of them bit for bit, and the report of the enclosures of Chapters~\ref{chap:rzeta} and \ref{chap:rzid} byte for byte. There are two exceptions: in \texttt{selberg.json}, $31$ of $4020$ values differ by up to $2.1\times10^{-8}$ relative, and in \texttt{improve.json} of \texttt{scale\_mixture} the densities agree to $2.9\times10^{-8}$ in absolute terms but not in relative terms in the far tail, where they are below $10^{-7}$ and no printed number depends on them. The enclosure program was corrected afterwards to bound the remainders of the sums in E3 and E5 and to compare every bound, rounded outward, with the number used in the text; its report replaced the archived one. The scripts changed since then and those added for the book were run on the cluster too, and the data files in the archive are their output or agree with it. On 28 September, after the last of these changes, the driver behind \texttt{compare\_report.md}, which by then ran every script of \texttt{code/}, the Bosch script and the five programs of \texttt{verification\_reciprocal\_zeta/} outside \texttt{torus/}, was run once more in an empty folder. All $65$ runs ended without error, and every data file that a script writes agreed with the archived one to a relative $10^{-9}$, apart from the running times that six of the files record, one host name and the same $31$ values of \texttt{selberg.json}; the report of the enclosures and the output of \texttt{spot\_checks.py} were reproduced byte for byte (\texttt{repro/compare\_report\_0928.md}). The scripts \texttt{vdpair.py}, \texttt{idsd\_height.py}, \texttt{threezones.py} and \texttt{cm\_orders.py}, added on 29 September after that run, were run on the cluster on their own, \texttt{vdpair.json}, \texttt{idsd\_height.json}, \texttt{fig/threezones.pdf} and \texttt{cm\_orders.json} are their output, and the driver now includes them. The scripts of Chapters~\ref{chap:maxwald} and~\ref{chap:phase}, added in October 2026 after that run, were run on the cluster on their own, and the data files of the archive are their output.

The rerun also showed what the archive lacked, and each item was followed up on the cluster in a folder of \texttt{repro/reconstruct/} with its scripts, its logs and a report. \texttt{primeside.py} imports a module \texttt{polya} that was not in the archive; with the reconstructed \texttt{polya.py}, its \texttt{results\_prime.json} agrees with the archived file in all $162$ values to $7.4\times10^{-16}$ relative, $148$ of them bit for bit, and \texttt{plots.py} run on it regenerates \texttt{extra.json} and \texttt{sens2.json} byte for byte. \texttt{ttest.py} and \texttt{ttest2.py} stopped because they were given no arguments; the driver now passes the command lines of Section~\ref{app:code:eval}, and with them \texttt{ttest2.py} reproduces the node counts and the errors of Table~\ref{tab:tilt}, while its times per point vary with the machine. Two lines of the Bosch script executed a file \texttt{gaptest.py} that is not in the archive, which defined the functions of block 12 again and an empty list \texttt{out}; they are replaced by \texttt{out=[]}, and the script runs as it stands; Appendix~\ref{app:bosch} says what it reproduces. \texttt{spot\_checks.py} writes no file, so the rerun had nothing to compare; run as in Section~\ref{app:code:scripts}, it reproduces the report in the archive byte for byte, and the $112$ numbers and statements of Chapters~\ref{chap:rzeta} and \ref{chap:rzid}, Section~\ref{ch:heat} and this appendix that the report bears on agree with it at the printed precision. Of the five data files that no script wrote, \texttt{hankel.json}, \texttt{spacing.json} and \texttt{sinh\_bench.json} are reproduced by the reconstructions of Section~\ref{app:code:scripts} and \texttt{riesz\_explicit.json} by \texttt{riesz\_explicit.py} of \texttt{basepoint\_one/wiener}; the fifth is \texttt{results\_main.json}.

Four gaps remain in these items. The printed output of the Bosch script is not the record of a single run: output lines 37 to 39 and 109 come from code that is not in the archive, and lines 93 to 102 from an earlier version of block 13; reconstructions in \texttt{repro/reconstruct/bosch\_v7} reproduce lines 37 to 39 and 109 and the density at $t=0.003$ at $150$ digits, but the output file is kept as the authors made it. \texttt{primeside.py} runs only with the reconstructed \texttt{polya.py}, and its P\'olya column then differs from the archived one by up to four units in the last place, a difference that none of $196$ tested conventions removes and that no printed number shows. \texttt{spot\_checks.py} still prints, beside the values it computes, three reference values from an earlier version of Chapters~\ref{chap:rzeta} and \ref{chap:rzid}, and its docstring gives two minutes for a run that takes about eight; the values it computes agree with the text. Finally, \texttt{data/results\_main.json} has no script that writes or reads it. A reconstruction in \texttt{repro/reconstruct/primeside} reproduces $117$ of its $135$ numbers, but not its distances \texttt{tv} and \texttt{sup}, which rest on a tail model that cannot be recovered, nor its timings; the book takes from it only $\gamma_{1700}=2197.2587$, which the zero lists also give.

Some computations of the book have no script in the archive. In Chapters~\ref{chap:foundations} to \ref{chap:heat} and \ref{chap:probability} these are Table~\ref{tab:ch4:Meta}, whose values \texttt{plots3.py} holds as constants, Tables~\ref{bh:tab:cm} and \ref{tab:prony}, and Figures~\ref{fig:ch5:lehmer}, \ref{fig:selberg}, \ref{fig:ch16:rho}, \ref{fig:ch18:mc}, \ref{fig:ch18:kent}, \ref{fig:ch18:kent2}, \ref{fig:ignogo} and \ref{fig:ch20:li}, most of whose data files exist although the code that drew them does not; the intermediate values of Section~\ref{ch:worked}, which come from calls of \texttt{polya\_flint.py} at its defaults; the check with the step $h$ halved and the time per evaluation in Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}; the double precision run with $\delta=0.15$ of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:budget}; and the numerical checks in the text of Section~\ref{ch:boschA}, made with \texttt{mpmath} as that section says. The Horner experiment of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:balls} is reproduced by \texttt{horner100.py} in \texttt{repro/reconstruct/ttest2}, outside \texttt{code/}; \texttt{lehmer.py} prints the timings of Section~\ref{sec:ch5:lehmer} but does not save them; and \texttt{kmono.json} holds only minima that underflow to zero, while \texttt{kmono.py} draws Figure~\ref{fig:ch12:kmono} from the values it computes.
