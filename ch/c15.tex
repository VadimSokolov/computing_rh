\chapter{The Thorin phase diagram}\label{chap:phase}

This chapter sets out the most promising computational path to RH that the book has found, and says exactly where it stops.

The sine squared lemma and the Thorin side of Chapter~\ref{chap:measure} offer no computational alternative to the de Bruijn Newman flow, only the right language for it. The Thorin side is the object to compute, prime free and well defined at every basepoint, and the flow is the only known source of an analytic tail below the basepoint $\frac78$. What separates the theorems $\Lambda\le0.22$ of Polymath15 \cite{Polymath} and $\Lambda\le0.2$ of Platt and Trudgian \cite{PT21} from RH is not anything a computed density shows; it is the existence of the analytic tail at $t=0.2$ and its absence at $t=0$. Sections~\ref{cp:sec:transfer}, \ref{cp:sec:cert} and the two experiments of Section~\ref{cp:sec:uniform} were first written as a working report by the authors \cite{FA26}, which this chapter supersedes. We use the normalisation of Section~\ref{ch:dbn}: $H_t(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi_{\rm dBN}(u)\cos(zu)\,du$, $\xi_t(s)=8H_t(-2i(s-\frac12))$, a zero $x+iy$ of $H_t$ is the zero $\frac12-\frac y2+\frac{ix}2$ of $\xi_t$, and $P_a(x)=a/(\pi(a^2+x^2))$ is the Cauchy density of scale $a>0$.
