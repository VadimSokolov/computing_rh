\section{Open problems and lessons for the Thorin programme}\label{ch:open}

The programme of computations below is aimed at the gap that Section~\ref{ch:path} located, positivity uniform in the height, and the lessons that follow it are drawn for anyone working on the Thorin route to RH, including the first author's own programme \cite{P26}. The open problems on the reciprocal zeta function are stated in Section~\ref{rz:sec:open}, those on the basepoint one and the critical strip in Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly}, and those of Pakes's framework in Section~\ref{pk:sec:open}.

\subsection{What computation can and cannot do on the path}\label{sec:open}\label{st:sec:role}

Computation cannot supply uniformity in height (Section~\ref{sec:ch25:contrib}); what it can do falls under four aims. It can extend and certify: prove positivity of the Thorin density on explicit windows, at every resolution, with interval arithmetic. It can test intermediate conjectures: the four questions of Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly} all have numerically testable consequences. It can calibrate: measure how much room positivity has, and compare with random matrix predictions, so that a proof knows which inequalities are sharp. And it can cross check: compute the same quantities from the primes, through the criteria of Riesz and Salem and the compensated L\'evy form, independently of evaluations of $\zeta$.

Five work packages follow: twisted Riesz functions as an arithmetic filter bank, the cross check (Section~\ref{ri:sec:filters}); certified Thorin densities at height (Section~\ref{st:sec:wp2}); the positivity margins against the circular unitary ensemble (Section~\ref{ag:sec:margins}); explicit tables of the almost GGC defect (Section~\ref{ag:sec:factor}); and testing stronger conjectures before pursuing them (Section~\ref{st:sec:wp5}).

\subsection{Certified Thorin densities at height}\label{st:sec:wp2}

At resolution $\varepsilon=\alpha-\frac12$ the Thorin density $\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)=\frac1\pi\re\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\alpha+i\theta)$ is a smooth function of $\theta$. Its positivity on a window of ordinates is what the absence of zeros with $\beta>\alpha$ predicts there; since an off line zero need not make the density negative (Section~\ref{sec:ch13:visibility}), a certificate for the window should also count such zeros. Two computations do this. The first evaluates $\rho_\varepsilon$ on a grid with rigorous error bounds, using the ball arithmetic Riemann Siegel and Euler Maclaurin evaluators of Arb \cite{Arb}, exposed in python flint, at moderate height and the P\'olya route where it is faster, and bounds the variation between grid points by the derivative. The second counts the zeros to the right of the line $\re s=\alpha$ through the zero density identity $M_\varepsilon(T)+\tau_\varepsilon(T)=N(T)-2\nu(\frac12+\varepsilon,T)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:zerodensity}. It is the count of Section~\ref{ch:turing}, which at $\varepsilon=\frac12$ is Backlund's method. Section~\ref{sec:ch9:count} followed the argument in steps of $\varepsilon/8$ ($\varepsilon/4$ at $\varepsilon=0.005$) without a bound on its change between grid points, so its counts are computed, not certified; with such a bound the count would certify a whole window. The deliverables are certified positivity of $\rho_\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon\in\{10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3}\}$ on windows of length $10^4$ at heights $10^6$, $10^8$ and $10^{10}$, together with the minimum density on each window, which feeds the margin statistics of Section~\ref{ag:sec:margins}. Below the verified height $3\times10^{12}$ the positivity itself needs no new computation: an off line pair makes the density negative only within $\frac12$ of its ordinate, so $\rho_\varepsilon>0$ on $[0,3\times10^{12}-\frac12]$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:dbeta:window}); there the certificates test the evaluators against the verification and measure the margins.

Below height $200$ the certificate is within reach now. The ball arithmetic of Section~\ref{ch:polya} certifies the rounding only. Adding the discretisation bound of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid} and of Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt}, bounds for the truncations of the theta series and of the $u$ axis, a bound on the change of the argument between grid points, and an explicit bound on the remainder of Proposition~\ref{prop:mass}, which is $O(\varepsilon^3)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:ch7:cubic}), turns positivity of $\rho_\varepsilon$ on $0<z\le200^2$ at each computed $\varepsilon$, the integer identity of Proposition~\ref{prop:zerodensity} and the HCM derivative signs of Section~\ref{sec:weak:hcm} into theorems on that window.

Above that window the evaluator changes. The tilted contour gives $25$ digits at height $5000$ and resolves the Lehmer pair near height $7005$ (Sections~\ref{sec:newcomp} and \ref{sec:ch5:lehmer}), but its number of nodes grows linearly with the height. Chirp transform batching of the P\'olya route, and the hybrid of Section~\ref{sec:ch5:hybrid}, a Riemann Siegel or Odlyzko Sch\"onhage evaluator at $\tfrac12+\varepsilon+i\theta$ with Odlyzko's zeros as the RH reference, would give the positivity margin, the matched total variation and the zero density scan near the $10^{12}$th \cite{Odlyzko87}, $10^{21}$st and $10^{22}$nd zeros \cite{Odlyzko01,OdlyzkoTables}. The same zeros would measure the cell constant of Section~\ref{ch:spacing} at large height, whose behaviour is open (Section~\ref{sec:ch15:spacings}), and allow a comparison of the cell errors with the prediction of the sine kernel process of the unitary ensemble.

\subsection{Calibration against known configurations}\label{sec:open:calib}

The diagnostics of Chapters~\ref{chap:measure} to \ref{chap:heat} are calibrated when they are run on configurations whose zeros are known exactly, and three such computations remain. Section~\ref{ch:deconv} ran the posterior predictive check of \cite[Section 19.3.7]{P26} at $\varepsilon=0.1$ on one window and calibrated it with a displaced zero. Three extensions are open: smaller resolutions, where the signature of a displaced zero is largest and the P\'olya route supplies $\rho_\varepsilon$ to about $60$ digits below height $200$ (Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}), whereas the deconvolution used double precision; continuous atom positions, which would remove the baseline of the grid; and larger heights. The square root law \eqref{eq:ch8:threshold} of Section~\ref{sec:ch8:calibration}, which follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:signature}, sets what the check can detect.

The de Bruijn Newman flow of Section~\ref{ch:dbn} manufactures off line pairs in a controlled way. Running these Thorin diagnostics along the flow through the collision of the Lehmer pair near $7005$ would calibrate the zero density scan, the matched total variation and the negative mass against configurations that are known exactly.

Generalised prime systems of Beurling whose zeta functions provably have only real ordinates give positive Thorin measures by construction. Measuring how fast their clock transforms approach $1/G$, in the distances of Section~\ref{ch:distances} and along a sequence with an accumulation point as the closure criterion of Corollary~\ref{cor:weak:closure} \cite[Propositions 18.10 and 18.14]{P26} allows, is the computational side of that criterion; the Kent approximants of Section~\ref{sec:ch18:kent} are the first instance.

Two computations of Chapter~\ref{chap:probability} remain at their edges. The Brownian boundary of Section~\ref{ch:boundaries} reproduces the law of the clock in the bulk, to $0.0020$ in Kolmogorov distance for the boundary of the Volterra equation and to $0.0059$ for the tangent approximation of Roberts and Shortland (Section~\ref{sec:ch19:zones}), but the zone of large times $t$ is checked by no simulation, since only about one path in ten million survives past $t=0.1$. Importance sampling with an exponentially tilted drift, or multilevel splitting, would check that zone and the logarithmic correction of the asymptote of the boundary directly. The Cauchy integral method of Section~\ref{ch:li} extends to $n$ in the thousands, at a cost of about $0.03n$ digits lost to cancellation. Comparing the P\'olya values of the Keiper Li coefficients with the sums $\sum_{\gamma>0}4\sin^2(n\arctan\frac1{2\gamma})$ over the zeros to large $n$ would test how the Li functional sees the zeros near height $n$.

\subsection{Testing stronger conjectures before pursuing them}\label{st:sec:wp5}

Conrey and Li showed that a positivity condition in de Branges' approach to RH is false even though RH may be true \cite{CL00}. Any intermediate statement proposed on the Thorin path should therefore be tested numerically first. The candidates are a lower bound $\rho_{\alpha-1/2}(\theta)\ge c(\alpha)\log\theta$ outside a sparse set, the first of the four questions of Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly}; monotonicity or convexity of the margin in $\alpha$; positivity of the Thorin density for $\alpha$ slightly below one, uniformly on large windows; hyperbolic complete monotonicity of the Laplace transform $\varphi_\alpha$ of $T_\alpha$ below one; and variogram inequalities at single scales. The first two are partly settled: the lower bound holds for $\rho_{\alpha-1/2}$ itself outside a set of heights of density zero, although not near the zeros where Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:cancel} would need it (Remark~\ref{rem:dbeta:average}), and the margin divided by $\alpha-\frac12$ decreases in $\alpha$, with the ratio $\rho_{\alpha-1/2}(\theta)/(\alpha-\frac12)$ nonincreasing in $\alpha$ at every height outside the windows of the zeros off the line (Theorems~\ref{thm:dbeta:comp} and \ref{thm:dbeta:minall}). Each can be evaluated on the computed windows and at the heights of Odlyzko's tables, and any statement that fails there should be discarded.

The variogram inequalities can be tested in finite dimension. The reformulation of Section~\ref{ch:sin2more} poses finite dimensional feasibility problems: is the regularised pole mass dominated by the prime and archimedean mass on the cone of variograms of positive definite functions of a given dimension? Solving them with explicit dual certificates at increasing dimension would measure how the margin behaves.

The cross checks test the zeros themselves. Agreement of the twisted Riesz functions (Proposition~\ref{ri:prop:twisted}) with the explicit formula over the computed zeros is a check that shares no code or method with the computation of the zeros (Remark~\ref{ri:rem:indep}).

\subsection{Open problems from free probability}\label{sec:wigfree:open}

The note \cite{P26wig} closes with eight problems, and five of them are settled or taken up in the book. They are settled by Theorem~\ref{thm:ch12:idsd} through Theorem~\ref{thm:wigfree:clock}, by Propositions~\ref{prop:wigfree:wishart} and~\ref{prop:wigfree:peel} with Theorem~\ref{thm:wigfree:nomix}, or taken up in Sections~\ref{sec:ch21:hankel} and~\ref{sec:wigbiane:brackets}. is the following.

\begin{problem}\label{prob:wigfree:jensen}
Jensen polynomials as finite free products. The multiplicative convolution $\boxtimes_d$ multiplies normalised coefficients and keeps roots real and of one sign (Lemma~\ref{lem:wigfree:laguerre}(i)), and the normalised coefficients of the reversed Jensen polynomial $x^dJ_{d,n}(1/x)$ are, up to the signs $(-1)^i$, the numbers $(n+i)!\,b_{n+i}$, $0\le i\le d$. A factorisation $(n+i)!\,b_{n+i}=\alpha_i\beta_i$ into the normalised coefficients of two polynomials with only real roots of one sign would therefore give real zeros of $J_{d,n}$. Find such factorisations for every shift $n$ at a fixed degree $d$, or show that none exists with factors of a given kind. The obvious split, a Gaussian multiplier sequence $q^{i^2}$, $0<q\le1$, times a residual close to the coefficients of $(1+x)^d$, fails, because a root of multiplicity $d$ splits into nonreal roots under a generic perturbation; and by Proposition~\ref{prop:wigfree:peel} the factor $1/(a)_i$ can be multiplied in but not divided out. A factorisation has to put real structure into both factors \cite{P26wig}.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:wigfree:laguerre}
Laguerre and Meixner Pollaczek expansions. Theorem~\ref{thm:wigfree:nomix} excludes scale mixtures of gamma laws for $|X|$ and $X^2$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:wigfree:peel} shows that removing a gamma layer keeps zeros real while adding one does not. Find a variable in which the P\'olya density, or $\Xi$ itself, has an expansion in Laguerre or Meixner Pollaczek polynomials with positive coefficients and truncations with only real zeros \cite{P26wig}. The symmetric Meixner Pollaczek expansions of Section~\ref{rz:sec:mpexp} converge slowly, and a real rooted construction from their coefficients is a problem of Section~\ref{rz:sec:open}.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:wigfree:jacobi}
Jacobi parameters. Under RH, where $\nu$ is a positive measure and all its Jacobi parameters exist, prove that $\alpha_n\sim2\beta_n\sim\frac12L_n$, with $L_n$ given by \eqref{eq:wigfree:abel} for the smooth count: a spectral form of the Riemann von Mangoldt formula. Table~\ref{tab:wigfree:jacobi} supports it to a few per cent for $n\le49$, but the local model of Section~\ref{sec:wigfree:jacobi} is a heuristic, outside the scaling of \cite{KVA99}. Decide between the rules $n(L_n)=n$ and $n(L_n')=n-\frac12$, which the data do not separate, and explain the scatter of the ratios, about six per cent, through the individual gaps between the zeros, as \cite{P26wig} proposes.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:wigfree:window}
The order at which a collision is seen. Section~\ref{sec:ch21:hankel} computes the failure times $t^*_N$ of the Hankel test along the flow to $N=50$ and explains their steps by collisions; the rule of \cite{P26wig} that the collision of $(\gamma_k,\gamma_{k+1})$ is registered from order $2k+2$ on fails at $N=50$ (Table~\ref{tab:ch21:nfail}). Find the rule that gives the order at which a pair is registered, as a function of the index $k$ of the pair and of its distance from the axis, and follow the staircase of $t^*_N$ to orders of a few hundred, towards the limit $0$ that it has under RH.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:wigfree:collide}
Unconditional collision brackets. Section~\ref{sec:wigbiane:brackets} brackets the collision times by quantities along the flow (Corollaries~\ref{cor:wigbiane:delta} and~\ref{cor:wigbiane:kappa}) and through the nonreal zeros (Theorems~\ref{thm:wigbiane:local} and~\ref{thm:wigbiane:upper}), and checks the hypotheses on a grid of times for the $58$ collisions of the census. Find the a priori control of the nonreal zeros of $H_t$, $t<0$, asked for in Problem~\ref{prob:wigbiane:control}, which would make the brackets statements about $H_t$ computed from $H_0$ alone.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{prob:wigfree:ladder}
Moving the ladder without new zeros. Theorems~\ref{thm:ch12:cmint} and~\ref{thm:ch12:window} prove the derivative signs $(-1)^kW^{(k)}>0$ on ranges that grow with the height to which RH is verified (Section~\ref{sec:ch12:reach}), and complete monotonicity of $W$ on $(0,\infty)$ is RH. Find a mechanism that extends these ranges with no new information on the zeros, for instance a functional equation for the law of $T$ under $\Upsilon$, as \cite{P26wig} suggests. Theorem~\ref{thm:wigfree:clock}(c) restricts the form such an equation can take: $T$ is not the Upsilon image of a law in BO, and under RH it is the image of the law $\mu_0$, which is neither a GGC nor in BO.
\end{problem}

\subsection{Lessons from the computations}\label{ch:lessons}

Each lesson is a statement that a computation in the book established or refuted.

Equivalent statements are not equally computable. The Thorin condition has at least eight equivalent faces: positivity of the Thorin measure, complete monotonicity of the heat trace, Stieltjes form of $G'/G$, Hankel positivity at one time, HCM of the clock, Lagarias positivity, Weil positivity and Li positivity. Mathematically they are the same statement. Computationally they differ by many orders of magnitude in conditioning. The heat trace at moderate time loses about $87t$ digits to cancellation (Section~\ref{ch:arith}); the normalised minors of the even Hankel block at $t=0.05$ fall to $10^{-44}$ by size five (Section~\ref{ch:heat}); the Keiper Li coefficients weight a zero at height $\gamma$ by $n^2/\gamma^2$ until $n$ reaches $\gamma$ (Section~\ref{ch:li}); the Thorin density at resolution $\varepsilon$ is local in the ordinate and sees each zero at full size $1/\varepsilon$ (Section~\ref{ch:distances}). A computational attack should choose its face by conditioning, and the local face is the Thorin density.

The primes compute the wrong end: the sine squared lemma is the right way to understand the structure and the wrong way to compute near the line, where $k$ digits at resolution $\varepsilon$ need primes up to about $10^{2k/\varepsilon}$ even when no zero lies to the right of the line (Section~\ref{sec:ch10:selberg}). Any argument that proposes to establish positivity near the line by bounding prime sums must overcome that exponential, and Section~\ref{ch:rz3} shows the same obstruction in another form (Proposition~\ref{rz:prop:bias}).

The heat trace meets the same obstruction in its own terms. By Proposition~\ref{prop:ch12:gauss}, RH is the positivity of the Gaussian smoothing $\mathcal G(u,t)$ of the zeros at every height $u$ and every $t>0$, and \eqref{eq:ch12:Garith} writes the symmetric smoothing $\mathcal G(u,t)+\mathcal G(-u,t)$ as a polar term plus an archimedean term $A(u,t)$ minus a prime sum $P(u,t)$. Bounding $|\cos(u\log n)|$ by one bounds $P(u,t)$ by $S(t)/\sqrt{2\pi t}$, where $S(t)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}e^{-(\log n)^2/8t}$ is, up to a factor, the prime term of \eqref{eq:ch2:Warith} at time $2t$, and so proves $\mathcal G(u,t)+\mathcal G(-u,t)>0$ for $t$ below the time $t_0(u)$ at which the bound meets the archimedean and polar terms, which agrees to five digits with the root of $2S(t)=\log(u/2\pi)$ (computed by \texttt{phasefree.py}): $t_0$ is $0.45$ at height $10^3$, $1.72$ at the verified height $3\times10^{12}$ and $3.64$ at $10^{50}$, to the digits shown (proved in ball arithmetic by \texttt{phasefree\_arb.py}), and it grows only doubly logarithmically in $u$, since $S(t)$ is asymptotic to $\sqrt{8\pi t}\,e^{t/2}$. These resolutions are too coarse to see the quadruples of Table~\ref{tab:ch13:first}: at their heights $t_0$ lies between $0.311$ and $0.511$, while within $8$ of each moved pair they make $\mathcal G$ negative only for $t>1.18$ (both proved in ball arithmetic). Nor can any bound on $P$ that holds uniformly in the height do better, since by Dirichlet's theorem on simultaneous approximation the phases $u\log n$ of any finite set of prime powers come arbitrarily close to multiples of $2\pi$ at arbitrarily large $u$, where $P(u,t)$ therefore comes arbitrarily close to $S(t)/\sqrt{2\pi t}$. Nor does positivity at a single resolution give a zero free strip: at fixed $t$ the bound $S(t)/\sqrt{2\pi t}$ on $P$ proves $\mathcal G(u,t)+\mathcal G(-u,t)>0$ at every height $u$ with $t_0(u)>t$, that is above about $2\pi e^{2S(t)}$, which is $8.1\times10^{212}$ at $t=6$, and $\mathcal G(u,6)>0$ itself for every $u\ge10^{213}$ (proved in ball arithmetic, with the bound on $|\mathcal G(-u,6)|$ above), whatever the zeros are, so a strip needs $t$ to grow with the height. A proof along the heat trace must use the phases, uniformly in the height.

Limits do not create positivity: positivity must be proved at every resolution, not in the limit. A proposal that reaches the Thorin condition as a limit must say where the positivity of its approximants comes from.

The perturbation theorem gives a test that a proposed proof of RH must pass. Replace $\xi$ by the function $F$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:ch13:perturb}, with $R$ above every height that the proof uses explicitly. A step that holds for $F$ as well as for $\xi$ cannot be the step that excludes zeros off the line, because $F$ has four. The steps that survive include the Riemann von Mangoldt formula with an explicit error, which $F$ satisfies with the error enlarged by one on $[A,B)$, the only heights where the two counts differ; the verified zeros below $R$; the small time expansion of the heat trace through the constant term, since $W_F-W=\big(\frac12(B-A)^2+\frac18\big)t+O(t^2)$; infinite divisibility, self decomposability and log convexity; the Hankel blocks of size $N$ when $R\ge T_N$ and the derivative signs up to order $10^{17}$ when $R\ge10^{12}$; and the order, type and functional equation. What separates $\xi$ from $F$ is arithmetic, the Euler product and the explicit formula with the prime weights $\Lambda(n)$, together with local counts of the zeros on the line in windows that contain the height $g$ of the quadruple (Section~\ref{sec:ch13:perturb}). A proof through the derivative signs can do without them only below the order at which the signs of $F$ first fail, which is about $t^*R^2$, with $t^*$ between $1.18$ and $4.03$ in the cases computed below height $2000$ and, by the model of Section~\ref{sec:ch13:perturb}, of order one or more at larger heights, far beyond $10^{17}$. The function $F$ exists unconditionally when $R+50\le3\times10^{12}$; for larger $R$ the test still applies, because a correct proof of RH makes the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:ch13:perturb}(b) true for every $R$. The criticality filter of Section~\ref{pk:sec:filter} is the companion test for properties of the P\'olya kernel: a step that holds for $e^{tv^2/4}\Phi(v)$ with some $t<0$, whose transform has nonreal zeros by the theorem of Rodgers and Tao, cannot be the step that excludes zeros off the line.
