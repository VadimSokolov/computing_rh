\section{Open problems and lessons for the Thorin programme}\label{ch:open}

The computations below aim at the gap located in Section~\ref{ch:path}, positivity uniform in the height; the lessons that follow are drawn for anyone working on the Thorin route to RH, including the first author's programme \cite{P26}. The open problems on the reciprocal zeta function are stated in Section~\ref{rz:sec:open}, those on the basepoint one and the critical strip in Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly}, those of free probability in Section~\ref{sec:wigfree:open}, and those of Pakes's framework in Section~\ref{pk:sec:open}.

\subsection{What computation can and cannot do on the path}\label{sec:open}\label{st:sec:role}\label{sec:ch25:contrib}

Computation cannot supply the uniformity in the height that steps 7 and 8 of Section~\ref{ch:path} require: neither finitely many heat trace tests (Theorem~\ref{thm:ch13:perturb}) nor positivity at a fixed resolution (Section~\ref{sec:ch13:visibility}) can exclude an off line zero above the verified height. The Thorin density at resolution $\varepsilon$ has minimum $0.0147\,\varepsilon$ on the window below height $200$ (Section~\ref{ch:motivation}), a margin that vanishes linearly and must be controlled uniformly; that this margin is the value at the origin is a theorem on the whole verified window, and at every height for every $\varepsilon<\frac12$ it is equivalent to RH (Theorem~\ref{thm:dbeta:minall}). Computation has four aims. It can certify positivity of the Thorin density on explicit windows, at every resolution, with interval arithmetic. It can test intermediate conjectures: the four questions of Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly} all have numerically testable consequences. It can calibrate how much room positivity has, against random matrix predictions, so that a proof knows which inequalities are sharp. And it can cross check, computing the same quantities from the primes, through the criteria of Riesz and Salem and the compensated L\'evy form, independently of evaluations of $\zeta$. The most concrete target for step 8 is the variogram form of the domination inequality (Section~\ref{ch:sin2more}), on which the linear programming formulation of \cite[Sections 18.2.8 and 18.2.9]{P26} works. Both are posed at the centre, where the variogram form is Weil's criterion and the linear program asks whether the clock $1/G$ is a GGC, so each is equivalent to RH. Section~\ref{ch:weak} and \cite[Section 18.2.3]{P26} give the closure criterion in its weakest form, the target for approximating families in step 8.

There are five work packages: twisted Riesz functions as an arithmetic filter bank, the cross check (Section~\ref{ri:sec:filters}); certified Thorin densities at height (Section~\ref{st:sec:wp2}); the positivity margins against the circular unitary ensemble (Section~\ref{ag:sec:margins}); explicit tables of the almost GGC defect (Section~\ref{ag:sec:factor}); and testing stronger conjectures before pursuing them (Section~\ref{st:sec:wp5}).

\subsection{Certified Thorin densities at height}\label{st:sec:wp2}

At resolution $\varepsilon=\alpha-\frac12$ the Thorin density $\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)=\frac1\pi\re\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(\alpha+i\theta)$ is a smooth function of $\theta$. Its positivity on a window of ordinates is what the absence of zeros with $\beta>\alpha$ predicts there; since an off line zero need not make the density negative (Section~\ref{sec:ch13:visibility}), a certificate should also count such zeros. The first of two computations evaluates $\rho_\varepsilon$ on a grid with rigorous error bounds, using the ball arithmetic Riemann Siegel and Euler Maclaurin evaluators of Arb \cite{Arb}, exposed in \texttt{python-flint}, at moderate height and the P\'olya route where it is faster, and bounds the variation between grid points by the derivative. The second counts the zeros to the right of the line $\re s=\alpha$ through the zero density identity $M_\varepsilon(T)+\tau_\varepsilon(T)=N(T)-2\nu(\frac12+\varepsilon,T)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:zerodensity}. This is the count of Section~\ref{ch:turing}, which at $\varepsilon=\frac12$ is Backlund's method. Section~\ref{sec:ch9:count} followed the argument in steps of $\varepsilon/8$ ($\varepsilon/4$ at $\varepsilon=0.005$) without a bound on its change between grid points, so its counts are computed, not certified; with such a bound the count would certify a whole window. The deliverables are certified positivity of $\rho_\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon\in\{10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3}\}$ on windows of length $10^4$ at heights $10^6$, $10^8$ and $10^{10}$, together with the minimum density on each window, which feeds the margin statistics of Section~\ref{ag:sec:margins}. Below the verified height $3\times10^{12}$ the positivity itself needs no new computation: an off line pair makes the density negative only within $\frac12$ of its ordinate, so $\rho_\varepsilon>0$ on $[0,3\times10^{12}-\frac12]$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:dbeta:window}); there the certificates test the evaluators against the verification and measure the margins.

Below height $200$ the certificate is within reach. The ball arithmetic of Section~\ref{ch:polya} certifies only the rounding. Adding the discretisation bound of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid} and of Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt}, bounds for the truncations of the theta series and of the $u$ axis, a bound on the change of the argument between grid points, and an explicit bound on the remainder of Proposition~\ref{prop:mass}, which is $O(\varepsilon^3)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:ch7:cubic}), turns positivity of $\rho_\varepsilon$ on $0<z\le200^2$ at each computed $\varepsilon$, the integer identity of Proposition~\ref{prop:zerodensity} and the HCM derivative signs of Section~\ref{sec:weak:hcm} into theorems on that window.

Above that window the evaluator changes. The tilted contour gives $25$ digits at height $5000$ and resolves the Lehmer pair near height $7005$ (Sections~\ref{sec:newcomp} and \ref{sec:ch5:lehmer}), but its number of nodes grows linearly with the height. Chirp transform batching of the P\'olya route, and the hybrid of Section~\ref{sec:ch5:hybrid}, a Riemann Siegel or Odlyzko Sch\"onhage evaluator at $\tfrac12+\varepsilon+i\theta$ with Odlyzko's zeros as the RH reference, would give the positivity margin, the matched total variation and the zero density scan near the $10^{12}$th \cite{Odlyzko87}, $10^{21}$st and $10^{22}$nd zeros \cite{Odlyzko01,OdlyzkoTables}. The same zeros would measure the cell constant of Section~\ref{ch:spacing} at large height, whose behaviour is open (Section~\ref{sec:ch15:spacings}), and allow a comparison of the cell errors with the prediction of the sine kernel process of the unitary ensemble.

\subsection{Calibration against known configurations}\label{sec:open:calib}

The diagnostics of Chapters~\ref{chap:measure} to \ref{chap:heat} are calibrated by running them on configurations whose zeros are known exactly. Section~\ref{ch:deconv} ran the posterior predictive check of \cite[Section 19.3.7]{P26} at $\varepsilon=0.1$ on one window and calibrated it with a displaced zero. Three extensions are open: smaller resolutions, where the signature of a displaced zero is largest and the P\'olya route supplies $\rho_\varepsilon$ to about $60$ digits below height $200$ (Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}), whereas the deconvolution used double precision; continuous atom positions, which would remove the baseline of the grid; and larger heights. The square root law \eqref{eq:ch8:threshold} of Section~\ref{sec:ch8:calibration}, which follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:signature}, sets what the check can detect.

The de Bruijn Newman flow of Section~\ref{ch:dbn} manufactures off line pairs in a controlled way. Running the diagnostics along the flow through the collision of the Lehmer pair near $7005$ would calibrate the zero density scan, the matched total variation and the negative mass.

Beurling generalised prime systems whose zeta functions provably have only real ordinates do not give positive Thorin measures, since without a Gamma factor the Thorin density is negative at every level above one (Remark~\ref{bsy:rem:beurling}). Measuring how fast the clock transforms of positive approximants approach $1/G$, in the distances of Section~\ref{ch:distances} and along a sequence with an accumulation point as the closure criterion of Corollary~\ref{cor:weak:closure} \cite[Propositions 18.10 and 18.14]{P26} allows, is the computational side of that criterion; the Kent approximants of Section~\ref{sec:ch18:kent} are the first instance.

Two computations of Chapter~\ref{chap:probability} remain at their edges. The Brownian boundary of Section~\ref{ch:boundaries} reproduces the law of the clock in the bulk, to $0.0020$ in Kolmogorov distance for the boundary of the Volterra equation and to $0.0059$ for the tangent approximation of Roberts and Shortland (Section~\ref{sec:ch19:zones}), but the zone of large times $t$ is checked by no simulation, since only about one path in ten million survives past $t=0.1$. Importance sampling with an exponentially tilted drift, or multilevel splitting, would check that zone and the logarithmic correction of the asymptote of the boundary directly. The Cauchy integral method of Section~\ref{ch:li} extends to $n$ in the thousands, at a cost of about $0.03n$ digits lost to cancellation. Comparing the P\'olya values of the Keiper Li coefficients with the sums $\sum_{\gamma>0}4\sin^2(n\arctan\frac1{2\gamma})$ over the zeros to large $n$ would test how the Li functional sees the zeros near height $n$.

\subsection{Testing stronger conjectures before pursuing them}\label{st:sec:wp5}

Conrey and Li showed that a positivity condition in de Branges' approach to RH is false even though RH may be true \cite{CL00}, so any intermediate statement proposed on the Thorin path should be tested numerically first. The candidates are a lower bound $\rho_{\alpha-1/2}(\theta)\ge c(\alpha)\log\theta$ outside a sparse set, the first of the four questions of Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly}; monotonicity or convexity of the margin in $\alpha$; positivity of the Thorin density for $\alpha$ slightly below one, uniformly on large windows; hyperbolic complete monotonicity of the Laplace transform $\varphi_\alpha$ of $T_\alpha$ below one; and variogram inequalities at single scales. The first two are partly settled: the lower bound holds for $\rho_{\alpha-1/2}$ itself outside a set of heights of density zero, although not near the zeros where Lemma~\ref{ag:lem:cancel} would need it (Remark~\ref{rem:dbeta:average}), and the margin divided by $\alpha-\frac12$ decreases in $\alpha$, with the ratio $\rho_{\alpha-1/2}(\theta)/(\alpha-\frac12)$ nonincreasing in $\alpha$ at every height outside the windows of the zeros off the line (Theorems~\ref{thm:dbeta:comp} and \ref{thm:dbeta:minall}). Each can be evaluated on the computed windows and at the heights of Odlyzko's tables, and discarded if it fails there.

The variogram inequalities can be tested in finite dimension: the reformulation of Section~\ref{ch:sin2more} poses feasibility problems: is the regularised pole mass dominated by the prime and archimedean mass on the cone of variograms of positive definite functions of a given dimension? Solving them with explicit dual certificates at increasing dimension would measure how the margin behaves.

The cross checks test the zeros themselves. Agreement of the twisted Riesz functions (Proposition~\ref{ri:prop:twisted}) with the explicit formula over the computed zeros is a check that shares no code or method with the computation of the zeros (Remark~\ref{ri:rem:indep}).

\subsection{Lessons from the computations}\label{ch:lessons}

Equivalent statements are not equally computable. The Thorin condition has at least eight equivalent faces: positivity of the Thorin measure, complete monotonicity of the heat trace, Stieltjes form of $G'/G$, Hankel positivity at one time, HCM of the clock, Lagarias positivity, Weil positivity and Li positivity. They are the same statement mathematically and differ computationally by many orders of magnitude in conditioning. The heat trace at moderate time loses about $87t$ digits to cancellation (Section~\ref{ch:arith}); the normalised minors of the even Hankel block at $t=0.05$ fall to $10^{-44}$ by size five (Section~\ref{ch:heat}); the Keiper Li coefficients weight a zero at height $\gamma$ by $n^2/\gamma^2$ until $n$ reaches $\gamma$ (Section~\ref{ch:li}); the Thorin density at resolution $\varepsilon$ is local in the ordinate and sees each zero at full size $1/\varepsilon$ (Section~\ref{ch:distances}). A computational attack should choose its face by conditioning; the local face is the Thorin density.

The primes compute the wrong end: the sine squared lemma is the right way to understand the structure and the wrong way to compute near the line, where $k$ digits at resolution $\varepsilon$ need primes up to about $10^{2k/\varepsilon}$ even when no zero lies to the right of the line (Section~\ref{sec:ch10:selberg}). Any argument that bounds prime sums to establish positivity near the line must overcome that exponential, and Section~\ref{ch:rz3} shows the same obstruction in another form (Proposition~\ref{rz:prop:bias}).

The heat trace meets the same obstruction. A bound on the prime sum that ignores the phases proves the positivity of the Gaussian smoothing of the zeros only up to a time $t_0(u)$ that grows doubly logarithmically in the height, and no bound uniform in the height does better, so a proof along the heat trace must use the phases (Section~\ref{sec:ch12:phasefree}).

Limits do not create positivity: it must be proved at every resolution, not in the limit. A proposal that reaches the Thorin condition as a limit must say where the positivity of its approximants comes from.

The perturbation theorem gives a test that a proposed proof of RH must pass. Replace $\xi$ by the function $F$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:ch13:perturb}, with $R$ above every height that the proof uses explicitly. A step that holds for $F$ as well as for $\xi$ cannot exclude zeros off the line, because $F$ has four. Such steps include the Riemann von Mangoldt formula with an explicit error, which $F$ satisfies with the error enlarged by one on $[A,B)$, the only heights where the two counts differ; the verified zeros below $R$; the small time expansion of the heat trace through the constant term, since $W_F-W=\big(\frac12(B-A)^2+\frac18\big)t+O(t^2)$; infinite divisibility, self decomposability and log convexity; the Hankel blocks of size $N$ when $R\ge T_N$ and the derivative signs up to order $10^{17}$ when $R\ge10^{12}$; and the order, type and functional equation. What separates $\xi$ from $F$ is arithmetic, the Euler product and the explicit formula with the prime weights $\Lambda(n)$, together with local counts of the zeros on the line in windows that contain the height $g$ of the quadruple (Section~\ref{sec:ch13:perturb}). A proof through the derivative signs can do without them only below the order at which the signs of $F$ first fail, which is about $t^*R^2$, with $t^*$ between $1.18$ and $4.03$ in the cases computed below height $2000$ and, by the model of Section~\ref{sec:ch13:perturb}, of order one or more at larger heights, far beyond $10^{17}$. The function $F$ exists unconditionally when $R+50\le3\times10^{12}$; for larger $R$ the test still applies, because a correct proof of RH makes the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:ch13:perturb}(b) true for every $R$. The criticality filter of Section~\ref{pk:sec:filter} is the companion test for properties of the P\'olya kernel: a step that holds for $e^{tv^2/4}\Phi(v)$ with some $t<0$, whose transform has nonreal zeros by the theorem of Rodgers and Tao, cannot be the step that excludes zeros off the line.
