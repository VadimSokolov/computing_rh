\section{The Dedekind eta analogue at the basepoint one}\label{ch:bpeta}

The $L$ function of the real character $\chi_{12}$ modulo $12$, to which Section~\ref{ch:chi12} carried the pipeline of the book, has no pole. Its completed function is entire and is a Mellin transform of the Dedekind eta function, whose product formula makes the kernel positive. We repeat for $L(s,\chi_{12})$ the calculations that Chapter~\ref{chap:basepoint} made for $\zeta$ at the basepoint one.

The scripts are in the folder \texttt{basepoint\_one/eta} of the archive.

\subsection{The completed \texorpdfstring{$L$}{L} function and its Thorin measure}\label{bp:eta:sec}

We use the completed function $\xi(s,\chi_{12})=(12/\pi)^{s/2}\Gamma(s/2)L(s,\chi_{12})$, its kernel $K$ and the variable $Y$ with density $K/\xi(\frac12,\chi_{12})$ of Section~\ref{sec:ch16:eta}; the function satisfies $\xi(s,\chi_{12})=\xi(1-s,\chi_{12})$ and is entire. We computed its zeros on the critical line by sign changes of $\xi(\frac12+it,\chi_{12})e^{\pi t/4}$ with \texttt{zeros12.py}, finding $297$ below height $340$; Section~\ref{sec:ch16:eta} lists the first of them. In this section $b_\alpha=(\xi'/\xi)(\alpha,\chi_{12})$ and $v_\alpha(\theta)=\re(\xi'/\xi)(\alpha+i\theta,\chi_{12})$, and $\varphi_\alpha$, $T_1$, $\widetilde T$, $\Pi$, $A$, $A_\alpha$, $Q$ and $a_k=-(\rho_k-\frac12)^2$ are formed with $\xi(s,\chi_{12})$ and its zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ in place of $\xi$. So is the tilted eta variable $V_\alpha$, the Esscher tilt of $Y$ by $e^{(\alpha-\frac12)Y}$, with $\E e^{sV_\alpha}=\xi(\alpha+s,\chi_{12})/\xi(\alpha,\chi_{12})$. The constants we need, computed by \texttt{base.py}, are
\[
L(1,\chi_{12})=\frac{\log(2+\sqrt3)}{\sqrt3}=0.760346,\qquad L'(1,\chi_{12})=0.362495,
\]
\[
b_1=\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1,\chi_{12})=\frac12\log\frac{12}\pi+\frac12\psi\Big(\frac12\Big)+\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi_{12})=\sum_\rho\frac1\rho=0.1650833123,
\]
where $\psi$ is the digamma function. The Taylor coefficients of $\xi(\frac12+s,\chi_{12})/\xi(\frac12,\chi_{12})$ give the power sums $p_1=\sum_ka_k^{-1}=0.1664618$ and $p_2=\sum_ka_k^{-2}=0.0055985$, which are $\sum_{\gamma>0}\gamma^{-2}$ and $\sum_{\gamma>0}\gamma^{-4}$ under GRH; $p_1$ is the mean of the centre clock.

\label{bp:eta:sec:thorin}

\begin{proposition}\label{bp:eta:prop:thorin}
The function $\varphi_1(u)=\xi(1,\chi_{12})/\xi(1+\sqrt u,\chi_{12})$ is the Laplace transform of a GGC with no drift, with Thorin density in $\theta=\sqrt z$
\[
\frac1\pi v_1(\theta)=\frac1\pi\re\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1+i\theta,\chi_{12})=\frac1\pi\sum_\rho\frac{1-\beta}{(1-\beta)^2+(\theta-\gamma)^2}>0,
\]
the sum over the nontrivial zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ of $L(s,\chi_{12})$ in conjugate pairs. Positivity is unconditional because $L(1+it,\chi_{12})\ne0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof is that of Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:thorin}, with the Hadamard product of the entire function $\xi(s,\chi_{12})$ in place of that of $\xi$.
\end{proof}

The minimum on $[0,60]$ is at the origin, $v_1(0)/\pi=b_1/\pi=0.0525477$, and the cumulative mass tracks the zero count: $\int_0^{60}v_1/\pi=35.81$ against $N_{\chi_{12}}(60)=36$, where $N_{\chi_{12}}(T)$ counts the zeros with $0<\gamma\le T$ as in Section~\ref{sec:ch16:eta}. Table~\ref{bp:eta:tab:rho}, computed by \texttt{base.py}, compares direct values with the Poisson sum over the $297$ computed zeros; the nearly constant difference of $1.11\times10^{-3}$ is the contribution of the zeros above height $340$. Figure~\ref{bp:eta:fig:density} compares the density with that of $\zeta$: on this window the zeros of $L(s,\chi_{12})$ are more than twice as dense, and its density is correspondingly higher. As for $\zeta$, $\varphi_1(u)=1-b_1\sqrt u+O(u)$, so $T_1$ has infinite mean and $P(T_1>x)\sim b_1(\pi x)^{-1/2}=0.09314\,x^{-1/2}$.

\subsection{The L\'evy form without a pole, at and below the basepoint one}\label{bp:eta:sec:levy}



\begin{proposition}\label{bp:eta:prop:levy}
For $\re s>0$,
\[
\log\frac{\xi(1+s,\chi_{12})}{\xi(1,\chi_{12})}=b_1s+\int_0^\infty(e^{-sx}-1+sx)\,\Pi(dx),
\]
where, with $\Lambda_{\chi_{12}}(n)=\chi_{12}(n)\Lambda(n)$,
\[
\Pi(dx)=\frac{e^{-x}}{x(1-e^{-2x})}\,dx+\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda_{\chi_{12}}(n)}{n\log n}\,\delta_{\log n}(dx),
\]
the prime sum converging conditionally, as a limit over $n\le X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Binet's formula \cite[\S5.9]{DLMF} for $\log\Gamma(\frac12+\frac s2)$, in the integrated form of the proof of Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:levy} with $a=\frac12$, gives the gamma density; the factor $(12/\pi)^{s/2}$ and the linear terms combine into $b_1$; and for $\re s>0$ the Euler product gives
\[
\log L(1+s,\chi_{12})=\sum_n\frac{\Lambda_{\chi_{12}}(n)}{n\log n}\,e^{-s\log n}.
\]
The linear coefficient is $-\sum_n\Lambda_{\chi_{12}}(n)/n=\frac{L'}L(1,\chi_{12})$, which converges by the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions \cite[Chapter~20]{Davenport}, in the form $\psi(y,\chi_{12})=O(y\,e^{-c\sqrt{\log y}})$, where $\psi(y,\chi_{12})=\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda_{\chi_{12}}(n)$ is the Chebyshev function of $\chi_{12}$.
\end{proof}

The form differs from that of $\zeta$ in two ways. There is no pole, so no compensating measure: the prime atoms converge on their own at the basepoint one. And the measure is signed for a different reason. For $\zeta$ the gamma density minus the pole term $dx/x$ is negative beyond $x=0.281$ and the prime atoms are positive; here the gamma density $e^{-x}/(x(1-e^{-2x}))$ is positive everywhere and the atoms carry the sign of $\chi_{12}(n)$ (Figure~\ref{bp:eta:fig:levy}, left). Table~\ref{bp:eta:tab:levy}, computed by \texttt{arith.py}, checks the form.

The tilted eta variable $V_1$, with moment generating function $\xi(1+s,\chi_{12})/\xi(1,\chi_{12})$, is not quasi infinitely divisible. Its positive and its negative atoms each have infinite total mass, since $\sum_{\chi_{12}(p)=1}1/p$ and $\sum_{\chi_{12}(p)=-1}1/p$ both diverge by Dirichlet's theorem, and they cancel only conditionally, so the argument of Proposition~\ref{sb:prop:qid}(3) applies with $\xi(1+s,\chi_{12})$ in place of $\xi(1+s)$, as the discussion after its proof explains. At every basepoint $\alpha>1$, by contrast, $V_\alpha$ is quasi infinitely divisible: there the atoms of each sign have finite total mass, and the Euler product writes its characteristic function as that of an infinitely divisible gamma piece times a quotient of two compound Poisson characteristic functions.

The sine squared lemma in the form of Section~\ref{bp:sec:levy}, with $\int_0^\infty\log(1+s^2/\theta^2)\,d\theta=\pi s$, converts the L\'evy form, with $s=\sqrt u$, into
\[
v_1(\theta)=b_1+A(\theta)+Q(\theta),
\]
with
\[
A(\theta)=\tfrac12\big[\re\psi(\tfrac{1+i\theta}2)-\psi(\tfrac12)\big],\qquad Q(\theta)=\sum_n\frac{\Lambda_{\chi_{12}}(n)}n\,2\sin^2\Big(\frac{\theta\log n}2\Big).
\]
The drift becomes the stable one half factor $e^{-b_1\sqrt u}$, the gamma factor the smooth positive growth $A$, and the primes the oscillating $Q$, now without any divergent partner. With primes to $10^7$ the formula gives $0.84633$ at $\theta=10$ against the exact $0.84649$.\ref{bp:eta:fig:levy} (right) shows the three pieces: the prime piece ranges over both signs, and the positivity of the sum comes from the drift and the gamma factor, as Section~\ref{sec:ch16:sin2} found at the basepoint two.

\label{bp:eta:sec:below}Below the basepoint one, at $\alpha=1-\varepsilon$, the converted Thorin density is $\pi^{-1}$ times
\[
b_\alpha+A_\alpha(\theta)+\lim_{X\to\infty}\sum_{n\le X}\frac{\Lambda_{\chi_{12}}(n)}{n^\alpha}\,2\sin^2\Big(\frac{\theta\log n}2\Big),
\]
with $A_\alpha(\theta)=\frac12[\re\psi(\frac{\alpha+i\theta}2)-\psi(\frac\alpha2)]$. Exactly as for $\zeta$ in Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:below}, the limit exists and gives the positive Thorin density $v_\alpha/\pi$ when $L(s,\chi_{12})$ has no zeros with $\re s>\alpha'$ for some $\alpha'<\alpha$, and conversely its existence for $\re s>0$ in the L\'evy form forces the absence of zeros with $\re s>\alpha$. The error at cutoff $X$ is given by the same explicit formula,
\[
\sum_\rho\Big[-\frac{X^{\rho-\alpha}}{\rho-\alpha}+\frac{X^{\rho-\alpha+i\theta}}{2(\rho-\alpha+i\theta)}+\frac{X^{\rho-\alpha-i\theta}}{2(\rho-\alpha-i\theta)}\Big]+O(X^{-\alpha}\log X),
\]
because $\psi(y,\chi_{12})=-\sum_\rho y^\rho/\rho+O(\log y)$ has no main term \cite[Chapter~19]{Davenport}.

For $\zeta$ the prime piece and the pole piece each grow like $X^{1-\alpha}/(1-\alpha)$ and must cancel; for $L(s,\chi_{12})$ the prime piece stays bounded. Table~\ref{bp:eta:tab:below}, computed by \texttt{arith.py}, shows the errors, which decay like $X^{1/2-\alpha}$, and what remains after subtracting the explicit formula over the $297$ computed zeros. The correction removes most of the error at $\theta=\gamma_1$; at $\theta=10$, between zeros, it helps less, because $297$ zeros are fewer than the $1700$ used for $\zeta$ and the missing zeros above height $340$ dominate what remains. At the centre and the first ordinate the sum grows like $\log X$: the atom $\delta_{\gamma_1}$ forming (Figure~\ref{bp:eta:fig:below}).

\subsection{The hitting time and its boundary}\label{bp:eta:sec:hit}

We computed the Brownian boundary $b(t)$ whose crossing time has the law of $T_1$ from the Volterra equation \eqref{eq:ch19:volterra}, with the distribution function from Talbot inversion for $t<0.12$ and, under GRH, from the subordination representation of Theorem~\ref{bp:thm:sub} beyond; its time scale is about seven times that of $\zeta$, the ratio of the means of the two centre clocks. In the right tail the exact boundary flattens at $-0.2747$, the Brownian form of the stable one half tail.

\subsection{The two examples compared}\label{bp:sec:compare}

Table~\ref{bp:eta:tab:cmp} sets the two basepoint one examples side by side. Structurally the eta case is the cleaner of the two: below the basepoint one its prime sums stay bounded, so the computation is far better conditioned. Nothing essential becomes easier, however. The negativity that $\zeta$ concentrates in the pole piece is carried here by the signs of $\chi_{12}(p)$; and, under GRH for $\chi_{12}$, the probabilistic picture is the same, with every constant scaled by the denser zeros.

The comparison confirms the two conclusions that fix the shape of the path to RH. First, at the basepoint one the constructions for the reciprocal Xi function are, with two exceptions, unconditional and explicit. The exceptions are the Krein string representation of Corollary~\ref{bp:cor:kent}, which assumes RH and simple zeros, and the Wald defect $\sum_\rho\rho^{-1}/\sqrt2$: Lemma~\ref{bp:lem:wald} gives it for a boundary bounded at infinity, and the boundedness of the computed Brownian boundary is observed, not proved. Second, below the basepoint one each positive statement for $\zeta$ holds, up to the boundary case, exactly when no zero lies to the right of the line $\re s=\alpha$ (Propositions~\ref{bp:prop:below} and \ref{wr:prop:wiener}, Theorems~\ref{bp:thm:alpha} and \ref{sm:thm:strip}). The eta example shows that the pole changes how the prime sums converge, not where they stop.
