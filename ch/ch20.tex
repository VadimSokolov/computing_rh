\section{Keiper Li coefficients from the P\'olya density}\label{ch:li}

The P\'olya density that computes the law of the clock also computes the coefficients of Li's criterion \cite{Li97}, which states that RH holds if and only if
\[
\lambda_n=\sum_\rho\Big[1-\Big(1-\frac1\rho\Big)^n\Big]\ge0\qquad\text{for every }n\ge1,
\]
the sum over the zeros taken in conjugate pairs. Keiper \cite{Keiper92} introduced the coefficients $\lambda_n$ and computed them before Li proved the criterion, and Bombieri and Lagarias \cite{BL99} derived an arithmetic expression for them from the explicit formula and related the criterion to Weil's.

\subsection{Taylor coefficients of \texorpdfstring{$\log\xi$}{log xi} at \texorpdfstring{$s=1$}{s=1}}\label{sec:ch20:compute}

Li \cite{Li97} expresses the coefficients as derivatives at $s=1$,
\[
\lambda_n=\frac1{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}\Big[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1}.
\]
With $\log\xi(1+w)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kw^k$ and $(1+w)^{n-1}=\sum_j\binom{n-1}jw^j$, comparing the coefficients of $w^n$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ch20:li}
\lambda_n=n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}j\,a_{n-j},
\end{equation}
so it suffices to compute the Taylor coefficients of $\log\xi$ at $s=1$. The zeros nearest to $s=1$ are $\frac12\pm14.1347i$, so $\log\xi(1+w)$ is analytic in the disc $|w|<R$, where $R=|\rho_1-1|=14.1436$.

The sum \eqref{eq:ch20:li} is exact, but its terms can be larger than $\lambda_n$. The Hadamard product $\xi(1+w)=\xi(1)\prod_\rho(1-w/(\rho-1))$ gives $ma_m=-\sum_\rho(\rho-1)^{-m}$ for $m\ge2$, and for large $m$ the two zeros nearest to $s=1$ dominate, so $|ma_m|$ is at most about $2R^{-m}$. Since $n\binom{n-1}{m-1}=m\binom nm$, the terms of \eqref{eq:ch20:li} are $\binom nm\,ma_m$ with $m=n-j$, and for large $n$ their absolute sum is at most about $2(1+1/R)^n$. Cancellation therefore costs at most about $\log_{10}\big(2(1+1/R)^n/\lambda_n\big)$ digits. This grows like $n\log_{10}(1+1/R)\approx0.03n$, about one digit at $n=100$, where $2(1+1/R)^{100}\approx1.9\times10^3$ and $\lambda_{100}=118.6$.

We compute the $a_k$ by the trapezoid rule for the Cauchy integral on the circle $|w|=6$,
\[
a_k\approx\frac1{Nr^k}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\log\xi\big(1+re^{2\pi ij/N}\big)e^{-2\pi ijk/N},\qquad r=6,\ N=512,
\]
which converges geometrically. The rule returns $a_k+a_{k+N}r^N+a_{k+2N}r^{2N}+\cdots$, so aliasing changes $a_k$ by a relative amount of about $(r/R)^N\approx10^{-191}$. Rounding errors are amplified in $a_k$ by about $(R/r)^k$, which is $10^{37}$ at $k=100$ and is absorbed by the working precision.

Each value $\xi(1+w)=\xi(\frac12+(\frac12+w))$ comes from the P\'olya route at $600$ bits; the imaginary part of $\frac12+w$ never exceeds $6$, so the P\'olya sum loses almost nothing to cancellation. The principal branch of the logarithm is the analytic one, because $|\arg\xi(1+w)|<1$ on the circle: in the Hadamard product \eqref{eq:ch2:hadamard} with $s=\frac12+w$, so that $|s|\le6.5$, the factor $1+s^2/\gamma^2$ of the zeros $\frac12\pm i\gamma$ has argument at most $\arcsin(6.5^2/\gamma^2)\le1.01\cdot6.5^2/\gamma^2$, and since $\sum_{\gamma>0}\gamma^{-2}=0.0231$ these arguments add up to less than $0.99$.

The first coefficient has the closed form $\lambda_1=1+\frac{\gamma_E}2-\frac12\log4\pi$, where $\gamma_E$ is Euler's constant. The P\'olya computation gives
\[
\lambda_1=0.02309570896612103381431025,
\]
in agreement with the closed form to all twenty five digits. The same number $\lambda_1=\sum_\rho\rho^{-1}=(\xi'/\xi)(1)$ reappears at the basepoint one (Section~\ref{bp:sec:thorin}): the shifted clock $T_1$ there, with Laplace transform $\xi(1)/\xi(1+\sqrt u)=1-\lambda_1\sqrt u+O(u)$ near $u=0$, has a tail of order $x^{-1/2}$, and $\lambda_1/\pi$ is its Thorin density at the origin in the chart $\theta=\sqrt z$. Table~\ref{tab:ch20:li} lists further values with Keiper's asymptotic $\lambda_n\approx\frac n2(\log n-1-\log2\pi+\gamma_E)$ \cite{Keiper92}.

\subsection{Li's criterion as positivity of the Thorin functional}\label{sec:ch20:thorin}

Li's criterion is a statement about the Thorin functional $U$ of Section~\ref{ch:arith}, which has a unit atom at $-(\rho-\frac12)^2$ for each pair $\{\rho,1-\rho\}$ of zeros, on the line or off it. For a zero $\rho$ put $r=1-1/\rho$. Then $1-1/(1-\rho)=1/r$, so the pair contributes $2-r^n-r^{-n}$ to $\lambda_n$; and if $a=-(\rho-\frac12)^2$, with the square root chosen so that $\rho-\frac12=i\sqrt a$, then $r=(2\sqrt a+i)/(2\sqrt a-i)$. This proves the following.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ch20:thorin}
Without any hypothesis on the zeros,
\[
\lambda_n=\sum_{\{\rho,1-\rho\}}\varphi_n\Big(-\big(\rho-\tfrac12\big)^2\Big)=\langle U,\varphi_n\rangle,
\]
where
\[
\varphi_n(z)=2-\Big(\frac{2\sqrt z+i}{2\sqrt z-i}\Big)^n-\Big(\frac{2\sqrt z-i}{2\sqrt z+i}\Big)^n
\]
is a rational function of $z$, and $\varphi_n(z)=4\sin^2\big(n\arctan\frac1{2\sqrt z}\big)\ge0$ for $z>0$.
\end{proposition}

The two fractions are exchanged when $\sqrt z$ changes sign, which is why $\varphi_n$ is rational in $z$; it has a pole of order $n$ at $z=-\frac14$. At infinity $\varphi_n(z)\sim n^2/z$, so the sum converges absolutely.

For $z>0$ the two fractions are conjugate numbers of modulus one. On the critical line, for $\rho=\frac12+i\gamma$ with $\gamma>0$, the number $1-1/\rho=(\rho-1)/\rho$ has modulus one and argument $\phi_\gamma=2\arctan\frac1{2\gamma}$, and the conjugate zero gives $-\phi_\gamma$. Adding the contributions of $\rho$ and $\bar\rho$,
\[
\lambda_n=\sum_{\gamma>0}2\big(1-\cos(n\phi_\gamma)\big)=\sum_{\gamma>0}4\sin^2\Big(n\arctan\frac1{2\gamma}\Big)\qquad\text{under RH}.
\]
Li's criterion thus says that RH holds if and only if the Thorin functional is nonnegative on the cone spanned by the $\varphi_n$.

The test functions are Laplace transforms, so Li's coefficients can be read off the heat trace $W$ of Section~\ref{ch:heat} and off the clock tilted at $u=\frac14$ \cite{P26pg3}. Let $q(y)=y/(1-y)^2$ and define polynomials $\ell_n$ by
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\ell_n(t)\,y^{n-1}=q'(y)\,e^{-tq(y)}=\frac{1+y}{(1-y)^3}\exp\Big(-\frac{ty}{(1-y)^2}\Big),
\]
so that $\ell_1=1$, $\ell_2=4-t$, $\ell_3=9-6t+\frac12t^2$, and $\ell_n$ has degree $n-1$ and leading coefficient $(-1)^{n-1}/(n-1)!$. Let $\widetilde T$ be the Esscher tilt of the clock $T$ at $u=\frac14$ (the variable $\widetilde T$ of Theorem~\ref{bp:thm:sub}), the law $e^{-x/4}P(T\in dx)/\E e^{-T/4}$, with Laplace transform $G(\frac14)/G(\frac14+u)$, and write $\kappa_j(\widetilde T)$ for its cumulants.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ch20:heat}
Without any hypothesis on the zeros:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\varphi_n(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{-(z+\frac14)t}\ell_n(t)\,dt$ for $\re z>-\frac14$, and hence
\[
\lambda_n=\int_0^\infty W(t)\,e^{-t/4}\,\ell_n(t)\,dt ;
\]
\item[(ii)] $\kappa_j(\widetilde T)=\int_0^\infty t^{j-1}e^{-t/4}W(t)\,dt=(j-1)!\sum_{\{\rho,1-\rho\}}\big(\rho(1-\rho)\big)^{-j}$ for $j\ge1$, and
\[
\lambda_n=\sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{j+1}\frac n{j!}\binom{n+j-1}{2j-1}\,\kappa_j(\widetilde T),\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) For $z>0$ put $r=(2\sqrt z+i)/(2\sqrt z-i)$, so that $\varphi_n(z)=2-r^n-r^{-n}$ and $r+r^{-1}=2-1/(z+\frac14)$. Then $(1-ry)(1-y/r)=(1-y)^2\big(z+\frac14+q(y)\big)/(z+\frac14)$, and for small $|y|$
\[
\begin{aligned}\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi_n(z)\,y^{n-1}&=\frac d{dy}\log\frac{(1-ry)(1-y/r)}{(1-y)^2}=\frac{q'(y)}{z+\frac14+q(y)}\\&=\int_0^\infty e^{-(z+\frac14)t}\,q'(y)e^{-tq(y)}\,dt .\end{aligned}
\]
Comparing coefficients gives the formula for $\varphi_n(z)$ when $z>0$, and both sides are analytic in $\re z>-\frac14$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:ch20:thorin}, $\lambda_n$ is the sum of $\varphi_n(a)$ over $a=-(\rho-\frac12)^2$, one for each pair $\{\rho,1-\rho\}$. For $\rho=\frac12+\delta+i\gamma$ with $|\delta|<\frac12$ one has $\re a+\frac14>\gamma^2$, and $\int_0^\infty e^{-ct}|\ell_n(t)|\,dt=O(1/c)$ as $c\to\infty$, so the sum of the integrals converges absolutely, and $W(t)=\sum e^{-at}$ gives the formula for $\lambda_n$.
(ii) Since $1+w=1/(1-y)$ for $w=y/(1-y)$, and $[y^n]w^k=\binom{n-1}{n-k}$, \eqref{eq:ch20:li} says $\lambda_n=n[y^n]\log\xi\big(1/(1-y)\big)$. With $\sigma=1/(1-y)$ one has $\sigma(\sigma-1)=q(y)$, so $\sigma=\frac12+\big(\frac14+q(y)\big)^{1/2}$ and $\log\xi(\sigma)=\log\xi(\frac12)+\log G\big(\frac14+q(y)\big)$ near $y=0$. By Section~\ref{ch:heat},
\[
\log\frac{G(\frac14+v)}{G(\frac14)}=-\log\E e^{-v\widetilde T}=\int_0^\infty\big(1-e^{-vt}\big)e^{-t/4}W(t)\,\frac{dt}t=\sum_{j\ge1}(-1)^{j+1}\kappa_j(\widetilde T)\,\frac{v^j}{j!},
\]
which gives the first formula for $\kappa_j(\widetilde T)$; integrating $W(t)=\sum e^{-at}$ term by term, with $a+\frac14=\rho(1-\rho)$, gives the second. Finally $[y^n]q(y)^j=[y^{n-j}](1-y)^{-2j}=\binom{n+j-1}{2j-1}$.
\end{proof}

In terms of the sums $S_j=\sum\big(\rho(1-\rho)\big)^{-j}$, the relation (ii) is that of Voros \cite[Section 8.6.2]{Voros10}; the proposition adds that $(j-1)!\,S_j$ are the cumulants of a probability law, the clock tilted to the point $u=\frac14$, which $\sigma=\frac12+\sqrt u$ sends to $\sigma=1$. The relation is triangular with diagonal $(-1)^{n+1}/(n-1)!$, so the $\lambda_n$ and the cumulants of $\widetilde T$ determine each other; in particular $\E\widetilde T=\lambda_1$ and $\operatorname{Var}\widetilde T=4\lambda_1-\lambda_2=3.71006364\times10^{-5}$. From $100$ independently computed cumulants, (ii) reproduces all $20$ digits of Table~\ref{tab:ch20:li} (the rows $n=1,2,3,5,10,20,50,100$), and (i), with $W$ from \eqref{eq:ch2:Warith} for $t<0.004$ and from the zeros above, reproduces $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_4$ to $30$ digits (computed).

Under RH, $W(t)=\sum_{\gamma>0}e^{-\gamma^2t}$, and (i) returns the sum of the nonnegative terms $\varphi_n(\gamma^2)$ displayed above. The functional $f\mapsto\int_0^\infty f(t)e^{-t/4}\ell_n(t)\,dt$ is nonnegative on every completely monotone $f$ for which it converges absolutely, by Bernstein's theorem and $\varphi_n\ge0$ on $[0,\infty)$. It is not nonnegative on nonnegative $f$, since $\ell_2<0$ on $(4,\infty)$, nor, in general, on $k$ monotone $f$: $(1-t/20)_+^2$ is $3$ monotone, and
\[
\int_0^{20}e^{-t/4}\,\ell_3(t)\Big(1-\frac t{20}\Big)^2dt=\frac{4-2824e^{-5}}{25}<0 .
\]
Read on $W$, Li's criterion asks for the complete monotonicity of Section~\ref{ch:heat}, which positivity of $W$ does not give.

The test function $\varphi_n$ is about $n^2/z$ for $\sqrt z\gg n$ and oscillates between $0$ and $4$ for $\sqrt z\lesssim n/\pi$, so $\lambda_n$ weighs the zeros below height of order $n$ at full size and the higher zeros by about $n^2/\gamma^2$ (Figure~\ref{fig:ch20:test}). Every computed $\lambda_n$ is positive, as it must be: every zero below height $3\times10^{12}$ lies on the line \cite{PT21} and contributes a nonnegative term, while a zero at height $\gamma\gg n$ contributes only about $n^2/\gamma^2$ in absolute value, so the zeros above that height cannot change the sign of $\lambda_n$ for $n\le100$.

An off line zero makes $|1-1/\rho|\ne1$. For $\rho=\frac12-d+i\gamma$ with $d>0$ one has $|1-1/\rho|^2=1+2d/((\frac12-d)^2+\gamma^2)$, so for large $n$ the term $(1-1/\rho)^n$ grows like $e^{nd/\gamma^2}$, which would eventually make $\lambda_n$ negative (Exercise~\ref{ex:ch20:offline}). The index at which this happens grows with the height of the zero, so finitely many coefficients, like the finite tests of Section~\ref{ch:finite} (Table~\ref{tab:ch13:see}), cannot exclude an off line zero at great height.

Summing $4\sin^2(n\arctan\frac1{2\gamma})$ over the first $1700$ zeros, computed with \texttt{mpmath} \cite{mpmath} as in Section~\ref{ch:distances}, and adding the tail above $\gamma_{1700}$ with the smooth zero density $\log(\gamma/2\pi)/2\pi$ gives the P\'olya values with relative residuals near $5\times10^{-6}$ for $n\le20$ and $10^{-5}$ at $n=100$. The absolute residual grows like $n^2$ and comes from the cutoff: at $\gamma_{1700}$ the zero count exceeds its smooth part by about one half, so the smooth tail counts about half a term, $\frac12\varphi_n(\gamma_{1700}^2)\approx n^2/(2\gamma_{1700}^2)$, too many.

As a diagnostic $\lambda_n$ is poorly localised: it behaves like the heat trace and Bernstein diagnostics of Table~\ref{tab:ch3:diag} and unlike the Thorin density, which is local in the ordinate.

Weil's criterion has the same form as Li's, with the functions of the class $\mathcal T$ of Section~\ref{ch:arith} that are nonnegative on $(0,\infty)$ in place of the $\varphi_n$ (Section~\ref{sec:ch10:positivity}), but the two cones are different. The pole of $\varphi_n$ lies at $-\frac14$, the point at which \eqref{eq:ch14:U} evaluates its test functions, inside the region where $\mathcal T$ requires holomorphy, and $\varphi_n$ decays only like $1/z$. So $\varphi_n$ is not in $\mathcal T$, $\langle U,\varphi_n\rangle$ is defined through the zeros, and the prime side of $\lambda_n$ is the regularised arithmetic formula of Bombieri and Lagarias \cite{BL99}.
