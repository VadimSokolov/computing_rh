\chapter{The P\'olya density as a maximum order statistic and the Thorin measure of the clock}\label{chap:maxwald}\chaptermark{The P\'olya density as a maximum}

This chapter is based on \cite{P26mw}. It starts from one observation: the law behind the P\'olya kernel of $\Xi$ is built from the maximum $M_4$ of independent exponential variables of Section~\ref{sec:maxexp:products}, in the sense that $e^{2Y}$ is a sum of two independent copies of $M_4$ (Proposition~\ref{mw:prop:sum}), and it asks which parts of that observation survive the passage to the Thorin measure of the clock, with Laplace transform $1/G(\lambda)=\xi(\frac12)/\xi(\frac12+\sqrt\lambda)$. The unconditional statements about the clock one level below the GGC property are those of Section~\ref{ch:heat}. The last sections read the P\'olya density as an extreme value law: each term of its theta series is a combination of two Gumbel kernels, which gives explicit approximations, a Hankel test from kernel moments and a reading of the clock as the hitting time of a Krein string.
