\chapter{The Thorin measure at finite resolution}\label{chap:measure}

The Thorin condition is RH (Proposition~\ref{prop:rigid}), and by Lagarias's criterion RH is the positivity of $\rho_\varepsilon$ at every resolution (Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline}); the question is what finite computations of $\rho_\varepsilon$ can establish about it. The answer, which Chapters~\ref{chap:hcm} and \ref{chap:heat} complete, has two sides. On every window where the zeros are verified, the computed Thorin measure $\mu_\varepsilon$ converges to $U_{\RH}$, the discrete measure demanded by RH, at explicit rates as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Above the computed range every finite computation of the kinds in this book remains consistent with a failure of the Thorin condition, and the step that remains, positivity at every resolution and every height, is not a computational one (Section~\ref{ch:finite}).
