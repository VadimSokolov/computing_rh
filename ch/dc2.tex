\section{The ladder}\label{dc:sec:ladder}

Restrict the support of $h$. For $a>0$ and $\varepsilon\ge0$ let
\begin{multline*}
\lambda_{\min}\big(\tfrac12+\varepsilon,a\big)=\inf\Big\{\frac{Q_\varepsilon(h)}{\|h\|_2^2}:h\text{ bounded, real, of bounded variation,}\\ \text{supported in }[-a,a],\ h\ne0\Big\}.
\end{multline*}
This is the least eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\alpha,a)$, $\alpha=\frac12+\varepsilon$, of Chapter~\ref{chap:yoshida}. The function $e^{-\varepsilon|x|}(h*\tilde h)$ is supported in $[-2a,2a]$, so $Q_\varepsilon$ involves only the prime powers $n\le e^{2a}$: each $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)$ is the bottom of the spectrum of an explicit form. RH is $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)\ge0$ for every $a$. Yoshida~\cite[Theorem 1]{Yoshida92} proved positivity of Weil's form for $a\le\frac12\log2$, the range in which no prime enters, Bombieri~\cite{Bombieri} treated other test functions of small support, and Connes and Consani reproved Yoshida's range from the semilocal trace formula~\cite{ConnesConsani21}. Zhu certifies the lower bound $8.9\times10^{-18}$ for $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)$ at $a=0.8$ (Corollary 6.3 of \cite{Zhu26}), and reports an empirical law for the decay of $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)$~\cite{Zhu26} (Chapter~\ref{chap:zhu}), and Connes, Consani and Moscovici find that the Fourier transform of the lowest eigenvector of a truncated form reproduces the low zeros to high accuracy~\cite{CCM25}. The last two papers are cited as preprints.

\begin{theorem}[The ladder at depth $\varepsilon>0$]\label{dc:thm:ladder}
Let $\varepsilon>0$ be to the right of the zeros, and suppose that $v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(\theta)>v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(0)$ for $\theta\ne0$, with $v_{1/2+\varepsilon}''(0)>0$ and $\liminf_{|\theta|\to\infty}v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(\theta)>v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(0)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)$ is nonincreasing in $a$, and $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)\ge2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}>0$ for every $a$.
\item[(b)] $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)\to2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ as $a\to\infty$, and there are constants $c,a_0>0$ with
\[
\frac{c}{a^2}\le\lambda_{\min}\big(\tfrac12+\varepsilon,a\big)-2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}\le\frac{\pi^2v_{1/2+\varepsilon}''(0)/4+o(1)}{a^2}\qquad(a\ge a_0).
\]
\item[(c)] At $\varepsilon=0$, with none of the hypotheses above, under RH, $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)\ge0$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)=O(a^{-N})$ for every $N$, so $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)\to0$; if RH fails, $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)<0$ for all large $a$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) is Theorem~\ref{yo:thm:coercive}(i) of Chapter~\ref{chap:yoshida} at $\alpha=\frac12+\varepsilon$, whose $m_\alpha=\inf_\theta v_\alpha(\theta)$ is $b_\alpha$ here, the minimum being at the origin; and $b_{1/2+\varepsilon}=\sum_\rho\re(\frac12+\varepsilon-\rho)^{-1}>0$ because every term is positive. We use the first step of that proof: by~\eqref{dc:eq:Qw} and Parseval, $\|\hat h\|_2^2=2\pi\|h\|_2^2$, so $Q_\varepsilon(h)/\|h\|_2^2=2\langle v_{1/2+\varepsilon}\rangle_h$, the average of $v_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ against the probability density $|\hat h|^2/\|\hat h\|_2^2$, which is at least $2\min v_{1/2+\varepsilon}=2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}$.

(b) Write $v=v_{1/2+\varepsilon}$. Upper bound: take $h(u)=\cos(\pi u/2a)$ on $[-a,a]$. Then $\langle\theta^2\rangle_h=\|h'\|_2^2/\|h\|_2^2=\pi^2/4a^2$, and the normalised density satisfies $|\hat h(\theta)|^2/\|\hat h\|_2^2=O(a^{-3}\theta^{-4})$ for $|\theta|\ge1/a$. Write $v(\theta)=v(0)+\frac12v''(0)\theta^2+R(\theta)$, with $|R(\theta)|\le C\theta^4$ for $|\theta|\le\eta$ and $|R(\theta)|\le C\theta^2\log(2+|\theta|)$ for all $\theta$. Splitting at $|\theta|=\eta$ gives $\langle R\rangle_h=O(\eta a^{-3})+O(a^{-3}\eta^{-1})$, and with $\eta=a^{-1/2}$ this is $o(a^{-2})$. Hence $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)\le2v(0)+v''(0)\pi^2/4a^2+o(a^{-2})$. Lower bound: by the hypotheses there is $c_1>0$ with $v(\theta)-v(0)\ge c_1\min(\theta^2,1)$. For $h$ supported in $[-a,a]$ the fraction of $\|\hat h\|_2^2$ carried by $|\theta|\le\kappa/a$ is at most the largest eigenvalue $\ell_0(\kappa)$ of the time and band limiting operator with product $\kappa$, which is less than $1$ for every finite $\kappa$ and tends to $0$ with $\kappa$~\cite{SlepianPollak61}. So $\langle\min(\theta^2,1)\rangle_h\ge(\kappa/a)^2(1-\ell_0(\kappa))$ once $\kappa\le a$, which is the lower bound with $c=2c_1\kappa^2(1-\ell_0(\kappa))$. Convergence $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)\to2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ follows from the two bounds.

(c) Under RH, $Q_0(h)=\sum_\gamma|\hat h(\gamma)|^2\ge0$ by Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:weil}. Fix a real $h_1\in C_c^\infty[-1,1]$, $h_1\ne0$, and put $h_a(u)=h_1(u/a)$. Then $\hat h_a(\theta)=a\hat h_1(a\theta)$ with $|\hat h_1(t)|\le C_N|t|^{-N}$, so $Q_0(h_a)/\|h_a\|_2^2\le C_N'a^{1-2N}\sum_\gamma\gamma^{-2N}$, every $|\gamma|$ being larger than $14$. If RH fails, Weil's criterion gives $h\in C_c^\infty$, which may be taken real, with $Q_0(h)<0$; it is supported in some $[-a_0,a_0]$, and by monotonicity $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)\le\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a_0)<0$ for $a\ge a_0$.
\end{proof}

At $\varepsilon=\frac12$ the hypotheses of the theorem hold with no assumption on the zeros: the level is to the right of the zeros because $\zeta$ has no zeros on $\re s=1$, and Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:v1min} gives the minimum at the origin, with $v_1''(0)>0$, and in its proof $v_1>0.05>b_1$ for $|\theta|\ge2000$, which is the third condition. For $0<\varepsilon<\frac12$ the level is to the right of the zeros exactly when the closed half plane $\re s\ge\frac12+\varepsilon$ is free of zeros, which the announced half plane gives only for $\varepsilon>\frac38$; the minimum at the origin is certified for $0<|\theta|\le H_0-\frac{1+\sqrt2}2$, $H_0=3\times10^{12}$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:dbeta:minall}(ii) (Table~\ref{tab:dbeta:minall}), and its holding on every line with $\frac12<\alpha<1$ jointly is equivalent to RH (part (vi) there). The third condition is not known for $0<\varepsilon<\frac12$ without RH; under RH it holds, because $v_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ is then a sum of Poisson kernels of the zeros and tends to infinity. For $\varepsilon=\frac12$ the value $2b_1=2\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(1)=2+\gamma_E-\log4\pi=0.0461914\ldots$ is the sharp constant of Corollary~\ref{yo:cor:half}, $Q_{1/2}(h)\ge2b_1\|h\|_2^2$ for every bounded real $h$ of bounded variation supported in $[-a,a]$ and every $a>0$; what the ladder adds is the rate in $a$ of Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}(b).

\subsection{Numerical results}\label{dc:sec:numerics}

We computed $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)$ variationally, with $h$ in the span of the first $M$ Legendre polynomials $q_0,\dots,q_{M-1}$ on $[-a,a]$, normalised in $L^2$: the correlations $q_j*\tilde q_k$ by Gauss Legendre quadrature, which is exact for polynomials; the archimedean and pole integrals by Gauss Legendre quadrature on $[0,2a]$, plus the exact tail of the $g(0)$ term; and the primes exactly. We repeat this for $M=24$ to $128$ and compute the same eigenvalue on a second basis, the normalised Fourier modes $e^{i\pi nu/a}$, $|n|\le N$, for $N=24$ to $1536$, from the matrices of Chapter~\ref{chap:yoshida}. Every computed eigenvalue is a variational upper bound for $\lambda_{\min}$ and needs no hypothesis on the zeros, because $Q_\varepsilon$ is an explicit finite form; the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder} enter only in what the numbers are compared with. Rounding is not the limit: double precision agrees with thirty digit arithmetic to $1.4\times10^{-14}$ at most, and $1.5$ times the quadrature nodes changes the eigenvalue by at most $2\times10^{-15}$. The limit is the size of the basis.

Between $M=24$ and $M=32$ the Legendre bounds change by at most $2.6\times10^{-6}$, but they are not converged at that level: for $\varepsilon>0$ the $M=32$ values lie above the converged values defined next by $0.6\times10^{-7}$ to $1.8\times10^{-6}$, so the change between two sizes understates the error. The Fourier sections are nested, and their eigenvalues converge like a power, $\lambda_N-\lambda_\infty\propto N^{-p}$ with $p$ between $1.18$ and $1.69$ over the whole grid. The \emph{converged value} is the power law extrapolation from the three largest sizes $N=384$, $768$, $1536$, and its uncertainty is the shift of that value when the largest size is dropped (sizes $192$, $384$, $768$): at most $2.7\times10^{-8}$ at $a=0.25$, $3.0\times10^{-9}$ at $a=0.5$ and $1.7\times10^{-9}$ for $a\ge0.75$. This is an error estimate, not a bound, and two checks support it. The Legendre bound with $M=128$ lies above the converged value in all $40$ entries with $\varepsilon>0$, by $1.8\times10^{-8}$ to $1.5\times10^{-7}$, as an upper bound must. And the converged values of Table~\ref{yo:tab:lam} of Chapter~\ref{chap:yoshida}, obtained there by Richardson extrapolation of the sizes $N=96$, $192$, $384$, agree with the values computed here for the same cells to $2.0\times10^{-8}$ at most over its twenty entries with $\alpha>\frac12$ and to $4.1\times10^{-9}$ for $a\ge0.8$.

For every level in Table~\ref{dc:tab:ladder} the converged values decrease to $2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ and never cross it (nor does any computed upper bound, in either basis), as Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}(a) predicts when its hypotheses hold; at $\varepsilon=\frac12$ this is Corollary~\ref{yo:cor:half}, and at $\varepsilon=\frac38$ the announced half plane gives $\lambda_{\min}(\frac78,a)\ge0$ (Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:damped}), while $\lambda_{\min}\ge2b_{7/8}$ needs in addition the minimum of $v_{7/8}$ at the origin, which Theorem~\ref{thm:dbeta:minall}(ii) certifies only up to height $3\times10^{12}$. The margin is linear in $\varepsilon$. With no hypothesis $2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}\le2V_\xi\,\varepsilon$, where $V_\xi=(\log\xi)''(\frac12)=0.04620998623$ is the constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:dbeta:minall}(i), and $2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}/\varepsilon\to2V_\xi=0.0924200$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$; under RH $V_\xi=2p_1$ with $p_1=\sum_{\gamma>0}\gamma^{-2}=0.0231050$.

The rate of approach changes character at the line. Table~\ref{dc:tab:rate} compares the scaled excess $a^2(\lambda_{\min}(\frac12+\varepsilon,a)-2b_{1/2+\varepsilon})$ of the converged values with the upper constant $\bar c_\varepsilon=\pi^2v_{1/2+\varepsilon}''(0)/4$ of Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}(b): the ratio falls towards $1$ as $a$ grows. Upper bounds approaching the constant from above cannot tell whether the limit is that constant or a smaller one, so we fit the converged values: a least squares fit on $2\le a\le7$ (prime powers to $e^{14}$ at $a=7$) of $a^2(\lambda_{\min}-2b_{1/2+\varepsilon})=\bar c_\varepsilon(C_0+C_1/a+C_2/a^2)$ gives $C_0=1.0002$, $1.0003$, $1.0005$ at $\varepsilon=\frac12$, $0.2$, $0.1$, with an uncertainty from the errors of the data of $0.00004$, $0.0001$, $0.0002$, and over the choice of the window ($a\ge1.5$, $2$ or $3$) and of the degree ($2$ or $3$) $C_0$ ranges over $0.9999$ to $1.0004$, $0.9995$ to $1.0010$ and $0.9991$ to $1.0016$. The data support the limit $\bar c_\varepsilon$ to within $0.04$ to $0.2$ percent, with a positive correction $C_1=0.139$, $0.159$, $0.166$ and $C_2=0.037$, $0.045$, $0.051$ and residuals below $1.1\times10^{-4}\,\bar c_\varepsilon$; the term in $a^{-2}$ is needed, a fit by $C_0+C_1/a$ alone being rejected by its residuals. This is numerical evidence and not a proof (Problem~\ref{dc:prob:rate}). Numerically, $\bar c_\varepsilon$ is not a bound at finite $a$: the scaled excess exceeds it at every computed $a$ ($1.024\,\bar c_\varepsilon$ at $a=6$, $\varepsilon=\frac12$).

The decay of the rung at $\varepsilon=0$ is stated four ways in this book: Section~\ref{cp:sec:weil} observes that the least eigenvalue on hat functions falls roughly geometrically in the support length $L=2a$, up to $L=8$; Chapter~\ref{chap:yoshida} observes that the finite sections follow $\exp(-2c\,ae^{2a})$ with $4.1\le c\le5.1$ for $0.25\le a\le0.8$, and does not prove it; Zhu proves, under RH and for large $a$, that the least eigenvalue over real even test functions, and so $\lambda_{\min}(\frac12,a)$, is at most $\exp(-ae^a)$ (Theorem 1.3 of \cite{Zhu26}, Chapter~\ref{chap:zhu}, a preprint); and Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}(c) proves, under RH, that the rung is $O(a^{-N})$ for every $N$. The four do not conflict: each fall is faster than any power of $a$, and Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}(c) is the weakest conditional bound and the only one proved in the text.

Two questions of an unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026) on this decay, the least level at which the form is positive on a window of given length and the size of the step in the level, are answered here from the book's own computations. Let $c^*(L)$ be the least level $c$ at which $Q$ is positive on real functions supported in an interval of length $L=2a$, that is, the least $c$ with $\lambda_{\min}(c,L/2)\ge0$. For $c<\frac12$ the forms $Q_\varepsilon$ and $\lambda_{\min}(c,a)$ are defined by the same formulas, only a finite window enters, and Theorem~\ref{yo:thm:damped}(iii) holds there with the same averaging proof, so $c^*(L)$ is well defined and a negative section at any $c$ bounds it from below. By Theorem~\ref{yo:thm:damped}(iii) and monotonicity in $a$, $c^*(L)$ is nondecreasing in $L$, by Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:damped} it tends to the supremum $\sigma_{\max}$ of the real parts of the zeros, and under RH it is at most $\frac12$ (proved). Legendre sections give unconditional lower bounds, not certified: $c^*(0.5)\ge0.2109$ (no prime power in the window), $c^*(1)\ge0.4999909$, and $c^*(L)>0.4999$ for $2\le L\le6$, since the sections have negative values from $-1.5\times10^{-4}$ to $-7.6\times10^{-4}$ at $c=\frac12-10^{-4}$ (computed). Table~\ref{dc:tab:cstar} sets these beside the converged values at $c=1$ and $c=\frac12$ of Table~\ref{dc:tab:ladder} and of Table~\ref{yo:tab:lam}. Piecewise constant cells on a window give instead positive values of order $M^{-2}$ in the number $M$ of cells at $c=\frac12$ (for example $1.0\times10^{-4}$ at $L=6$ and $M=160$); these are discretisation error and not eigenvalues, and a positive value from cells proves nothing.

The same form built from the explicit formula of the Davenport Heilbronn function \cite{DH36} (Section~\ref{ht:sec:dh}) is a control. At $c=\frac12$ it is positive to rounding for $L\le3.5$ and indefinite at $L=4$ (prime powers to $54$), with $\lambda_{\min}\le-0.76$, and the negative direction is concentrated near frequency $84.6$, within the resolution $2\pi/L$ of the ordinate of its first off line zero $0.808517+85.699348i$, while the sections for $\zeta$ stay at rounding level (computed). So a finite window sees an off line zero once $L$ is large enough for its height, which is the heuristic of Problem~\ref{dc:prob:visible}.

\begin{proposition}[A Schur bound for the step in the level]\label{dc:prop:schur}
Let $\frac12<c\le1$ and $0<\eta<c-\frac12$, and write $Q_c$ for the form at level $c$. On real functions supported in an interval of length $L$, the operator norm of $Q_{c-\frac12-\eta}-Q_{c-\frac12}$ is at most $\eta B_c(L)$, where for an absolute constant $C_0$
\[
B_c(L)\le2\sum_{n\le e^L}\frac{\Lambda(n)\log n}{n^{c-\eta}}+4L\int_0^L\cosh\tfrac x2\,e^{-(c-\eta-\frac12)x}\,dx+C_0.
\]
Consequently, if $\lambda_{\min}(c,L/2)\ge\mu>0$ then $\lambda_{\min}(c-\eta,L/2)\ge0$ for $0<\eta<c-\frac12$ with $\eta B_c(L)\le\mu$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The difference is the form of $(e^{\eta|x|}-1)e^{-(c-\frac12)|x|}$ against $F=h*\tilde h$, and $|F|\le\|h\|_2^2$, $\|F\|_1\le L\|h\|_2^2$. Bound each term of the explicit formula by the Schur test. The prime terms give $2\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}(n^\eta-1)n^{-(c-\frac12)}$ with $n^\eta-1\le\eta\log n\,n^\eta$, the pole kernel gives the integral, and in the archimedean kernel the singularity of order $1/|x|$ at the origin is cancelled by $e^{\eta|x|}-1=O(\eta|x|)$, which needs only the constant $C_0$.
\end{proof}

The bound is proved but it measures the method. It is of size $e^{(1-c+\eta)L}L/(1-c)$ for $c<1$, while on Legendre sections of degree $60$ to $120$ the true derivative in the level has eigenvalues in $[-1.6,4.2]$ at $c=1$ and in $[-3.2,8.8]$ at $c=\frac34$ for $L=2$, $4$, $6$, $8$, against $15$ to $209$ and $20$ to $703$ for the first two terms of $B_c(L)$ at $\eta=10^{-3}$ (computed); any exponential size can only come from high frequencies (heuristic). A descent by this bound alone therefore loses a factor of order $e^{-(1-c)L}$ at each step, and it is not a route to the half plane.

Table~\ref{dc:tab:bracket} runs the proof of Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:ladder}(b) with numbers. Write $\psi(\theta)=v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(\theta)-v_{1/2+\varepsilon}(0)$. The infimum $c_1$ of $\psi(\theta)/\min(\theta^2,1)$ is the limit $v_{1/2+\varepsilon}''(0)/2$ at the origin, on a grid of $600$ points to height $40$ and with the assumption that $\psi$ stays above its minimum on $[20,40]$ beyond $40$, and $\max_\kappa\kappa^2(1-\ell_0(\kappa))=0.5348$ at $\kappa=1.54$, where $\ell_0(1)=0.5726$ and $\ell_0(2)=0.8806$. So the lower constant of the theorem is $c=2c_1\cdot0.5348=0.2167\,\bar c_\varepsilon$ for $a\ge1.54$. Using at every radius at once the bound $P(|\theta|\le r)\le\ell_0(ar)$ for the probability density $|\hat h|^2/\|\hat h\|_2^2$ gives the layer cake bound $\lambda_{\min}-2b_{1/2+\varepsilon}\ge2\int_0^\infty\tilde\psi'(r)\,(1-\ell_0(ar))\,dr$, with $\tilde\psi$ the nondecreasing minorant of $\psi$ in $|\theta|$; it is $0.666\,\bar c_\varepsilon$ at $a=4$, $\varepsilon=\frac12$ ($3.66\times10^{-4}$), and tends to $8I_1/\pi^2\,\bar c_\varepsilon=0.683\,\bar c_\varepsilon$ as $a\to\infty$, with $I_1=\int_0^\infty\kappa\,(1-\ell_0(\kappa))\,d\kappa=0.8425$. These lower bounds are floating point computations on a grid, not certificates, and for $\varepsilon<\frac12$ the identity $Q_\varepsilon(h)/\|h\|_2^2=2\langle v_{1/2+\varepsilon}\rangle_h$ behind them presupposes that the level is to the right of the zeros.

The cosine trial function of the proof gives upper bounds, computed from the prime side (Table~\ref{dc:tab:bracket}, last column). At $\varepsilon=\frac12$ the ratio of its scaled excess to $\bar c_\varepsilon$ is $1.52$, $1.37$, $1.17$ and $1.11$ at $a=1$, $2$, $4$ and $6$, and $a$ times its remainder over $\bar c_\varepsilon$ is $0.65$ to $0.68$ for $3\le a\le8$, a positive correction of order $a^{-1}$. At $\varepsilon=0.2$ and $0.1$ the remainder oscillates in $a$ ($a$ times it over $\bar c_\varepsilon$ lies between $1.4$ and $2.2$ at $\varepsilon=0.2$ and between $2.3$ and $4.8$ at $\varepsilon=0.1$ for $1.5\le a\le8$), because the tail of $|\hat h|^2$, of order $\cos^2(a\theta)/\theta^4$, meets the peaks of $v_{1/2+\varepsilon}$ at the zeros, of height $1/\varepsilon$, and the cosine is a poor trial function for small $\varepsilon$.
