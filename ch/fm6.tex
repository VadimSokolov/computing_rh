\section{Open problems}\label{ch:fmopen}

Problems~\ref{fm:prob:sbp} and~\ref{fm:prob:powers} concern the variable $e^{2X}$ of Section~\ref{ch:fmexp}, where no hypothesis on the zeros enters, and the last two concern the spin chains of Section~\ref{ch:fmchain}.

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:shoulder}
Mixed shoulders. Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:shoulder} decides the symmetric laws on $[-1,1]$ whose density is concave on $[0,1]$ and those whose density is convex there and not linear on its support: by \eqref{fm:eq:sine} the question is whether the sine transform of the slope $g=-p'$ has only real zeros, and the theorem answers it when $g$ is monotone. Decide the slopes that first rise and then fall. One class is settled: if $g$ is symmetric about $\frac12$, then $p(0)=\int_0^1g=1$, because $1=2\int_0^1p=2\int_0^1xg(x)\,dx=\int_0^1g$ by the symmetry of $g$, so $u\mapsto g(\frac12+u)$ is a probability density on $[-\frac12,\frac12]$; and $X\overset d=\frac12U+B$, with $B$ independent of $U$ and distributed with that density, since the density of $\frac12U+B$ has slope $g$ on $(0,1)$. As $\sin(z/2)/(z/2)$ has only real zeros, $X$ is then ferromagnetic exactly when $B$ is. For the beta slopes $g(y)\propto y^a(1-y)^b$, $a,b\ge0$, this decides the diagonal $a=b$, where $B$ is a symmetric beta law and $X$ is ferromagnetic (Section~\ref{fm:sec:examples}), and Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:shoulder} decides the edges: $X$ is ferromagnetic for $b=0$, where $p$ is concave, and not for $a=0<b$, where $p$ is convex and not linear. The rest of the quadrant is open. On the half line the slope of the P\'olya law rises and then falls (Section~\ref{fm:sec:polyamix}), and RH is the statement that its sine transform has only real zeros.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:mellin}
Lines of zeros of the Mellin transform of a clock. For which ferromagnetic laws $X$ does $\mathcal D_X$ of Section~\ref{ch:fmmellin} have nonreal zeros, all of them on one vertical line? The uniform pair $\pi U+\pi U'$ has this property if and only if RH holds, by \eqref{fm:eq:Dpair}. The sums of one, four and six independent copies of $\pi U$ do not have it (Proposition~\ref{fm:prop:kspins}; for four and six computed, Section~\ref{ch:fmmellin}), and neither do the pairs $X_\varepsilon+X_\varepsilon'$ of Section~\ref{ch:fmmellin} for small $\varepsilon>0$ (computed), so no theorem whose hypotheses hold for every symmetric density on an interval that is nondecreasing in $|t|$ can give it. Is the uniform pair, up to scale, the only pair $X+X'$ with $X$ in the class of Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:ushaped} that has it? A first step is the first order motion of the zeros along the densities $\frac1{2\pi}+\varepsilon g$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$, which is linear in $g$: is there an even $g\ne0$ with $\int g=0$ along which every zero $\frac{\rho_k-1}2$ keeps its real part to first order? This problem is based on \cite{P26tus,P26lyzeta}.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:gs}
The Griffiths Simon class. Newman's theorem places the P\'olya law in the class of Ellis, Monroe and Newman (Section~\ref{fm:sec:polyanec}). Does it belong to the Griffiths Simon class of Section~\ref{fm:sec:def}? The laws of that class are ferromagnetic, so a positive answer would imply RH, and under Ingham's hypothesis the spins of any approximating system would have to be coupled (Section~\ref{fm:sec:nosqrt}).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:split}
Splitting the zeros. Assume RH, so that $\varphi_X(t)=\prod_\gamma(1-t^2/\gamma^2)$ over the positive ordinates, with no Gaussian factor by Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:nosqrt}. Can the ordinates be split into two nonempty sets $A$ and $A'$ such that $\prod_{\gamma\in A}(1-t^2/\gamma^2)$ and $\prod_{\gamma\in A'}(1-t^2/\gamma^2)$ are both characteristic functions? This is the same as writing $X=D+R$ with $D$ and $R$ independent, ferromagnetic and not $0$, and by Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:nosqrt} both sets are then infinite. Under Ingham's hypothesis no three ordinates are in arithmetic progression, so no piece is a scaled fair sign or a scaled uniform variable, whose zeros form arithmetic progressions.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:zc}
Positivity of the zero completed laws. The laws $f_K$ of Section~\ref{fm:sec:zerocompleted} are proved to be ferromagnetic probability densities for $K\le16$ by the Maxwell construction, and for every $K\le200$ and for $K=300$, $400$, $600$ and $800$ by a computer assisted proof in ball arithmetic. Compute the threshold $v^*(K)$ near $K=5000$, where the linear and the power law fits of the ratio $v^*(K)/2\beta_K$ part; the cancellation there calls for a working precision of tens of thousands of bits. Is $v^*(K)<2\beta_K$ for every $K$? If so, every $f_K$ is a ferromagnetic probability density by Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:zc}(ii), and with no hypothesis their limit in law, with characteristic function $e^{-\beta_\infty\theta^2}\prod_k(1-\theta^2/\gamma_k^2)$ over the zeros on the critical line, is a ferromagnetic law by Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:closed}; by Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:zc}(iii) it is the P\'olya law exactly when RH holds.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:omega}
The cosine constant. By Section~\ref{fm:sec:tilts}, $\Omega\le\frac12$ with no hypothesis, $\Omega\le\frac38$ if the half plane $\re s>\frac78$ announced in October 2026 is free of zeros (Section~\ref{sec:ch25:open}), and RH holds if and only if $\Omega=0$. Is the set of $a\ge0$ for which $\cosh(at)\,p$ is ferromagnetic an interval? Improve these bounds by computation, as computations with the zeros have improved de Bruijn's bound $\Lambda\le\frac12$ on the de Bruijn Newman constant, to $\Lambda\le0.2$ \cite{PT21}. A first case is $a=\sqrt{0.1}$, below both bounds: the law $\cosh(\sqrt{0.1}\,t)\,p$ is as close to $p$ as the flow at $\lambda=0.2$, which is ferromagnetic, and deciding it is a computation with the zeros of $\frac12(\xi(s+a)+\xi(s-a))$, of the kind that gave the bound $0.2$ for the flow.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:sbp}
The size bias principle for real shapes. Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:sbp} is proved for integer shapes. Does it hold for all real shapes $t_n>0$? For $K=1$ it is the gamma identity, with $G=0$. Integer shapes enter the proof twice, through the divided difference formula for the density of $Y$ and through Lemma~\ref{fm:lem:sign}, and together they show that on each gap $(\tau_k,\tau_{k+1})$ the phase of the Laplace transform of $Y_q$ is nonincreasing and falls by exactly $q$. Is this still true for real shapes? With it the rest of the proof goes through, provided the estimates of the transform near the points $-\tau_n$ and at infinity also hold.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:powers}
The powers of $e^X$. For $2<|\beta|$ below the HCM threshold, which lies between $5.5791395$ and $\pi/0.563096=5.57914219\ldots$ (Proposition~\ref{fm:prop:hcm}), is $e^{\beta X}$ infinitely divisible, self decomposable or a GGC? Below the threshold it has no HCM density, which leaves the GGC property open, and Theorem~\ref{fm:thm:sd} gives self decomposability only at $|\beta|=2$. Is the HCM threshold exactly $\pi/v_m=5.5791396$? That requires $h\ge0$ on the open strip $0<v<v_m$, where $h(0,v)\to0$ as $v\uparrow v_m$, so a certificate must replace the balls with positive lower bounds near $iv_m$ by a local argument.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:profile}
The depth profile of Knauf's chain. Prove Conjecture~\ref{fm:conj:R}, or find the strips on which it holds. By Proposition~\ref{fm:prop:perron}, $R_N$ measures, heuristically, how far the profile $w_N=c_N/\varphi$ of Section~\ref{fm:sec:perron} is from being regular on the scale of the integers. The fraction $a/m$ is a child of $a/(m-a)$ in the Calkin Wilf tree, and each step down the tree adds one to the sum of the partial quotients, the root $1/1=[1]$ having sum $1$; so by Proposition~\ref{fm:prop:stern}(i), for $m\ge2$, $c_N(m)$ is the number of $a$ prime to $m$, $1\le a<m$, for which $a/m=[0;b_1,\dots,b_k]$ has $b_1+\dots+b_k\le N+1$. Yao and Knuth proved that the mean of $b_1+\dots+b_k$ over $1\le a\le m$ is asymptotic to $\frac6{\pi^2}\log^2m$ \cite{YaoKnuth75}; over the $a$ prime to $m$, which are those counted here, a quadratic fit in $\log m$ to the computed means at $m=10^5,\dots,10^8$ has leading coefficient $0.6073$ (computed). The consequence of the conjecture does not decide RH (Remark~\ref{fm:rem:neutral}), and the convergence in Remark~\ref{fm:rem:neutral}(iii) would imply it.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{fm:prob:fareyly}
The Lee and Yang threshold of the Farey chain. Prove Conjecture~\ref{fm:conj:ly}. By Lemma~\ref{fm:lem:circle} its first half follows if, for every inverse temperature $\beta>2$, the words other than $A^N$ and $B^N$ carry less weight than these two for all large $N$, that is $\sum(2/\operatorname{tr}\varpi_1\cdots\varpi_N)^\beta<2$ over the other words. The computed $\beta^*(N)$ falls from $2.1056$ to $2.0525$ for $10\le N\le36$, and for $2<\beta\le\beta^*(N)$ the lemma does not decide. The second half asks that for every $\beta$ with $0<\beta<2$ some zeros leave the circle once $N$ is large; at the computed thresholds the zeros that leave it first are at the angle $\theta_N$, with $N\theta_N$ between $8.61$ and $9.95$ (computed; Table~\ref{fm:tab:ly} for $N\le32$).
\end{problem}
