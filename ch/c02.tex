\chapter{Evaluating the Xi function}\label{chap:evaluation}

Values of $\xi$ and $\xi'$ at complex arguments are the raw material of every later computation. The computations of Chapters~\ref{chap:measure} to \ref{chap:heat}, and many of those of Chapter~\ref{chap:probability}, start from such values at points $\tfrac12+\varepsilon+i\theta$ with $\varepsilon\ge0$. The reason lies in Chapter~\ref{chap:foundations}: the information about the zeros enters the Thorin condition through analyticity, which a computation must supply by evaluating $\xi$ in the complex plane (Section~\ref{sec:ch3:diag}). Section~\ref{ch:intro} named three ways of evaluating $\xi$. The third, the explicit formula, works from the primes and is a poor evaluator near the critical line: as the basepoint decreases to one its prime sum becomes ill conditioned, below one it diverges (Section~\ref{ch:motivation}), and Selberg's smoothed form reaches resolution $\varepsilon$ only at a cost exponential in $1/\varepsilon$ (Section~\ref{ch:sin2more}).

Section~\ref{ch:polya} develops the first, the P\'olya route: the trapezoid rule on the whole line applied to P\'olya's integral, organised as two fixed polynomials evaluated in ball arithmetic, and made practical at large height by tilting the contour into the strip where the kernel is analytic. Section~\ref{ch:classical} describes the second, the Riemann Siegel formula and the Odlyzko Sch\"onhage algorithm, together with Turing's method for certifying a count, and compares them with the P\'olya route on a Lehmer pair near height $7005$ and on the count at height $1000$, which the P\'olya route reads from the Thorin mass at the basepoint one and the argument along a horizontal segment. The comparison ends in a division of labour, stated at the end of Section~\ref{sec:ch5:count}: the classical evaluators, faster by orders of magnitude, count and locate the zeros at height, and the P\'olya route supplies what Chapters~\ref{chap:measure} to \ref{chap:heat} need, Thorin densities off the line and their distances to the measure demanded by RH. Section~\ref{ch:worked} then runs the untilted route once at low height and prints every intermediate number.
