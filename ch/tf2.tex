\section{The basepoint family: a Poisson semigroup to the right of the zeros}\label{tf:sec:poisson}

For real $\varepsilon$ and $\theta$ such that $\tfrac12+\varepsilon+i\theta$ is not a zero of $\xi$, let
\[
\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)=\frac1\pi\,\re\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\big(\tfrac12+\varepsilon+i\theta\big)
\]
be the density of~\eqref{eq:rho}, now for every real $\varepsilon$, and let $P_a(x)=a/(\pi(a^2+x^2))$ for real $a\ne0$ be the Poisson kernel of Section~\ref{ch:clock}, with $P_a*P_b=P_{a+b}$ for $a,b>0$. For $\varepsilon>0$ the shifted clock at the basepoint $\tfrac12+\varepsilon$ has Thorin density $\rho_\varepsilon$ in the chart $\theta=\sqrt z$ whenever it is a GGC and no zero lies on the line $\re s=\tfrac12+\varepsilon$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson}). The function $(\theta,\varepsilon)\mapsto\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)$ is harmonic off the zeros, odd in $\varepsilon$ by the functional equation, and Hadamard's product gives the absolutely convergent expansion
\begin{equation}\label{tf:eq:poissonform}
\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)=\sum_\rho P_{\varepsilon-(\beta-\frac12)}(\theta-\gamma),\qquad \varepsilon\ne\beta-\tfrac12,
\end{equation}
over all zeros with multiplicity (Proposition~\ref{prop:struct}). A zero with $\beta-\tfrac12<\varepsilon$ contributes a positive Cauchy density, one with $\beta-\tfrac12>\varepsilon$ a negative one.

\begin{theorem}[Where the Poisson semigroup holds]\label{tf:thm:poisson}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $\varepsilon_0>\beta-\tfrac12$ for every zero, then $\rho_{\varepsilon_0+a}=P_a*\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ for every $a>0$, and $\rho_\varepsilon>0$ for every $\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon_0$. This holds for every $\varepsilon_0\ge\tfrac12$, with no hypothesis.
\item[(b)] If $\varepsilon_0\ge0$ lies at positive distance from the set $\{\beta-\tfrac12\}$ and some zero has $\beta-\tfrac12>\varepsilon_0$, then $\rho_{\varepsilon_0+a}\ne P_a*\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ for some $a>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Every term of~\eqref{tf:eq:poissonform} at $\varepsilon_0$ is a Cauchy density $P_c(\theta-\gamma)$ with $c=\varepsilon_0-(\beta-\tfrac12)>0$, and $P_a*P_c=P_{a+c}$. The terms are positive, so the convolution passes through the sum by monotone convergence. For $\varepsilon_0\ge\tfrac12$, $c\ge1-\beta>0$. (b) Now $|c|\ge c_0>0$ for every term, and $|c|<1$ because $0\le\varepsilon_0<\tfrac12$, so $|P_c(\theta-\gamma)|\le\min\big((\pi c_0)^{-1},(\pi(\theta-\gamma)^2)^{-1}\big)$, and by the Riemann von Mangoldt formula the sum is $O(\log(2+|\theta|))$. Hence $w(\theta,a)=(P_a*\rho_{\varepsilon_0})(\theta)$ is defined and harmonic in $a>0$. If $w(\cdot,a)=\rho_{\varepsilon_0+a}$ for every $a>0$, then off the zeros $\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)$ would agree with a function harmonic on the whole half plane $\{\varepsilon>\varepsilon_0\}$, and in particular bounded near every point $(\gamma,\beta-\tfrac12)$ with $\beta-\tfrac12>\varepsilon_0$. But near a zero, $\xi'/\xi(s)=m_\rho/(s-\rho)+O(1)$, so $\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)$ is unbounded there.
\end{proof}

Part (a) is the semigroup of Remark~\ref{cp:rem:semigroups}, and (b) is its converse (a rightmost zero need not exist).


\begin{proposition}[defect formula]\label{tf:prop:defect}
Let $a>0$, $\varepsilon_0\ge\tfrac12$ and $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_0-a\ge0$, and suppose no zero has $\beta-\tfrac12=\varepsilon_1$. Then $P_a*\rho_{\varepsilon_1}$ is a function and
\[
\rho_{\varepsilon_0}-P_a*\rho_{\varepsilon_1}=\sum_{\varepsilon_1<\beta-\frac12<\varepsilon_0}\Bigl[P_{\varepsilon_0-(\beta-\frac12)}(\cdot-\gamma)+P_{2a-(\varepsilon_0-(\beta-\frac12))}(\cdot-\gamma)\Bigr]\ \ge0,
\]
where the two widths of each term add up to $2a$. Status: proved.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $b=\beta-\tfrac12<\tfrac12\le\varepsilon_0$ and use~\eqref{tf:eq:poissonform}. A zero with $b<\varepsilon_1$ contributes $P_{\varepsilon_1-b}$ to $\rho_{\varepsilon_1}$, and $P_a*P_{\varepsilon_1-b}=P_{\varepsilon_0-b}$, the same as to $\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$. A zero with $\varepsilon_1<b<\varepsilon_0$ contributes $P_{\varepsilon_0-b}$ to $\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ and $-P_{b-\varepsilon_1}$ to $\rho_{\varepsilon_1}$, and $P_a*(-P_{b-\varepsilon_1})=-P_{a+b-\varepsilon_1}$, so the difference is $P_{\varepsilon_0-b}+P_{a+b-\varepsilon_1}$ with widths adding to $\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1+a=2a$. The convolution passes through the sum by Tonelli: $P_a*|P_c|=P_{a+|c|}$, and $\sum_\rho P_{a+|c_\rho|}(\theta-\gamma)$ converges because the widths lie in $[a,a+\varepsilon_0+\tfrac12]$ and the zeros are counted by the Riemann von Mangoldt formula.
\end{proof}

The identity is the pointwise form of the mass deficit of Proposition~\ref{cx:prop:deficit}. An off line zero shows up as a pair of positive Cauchy bumps of total width $2a$ that the forward model $P_a*\rho_{\varepsilon_1}$ cannot explain. The proposition is from an unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026).

\begin{proposition}[a local bound from the verified height]\label{tf:prop:local}
Let $H=3\cdot10^{12}$, so that every zero with $|\gamma|\le H$ has $\beta=\tfrac12$ (Platt and Trudgian \cite{PT21}), let $0<a<\tfrac12$, and suppose no zero has $\beta=1-a$. For $|\theta|\le H/2$,
\[
0\le\rho_{1/2}(\theta)-\bigl(P_a*\rho_{1/2-a}\bigr)(\theta)\le\frac{8a}\pi\sum_{|\gamma|>H}\frac1{\gamma^2}\le7.54\times10^{-12}\,a .
\]
Status: proved; the numerical constant is computed from the bound for $N(T)$ of \cite{HSW22}, and the error terms of that bound change only its sixth digit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{tf:prop:defect} with $\varepsilon_0=\tfrac12$ and $\varepsilon_1=\tfrac12-a>0$ only zeros with $|\gamma|>H$ contribute, since the zeros with $|\gamma|\le H$ have $b=0<\varepsilon_1$. Each contributes at most $2a/(\pi(\theta-\gamma)^2)\le8a/(\pi\gamma^2)$, because the widths add up to $2a$, $P_c(x)\le c/(\pi x^2)$ and $|\theta-\gamma|\ge|\gamma|/2$. The tail sum follows from the Riemann von Mangoldt formula with the explicit bound of \cite{HSW22}.
\end{proof}

It is a statement about the true function and not a certificate for the deconvolution of the data, which is a global object, and it gives no zero free region. Positivity of $\rho_{1/2}$ is already Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:poisson}(a); global positivity of the deconvolution is the Riemann hypothesis (Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}(b)).

\begin{theorem}[Deconvolution form of RH]\label{tf:thm:deconv}
Let $\varepsilon_0>\beta-\tfrac12$ for every zero (for instance $\varepsilon_0\ge\tfrac12$), and let $0\le\varepsilon_1<\varepsilon_0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] There is a positive measure $\nu$ on $\R$ with $\int(1+x^2)^{-1}\nu(dx)<\infty$ and $\rho_{\varepsilon_0}=P_{\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1}*\nu$ if and only if $\beta-\tfrac12\le\varepsilon_1$ for every zero. When it exists, $\nu$ is unique: it is $\rho_{\varepsilon_1}\,d\theta$ if $\beta-\tfrac12<\varepsilon_1$ for every zero, and otherwise the vague limit of $\rho_\varepsilon\,d\theta$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow\varepsilon_1$, which carries point masses $m_\rho\delta_\gamma$ at the zeros with $\beta-\tfrac12=\varepsilon_1$.
\item[(b)] In particular RH holds if and only if $\rho_{\varepsilon_0}=P_{\varepsilon_0}*\mu$ for a positive measure $\mu$ (with $\int(1+x^2)^{-1}\mu(dx)<\infty$, which is automatic since $P_{\varepsilon_0}*\mu=\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ is finite), and then $\mu=\sum_\gamma\delta_\gamma$, the sum over the ordinates of all zeros, of both signs, with multiplicity.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If every zero has $\beta-\tfrac12<\varepsilon_1$, Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:poisson}(a) gives $\nu=\rho_{\varepsilon_1}\,d\theta$. If every zero has $\beta-\tfrac12\le\varepsilon_1$ with equality for some, write $\rho_{\varepsilon_0}=P_{\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon}*\rho_\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon_1<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$ and let $\varepsilon\downarrow\varepsilon_1$: by~\eqref{tf:eq:poissonform} the terms of the zeros with $\beta-\tfrac12<\varepsilon_1$ converge to Cauchy densities and those with $\beta-\tfrac12=\varepsilon_1$ to point masses at their ordinates, and the sum over the zeros passes to the limit by the domination of the discussion after Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline}, so $\rho_\varepsilon\,d\theta$ converges vaguely to a positive measure $\nu$, the sum of these limits. Then $P_{\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1}*\nu=\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ term by term, because $P_{\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1}*P_{\varepsilon_1-(\beta-\frac12)}=P_{\varepsilon_0-(\beta-\frac12)}$ and $P_{\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1}*\delta_\gamma=P_{\varepsilon_0-(\beta-\frac12)}(\cdot-\gamma)$ when $\beta-\tfrac12=\varepsilon_1$, all terms being positive; in particular $\int(1+x^2)^{-1}\nu(dx)<\infty$. Conversely, suppose $\nu$ exists and some zero has $\beta-\tfrac12>\varepsilon_1$. The Poisson integral $u(\theta,\varepsilon)=(P_{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_1}*\nu)(\theta)$ is harmonic on $\{\varepsilon>\varepsilon_1\}$, and for $\varepsilon>\varepsilon_0$ it equals $P_{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_0}*\rho_{\varepsilon_0}=\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)$ by Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:poisson}(a). On the connected set $\{\varepsilon>\varepsilon_1\}$ minus the zeros, $u-\rho_\varepsilon$ is harmonic and vanishes for $\varepsilon>\varepsilon_0$, so $u=\rho_\varepsilon$ there. Then $\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)$ is bounded near a zero with $\beta-\tfrac12>\varepsilon_1$, which contradicts the pole of $\xi'/\xi$. Uniqueness is Proposition~\ref{cd:prop:unique} with $a=\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1$. Part (b) is the case $\varepsilon_1=0$: $\beta-\tfrac12\le0$ for every zero means $\beta=\tfrac12$ for every zero by the symmetry $\rho\mapsto1-\rho$, and~\eqref{tf:eq:poissonform} at $\beta=\tfrac12$ gives $\mu$.
\end{proof}

The \emph{if} direction of (b), and the atoms at a boundary line in (a), are Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson}, Proposition~\ref{prop:struct} and Theorem~\ref{cp:thm:transfer}; what this chapter adds is the \emph{only if} direction. Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv} makes the obstruction of Section~\ref{ag:sec:exactly} exact: $\sigma_{\max}-\tfrac12$ is the depth to which the Thorin density at a basepoint beyond the zeros can be deconvolved into a positive measure. Two remarks qualify it. First, positivity of $\rho_{\varepsilon_1}$ is necessary for deconvolvability to $\varepsilon_1$ but not sufficient: below $\sigma_{\max}-\tfrac12$ the function $\rho_{\varepsilon_1}$ may well be positive (Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline} and Section~\ref{tf:sec:window}), and still $P_{\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1}*\rho_{\varepsilon_1}\ne\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$. Second, deconvolving by $P_a$ multiplies the weight $\Lambda(n)n^{-\alpha}$ of the frequency $\log n$ by $n^{a}$, so below basepoint one the prime sums need cancellation (Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:below}; the deconvolution is unbounded by Proposition~\ref{cd:prop:range}(b)).

\subsection{Rigidity on a window}\label{tf:sec:rigid}

The deconvolution of Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv} is rigid: it cannot be matched on one window without being matched everywhere. The results of this subsection are from an unrefereed note of Polson (November 2026). They are the analytic continuation step of the proof of Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv} applied on a window, and they are proved. Let $\varepsilon_0>\beta-\tfrac12$ for every zero and $0\le\varepsilon_1<\varepsilon_0$ be as there, and put $c=\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1$. No zero lies on the line $\re s=\tfrac12+\varepsilon_0$, so $\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ is real analytic on $\R$. For $C>0$ let
\[
\mathcal M_C=\bigl\{\mu\ge0\text{ on }\R:\ \mu((-T,T))\le C(1+T)\log(2+T)\ \text{for all }T>0\bigr\}.
\]
By the Riemann von Mangoldt formula the measures $\rho_\varepsilon\,d\theta$ for $\varepsilon_1\le\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ and the measure $\nu$ of Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}(a) lie in $\mathcal M_C$ once $C$ is large enough; fix such a $C$.

\begin{lemma}[Compactness]\label{tf:lem:compact}
$\mathcal M_C$ is compact for vague convergence. If $\mu_n\to\mu$ vaguely in $\mathcal M_C$, then $P_c*\mu_n\to P_c*\mu$ uniformly on compact sets, for every $c>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The masses of bounded intervals are uniformly bounded, so Helly's selection theorem gives vague sequential compactness, and the defining inequalities pass to vague limits on open intervals by lower semicontinuity. For $\theta$ in a compact set $K$, $P_c(\theta-x)\le c_K/(1+x^2)$, and summing over the dyadic ranges $2^kR\le|x|<2^{k+1}R$ shows that $\int_{|x|>R}(1+x^2)^{-1}d\mu\le C'\log R/R$ uniformly over $\mathcal M_C$. On $|x|\le R$, with $\mu(\{\pm R\})=0$, the integrand is continuous and the measures converge vaguely. Equicontinuity of $\theta\mapsto P_c(\theta-x)$ gives uniform convergence on $K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Window rigidity]\label{tf:thm:rigid}
Let $W$ be a nonempty open interval and
\[
D_W(c)=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal M_C}\ \sup_{\theta\in W}\bigl|(P_c*\mu)(\theta)-\rho_{\varepsilon_0}(\theta)\bigr|.
\]
The infimum is attained, and $D_W(c)=0$ if and only if $\beta-\tfrac12\le\varepsilon_1$ for every zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Attainment follows from Lemma~\ref{tf:lem:compact}: extract a vague limit of a minimising sequence in $\mathcal M_C$ and use uniform convergence on the compact closure of $W$. If $\beta-\tfrac12\le\varepsilon_1$ for every zero, the measure $\nu$ of Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}(a) gives $D_W(c)=0$. Conversely let $D_W(c)=0$, attained at $\mu$. Then $P_c*\mu=\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ on $W$. The left side is the restriction to the line $a=c$ of the harmonic function $(a,\theta)\mapsto P_a*\mu$, hence real analytic in $\theta$, and so is the right side, so the two agree on $\R$. By Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}(a), $\beta-\tfrac12\le\varepsilon_1$ for every zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[RH from any window]\label{tf:cor:window}
Let $\varepsilon_0\ge\tfrac12$ and let $W$ be any open interval. Then RH holds if and only if $D_W(\varepsilon_0)=0$, the case $\varepsilon_1=0$. In particular, if RH fails, the positive deconvolution to the critical line misfits the data on every window, however far the window lies from the offending zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Why this is not yet a proof]\label{tf:rem:window}
Rigidity is qualitative. If $\rho_0=\beta_0+i\gamma_0$ is an off line zero, the misfit on a window at height $Y$ is positive but can be astronomically small, since it is transmitted by analytic continuation across the distance $|\gamma_0-Y|$ (Corollary~\ref{tf:cor:hidden} makes this precise for one quadruple). An effective version, $D_W\ge\delta(\beta_0-\tfrac12,|\gamma_0-Y|)$ with explicit $\delta$, together with a computation showing $D_W<\delta$, would exclude off line zeros up to a given height. Heuristically such a window exclusion test cannot reach every height without further structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Stability of positive deconvolutions]\label{tf:thm:stab}
Let $\beta-\tfrac12\le\varepsilon_1$ for every zero, let $\mu_n\in\mathcal M_C$, and let $\sup_{\theta\in W}|P_c*\mu_n-\rho_{\varepsilon_0}|\to0$ on some open interval $W$. Then $\mu_n\to\nu$ vaguely, where $\nu$ is the measure of Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}(a).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every subsequence has a further subsequence converging vaguely to some $\mu\in\mathcal M_C$ (Lemma~\ref{tf:lem:compact}), and then $P_c*\mu=\rho_{\varepsilon_0}$ on $W$. As in Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:rigid} this extends to $\R$, and the uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}(a) gives $\mu=\nu$.
\end{proof}

So any algorithm that produces positive measures in $\mathcal M_C$ whose blurred version fits the data on some window, EM included, converges to the Thorin measure. This is a consistency statement under exact data and the exact far field; in the computations of Section~\ref{cx:sec:blind} the far field is modelled, the hypotheses fail, and the theorem is a guide and not a guarantee.

\subsection{The smoothed prime sums at a basepoint}

\begin{proposition}[The smoothed prime sums at a basepoint]\label{tf:prop:mellin}
Let $\chi$ be a primitive nonprincipal character, $N>0$, and $s=\alpha+ib$ such that $s-k$ is not a zero of $L(s,\chi)$ for any integer $k\ge0$. Put
\[
E(N,s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\chi(n)\Lambda(n)n^{-s}e^{-n/N}+\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi),
\]
the difference between the smoothed prime sum and $-L'/L(s,\chi)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{tf:eq:mellin}
\begin{aligned}
E(N,s)={}&-\sum_\rho m_\rho\,\Gamma(\rho-s)N^{\rho-s}\\
&+\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}\Big(-\frac{L'}{L}\Big)(s-k,\chi)N^{-k},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ runs over all zeros of $L(s,\chi)$, trivial ones included, with multiplicity $m_\rho$. Hence, for fixed $s$, $E(N,s)=O(N^{\sigma_{\max}(\chi)-\alpha})+O(N^{-1})$ as $N\to\infty$, where $\sigma_{\max}(\chi)$ is the supremum of $\beta$ over the nontrivial zeros $\beta+i\gamma$ of $L(s,\chi)$. Under GRH the sum over the nontrivial zeros is $N^{1/2-\alpha}F_s(\log N)$, where $F_s(x)=-\sum m_\rho\Gamma(\rho-s)e^{i(\gamma-b)x}$, summed over the nontrivial zeros $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$, is an almost periodic function none of whose coefficients vanishes; so $F_s(\log N)$ does not tend to zero, and for $\alpha<\tfrac32$ the error $E(N,s)$ is not $o(N^{1/2-\alpha})$.
\end{proposition}

% CHECK: Davenport pinpoint changed from Chapters 16 and 17 (source) to Chapters 16 and 19 (review of 17 October 2026, MINOR at L132, medium confidence): Chapter 16 is N(T,chi) with the local formula for L'/L, Chapter 19 the explicit formula for psi(x,chi); to be confirmed by the citation verifier.
\begin{proof}
This is the explicit formula for the smoothed prime sum (compare \cite[Chapter~19]{Davenport}), derived here for the order of the error. Insert $e^{-x}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Gamma(w)x^{-w}dw$ with $c>\max(0,1-\alpha)$ and sum: the smoothed prime sum is $\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}(-L'/L)(s+w,\chi)\Gamma(w)N^w\,dw$. Move the contour to $\re w=-R$ with $R\to\infty$ along a sequence avoiding the poles; $\Gamma(w)$ decays like $e^{-\pi|\im w|/2}$ and $L'/L$ grows at most polynomially on suitable horizontal segments and vertical lines~\cite[Chapters 16 and 19]{Davenport}, so the integrals vanish in the limit. The poles are simple by the hypothesis on $s$. The residues are $(-L'/L)(s,\chi)$ at $w=0$, which is moved to the left side, $-m_\rho\Gamma(\rho-s)N^{\rho-s}$ at $w=\rho-s$, since $-L'/L$ has residue $-m_\rho$ at $\rho$, and $(-1)^k(k!)^{-1}(-L'/L)(s-k,\chi)N^{-k}$ at $w=-k$. The sum over $\rho$ converges absolutely and is dominated by the zeros with $|\gamma-b|$ small, because $|\Gamma(\rho-s)|$ decays exponentially in $|\gamma-b|$; the nontrivial zeros contribute $O(N^{\sigma_{\max}(\chi)-\alpha})$, the trivial zeros $O(N^{-\alpha})$, and $O(N^{-1-\alpha})$ for odd $\chi$, and the last sum is $O(N^{-1})$. Under GRH each nontrivial term is $|\Gamma(\rho-s)|N^{1/2-\alpha}$ times a phase, $\Gamma$ has no zeros, and the series defining $F_s$ converges absolutely, so $F_s$ is almost periodic in the sense of Bohr with the nonzero coefficients $-m_\rho\Gamma(\rho-s)$, and its mean square is the sum of the squares of their moduli, which is positive.
\end{proof}

So recovering the Thorin density at basepoint $\alpha$ to accuracy $\eta$ from the primes with this smoothing needs primes up to about $N\approx\eta^{-1/(\alpha-\sigma_{\max}(\chi))}$, which is unbounded as $\alpha\downarrow\sigma_{\max}(\chi)$. This is a heuristic reading of the proposition as the deconvolution of Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv} paid for in primes: the identity is for the weight $e^{-n/N}$ and does not bound every method that uses the primes up to $N$. Proposition~\ref{bp:prop:below} gives the same exponent for the compensated prime sums of $\xi$ (the book writes $\Theta$ for $\sigma_{\max}$ there), and Section~\ref{bp:eta:sec:levy} treats $L(s,\chi_{12})$, where no pole needs compensating.
