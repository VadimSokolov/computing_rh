\label{chap:es}

Chapters~\ref{chap:lo} and~\ref{chap:cx} write the Thorin measure at level $\theta$ as the Cauchy smoothing of the zeros at width $\theta-\tfrac12$ (under the Riemann hypothesis; in general the widths are $\theta-\sigma_\rho$), turn the passage from level $1$ to level $\theta$ into a deconvolution, and solve it by EM to about $10^{-3}$ in the positions of the zeros, with a certificate. At level $\tfrac12$, under the Riemann hypothesis, the Thorin measure is the counting measure of the zeros, the data are $u_1(v)=\pi^{-1}\Re(\xi'/\xi)(1+iv)$, and the deconvolution, in Fourier variables, is Weil's explicit formula: the multiplier $e^{|\omega|/2}$ that undoes the Cauchy kernel is the factor $\sqrt n$ that turns the weights $\Lambda(n)/n$ of the level one spectrum into the weights $\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n$ of the formula.

This chapter is based on \cite{P26es}. Its numbers were recomputed here from the mathematics, except those marked as computed by the author, which were not rerun; where a recomputation differed from the source the recomputed value is printed. The Lorentzian representation of $u_1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:struct}), the positivity criterion for the deconvolution (Theorem~\ref{tf:thm:deconv}), the explicit formula for even test functions (Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:weil}, Section~\ref{dc:sec:weil}), the EM, grid and certificate methods (Chapters~\ref{chap:lo} and~\ref{chap:cx}) and the survey of Cauchy deconvolution (Chapter~\ref{chap:cd}) are cited, not restated.

Notation. A level $\theta$ is the real part of $s=\theta+iv$, and $u_\theta(v)=\pi^{-1}\Re(\xi'/\xi)(\theta+iv)$; in the notation of Chapter~\ref{chap:tf} (equation~\eqref{eq:rho}) it is $\rho_{\theta-1/2}(v)$. For a zero $\rho=\sigma_\rho+i\gamma_\rho$ the width at level $1$ is $a_\rho=1-\sigma_\rho\in(0,1)$. The Cauchy density is $P_a(x)=(a/\pi)/(a^2+x^2)$, and $\mathfrak U_\theta=\sum_\rho\mathrm{Cauchy}(\gamma_\rho,\theta-\sigma_\rho)$ is the Thorin measure with no factor $\tfrac12$, as in Chapter~\ref{chap:cx}, so that $\mathfrak U_\theta=2\widetilde U_\theta$ with $\widetilde U_\theta$ of Theorem~\ref{lv:thm:semigroup}; $\gamma_E$ is Euler's constant and $\Lambda$ the von Mangoldt function. In the Bayesian sections $\tau$ is the standard deviation of each real coordinate of the noise. Certified is used only for the sign changes of Hardy's function $Z$ computed in ball arithmetic in Sections~\ref{es:sec:certpencil}, \ref{es:sec:above} and~\ref{es:sec:above13}; everything else done in floating point is computed. The results are these.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The explicit formula of Guinand and Weil is the Fourier side of the deconvolution from level $1$ to level $\tfrac12$: for every test function in $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, unconditionally, the zero side $\sum_\rho\int g\,e^{(\sigma_\rho-1/2)|\omega|}e^{-i\omega\gamma_\rho}d\omega$ equals the prime side with the pole term and the finite part of the Gamma factor; for even $g$ this is Theorem~\ref{dc:thm:weil}, and under the Riemann hypothesis it reads $\sum_\rho e^{-i\omega\gamma_\rho}=e^{|\omega|/2}(1+\widehat G)-\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}(\delta_{\log n}+\delta_{-\log n})$ (Theorem~\ref{es:thm:explicit}, proved; the reading is conditional). A Gaussian perturbation of the measure of total variation $1$ that data of accuracy $\varepsilon$ cannot see exists as soon as $0.72\,e^{-\Omega/2}\le2\varepsilon$ (Proposition~\ref{es:prop:resolution}, proved); that resolution $\delta$ needs the primes to $e^{\pi/\delta}$ is heuristic (Remark~\ref{es:rem:resolution}).
\item The tapered Fourier transform of $u_1$ on a window is a sum of damped exponentials, one per zero, the pole $e^{\Delta(-a_\rho+i(\gamma_\rho-c))}$ carrying both coordinates of the zero, plus a remainder of order $e^{-\ell^2\omega^2/2}$, with no Gamma terms (Theorem~\ref{es:thm:taper}, proved). The matrix pencil recovers the poles of an exact sum (Lemma~\ref{es:lem:pencil}, proved); its error analysis is heuristic. At height $200$ the eleven zeros within $10$ of the centre are placed to within $8\times10^{-10}$ in both coordinates, and a synthetic pair off the line at $60.089$ is read to seven digits (Section~\ref{es:sec:computation}, computed by the author, not rerun).
\item Posterior concentration on a window holds at the rate $\tau$ for zeros with separated ordinates, under explicit hypotheses (Theorem~\ref{es:thm:concentration}, proved); the hypotheses fail at the settings of the computations (Remark~\ref{es:rem:settings}, computed), and concentration cannot prove the hypothesis, since a finite computation settles finitely many zeros.
\item The functional equation makes the model singular: with one amplitude common to its two members, a pair at $\tfrac12\pm\delta$ enters the data through $\delta^2$ (Lemma~\ref{es:lem:even}, proved). The posterior of $\delta^2$ concentrates at the rate $\tau$, so the real part is resolved at the rate $\tau^{1/2}$ when the line is the truth; no method, neural networks included, separates $\delta=0$ from $\delta=c\,(\tau/\|t_\perp\|)^{1/2}$, $t_\perp$ the projected tangent of the pair, with error probabilities summing to less than $1-c^2/2-O(\tau)$; and the matched filter and the likelihood ratio test attain the scale $\tau^{1/2}$ (Theorem~\ref{es:thm:singular}, proved for data simulated from the model in a window without aliasing, not for the runs on the zeros of $\zeta$).
\item Bayes factors with the right alternative, the pair and not a single zero: under the line the Bayes factor of the line against the pair is of order $\tau^{-1/2}$ in probability, under a fixed pair it is exponentially small, and against a single zero alternative it is of order $\tau^{-1}$ (Theorem~\ref{es:thm:bf}, proved in the common amplitude model). For the zero at $201.264752$, at an assumed noise level and on the assumed design of the detection experiment, the computed $\log_{10}$ of the Bayes factor is $6.1$ against the pair and $11.8$ against the single zero alternative: the functional equation takes roughly the square root of the evidence (computed, an illustration).
\item An adaptive prior, the sine kernel process on the distinct ordinates at the Riemann and von Mangoldt density and a spike and slab on the pairs for the real parts, is a heuristic choice resting on a conjecture about the ordinates; the properties used here are proved (Remarks~\ref{es:rem:singular} and~\ref{es:rem:dpp}). A detection experiment compares the pencil and a plug in matched filter with the benchmark of Theorem~\ref{es:thm:singular}(iii) (computed).
\item The open problems of the source are worked: certification of the pencil's output by sign changes and a winding number (logic proved, eleven zeros near height $200$ computed), close pairs, the levels $\tfrac34$ and $0.6$ and the signed level $\theta$ transform (Proposition~\ref{es:prop:level}, proved), the level as a cost parameter, and many windows (173 zeros on $[100,400]$ from 31 windows, computed by the author).
\item A window at $c=3\times10^{12}+100$ lies inside the range $0<\gamma\le3\,000\,175\,332\,800$ where Platt and Trudgian \cite{PT21} verify the hypothesis, so the run is a demonstration near the limit of the verification: the data are values of $\xi'/\xi$ at $\Re s=0.6$ computed in ball arithmetic, no primes are used and no new zero is found. The pencil returns $52$ components; an independent scan of Hardy's function finds exactly $52$ certified sign changes there, and the one by one comparison with the pencil's ordinates has not been made. The selection of components in the source's runs presumes the real part $\tfrac12$ (Section~\ref{es:sec:above}, computed).
\item A window at $c=10^{13}+100$, above the verified height by a factor $3.3$, is run with the book's own pencil, whose selection uses the width of each pole: $53$ components, real parts within $5\times10^{-9}$ of $\tfrac12$ (computed, an uncertified estimate), every ordinate within $10^{-7}$ of one of $53$ certified sign changes of $Z$ and no certified sign change without a pencil ordinate. The count of zeros in the window and the real parts are not certified, and nothing is proved about any zero outside the $53$ (Section~\ref{es:sec:above13}).
\end{enumerate}
The open problems close the chapter.
