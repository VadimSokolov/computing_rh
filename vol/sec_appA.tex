\section{The P\'olya evaluators}\label{app:code}

Ball arithmetic is that of Arb \cite{Arb}, through its Python interface; reference values in floating point use \texttt{mpmath} \cite{mpmath}.
\label{app:code:eval}

The evaluator of Section~\ref{ch:polya} on the real $u$ axis works in ball arithmetic: it computes the node values $\Phi_j=\Phi(jh)$, $0\le j\le J$, from the theta series \eqref{eq:Phi}, and from them $P(E)+P(E^{-1})$ and $Q(E)-Q(E^{-1})$ with $E=e^{sh}$ and the polynomials $P$ and $Q$ of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}, the trapezoid approximations to $\xi(\frac12+s)$ and $\xi'(\frac12+s)$. The defaults are $240$ bits, $h=1/200$ and $|u|\le3$, so that $J=600$. Most runs use $420$ bits, the parameters of Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}; the Keiper Li coefficients of Section~\ref{ch:li} use $600$ bits, and the Selberg computation of Section~\ref{ch:sin2more} uses $300$.

The tilted evaluator implements Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt}. With the nodes $u_j=x_j+i\eta$, $x_j=jh$, $|j|\le J$, where $J=\lceil x_{\max}/h\rceil$ for the cut $|x|\le x_{\max}$, it returns the trapezoid sums of the two integrals of the proposition. The production runs evaluate each exponential directly, not by Horner's rule, whose ball radii grow (Section~\ref{sec:ch4:balls}). The Lehmer pair and its de Bruijn Newman flow use $\delta=0.01$, $256$ bits, $h=6\times10^{-4}$ and $|x|\le3.9$; the count at height $1000$ in Section~\ref{sec:ch5:count} uses $\delta=0.1$, $200$ bits, $h=0.004$ and $|x|\le4.2$; the Laplace transform of the clock for Sections~\ref{ch:clocklaw} and \ref{ch:boundaries} switches to the tilted evaluator with $\delta=0.1$, $320$ bits, $h=0.003$ and $|x|\le4.5$ when $|\im s|\ge120$; the runs of Table~\ref{tab:tilt} use other $\delta$, precision, $h$, cut and heights.

In both evaluators the radii of the returned balls bound only the rounding errors of the finite sums. Three errors lie outside them: the discretisation error of the trapezoid rule, bounded in Section~\ref{sec:ch4:trapezoid}; the cut of the axis at $|u|\le3$, where $\Phi<10^{-500}$, or at $|x|\le x_{\max}$ on the tilted line; and the truncation of the theta series, whose loop stops at the first term with midpoint below $2^{-b-20}$ at working precision $b$ and does not add the tail to the radius. The stopping test compares double precision numbers, so for $b\ge1055$ its threshold underflows to zero and the test is never met. The untilted route needs more than $1054$ bits for thirty digits above height about $840$ (Table~\ref{tab:ch4:budget}), and there the test has to be made in ball arithmetic. The runs in this book use at most $600$ bits and are not affected.
