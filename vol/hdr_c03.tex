\label{chap:measure}

The Thorin condition is RH (Proposition~\ref{prop:rigid}), and by Lagarias's criterion RH is the positivity of $\rho_\varepsilon$ at every resolution (Remark~\ref{rem:ch2:oneline}). This chapter asks what finite computations of $\rho_\varepsilon$ establish. On a window where the zeros are verified, the computed Thorin measure $\mu_\varepsilon$ converges to $U_{\RH}$, the discrete measure demanded by RH, at explicit rates as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, and the rate depends on the metric (Section~\ref{ch:distances}). An off line zero changes each of these distances in closed form and is most visible at resolutions comparable to its distance from the line (Section~\ref{ch:offline}). Without any assumption on the zeros below the window, the argument principle turns the cumulative Thorin mass into an exact count of all zeros and of the zeros to the right of the line of evaluation (Section~\ref{ch:turing}). Above the computed range every finite computation of these kinds remains consistent with a failure of the Thorin condition (Section~\ref{ch:finite}).
